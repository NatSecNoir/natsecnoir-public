The House Select Committee on the Strategic Competition Between the United States and the Chinese Communist Party requests information from Sequoia Capital about its investments in PRC artificial-intelligence, semiconductor, and quantum-computing companies and the national-security implications of its planned separation from Sequoia Capital China, which would operate as HongShan.

**What it does** The October 17, 2023 letter asks Sequoia to identify relevant investments since 2010; provide investment amounts and dates; describe business advice, control, risk assessment, and relationships with PRC government or Party entities; identify PRC-affiliated or state-backed funding; disclose applicable U.S. sanctions or trade restrictions; explain communications with limited partners; and describe responses to a future listing of a portfolio company on a sanctions or trade-restriction list. It also directs the preservation of hard-copy and electronic records.

**Who it affects** The requests address Sequoia Capital, Sequoia Capital China, affiliated investment vehicles and personnel, their portfolio companies, and limited partners. The letter specifically discusses EverSec, 4Paradigm, DJI, DeepGlint, and ByteDance, while seeking a broader inventory of PRC-linked investments in AI or machine learning, semiconductors, and quantum technologies.

**Why it matters** The committee argues that the announced split could reduce shared U.S. managerial and technological functions while leaving U.S. capital exposed to PRC companies connected to military modernization, surveillance, human-rights abuses, or other government priorities. It also questions whether the separation could affect the reach of forthcoming outbound-investment restrictions and enable reverse flows of emerging U.S. technology to HongShan.

**Key dates and numbers**
- October 17, 2023: date of the letter.
- March 2024: announced target for the separation of Sequoia Capital China, which would use the name HongShan.
- November 1, 2023: requested deadline for Sequoia’s response.
- Since 2010: period covered by the requested investment inventory.
- Approximately 50%: stated current share of Sequoia Capital China limited partners believed to be U.S.-based; the letter asks Sequoia to confirm the figure.
