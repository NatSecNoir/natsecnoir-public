The House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party asked the Navy Exchange to stop selling Lenovo computers and IT products to U.S. servicemembers and to explain its decision to do so.

**What it does** The October 5, 2023 letter from Chairman Mike Gallagher to Navy Exchange CEO Robert Bianchi argues that Lenovo products are linked to the Chinese Communist Party and the People’s Liberation Army and pose a cybersecurity concern. It requests removal of the products from the Navy Exchange’s discounted marketplace and asks Bianchi to provide a briefing on the sales decision.

**Who it affects** The request is directed at the Navy Exchange and concerns U.S. servicemembers who can purchase products through that marketplace. The document identifies Lenovo as the affected technology company and references the Department of Defense and Federal Trade Commission in connection with earlier security warnings.

**Why it matters** The letter connects procurement and retail availability of Chinese technology products with defense cybersecurity. It cites a 2016 Pentagon warning that Lenovo computers could introduce compromised hardware into the Defense Department, as well as a 2018 FTC caution concerning software installed on U.S.-bound Lenovo computers. The committee also links the requested action to the Department of Defense’s 2023 Cyber Strategy.

**Key dates and numbers**
- October 5, 2023: letter date.
- October 20, 2023: requested deadline for the Navy Exchange briefing.
- At least 10: Lenovo computer and IT products identified as available through the Navy Exchange.
- 2016: Pentagon warning cited in the letter.
- 2018: FTC caution cited in the letter.
