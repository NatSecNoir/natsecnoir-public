The House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party and the House Committee on Ways and Means request documents and answers from Ford about its proposed licensing partnership with Contemporary Amperex Technology Co. Limited (CATL), citing concerns about Chinese labor, technology, critical-mineral supply chains, and tax credits.

**What it does** The July 21, 2023 letter asks Ford CEO Jim Farley to provide the Ford-CATL licensing agreement and appendices in English and Chinese, related communications in their original language, and communications with the Biden Administration concerning the agreement and achievable tax credits. It also poses questions about Chinese nationals working at BlueOval Battery Park Michigan, visa use, battery-material sourcing, imports, iron phosphate, commercial-partner safeguards, and forced-labor due diligence.

**Who it affects** The requests directly affect Ford and its proposed Michigan battery project, CATL, CATL-related entities, and the federal administration involved in implementing relevant tax incentives. The lawmakers focus on CATL’s alleged continuing connections to Xinjiang Zhicun Lithium and related supply chains.

**Why it matters** The letter challenges Ford’s claims that the arrangement will create American jobs, advance domestic battery technology, and support sustainability and human rights. It argues that the structure could leave Ford reliant on PRC-controlled technology, employees, components, and critical minerals while potentially benefiting from the Section 30D clean vehicle credit, and raises concerns under the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act.

**Key dates and numbers**
- February 2023: Ford announced the partnership with CATL.
- April 17, 2023: The Committee on Ways and Means sent Ford an earlier letter.
- August 10, 2023: Deadline requested for documents, communications, and answers.
- Ford represented that the project would create at least 2,500 U.S. jobs; the letter says several hundred may go to PRC employees and that PRC employees may remain until approximately 2038.
- CATL reportedly divested a 23.6% stake in Xinjiang Zhicun Lithium, while a limited partnership later acquired 61.2%; CATL allegedly provided more than one-third of that partnership’s initial investment.
