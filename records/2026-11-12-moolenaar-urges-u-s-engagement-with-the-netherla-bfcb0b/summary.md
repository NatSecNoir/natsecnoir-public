The letter urges the Secretary of State to press the Netherlands to prevent ASML from shipping its NXT:1965i deep-ultraviolet immersion lithography system to Chinese semiconductor fabs and to coordinate a unilateral U.S. export-control backstop.

**What it does** The letter identifies ASML’s planned NXT:1965i shipments as a potential loophole in allied controls because existing parameters cover tools already on the market, while ASML is reviving a discontinued system. It asks the State Department to ensure the NXT:1965i is treated no more permissively than the NXT:1980i when sold to Chinese fabs. It also recommends that Commerce remove the upper limit on “dedicated chuck overlay” in ECCN 3B993.f.1.b.2 and, if necessary, apply the Foreign Direct Product Rule.

**Who it affects** The requested actions concern the State and Commerce Departments, the Dutch government, ASML, Chinese advanced fabs, and chipmakers including CXMT and Huawei. The letter says the tools could support Huawei’s Ascend AI-chip production.

**Why it matters** The author argues that shipments could accelerate China’s production of advanced AI chips and undermine a key allied semiconductor-equipment chokepoint. The letter frames the response as time-sensitive because the policy debate could shift after deliveries begin.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter date shown on page 1: April 20, 2026; the known catalogue date is April 21, 2026.
- Requested State Department briefing deadline: May 4, 2026.
- Potential first shipments: as early as the fourth quarter of 2026.
- Referenced controls: ECCN 3B993.f.1.b.2 and the NXT:1980i comparison tool.
