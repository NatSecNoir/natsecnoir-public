Flock FZ-LLC commits to national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety safeguards in exchange for the Department of Justice’s non-objection to the FCC’s approval of transferring control from Divyank Turakhia to Bhavin Turakhia.

**What it does** The Letter of Agreement (LOA) imposes controls on Flock’s handling of U.S. Records, domestic communications, communications infrastructure, lawful intercept capabilities, and network operations. Flock must comply with CALEA and lawful U.S. process; keep responsive information in the United States; restrict foreign access, disclosure, and government requests; maintain a U.S. law-enforcement point of contact; submit cybersecurity and network security plans; report security incidents; obtain review of outsourced or offshore providers; provide network and service information; and permit DOJ site visits. DOJ may seek FCC action against relevant authorizations if Flock breaches the commitments. The LOA supersedes Flock’s August 13, 2014 agreement with DOJ.

**Who it affects** The commitments bind Flock, its personnel, affiliates, contractors, managed service providers, and other parties with access to Flock’s U.S. systems or records. DOJ, including the FBI, and other Team Telecom agencies receive notice, review, reporting, and oversight rights.

**Why it matters** The agreement makes protection of U.S. communications data and access to lawful process conditions surrounding a foreign-based company’s FCC authorization transfer, while preserving government leverage through possible license remedies.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Executed March 29, 2020.
- FCC application: ITC-T/C-20190319-00090.
- Annual reports begin January 31, 2021.
- Selected deadlines include 30 days for many notices, 48 hours for incident reports, 60 days for security plans, and seven business days for certain CPNI notifications.
