The Letter of Agreement requires the American Samoa Telecommunications Authority (ASTCA) to apply specified national-security and compliance safeguards to the proposed Le Vasa submarine cable system as a condition of FCC licensing.

**What it does** ASTCA commits to operate the Le Vasa Cable System—linking American Samoa to Google’s Bulikula Cable System—and to comply with designated provisions of the Bulikula National Security Agreement. The commitments cover access to cable-system information, operational requirements, compliance with objections and security-incident reporting, annual reporting, consultations, and visits. ASTCA must also cooperate with the compliance-monitoring agencies and report ownership or control changes within 30 days. The agreement defines “Foreign Individual” by reference to U.S. national status for purposes of ASTCA’s compliance.

**Who it affects** The obligations apply primarily to ASTCA, an agency wholly owned and controlled by the government of American Samoa. They also implicate DHS, DOJ, the Department of War, the FCC, Google, and Starfish Infrastructure in their oversight, licensing, or related national-security-agreement roles.

**Why it matters** The LOA supplies the Team Telecom mitigation framework for a strategically important submarine-cable connection and permits the Committee to notify the FCC that further review is unnecessary if the LOA is made a licensing condition. It extends existing Bulikula and Honomoana agreement obligations to ASTCA as a covered third party.
