The letter urges Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to maintain stringent guardrails on Nvidia’s proposed H20 chip sales to China and to brief the Select Committee on the administration’s licensing and enforcement plans.

**What it does** The letter argues that the H20 substantially exceeds China’s indigenous AI-chip capability and could strengthen Chinese AI development, including military applications, if exported in large volumes. It requests a briefing on licensing standards, expected export quantities and recipients, the rationale for any policy change, safeguards against diversion to PLA-affiliated or other unauthorized users, Nvidia and BIS compliance monitoring, pending applications involving advanced AI, and possible further export-control changes. The document also includes the Committee’s findings and recommendations concerning DeepSeek, including alleged data flows through China Mobile infrastructure, censorship, possible model distillation from U.S. systems, and use of restricted Nvidia chips.

**Who it affects** The request targets Commerce and BIS decision-making on Nvidia H20 and similar advanced chips, while implicating Nvidia, Chinese AI companies including DeepSeek, potential intermediaries, and U.S. AI and semiconductor firms.

**Why it matters** The letter frames the relevant benchmark as China’s domestic chip capability—not the performance of chips available in the United States—and calls for controls that prevent U.S. technology from accelerating PRC AI capabilities or becoming embedded in globally influential Chinese models.

**Key dates and numbers**
- July 18, 2025: letter date.
- August 8, 2025: requested deadline for the Commerce briefing.
- The letter cites an estimate of about 200,000 advanced chips for Huawei in 2025 versus more than 14 million AI chips expected to be deployed by U.S. companies.
- The attached Committee report estimates DeepSeek may have access to about 60,000 Nvidia chips, including 30,000 H20s.
