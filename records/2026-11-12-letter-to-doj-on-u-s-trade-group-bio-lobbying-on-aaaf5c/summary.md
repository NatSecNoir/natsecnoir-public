The House Select Committee on the CCP asks the Department of Justice to examine whether BIO’s lobbying on behalf of WuXi AppTec may trigger Foreign Agents Registration Act (FARA) requirements and to consider legislative reforms requiring greater disclosure.

**What it does** The March 5, 2024 letter from Chairman Mike Gallagher requests a briefing for Select Committee staff on the applicability of FARA to BIO’s advocacy, possible FARA exemptions, and changes that would require trade associations to register when lobbying for a foreign-adversary, military-linked commercial entity against U.S. national-security legislation. It identifies BIO as a biotechnology trade association whose membership includes WuXi AppTec and points to BIO CEO Rachel King’s February 2024 advocacy against the BIOSECURE Act.

**Who it affects** The request concerns DOJ, BIO, WuXi AppTec, and policymakers considering restrictions on foreign-adversary biotechnology providers. The letter characterizes WuXi AppTec as a PRC-based company with ties to the CCP and PLA and alleges that BIO’s advocacy advances PRC and CCP interests; these are the committee’s assertions and requests for review, not a DOJ determination.

**Why it matters** The document links lobbying-transparency questions under FARA to biotechnology supply-chain and national-security concerns. It also illustrates congressional scrutiny of trade-association advocacy involving companies alleged to be connected to China’s military-civil fusion strategy.

**Key dates and numbers**
- March 5, 2024: letter date.
- February 2024: BIO CEO Rachel King’s cited letter opposing the BIOSECURE Act.
- 30 days: requested period for DOJ to brief committee staff.
- H.R. 7805 and H.R. 7085: bill numbers cited in the letter and its footnotes.
