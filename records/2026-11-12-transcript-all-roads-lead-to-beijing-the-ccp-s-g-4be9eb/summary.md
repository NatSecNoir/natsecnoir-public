The hearing examines the Belt and Road Initiative as a vehicle for expanding Chinese economic, political, military, and technological influence and considers how the United States and its partners should respond.

**What it does** The transcript records opening statements and testimony from David Trulio, Daniel Runde, and Brad Parks, followed by questions from committee members. Witnesses discuss Chinese infrastructure lending, ports, telecommunications networks, surveillance technology, debt distress, project quality, and the shift from BRI 1.0 to a risk-management-focused “BRI 2.0.” They recommend stronger coordination across U.S. agencies, expanded use of the Development Finance Corporation and Export-Import Bank, faster project decisions, allied consortia, trade access, and private-capital mobilization.

**Who it affects** The discussion concerns developing countries receiving Chinese financing and infrastructure, U.S. agencies and companies competing for overseas projects, allied governments, and telecommunications, maritime, energy, mining, pharmaceutical, and semiconductor supply chains. Huawei, ZTE, Alipay, and the Chinese LOGINK maritime tracking system are identified as technology or security concerns.

**Why it matters** The hearing frames overseas development finance and infrastructure as national-security tools. It highlights competing claims about debt, dependence, dual-use ports, surveillance, and digital-network control while arguing that U.S. alternatives must be credible, coordinated, transparent, high quality, and sufficiently fast.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Hearing held May 16, 2024, from 9:30 to 10:57 a.m.
- Witnesses state that BRI involves more than $1 trillion in investment and loans across more than 100 countries.
- Testimony compares approximately $9 billion in annual DFC deal flow with about $90 billion for BRI.
