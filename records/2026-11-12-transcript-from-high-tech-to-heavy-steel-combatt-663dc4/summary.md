The hearing examines the PRC’s efforts to dominate semiconductors, shipbuilding, and drones and considers trade, investment, industrial, and alliance policies to preserve U.S. economic and national security.

**What it does** The transcript records testimony and questioning before the House Select Committee on the CCP. Witnesses Adam Bry of Skydio, Christopher Miller of Tufts University and the American Enterprise Institute, and Scott Paul of the Alliance for American Manufacturing describe alleged PRC subsidies, overcapacity, market access restrictions, technology transfer, and supply-chain dependencies. Members discuss the CHIPS Act, export controls, tariffs, Section 301 proceedings concerning shipbuilding, possible revival of Section 421, domestic procurement, workforce development, critical minerals, and cooperation with allies.

**Who it affects** The discussion concerns U.S. semiconductor, shipbuilding, drone, manufacturing, energy, defense, and critical-infrastructure sectors, as well as policymakers, allied suppliers, military planners, farmers, first responders, and other users of drones and foundational chips. Companies and technologies named include DJI, Skydio, Huawei, SMIC, YMTC, TSMC, and LOGINK.

**Why it matters** Members and witnesses frame dependence on PRC production as a national-security, economic-security, privacy, and deterrence risk. They emphasize that foundational semiconductors support civilian and military systems; Chinese drones can capture data and connect to foreign servers; and shipbuilding capacity affects maritime resilience and naval surge capacity. The hearing generally supports reducing risky dependencies while acknowledging adjustment costs and the need to coordinate with allies.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Hearing held June 26, 2024; questions for the record were due one week later.
- Testimony cites China’s control of roughly 51–54% of global shipbuilding and DJI’s control of roughly 80–90% of the U.S. drone market, depending on the market measure.
- China began construction on nearly 1,800 large ocean-going vessels in 2022, compared with five in the United States, according to testimony.
- Witnesses discuss China’s projected growth in foundational-chip capacity through 2030 and U.S. advanced-semiconductor production projections for 2032.
