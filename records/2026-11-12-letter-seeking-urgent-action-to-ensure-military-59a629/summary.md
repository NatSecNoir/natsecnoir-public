The House Select Committee on the Strategic Competition Between the United States and the Chinese Communist Party asks senior Defense and Transportation officials to explain how the United States will ensure adequate sealift for a potential Indo-Pacific conflict.

**What it does** The February 6, 2024 letter describes the military sealift fleet, commercial maritime capacity, mariner workforce, and readiness as insufficient for sustained operations. It requests written responses by February 28, 2024, addressing fleet size, vessel age, acquisition of U.S. or foreign merchant ships, mariner recruitment, reliance on foreign-flag and foreign-crewed vessels, associated risks, and whether plans involve acquiring ships from China. The committee cites its authority under H. Res. 11 to investigate the Chinese Communist Party’s economic, technological, and security progress and competition with the United States.

**Who it affects** The requests are directed to General Jacqueline D. Van Ovost, Commander of Transportation Command, and Ann C. Phillips, Administrator of the Maritime Administration. The letter also concerns the Department of Defense, the Department of Transportation, the Ready Reserve Force, Military Sealift Command, the Maritime Security Program, U.S. mariners, and foreign shipping providers.

**Why it matters** The letter frames sealift as a national-security vulnerability affecting the ability to move and sustain forces thousands of miles from the United States. It highlights an aging fleet, limited commercial capacity, a mariner shortage, and poor mobilization performance as risks to deterrence and contingency operations in the Indo-Pacific.

**Key dates and numbers**
- February 6, 2024: letter date.
- February 28, 2024: requested response deadline.
- 45 Ready Reserve Force ships and 15 Military Sealift Command ships comprise the stated 60-ship military sealift fleet.
- The Ready Reserve Force averages 45 years old; 17 ships are at least 50 years old.
- A 2019 exercise found only 40 percent of the then-61-ship fleet ready for mobilization.
- The letter states that more than 1,800 mariners are needed to sustain sealift in a crisis beyond six months.
