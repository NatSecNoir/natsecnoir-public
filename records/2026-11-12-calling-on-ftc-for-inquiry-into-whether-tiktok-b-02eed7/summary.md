The House Select Committee on the CCP asks the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) to investigate whether TikTok violated children’s privacy and consumer-protection laws through pop-up messages urging users to contact Congress about the Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act.

**What it does** The letter requests an examination of whether TikTok pushed deceptive messages to children under 13 in violation of the Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA), including whether the prompts appeared on TikTok’s “TikTok for Younger Users” application or revealed failures in TikTok’s age controls. It also asks the FTC to assess whether TikTok’s messages to minors and other users violated the platform’s Terms of Service and Section 5 of the FTC Act. The committee additionally seeks a private staff briefing on the FTC’s 2019 consent decree with TikTok concerning prior COPPA violations.

**Who it affects** The request focuses on TikTok, children and other minors who received the prompts, the FTC, and users whose ZIP codes were collected to route calls to Members of Congress’ offices. It is signed by Chairman John Moolenaar and Ranking Member Raja Krishnamoorthi and addressed to FTC Chair Lina Khan.

**Why it matters** The letter raises concerns that deceptive or coercive in-app design could solicit children’s personal information and mobilize them politically, while citing reports that children received the messages and that at least one child threatened self-harm. It asks the FTC to determine whether enforcement or other action is warranted, but does not itself make a legal finding.

**Key dates and numbers**
- May 2, 2024: date of the letter.
- March 7, 2024: date TikTok reportedly pushed the message.
- Two requested FTC inquiries: COPPA and Section 5 of the FTC Act.
- 2019: year of the FTC consent decree referenced in the letter.
