The executed Letter of Agreement conditions national-security and law-enforcement clearance for the proposed transfer of control of GCX Companies and their domestic and international Section 214 authorizations to Reef Bidco Limited, a 3i Infrastructure subsidiary.

**What it does** The LOA requires GCX Holdings, Vanco US, Vanco Solutions, Reliance Globalcom Services, and 3i Infrastructure to maintain U.S.-based law-enforcement and security contacts, support lawful interception and requests for U.S. records, and restrict disclosure or access by foreign governments, entities, and persons absent U.S. approval. It also imposes personnel screening, cybersecurity and system-security plans, incident reporting, controls over principal equipment and outsourced or offshored providers, advance notice of ownership and service changes, annual reporting, audits, and DOJ site-visit rights.

**Who it affects** The commitments bind the GCX Companies and 3i Infrastructure and apply to their telecommunications infrastructure, U.S. records, personnel, vendors, managed network providers, network operations centers, and foreign service relationships. DOJ, including the FBI, receives review, objection, inspection, and enforcement functions, while the FCC application and authorizations remain the regulatory vehicle.

**Why it matters** The agreement operationalizes Team Telecom mitigation for a Section 214 ownership transfer, linking telecommunications authorization to continuing controls over data access, lawful process, cybersecurity, supply chain risk, and foreign ownership. Breach or unresolved risk may support FCC action against relevant authorizations.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Executed July 18, 2022; it supersedes the November 25, 2020 Letter of Assurances upon FCC approval.
- U.S. records requests generally require response within five days; security incidents must be reported within 48 hours.
- The first annual report is due one year after the Date of FCC Approval and annually thereafter.
- GCX holds one blanket domestic and three international Section 214 authorizations described in the LOA.
