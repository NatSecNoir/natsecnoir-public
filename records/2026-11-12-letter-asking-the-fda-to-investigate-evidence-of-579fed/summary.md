The House Select Committee on the CCP asks FDA Commissioner Robert Califf to investigate U.S. biopharmaceutical clinical trials conducted with Chinese military institutions or at sites in Xinjiang and to report on FDA oversight, access, ethics, and technology-transfer risks.

**What it does** The August 19 letter describes concerns that clinical-trial collaborations with People’s Liberation Army (PLA) hospitals could expose sensitive clinical, intellectual-property, and drug-composition data to the PRC, while trials in the Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region could involve coerced participation and unreliable consent. It asks FDA to answer seven questions, including whether it has inspected PLA facilities, been denied access to Chinese sites, reviewed PLA-affiliated facilities, assessed adjudication costs, received PLA-linked trial data, notified companies about relevant studies, and measured IP and technology-transfer risks.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to FDA and concerns U.S. biopharmaceutical companies, their clinical-trial sponsors and research partners, PLA medical institutions, and hospitals in Xinjiang. It specifically references a current donanemab trial involving the PLA General Hospital and Medical School and the PLA Air Force Medical University, as well as an earlier axitinib trial at the PLA’s 307 Hospital. The letter also notes that the Academy of Military Medical Sciences is on the Commerce Entity List.

**Why it matters** The committee frames overseas clinical research as a national-security, data-integrity, human-rights, and patient-safety issue. Its requested information would clarify how FDA scrutinizes foreign trial data and whether existing regulatory authorities are being used to address PLA ties, Xinjiang-related ethical risks, and potential transfer of sensitive technology.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter date: August 19, 2024; metadata date: August 20, 2024.
- Requested FDA response deadline: October 1, 2024.
- The letter asks seven numbered questions.
- It cites hundreds of China-based trials over the prior ten years involving at least one entity with “PLA” in its name.
