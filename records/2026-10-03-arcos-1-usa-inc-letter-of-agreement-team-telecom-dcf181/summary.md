DHS, with DOJ and DoD concurrence, withdraws its request to defer action and states that it has no objection to the Cable & Wireless/Liberty Global transfer applications, provided the applicants continue complying with the 2015 Letter of Assurance (LOA).

**What it does** The filing carries forward national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety mitigation for the CFX-1 and ARCOS-1 submarine cable systems. The LOA requires Columbus to maintain and provide principal-equipment lists; give advance notice of new vendors and contractors; protect against unauthorized access; report qualifying incidents; produce network, security, and architecture information on request; maintain U.S.-based points of contact; provide annual reports; permit government site visits and interviews; and give advance notice of significant new U.S. communications services. DHS may seek FCC action against relevant authorizations if the commitments are breached.

**Who it affects** The obligations bind Columbus, Cable and Wireless, Liberty Global, and successors, assigns, subsidiaries, and affiliates associated with the cable licenses and systems.

**Why it matters** The document illustrates Team Telecom mitigation attached to FCC approval of submarine cable-control transfers, preserving agency access, reporting, oversight, and remedial leverage after ownership changes.

**Key dates and numbers**
- March 8, 2017: DHS withdrawal of its deferral request and no-objection letter.
- September 11, 2015: LOA date; September 18, 2015: DHS petition to adopt conditions.
- 30 days: advance notice for new principal-equipment vendors and significant new U.S. services.
- 10 business days: incident reports and responses to DHS recommendations; 15 days: requested updated information.
- FCC files: SCL-T/C-20151130-00035 and -00036; cable licenses include SCL-LIC-19981222-00032 and SCL-LIC-20070516-00008.
