The Committees request information from Google, Microsoft, Meta, and Amazon Web Services about their ownership, operations, vendors, safeguards, and exposure to Chinese or Russian risks involving undersea telecommunications cables.

**What it does** The July 21, 2025 letter asks each company to submit a separate written response by 5:00 p.m. on August 4, 2025. The companies must identify cable systems in which they or affiliated entities have ownership, voting, financial, or operational interests; provide landing points, capacity, status, co-owners, and landing-station operators; and identify PRC-origin technology and service providers. The request also covers construction, repair, and maintenance contractors since January 1, 2018, safeguards used during those activities, suspected tampering or other irregularities, vessel-monitoring procedures, vendor vetting, and relevant government or industry threat briefings. The companies are additionally asked to brief the three Committees by August 8, 2025.

**Who it affects** The direct recipients are Google, Microsoft Corporation, Meta Platforms, and Amazon Web Services. The inquiry focuses particularly on possible links involving S.B. Submarine Systems, China Telecom, China Unicom, Huawei Marine, and related affiliates or successor entities, as well as vessels and service providers tied to the PRC or Russian Federation.

**Why it matters** The letter frames subsea cables as essential to global data transmission and U.S. national-security communications. It highlights both physical sabotage and the possibility that commercial access to cable construction, maintenance, landing stations, or equipment could enable espionage, disruption, or exploitation. It also references the FCC’s then-planned rule concerning PRC-manufactured technology in U.S.-landing subsea cables.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated July 21, 2025; known catalogue date is July 25, 2025.
- Responses requested by 5:00 p.m. on August 4, 2025.
- Committee briefings requested by August 8, 2025.
- Maintenance and repair records requested from January 1, 2018 onward.
- The letter states that subsea cables transmit over 95 percent of intercontinental data.
