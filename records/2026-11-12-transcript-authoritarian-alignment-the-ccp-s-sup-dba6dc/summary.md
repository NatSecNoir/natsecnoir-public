The hearing examined testimony that the Chinese Communist Party is coordinating with or supporting authoritarian and adversarial actors, and debated how U.S. military, economic, diplomatic, and political strength should respond.

**What it does** The transcript records opening statements, testimony, and questions from former Secretaries of State and Defense Michael R. Pompeo and Leon E. Panetta. Witnesses discussed China’s relationships with Russia, Iran, and North Korea; deterrence and Taiwan; economic competition, supply chains, intellectual-property theft, export controls, energy, and foreign investment; influence operations inside the United States; and proposed or pending assistance for Ukraine, Israel, and Taiwan. Members also questioned the witnesses about TikTok, Huawei, the Commerce Entity List, Chinese investment, Belt and Road activities, and the role of U.S. alliances.

**Who it affects** The discussion addresses Congress and the executive branch, U.S. allies and partners, Taiwan, Ukraine, Israel, American businesses and manufacturers, farmers, technology and energy sectors, and state and local governments exposed to Chinese influence efforts. It also names the CCP, Russia, Iran, North Korea, and associated state or commercial activities as national-security concerns.

**Why it matters** The hearing links telecommunications and technology concerns—including Huawei, artificial intelligence, social-media algorithms, and dual-use technologies—to broader national-security, economic, and democratic-resilience questions. The witnesses argued that deterrence depends on credible U.S. commitments, stronger alliances, protection of critical technologies, and the capacity to act decisively; members also debated the costs and limits of decoupling and economic pressure.
