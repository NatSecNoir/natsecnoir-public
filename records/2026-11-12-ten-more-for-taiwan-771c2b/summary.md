The report concludes that the United States should urgently deepen economic, defense, diplomatic, and societal ties with Taiwan to deter Chinese Communist Party aggression and preserve peace in the Taiwan Strait.

**What it does**
Building on the committee’s 2023 *Ten for Taiwan* report, the document presents ten bipartisan findings and recommendations. It calls for clearer political signaling; stronger trade, tax, travel, technology, and supply-chain ties; faster security assistance and defense-industrial cooperation; expanded U.S. munitions, logistics, air and missile defense, and space-launch capacity; stronger allied planning, access, exercises, and intelligence sharing; and greater Taiwanese resilience against invasion, blockade, cyber-enabled economic warfare, and disinformation. It also recommends expanded civil-defense training, international support for Taiwan’s participation in multilateral organizations, and costs on the PRC for enabling Russia’s war in Ukraine.

**Who it affects**
The recommendations primarily address Congress, the Departments of Defense, State, Treasury, and Commerce, U.S. Indo-Pacific Command, Taiwan’s government and Ministry of National Defense, U.S. allies and partners, and industries supplying semiconductors, defense systems, energy, agriculture, and critical infrastructure.

**Why it matters**
The report treats Taiwan’s security as directly connected to U.S. national security, allied defense, semiconductor supply chains, and global economic stability. It argues that deterrence requires preparation before a crisis, including stockpiles, resilient infrastructure, interoperable forces, coordinated sanctions, and credible political commitments.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Adopted December 18, 2025.
- Identifies ten additional findings and recommendations.
- Notes Taiwan’s projected ten-day natural-gas reserves and 2.4 million daily cyberattacks in 2024.
- Cites a proposed increase of the Taiwan Security Cooperation Initiative to $1 billion.
- Highlights 2027 PLA capability milestones and TSMC’s announced $165 billion U.S. investment.
