The Letter of Agreement conditions the national-security and law-enforcement mitigation supporting Alleswolke Wireless LLC’s Section 214 application on extensive controls over U.S. communications, records, personnel, equipment, service providers, and foreign access.

**What it does** Alleswolke commits to maintain a U.S.-based law-enforcement point of contact approved by the Department of Justice (DOJ) and FBI, a cleared U.S. security officer, CALEA compliance, and procedures for responding to lawful U.S. process. It must keep responsive information in the United States, restrict disclosure or access by foreign governments and persons, submit cybersecurity and system-security plans, report specified incidents within 48 hours, and provide DOJ with detailed lists of countries, principal equipment, service providers, network operations centers, and foreign persons with access. DOJ receives objection or non-objection rights over many changes and may require audits, site visits, and annual reports.

**Who it affects** The agreement binds Alleswolke, its successors and assigns, and contractors or other service providers with access to domestic communications infrastructure or U.S. records. It specifically requires a plan to discontinue use of China Mobile International Limited as a service provider.

**Why it matters** The LOA illustrates Team Telecom mitigation for an international telecommunications authorization, linking operational cybersecurity and data-governance obligations to continued FCC authority. Breach or unresolved risks may prompt FCC modification, conditioning, revocation, cancellation, or termination of the authorization.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Executed March 29, 2024, by CEO Rui Zhu.
- Initial disclosures generally fall due within 30, 60, or 90 days after the Date of FCC Approval.
- Security incidents must be reported to DOJ within 48 hours.
- The discontinuation plan for China Mobile International Limited is due within 90 days of FCC approval, with six-month progress reports thereafter.
- Annual reports begin one year after FCC approval.
