The House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party urges President Biden to adopt broad, transparent, and predictable restrictions on U.S. outbound investment to the People’s Republic of China (PRC).

**What it does** The August 3, 2023 letter from Chairman Mike Gallagher responds to a reported draft executive order on outbound investment in critical technologies. It recommends accounting for total U.S. exposure and ultimate beneficiaries; covering both private-market and public-market investments; restricting capital connected to national-security sectors, the Chinese Communist Party’s technological ambitions, forced labor, genocide, and the People’s Liberation Army; and addressing risks from variable interest entity structures. It also urges comparable due-diligence standards, avoidance of an onerous case-by-case screening system, a clear compliance runway, and advance consultation with allies.

**Who it affects** The recommendations are directed to President Biden and would affect U.S. investors, private-equity and venture-capital funds, limited partners, public-market investors, companies issuing or trading securities tied to PRC firms, and PRC organizations connected to military, technology, or human-rights abuses. The letter also calls on U.S. allies and partners to adopt parallel restrictions.

**Why it matters** The committee argues that existing mechanisms do not require disclosure of, or restrict, outbound U.S. capital flows to foreign-adversary companies. It frames outbound investment controls as necessary to reduce national-security, human-rights, supply-chain, and systemic financial risks while preserving investor certainty and shareholder protections.

**Key dates and numbers**
- August 3, 2023: letter date.
- The letter cites estimates of more than $200 billion in U.S. private-market capital and more than $1.1 trillion in public-market capital invested in PRC companies.
- It recommends beginning with five to six priority sectors among critical and emerging technologies.
