Three House committees request that the Environmental Protection Agency provide information and procedures concerning foreign exploitation and national-security risks in its Small Business Innovation Research (SBIR) and Small Business Technology Transfer (STTR) programs.

**What it does** The February 26, 2025 letter asks EPA to report annual application outcomes and flags across Phases I, II, and III, including unsuccessful applications and flags involving foreign-adversary ties, export-control or dual-use concerns, inadequate vetting, and other security red flags. It also requests EPA’s disclosure requirements, flagging criteria, post-flag procedures, interagency communication procedures, and monitoring procedures for each phase. The requested information should cover activity dating to January 20, 2021.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to EPA Administrator Lee M. Zeldin and concerns U.S. small businesses applying for or receiving SBIR/STTR support, EPA program officials, and agencies involved in national-security or export-control review. The letter is signed by the chairmen of the House Committees on Small Business, Science, Space, and Technology, and the Select Committee on the CCP.

**Why it matters** The committees argue that CCP-linked venture capital, research partnerships, talent-recruitment programs, PRC-based subsidiaries, and state-backed investment can expose taxpayer-funded research and intellectual property. Citing a 2021 DoD-commissioned report, they say existing due diligence may be insufficient to identify these risks and seek information to support legislative and policy reforms.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter date: February 26, 2025.
- Requested response deadline: March 12, 2025.
- SBIR/STTR authorization was set to expire September 30, 2025.
- The requested data period begins January 20, 2021.
- The letter states that small businesses comprise 99.9 percent of U.S. businesses.
