The House Select Committee on the CCP urges Commerce Secretary Gina Raimondo to finalize an AI export-control framework that uses clear country- and company-level redlines to restrict access to advanced U.S. GPUs and closed-weight dual-use AI model weights where ties to the PRC create national-security risks.

**What it does** The letter supports BIS’s forthcoming “Export Control Framework for AI Diffusion,” described as a global licensing regime for advanced GPUs and model weights. It recommends denying advanced U.S. GPUs to countries that host a People’s Liberation Army base or comparable PLA presence, and restricting access to closed-weight dual-use AI model weights for countries that host Huawei cloud-computing infrastructure. It also asks BIS to update the rules routinely, rather than annually, using current intelligence about PRC circumvention and foreign cooperation.

**Who it affects** The recommendations would affect BIS, foreign governments seeking U.S. AI technology, U.S. technology companies, cloud-computing providers, and entities seeking advanced GPUs or model weights. The authors specifically cite concerns involving Microsoft, G42, the United Arab Emirates, Huawei, and PRC-linked access routes.

**Why it matters** The letter frames advanced AI systems as national-security capabilities, including offensive cyber tools and signals intelligence, and calls for U.S. technology leadership to move countries and companies away from Beijing’s orbit while keeping the most sophisticated AI models trained in the United States.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Dated January 2, 2025.
- Addresses the proposed AI Diffusion framework and recommends more frequent than annual updates.
- Signed by Chairman John Moolenaar and Ranking Member Raja Krishnamoorthi.
