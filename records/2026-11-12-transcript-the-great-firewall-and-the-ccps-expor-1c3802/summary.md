The hearing examines the Chinese Communist Party’s Great Firewall, its domestic censorship and surveillance architecture, and the export of related techno-authoritarian tools and practices abroad.

**What it does** The transcript records opening statements, witness testimony, and member questions about China’s replacement of the open internet with a controlled domestic ecosystem built around censorship, surveillance, platform governance, and state-backed information controls. Witnesses describe circumvention challenges, the role of the Cyberspace Administration of China, the effects of Chinese-language content reduction, and the risks posed by AI trained on censored data. Members and witnesses discuss policy responses including expanded internet-freedom funding, support for independent journalism and circumvention tools, disclosure requirements for U.S. companies that facilitate censorship, targeted sanctions, investment restrictions, and coordination with allies.

**Who it affects** The discussion concerns Chinese internet users, dissidents, journalists, human-rights defenders, U.S. technology and media companies, foreign governments acquiring Chinese digital infrastructure, and policymakers addressing Huawei, Hikvision, Dahua, ZTE, and other surveillance-related entities. It also addresses the implications for Taiwan, Xinjiang, Venezuela, Iran, Myanmar, Russia, and other countries adopting Chinese censorship or surveillance technologies.

**Why it matters** The hearing frames information control as both a human-rights issue and a national-security challenge. Witnesses warn that Chinese systems can travel through apps, telecommunications infrastructure, safe-city projects, and digital Belt and Road initiatives, potentially enabling censorship, data collection, and political manipulation beyond China’s borders.

- July 23, 2024: hearing date.
