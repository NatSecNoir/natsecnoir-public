The House Select Committee on the CCP asks Secretary of War Pete Hegseth to determine whether Airbus Space provided satellite imagery of U.S. military assets to a Chinese company before and during Operation Epic Fury, and separately alerts him to Safran’s China-linked aerospace activities and their potential military implications.

**What it does** The April 13 letter presents a simulation indicating that four Airbus commercial satellites had plausible opportunities to capture imagery later published by MizarVision, while acknowledging that MizarVision does not identify its imagery sources. It urges the Department of War to secure voluntary limits on Airbus Space’s provision of imagery that could expose U.S. forces. The enclosed analysis and the letter also describe Airbus Space’s joint venture with the Chinese Academy of Sciences’ Chinese Aerospace Information Research Institute. The March 19 letter describes Safran’s joint ventures, technology-development, training, and supply relationships involving Chinese aerospace and defense-linked entities.

**Who it affects** The principal subjects are Airbus Space, MizarVision, Safran, and their Chinese partners, including Chang Guang Satellite, AVIC-related companies, and Beihang University. The requested assurances concern U.S. troops, military assets, and defense contractors.

**Why it matters** The Committee connects near-real-time commercial imagery and civilian aerospace cooperation with possible support for adversary military capabilities. It highlights the Chinese Aerospace Information Research Institute’s Commerce Entity List status, AVIC’s 1260H-related designation, and Chang Guang Satellite’s U.S. sanctions history as indicators of national-security risk.

**Key dates and numbers**
- April 13, 2026: Airbus Space letter; March 19, 2026: Safran letter.
- Four Airbus satellites were modeled across a 48-hour window.
- The simulation identified two daily collection windows of roughly 10 hours each.
- The March 27, 2026 strike at Prince Sultan Air Base reportedly wounded at least 12 U.S. service members.
