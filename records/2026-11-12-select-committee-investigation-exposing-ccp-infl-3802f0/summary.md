The Committees conclude that Harvard University’s research, compliance, and foreign-funding practices systematically prioritized collaboration and financial ties with China over U.S. national-security, research-security, and transparency concerns.

**What it does** The report presents nine findings from a joint investigation based on thousands of pages of Harvard-produced documents, Chinese government materials, transcribed interviews, and witness testimony. It examines Harvard’s research collaborations with Chinese military-linked institutions, including Beihang University, the National University of Defense Technology, and other “Seven Sons of National Defense” universities; executive education involving China’s National Healthcare Security Administration and potentially the Treasury-sanctioned Xinjiang Production and Construction Corps; and the role of Harvard Global as a possible intermediary for foreign funding. It recommends adopting the DETERRENT Act and SIRA Act, centralizing Harvard’s research-security functions, improving sanctions and partnership screening, professionalizing research-security staffing, and preventing affiliated entities from circumventing disclosure requirements.

**Who it affects** The principal subject is Harvard, including its central administration, schools, researchers, compliance officials, and Harvard Global. The report also discusses Chinese government and military-linked institutions, sanctioned entities, U.S. federally funded research, Congress, and universities generally.

**Why it matters** The report argues that technically lawful collaborations can still expose dual-use research, intellectual property, talent, and taxpayer-funded work to foreign military and intelligence systems. It also links these concerns to federal restricted-party regimes, including Commerce Entity List controls, Treasury sanctions, DoD’s section 1260H list, and the FCC Covered List, as examples of lists the proposed SIRA Act would encompass.

**Key dates and numbers**
- The report is dated August 13, 2026.
- Harvard’s NHSA executive-education series began in November 2019; the XPCC was listed by Treasury in 2020.
- The Committees identified more than 140 publications involving Harvard-affiliated and “Seven Sons” university co-authors and more than 3,500 Harvard-China co-authored publications.
- Section 117’s existing foreign-gift reporting threshold is $250,000; the proposed DETERRENT Act would reduce it to $50,000 generally and $0 for countries of concern.
- The report states that Harvard received more than $600 million from China and that Harvard Global’s 2023 revenue exceeded $7 million.
