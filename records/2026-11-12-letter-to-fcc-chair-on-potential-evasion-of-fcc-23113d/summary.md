The House Select Committee on the CCP asks FCC Chair Jessica Rosenworcel to investigate whether the sale of Dahua Technology USA to Luminys Systems could circumvent U.S. restrictions on Dahua video-surveillance equipment while leaving the underlying PRC-controlled technology and national-security risks unchanged.

**What it does** The November 2024 letter describes FCC restrictions on certain Dahua cameras and requests that the Commission examine the transaction and brief Select Committee staff on whether it is an effort to evade statutory and regulatory limits. The members focus especially on the continued development and control of Dahua firmware and software in the PRC, rather than only on the ownership or branding of the U.S. business.

**Who it affects** The request concerns Zhejiang Dahua Technology, Dahua Technology USA, Luminys Systems, and Foxlink, as well as the FCC’s administration of equipment-authorization and procurement-related restrictions affecting video-surveillance equipment. It was signed by Committee Chairman John Moolenaar and Ranking Member Raja Krishnamoorthi.

**Why it matters** The letter presents a potential distinction between nominal ownership or manufacturing arrangements and actual control of software and firmware. It links Dahua to FCC national-security restrictions, the Commerce Department Entity List, the Department of Defense list of Chinese military companies, and Section 889 procurement limits, while also citing alleged human-rights concerns related to Xinjiang. The requested review would test whether a corporate transaction changes the regulatory risk or merely creates a workaround.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter date: November 19, 2024; catalogue date: November 21, 2024.
- Dahua Technology USA was reportedly sold to Luminys Systems earlier in 2024.
- The letter cites Section 889 of the 2019 National Defense Authorization Act and FCC restrictions on certain national-security uses of Dahua cameras.
