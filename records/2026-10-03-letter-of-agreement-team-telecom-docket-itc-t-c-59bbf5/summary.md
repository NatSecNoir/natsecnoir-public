The Department of Justice, including the FBI, and the Department of Homeland Security asked the FCC to approve the proposed transfer of control of DSCI, LLC to U.S. TelePacific Corp. subject to TelePacific’s commitments in a September 2, 2016 Letter of Assurances.

**What it does** This Section 1.41 petition states that the Agencies have no objection to approval of the transaction if the Commission conditions its authorizations and licenses on compliance with the Letter of Assurances. The proposed transaction would give U.S. TelePacific Corp. control of DSCI, LLC; the petition says that no assignment of licenses, assets, or customers would occur. The attached assurances are described as additional commitments intended to support law-enforcement, national-security, and public-safety responsibilities.

**Who it affects** The conditions would bind U.S. TelePacific Corp. and U.S. TelePacific Holdings Corp., identified together as TelePacific, while the underlying application concerns DSCI Holdings Corporation, DSCI, LLC, and the transfer of control to U.S. TelePacific Corp. The petition was signed by Richard C. Sofield of DOJ’s National Security Division, Foreign Investment Review Staff.

**Why it matters** This filing illustrates the Team Telecom process in which executive-branch agencies recommend that the FCC condition communications authorization on national-security and law-enforcement commitments. It also records the Agencies’ position that approval is acceptable only with those commitments incorporated as conditions.

**Key dates and numbers**
