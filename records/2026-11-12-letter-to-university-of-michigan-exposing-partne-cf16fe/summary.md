The House Select Committee on the CCP urges University of Michigan President Santa Ono to conduct a national-security review and close U-M’s partnership with Shanghai Jiao Tong University and their Joint Institute.

**What it does** The October 31, 2024 letter, signed by Chairman John Moolenaar, presents the Joint Institute as a channel for transferring U.S. research expertise and technology to the PRC’s defense and intelligence ecosystem. It points to Shanghai Jiao Tong’s supervision by China’s defense-industrial authorities, military-aligned laboratories, dual-use research, ties to the PLA, and participation in talent-recruitment programs. It also describes the Joint Institute’s 35 specialized laboratories, four cross-disciplinary centers, research in areas including propulsion, imaging, 6G communications, and advanced materials, and partnerships with PRC companies and defense-linked institutions.

**Who it affects** The principal recipient is the University of Michigan, particularly its leadership, federally funded research enterprise, faculty, students, and international-student vetting processes. The letter also implicates Shanghai Jiao Tong, the Joint Institute, and referenced corporate and academic partners including CATL, Transfar Group, CASC, and the University of Electronic Science and Technology of China.

**Why it matters** The committee frames the partnership as a potential conduit for military-civil fusion, unauthorized technology transfer, and PRC talent development. It cites the federal charging of five PRC nationals connected to the Joint Institute over an alleged concealed visit to Camp Grayling and notes that Georgia Tech and UC Berkeley had decided to shutter comparable PRC joint institutes.

**Key dates and numbers**
- October 31, 2024: letter date.
- 2021: Joint Institute graduate and undergraduate study cited as having expanded across critical and emerging technologies.
- 35 specialized laboratories and four cross-disciplinary centers are identified.
- 2017: Shanghai Jiao Tong reportedly entered an agreement with the PLA Strategic Support Force to train high-level new combat forces.
- Five PRC nationals connected to the Joint Institute were charged over the Camp Grayling visit.
