The letter urges the State and Commerce Departments to work with allies to close gaps in export controls on semiconductor manufacturing equipment and components destined for China.

**What it does** The bipartisan congressional letter supports robust controls on semiconductor manufacturing equipment (SME), particularly advanced lithography and other chokepoint tools, and asks the Administration to pursue countrywide restrictions rather than relying only on entity-specific controls. It also recommends allied restrictions on relevant subcomponents and servicing, with clear expectations and deadlines. If allies do not act, the authors urge the United States to consider measures such as prohibiting U.S.-origin components from being used to produce chokepoint tools destined for China. The chairmen request an Administration briefing within the next month on its strategy and timeline.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Secretary of Commerce Howard Lutnick. It concerns U.S. export-control policy, allied governments—especially those supplying advanced equipment—and companies involved in semiconductor manufacturing equipment, components, and servicing. The stated national-security concern includes China’s ability to produce advanced AI chips and legacy chips used in PLA weapons systems and intelligence platforms.

**Why it matters** The authors argue that equipment entering China creates a lasting loss of U.S. leverage, while entity-specific controls are difficult to enforce after equipment crosses the border. They cite escorted and delayed verification visits, rising Chinese imports of foreign-produced chokepoint equipment, and efforts to develop domestic substitutes. The letter presents allied coordination and possible unilateral U.S. action as time-sensitive measures to preserve the U.S. and allied semiconductor advantage.

**Key dates and numbers**
- February 10, 2026: Letter date.
- The requested Administration briefing is due within one month.
- Dutch sales to China of advanced lithography equipment doubled from 2022 to 2023 and again from 2023 to 2024, according to the letter.
- The letter identifies EUV lithography tools and electrostatic chucks as examples of chokepoint equipment or components.
