The House Select Committee on the CCP asks Google to explain and strengthen its efforts to detect and prevent CCP-linked online scams, including pig-butchering and tech-support fraud, that harm Americans.

**What it does**
The letter commends Google’s use of artificial intelligence to filter scam text messages and combat malicious online advertisements, then requests information on four areas: browser and operating-system initiatives to block malicious ads and browser-based tech-support scams; AI-driven scam detection in Chrome and text-message/Rich Communication Services filtering; measures to reduce malicious advertising and whether protections require user opt-in; and Google’s engagement with federal law enforcement. The lawmakers characterize the scams as often connected to criminal enterprises linked to the Chinese Communist Party and operating through forced-labor scam centers in Southeast Asia.

**Who it affects**
The request is directed to Google CEO Sundar Pichai and concerns Google users, particularly older Americans targeted by investment, pig-butchering, and tech-support scams. It also implicates Google Chrome, browser and operating-system protections, advertising platforms, and RCS communications.

**Why it matters**
The letter frames online scams as a national and consumer-security concern, citing substantial reported losses and the disproportionate impact on older Americans. It seeks transparency about Google’s safeguards and coordination with federal law enforcement rather than imposing a regulatory requirement.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Dated July 9, 2025; requests a response by “June XX, 2025,” as printed.
- The letter cites more than $6.5 billion in 2024 investment-scam losses and nearly $4.9 billion in reported fraud losses for older Americans.
- It cites $982 million in 2024 tech-support-scam losses among victims age 60 and older.
- The requested response covers four categories of information.
