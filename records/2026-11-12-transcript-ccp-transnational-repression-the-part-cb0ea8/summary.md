The hearing examines the Chinese Communist Party’s transnational repression of critics, dissidents, journalists, students, and diaspora communities and considers U.S. responses to protect speech, safety, and democratic institutions.

**What it does** The transcript records opening statements, witness testimony, and member questioning about formal and informal repression attributed to the CCP and its proxies. Witnesses describe overseas police stations, surveillance, stalking, assaults, threats against family members in China, online harassment, spyware, coercion through Chinese platforms, and efforts to intimidate activists on U.S. campuses and at public demonstrations. They also discuss private investigators, United Front networks, Chinese Students and Scholars Associations, diplomatic personnel, and the use of economic and family pressure. Proposed responses include stronger investigations and prosecutions, law-enforcement training, multilingual hotlines, community outreach, asylum support, diplomatic pressure, vetting of visiting personnel, digital-security assistance, and possible legislation criminalizing transnational repression.

**Who it affects** The hearing focuses on Hong Kong, Uyghur, Tibetan, and Chinese dissidents; students and scholars; journalists; asylum seekers; U.S. citizens and lawful residents with vulnerable relatives overseas; affected communities; universities; and law-enforcement and diplomatic agencies. Anna Kwok, Jinrui Zhang, and Sophie Richardson provide principal testimony.

**Why it matters** The witnesses and members frame transnational repression as both a human-rights violation and a threat to free expression, academic freedom, public safety, and democratic institutions in the United States. The testimony argues that fear-induced silence and pressure on relatives can suppress criticism even when targets are physically outside China.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Hearing held December 13, 2023, from 7:00 p.m. to 8:44 p.m.
- Testimony cited 102 overseas Chinese police stations in 53 countries.
- Anna Kwok testified that she and seven other overseas Hongkongers received a 1-million Hong Kong-dollar bounty and arrest warrant.
- Kwok cited more than 1,700 political prisoners and more than 20,000 arrests in Hong Kong.
