The letter updates the national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety commitments governing TPG Telecom’s PPC 1 submarine cable system and provides for DHS to seek FCC conditioning of the cable landing license on compliance.

**What it does** The letter replaces and updates commitments originally made by the PPC 1 Licensees in a 2008 National Security Agreement, which TPG Telecom reaffirmed after acquiring control from PIPE Networks. It defines the domestic communications and infrastructure subject to oversight and requires a U.S.-based, DHS-reviewed security point of contact; prompt production of network, maintenance, restoration, equipment, and vendor information; and the technical ability to interrupt U.S.-bound or U.S.-originating traffic. TPG Telecom must maintain cybersecurity, physical-security, personnel-screening, incident-reporting, training, and annual-compliance-reporting programs, and must provide advance notice of specified equipment, vendor, service, and operational changes.

**Who it affects** The obligations bind TPG Telecom and extend to successors, assigns, subsidiaries, and affiliates, as well as personnel, contractors, vendors, and subcontractors involved with the PPC 1 Cable System. DHS receives review, consultation, information-access, and site-visit rights; the FCC is the licensing authority whose authorization may be conditioned or modified.

**Why it matters** This is a Team Telecom mitigation instrument for a privately operated submarine cable connecting Australia, Guam, and Papua New Guinea. It gives DHS continuing visibility into U.S.-located cable infrastructure and operational control points while preserving a remedy tied to the FCC license if commitments are breached.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Original National Security Agreement: September 4, 2008.
- TPG Telecom reaffirmed the commitments on January 20, 2010.
- The letter is dated July 31, 2018; many initial submissions are due within 60 business days.
- Material equipment changes generally require 15 business days’ notice; vendor or operational changes generally require 30 business days’ notice.
- Foreign ownership or control above 10 percent triggers notice within 10 business days.
