The Agreement requires Starfish Infrastructure, Inc. and Google LLC to implement extensive national-security and law-enforcement safeguards for the proposed Honomoana submarine cable as a condition of the FCC’s cable-landing licensing process.

**What it does** The National Security Agreement establishes compliance obligations monitored by the Committee for the Assessment of Foreign Participation in the United States Telecommunications Services Sector, represented by DHS, DOJ, and DoD. It requires Starfish to appoint U.S.-citizen security and compliance officials; provide information about the cable system, covered third parties, principal equipment, and service providers; obtain CMA non-objection for specified equipment, providers, sensing technology, and foreign personnel access; and maintain physical, logical, supply-chain, privacy, and cybersecurity controls. It also provides for incident reporting, annual reports, site visits, third-party audits, and possible FCC license remedies for breach.

**Who it affects** The primary regulated parties are Starfish and Google. The requirements extend to affiliates, contractors, service providers, equipment vendors, covered third parties, foreign personnel, and entities with interests in Honomoana capacity or access to its systems and records.

**Why it matters** The Agreement operationalizes Team Telecom mitigation for a submarine cable connecting the United States with French Polynesia, New Zealand, and Australia. It links continued licensing to controls over sensitive U.S. records, foreign access, equipment supply chains, lawful-process responsiveness, and cable traffic interruption capability.

**Key dates and numbers**
- FCC referred the application to the Committee on December 27, 2024.
- Signatures were completed July 11, 2025; the NSA becomes effective on the last signature date.
- Initial system, equipment, covered-third-party, and service-provider information is generally due within 60 days of effectiveness.
- Companies must report qualifying incidents within 48 hours; service providers must report breaches to Starfish within 72 hours.
- CMA objections or non-objections generally issue within 90 days, subject to extensions for requested information.
