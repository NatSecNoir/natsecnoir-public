The House Select Committee on the CCP requests that Stanford University disclose how it oversees Chinese national students and protects federally funded, sensitive, and dual-use research.

**What it does** The March 18, 2025 letter asks Stanford to provide written responses by April 1, 2025, covering the prior universities, research affiliations, funding sources, research activities, laboratories, programs, and country-by-country applications, admissions, and enrollments associated with Chinese national students. It also asks 14 questions about their share of graduate enrollment and tuition revenue, participation in federally funded research, safeguards for government-funded projects, monitoring of military or dual-use research, ties to China-based institutions, talent-program participation, export-controlled coursework, post-graduation destinations, and screening procedures.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to Jonathan Levin, president of Stanford University, and concerns the university’s Chinese national undergraduate, graduate, and PhD students, faculty collaborations, research programs, and laboratories. The letter specifically references research supported by DARPA, DoD, DOE, and NSF, as well as fields including artificial intelligence, quantum computing, semiconductors, robotics, aerospace, and related dual-use technologies.

**Why it matters** The committee frames foreign-student access to advanced research as a potential channel for technology transfer, intellectual-property theft, and support for China’s military and economic objectives. It invokes the Select Committee’s investigative authority under H. Res. 5, section 4(a), while seeking institutional transparency rather than announcing a binding regulatory action.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter date: March 18, 2025; catalogued release date: March 19, 2025.
- Response requested no later than April 1, 2025.
- The letter includes 6 requests for information and 14 questions.
- It cites a September 2024 joint committee report and H. Res. 5, section 4(a).
