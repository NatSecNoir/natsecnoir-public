The signatories urge Secretary Gina Raimondo to reject any U.S.-China working group that could allow the People’s Republic of China (PRC) to influence U.S. export-control policy and to signal that controls on sensitive technologies will become stricter rather than weaker.

**What it does** The August 18, 2023 letter responds to a reported proposal to establish a working group during Raimondo’s planned trip to China to discuss U.S. controls on advanced semiconductors. It argues that decisions about the nature and scope of export controls should be made in Washington, not Beijing. The letter also points to a separate report that the State Department was considering removing a PRC entity from the Entity List in connection with another potential arrangement, and asks Raimondo to publicly clarify before her trip that export controls are “non-negotiable.”

**Who it affects** The immediate recipient is Raimondo, Secretary of Commerce. The position concerns U.S. officials responsible for export controls, U.S. technology and compliance interests, and PRC entities subject to U.S. restrictions. The letter is signed by Mike Gallagher, Michael T. McCaul, Bill Hagerty, and Young Kim, and copies Secretary of State Antony Blinken.

**Why it matters** The document reflects congressional opposition to negotiating or relaxing technology controls through bilateral dialogue with China. It links that opposition to concerns about China’s Military-Civil Fusion strategy, access for U.S. export-compliance officers, military development, and human-rights abuses, while calling for expanded controls.

**Key dates and numbers**
- August 18, 2023: date of the letter.
- July 31, 2023: Bloomberg report cited on the proposed semiconductor-control working group.
- August 1, 2023: prior House committee letters referenced.
- The letter identifies four signatories and one copied Cabinet official.
