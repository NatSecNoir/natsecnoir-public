The House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party and Senator Marco Rubio asked Defense Secretary Lloyd Austin to immediately add Contemporary Amperex Technology Limited (CATL) to the Department of Defense’s Section 1260H list.

**What it does** This August 28, 2024 letter argues that CATL’s alleged ties to the Chinese Communist Party and the People’s Liberation Army, along with its role in China’s military-civil fusion policy, warrant its designation under Section 1260H. The lawmakers contend that CATL’s batteries create national-security risks by increasing U.S. dependence on the People’s Republic of China for energy infrastructure. They also cite the Fiscal Year 2024 National Defense Authorization Act, which prohibits DoD contracting with entities on the 1260H list and with CATL.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to DoD and concerns CATL, a Chinese electric-vehicle battery company. It also addresses U.S. companies considering partnerships with CATL and military or other infrastructure users of its batteries. The letter references a related company, Gotion, in connection with alleged slave-labor evidence.

**Why it matters** The document seeks to extend the 1260H national-security contracting restriction to CATL and frames battery procurement as both an energy-infrastructure and military-security issue. It follows lawmakers’ earlier opposition to CATL batteries at Camp Lejeune and states that DoD subsequently removed those batteries.

**Key dates and numbers**
- August 28, 2024: letter date.
- Section 1260H: requested designation framework for entities tied to the PLA or China’s military-civil fusion strategy.
- Fiscal Year 2024: NDAA provisions cited regarding DoD contracting restrictions.
- December: prior bipartisan, bicameral letter concerning CATL batteries at Camp Lejeune; the year is not specified in the document.
