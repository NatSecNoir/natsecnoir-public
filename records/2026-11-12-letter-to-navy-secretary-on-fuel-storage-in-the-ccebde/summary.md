The House Select Committee on the CCP asks Navy Secretary Carlos Del Toro to explain how the United States will replace and redistribute fuel formerly stored at Hawaii’s Red Hill facility and sustain Indo-Pacific operations.

**What it does** The January 17 letter requests written responses by February 7, 2024, on plans for new hardened underground storage, the adequacy of forward fuel facilities and refinery access, integration of allied, partner, and commercial infrastructure, the use and protection of afloat tankers, and expansion of the Tanker Security Program. It also asks whether the Navy has enough tankers to support contingency attrition and what peacetime and wartime force levels it seeks.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to the Secretary of the Navy and concerns the Department of Defense, Navy logistics planners, the Red Hill Joint Task Force, the Defense Logistics Agency, U.S. Transportation Command, allied and partner facilities, and commercial fuel-storage operators. The letter identifies Red Hill’s closure and fuel redistribution as an Indo-Pacific operational issue.

**Why it matters** The committee argues that dispersing or replacing Red Hill’s capacity may increase tanker movements, operational risk, and exposure to fuel degradation, while the available tanker fleet falls well short of a previously identified requirement. It frames resilient fuel storage and delivery as necessary to support deterrence and military mobility amid the Chinese Communist Party’s military buildup.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated January 17, 2024; requested response due February 7, 2024.
- Red Hill had capacity for 250 million gallons of fuel before its closure was announced in March 2022.
- The 2016 U.S. Transportation Command requirement identified 86 fuel tanker ships.
- The letter describes the Navy fleet as 16 replenishment oilers, five tankers, and two fast combat support ships, with nine additional ships in the Tanker Security Program.
