The House Select Committee on the CCP requests information from AbbVie about its clinical trials in China, particularly trials conducted in Xinjiang and at hospitals affiliated with the People’s Republic of China military, citing ethical, human-rights, data-security, and biotechnology-competition risks.

**What it does** The June 29, 2026 letter asks AbbVie to provide a detailed briefing on its due-diligence processes and Good Clinical Practice standards; relevant policies and communications; inspection data; internal risk assessments; trial-level information; information about engaged contract research and development/manufacturing organizations; safeguards for intellectual property and sensitive data; and Chinese licensing, equity, and joint-venture agreements since January 1, 2020. The letter includes an attachment listing identified trials and facilities.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to AbbVie CEO and Board Chairman Robert A. Michael. The committee says publicly available records show more than 100 AbbVie-sponsored or collaborative studies in China since 2007, including at least 17 trials involving Xinjiang hospitals and at least 16 involving PRC military medical centers or hospitals between 2015 and 2025. The letter also discusses AbbVie’s relationship with Chinese drugmaker RemeGen.

**Why it matters** The committee connects clinical-trial practices in Xinjiang to concerns about voluntary informed consent and human-rights abuses, and connects military-hospital research to possible exposure of American biotechnology intellectual property and medical data. It states that the letter does not assert AbbVie engaged in illegal activity or wrongdoing.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Response requested by July 17, 2026.
- Identified trial activity spans 2015–2025; the listed trials begin in July 2015 and include studies beginning in January and February 2025.
- The letter requests nine categories of information.
