The House Select Committee on the CCP urges Solicitor General D. John Sauer to recommend that the Supreme Court deny Cisco Systems, Inc.’s petition for a writ of certiorari in *Cisco Systems, Inc. v. Doe I*, arguing that review would be premature and would delay plaintiffs’ effort to litigate allegations concerning technology allegedly used to facilitate persecution of Falun Gong practitioners in China.

**What it does**
The October 29, 2025 letter asks the Solicitor General to oppose Supreme Court review in Case No. 24-856. Signed by Representatives Christopher Smith and John Moolenaar, it contends that the litigation is consistent with longstanding congressional concern about American companies’ involvement in Chinese human-rights abuses. The letter disputes Cisco’s arguments that review is needed because the case harms U.S. foreign policy or that Congress authorized the relevant technology sale.

**Who it affects**
The immediate audience is the Solicitor General and the Department of Justice, which may file the federal government’s views on the certiorari petition. The requested position would affect Cisco, the plaintiffs, and the Supreme Court’s consideration of the petition. The letter also addresses broader expectations for U.S. technology companies operating in or supplying technology to China.

**Why it matters**
The document frames private litigation as one tool for enforcing U.S. policy against complicity in human-rights abuses and emphasizes that the plaintiffs have been waiting for their day in court since 2011. It links the Cisco dispute to congressional scrutiny of technology supporting Chinese surveillance and repression, while advocating against additional appellate delay.

**Key dates and numbers**
- October 29, 2025: Letter date.
- Case No. 24-856: *Cisco Systems, Inc. v. Doe I*.
- 2011: Year the letter says the plaintiffs began waiting for their day in court.
- 2019: Year cited for a congressional statement on abuses against Falun Gong practitioners.
- 2006: Year cited for a congressional hearing on American technology and know-how supporting repression in China.
