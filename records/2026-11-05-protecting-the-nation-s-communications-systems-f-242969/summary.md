The FCC rescinds its January 2025 Declaratory Ruling interpreting CALEA as imposing broad cybersecurity obligations on telecommunications carriers and withdraws the accompanying NPRM.

**What it does** The Order on Reconsideration concludes that the earlier Declaratory Ruling unlawfully read CALEA section 105 too broadly and purported to announce enforceable statutory duties without implementing rules. It finds that the ruling improperly extended requirements beyond interception occurring “within [a carrier’s] switching premises,” and that its enterprise-wide cybersecurity practices were better suited to notice-and-comment rulemaking. The FCC also withdraws the NPRM because it was not published in the Federal Register, so the public-comment period never began. The Commission instead endorses targeted regulation, monitoring, enforcement, and collaboration with industry and federal partners.

**Who it affects** The decision directly affects telecommunications carriers and the broad range of providers that would have been covered by the withdrawn proposal, including broadband, wireline, wireless, VoIP, satellite, cable, broadcasting, 911, 988, and international section 214 providers. CTIA, NCTA, and USTelecom’s reconsideration petition is granted in substance; EPIC opposed rescission.

**Why it matters** The Order rejects a one-size-fits-all CALEA-based cybersecurity mandate after the Salt Typhoon attacks and emphasizes voluntary hardening, threat-information sharing, and targeted measures addressing specific risks. Commissioner Gomez dissented, arguing that rescission removes enforceable accountability, while Chairman Carr and Commissioner Trusty supported the collaborative approach.

**Key dates and numbers**
- January 15–16, 2025: Earlier Declaratory Ruling and NPRM adopted and released.
- February 18, 2025: CTIA, NCTA, and USTelecom filed for reconsideration.
- November 20, 2025: Order adopted; November 21, 2025: released.
- FCC 25-9 and 40 FCC Rcd 876: rescinded and withdrawn.
