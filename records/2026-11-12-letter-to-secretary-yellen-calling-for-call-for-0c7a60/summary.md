The House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party urges Treasury Secretary Janet Yellen to implement Executive Order 14105 broadly and urgently to restrict and monitor U.S. outbound investment in specified Chinese technology sectors.

**What it does** The October 30, 2023, letter recommends rapid issuance of Treasury rules and technical guidance; broad coverage of semiconductors and microelectronics, quantum information technologies, and certain artificial intelligence systems; few or no excepted transactions; notification requirements for all uncovered subcategories; and the maximum penalties allowed for violations. It also calls for stronger coordination with U.S. allies and partners and for addressing passive investment through mutual funds, exchange-traded funds, and index funds. The committee further asks that U.S. capital not support surveillance, genocide, or forced labor in Xinjiang.

**Who it affects** The recommendations are directed to Treasury and concern U.S. investors, businesses, funds, and other parties making investments in the People’s Republic of China, as well as Chinese entities developing or using advanced technologies. The letter focuses particularly on technologies linked to the Chinese Communist Party’s Military-Civil Fusion strategy and the People’s Liberation Army.

**Why it matters** The letter presents outbound investment controls as an initial national-security measure and argues that narrow definitions could be evaded or rendered ineffective by China’s access to foreign capital and expertise. It also frames allied coordination and scrutiny of passive public-market flows as necessary complements to restrictions on active investment.

**Key dates and numbers**
- October 30, 2023: Letter date.
- August 9, 2023: President Biden issued Executive Order 14105.
- Three technology sectors identified in the order: semiconductors and microelectronics, quantum information technologies, and certain artificial intelligence systems.
