The House Select Committee on the CCP urges the United Kingdom to reconsider plans for an expanded Chinese “super-embassy” in London and to coordinate with the United States on China-related security and human-rights concerns, including seeking Jimmy Lai’s unconditional release.

**What it does** The February 26, 2025 letter to UK Ambassador Lord Peter Mandelson registers congressional opposition to the proposed Royal Mint Court project, which the authors describe as a potentially dangerous diplomatic foothold for the Chinese Communist government. It cites alleged Chinese transnational repression, espionage, malign influence, forced labor, human-rights abuses, military and “grey zone” pressure on Taiwan, and threats involving undersea cables. The authors ask Mandelson to convey their concerns to Prime Minister Keir Starmer before his planned meeting with President Donald J. Trump.

**Who it affects** The letter is directed to the UK government and concerns the proposed Chinese diplomatic facility, UK and European dissidents and citizens, Hong Kong political prisoners including UK citizen Jimmy Lai, Uyghurs and Tibetans, and people affected by alleged Chinese influence and repression operations. It also invokes U.S.-UK alliance coordination.

**Why it matters** The document frames diplomatic infrastructure, economic ties, forced labor, and political repression as interconnected national-security issues. It urges the UK to use its relationship with the United States to address China’s conduct and to prioritize Lai’s release.

**Key dates and numbers**
- February 26, 2025: letter date.
- Upcoming meeting: Prime Minister Starmer and President Trump.
- 2023: cited APEC conference in San Francisco, where protesters were allegedly harassed.
- Royal Mint Court: proposed site of the expanded Chinese embassy.
