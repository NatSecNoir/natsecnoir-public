The Letter of Agreement establishes national-security and law-enforcement mitigation commitments for Telxius’s operation of the South America-1 (SAm-1) submarine cable system as a condition of FCC approval of a transaction transferring certain negative control rights from KKR to Pontegadea.

**What it does** The Telxius Parties must maintain a U.S.-citizen security point of contact; provide cable-system, equipment, vendor, and peering information; preserve the ability to interrupt U.S. traffic; obtain review of specified equipment, vendor, operational, and testing changes; and implement logical and physical security, personnel screening, incident reporting, training, annual reporting, audits, and agency visitation. The Compliance Monitoring Agencies may object to proposed equipment or provider changes and may recommend FCC action if the commitments are breached.

**Who it affects** The principal obligations bind Telxius Cable América, Telxius Cable USA, and Telxius Cable Puerto Rico. Telefónica, Telxius Telecom, and Pontegadea undertake related obligations, including not knowingly causing a breach and providing adequate resources. DHS, DOJ, and DoD serve as the Compliance Monitoring Agencies, while the FCC is asked to condition transaction approval on compliance.

**Why it matters** The LOA converts Team Telecom’s review concerns about a U.S.-connected submarine cable into continuing, enforceable oversight of infrastructure, personnel, cybersecurity, supply chain, records, and foreign control. It supersedes Telxius USA’s 2019 Letter of Assurances upon FCC approval.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Executed December 9, 2022.
- Security point of contact proposed within 30 days of execution.
- System information due within 60 days of FCC approval; principal-equipment list due within 30 days.
- Certain changes require 30 days’ notice; equipment testing requires 45 days’ notice.
- Incidents and breaches must be reported within 48 hours; third-party providers generally have 72 hours to report breaches.
