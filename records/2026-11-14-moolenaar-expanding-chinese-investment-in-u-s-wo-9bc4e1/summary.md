The House Select Committee on the CCP urges Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent to maintain heightened scrutiny of expanded Chinese investment and market access in the United States, particularly in critical manufacturing sectors.

**What it does** This March 5, 2026 letter congratulates Bessent on efforts to unwind malign Chinese investments and supports implementation of President Trump’s America First Investment Policy. Chairman John Moolenaar argues that Chinese state subsidies, preferential financing, tax concessions, land grants, and regulatory protections allow firms to sustain losses, export overcapacity, and displace American competitors. He specifically discusses lithium-ion batteries and automobiles, and cites recent enforcement involving HieFo Corporation’s required divestment of Emcore Corporation and an action against Suirui Corporation.

**Who it affects** The requested approach would affect Chinese companies seeking expanded U.S. market access, investment opportunities, production, or technology partnerships, as well as American manufacturers, workers, trusted allied competitors, and agencies reviewing foreign investment. The letter also copies U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer.

**Why it matters** Moolenaar frames inbound investment from China as both an economic-security and national-security issue. He warns that expanded access could deepen U.S. dependence on Chinese industry, undermine foreign-entity-of-concern safeguards and domestic-content incentives, and increase vulnerability to economic coercion in critical supply chains.

**Key dates and numbers**
- March 5, 2026: date of the letter.
- Approximately 4.5% of Chinese GDP: IMF-linked estimate cited for Chinese state subsidies.
- Nearly one-quarter of Chinese industrial firms: reported to have operated at a loss in 2025.
- $1.2 trillion: China’s cited record trade surplus.
