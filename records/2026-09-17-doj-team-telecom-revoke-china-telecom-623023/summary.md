The Executive Branch recommends that the FCC revoke and terminate China Telecom’s international Section 214 authorizations because the carrier presents substantial and unacceptable national-security and law-enforcement risks.

**What it does** This recommendation applies Team Telecom’s previously articulated assessment factors to China Telecom’s existing authorizations. It cites inaccurate statements to Team Telecom about the storage of U.S. records, inaccurate or potentially misleading statements to customers about cybersecurity, possible failures to comply with federal and state privacy and cybersecurity requirements, Chinese government ownership and control, and the risk of economic espionage, traffic disruption, and misrouting. It concludes that additional mitigation would not adequately address the concerns.

**Who it affects** The recommendation directly targets China Telecom’s U.S. entity and its international common-carrier authority to provide facilities-based and resale services between the United States and foreign points. It also affects U.S. customers and counterparties relying on China Telecom’s private lines, data, cloud, managed-network, and mobile-resale services, as well as the FCC’s review of foreign-affiliated carriers.

**Why it matters** The filing is a major Team Telecom challenge to an already-authorized Chinese telecommunications carrier, rather than an objection to a new application. It emphasizes that changed cyber and national-security conditions, combined with ownership and compliance concerns, can support revocation of existing Section 214 authority.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Authorizations were granted or transferred in 2001, 2002, and 2007; the last certification was in 2007.
- China Telecom Corp. Ltd. was described as 70.89% owned through a Chinese state-owned enterprise.
- The recommendation applies Team Telecom Factors 2–14, including trustworthiness, foreign control, compelled assistance, infrastructure risks, and law-enforcement access.
