The House Select Committee on the Strategic Competition Between the United States and the Chinese Communist Party asks Deputy Secretary of Defense Kathleen H. Hicks to assess faster, lower-cost ways to strengthen U.S. and Taiwanese deterrence in the Indo-Pacific.

**What it does** The December 18, 2023 letter argues that delays and limited production capacity for long-range precision-guided munitions threaten preparedness for a possible conflict involving Taiwan. It proposes adapting existing inventory and components into two potential solutions: a “MacGyver” Harpoon system using older Harpoon missiles and improvised land-based launch infrastructure, and Powered JDAMs that could convert 500-pound bombs into longer-range standoff weapons. The committee requests written answers to four sets of questions and, where appropriate, a classified briefing.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to the Department of Defense and concerns U.S. forces, Taiwan’s defense, and the broader U.S. munitions industrial base. It asks whether existing missiles, launchers, bombs, and related components could be repurposed or acquired more rapidly and affordably than advanced long-range PGMs.

**Why it matters** The letter frames munitions availability as a central deterrence and alliance issue. It states that war games indicate U.S. forces could exhaust long-range PGMs in less than one week and notes reported delays in delivering Taiwan’s purchased Harpoon systems. The proposals are presented as supplements—not replacements—for advanced anti-ship and other precision-guided munitions.

**Key dates and numbers**
- December 18, 2023: letter date.
- January 8, 2024: requested deadline for written responses.
- Taiwan is awaiting 400 ground-launched Harpoon missiles and 100 launch systems.
- The letter describes a potential Powered JDAM range of at least 300 nautical miles and cites a historical standard JDAM cost of about $30,000.
- H. Res. 11 is cited as the committee’s authority.
