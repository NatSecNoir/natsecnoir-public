The House Select Committee on the CCP requests closed-door briefings from Verizon, AT&T, and Lumen Technologies concerning the reported Salt Typhoon cyberattack and its potential access to systems used for court-authorized federal wiretapping requests.

**What it does** The October 11, 2024 letter, signed by Chairman John Moolenaar and Ranking Member Raja Krishnamoorthi, asks appropriate representatives of each company to brief the Committee. It seeks information about when the companies first learned of the matters reported by the Wall Street Journal, measures taken to protect lawful-intercept systems, and steps Congress and the federal government could take to help defend against PRC state-sponsored hacking.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to Verizon, AT&T, and Lumen Technologies, which the letter identifies as among the companies reportedly affected by the potential breach. It also concerns federal agencies and personnel relying on telecommunications systems to support court-authorized network wiretapping requests, as well as broader critical-infrastructure operators.

**Why it matters** The letter frames the reported intrusion as a national-security and cybersecurity concern because unauthorized access could reveal which targets interest the U.S. government, undermine intelligence collection, or enable disinformation. It places the request in the context of the Committee’s prior attention to Volt Typhoon and alleged PRC compromise of U.S. critical infrastructure, while acknowledging that a federal investigation may be ongoing.

**Key dates and numbers**
- October 5, 2024: Wall Street Journal report cited as the basis for the request.
- October 10, 2024: Letter date shown in the document; catalogue date is October 11, 2024.
- Three companies are asked to provide briefings.
- Three subjects are requested: initial awareness, protection of lawful-intercept systems, and possible government assistance.
