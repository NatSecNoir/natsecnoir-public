The letter urges Secretary of State Antony J. Blinken to pursue diplomatic and investigative action concerning alleged mistreatment, torture, and rights abuses against political prisoners and other detainees in Hong Kong.

**What it does** The signatories ask the U.S. Consul General in Hong Kong to evaluate prison conditions and determine whether Hong Kong is violating its laws and international human-rights obligations. They request a Department of State briefing on prison conditions, U.S. efforts to secure the release of political prisoners, and steps to hold Hong Kong authorities accountable for arbitrary detention. The letter also urges Blinken to call on U.N. High Commissioner for Human Rights Volker Türk to convene an urgent discussion at the U.N. Human Rights Council, and asks U.S. representatives in Geneva to support investigation and access for relevant Special Rapporteurs.

**Who it affects** The concerns include political prisoners such as Jimmy Lai and Owen Chow, Chow’s lawyer Phyllis Woo, younger detainees, and prisoners held in Hong Kong facilities. The requested actions would involve the State Department, U.S. diplomatic representatives, Hong Kong authorities, and U.N. human-rights mechanisms.

**Why it matters** The letter describes allegations of torture, physical and sexual abuse, poor conditions, restricted religious materials, solitary confinement, denial of independent medical care, and barriers to confidential complaints. It frames these conditions as part of a broader deterioration of human rights and the rule of law in Hong Kong.

**Key dates and numbers**
- The document is dated November 13, 2024; the catalogue date is November 15, 2024.
- The letter references Jimmy Lai’s detention in solitary confinement for over three and a half years.
- It notes that more than 50 U.N. independent human-rights experts previously requested discussion of Hong Kong’s human-rights situation.
- The cited Radio Free Asia article was published March 30, 2024.
