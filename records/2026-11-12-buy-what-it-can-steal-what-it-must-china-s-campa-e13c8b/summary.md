The report finds that China’s progress toward frontier AI leadership remains dependent on Western equipment, chips, cloud compute, and model capabilities, while gaps in U.S. policy and enforcement enable both lawful access and illicit acquisition.

**What it does** The Committee examines China’s access to the AI technology stack through eight findings: continued procurement of chipmaking equipment; lawful acquisition of advanced AI chips; offshore cloud access; post-sale servicing and verification gaps; shell-company circumvention of foundry due diligence; smuggling of restricted chips and servers; industrial-scale extraction of U.S. model capabilities; and inadequate penalties. It recommends legislation and administrative action, including the MATCH Act, AI OVERWATCH Act, SCALE Act, Remote Access Security Act, Chip Security Act, and STOP Shells Act. It also urges restrictions on cloud access, stronger servicing controls, treatment of adversarial distillation as industrial espionage, possible Entity List review of DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, and MiniMax, and increased civil and criminal penalties.

**Who it affects** The recommendations target Congress, BIS, DOJ, the State Department, DHS, U.S. AI developers, semiconductor and cloud companies, foreign foundries, exporters, brokers, and Chinese technology firms and intermediaries.

**Why it matters** The report frames AI security as a supply-chain and access-control problem extending beyond direct exports. It argues that offshore compute, gray-market model access, servicing, shell companies, and smuggling can preserve China’s access to capabilities that geographic export controls alone do not prevent.

**Key dates and numbers**
- China spent $38 billion on foreign chipmaking equipment in 2024; its share of ASML DUV sales rose from 26% in 2022 to 70% in 2024.
- BIS shifted H200 exports to China to case-by-case review in January 2026; reported approvals could cover more than 400,000 chips.
- Anthropic reported more than 16 million exchanges across 24,000 fraudulent accounts in disclosed distillation campaigns.
- The report is dated April 16, 2026.
