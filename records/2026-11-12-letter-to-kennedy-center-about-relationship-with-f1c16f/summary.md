The House Select Committee on the CCP asks Kennedy Center leaders to explain and reconsider the planned National Ballet of China performances in light of the ballet’s alleged ties to the Chinese Communist Party and the PRC’s suppression of artistic expression.

**What it does** The January 24, 2025 letter addresses Chairman David M. Rubenstein and President Deborah F. Rutter about “Chinese New Year (A Ballet in Two Acts),” scheduled for January 29 through February 2. It asks who is funding the performances, whether U.S. appropriated money or PRC-linked entities are involved, whether funding will be returned, and whether the Center will condition or end its relationship with the National Ballet. It also requests public attention to imprisoned and persecuted artists and meetings with Chinese dissidents.

**Who it affects** The immediate recipients are Kennedy Center leadership, and the questions concern the Center’s patrons, funders, Congress, U.S. taxpayers, the National Ballet of China, and entities associated with the PRC and CCP. An appendix identifies artists whom the letter describes as detained, imprisoned, censored, harassed, or otherwise targeted.

**Why it matters** The letter frames a cultural performance at a federally supported public building as a potential vehicle for CCP propaganda and transnational repression, while acknowledging support for free artistic expression and declining to judge the ballet’s artistic merits. It cites the Kennedy Center’s $44 million FY2024 congressional appropriation and seeks accountability for activities conducted in the building.

**Key dates and numbers**
- January 24, 2025: letter date.
- January 28, 2025: requested response deadline.
- January 29–February 2, 2025: scheduled performances.
- $44 million: FY2024 congressional appropriation cited for maintaining the Kennedy Center.
- Seven questions: inquiries concerning funding, censorship, dissidents, and the Center’s relationship with the National Ballet.
