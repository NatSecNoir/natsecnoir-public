The House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party urges Secretary of Commerce Gina Raimondo to strengthen the October 7, 2022 export controls on advanced computing and semiconductor-related items to prevent workarounds that could benefit the PRC and its military.

**What it does** The bipartisan letter asks the Administration to tighten the advanced-computing controls, including by lowering the 600 Gbyte/s bidirectional-transfer-rate threshold sufficiently to prevent engineering solutions that evade the rules. It also asks officials to consider safeguards against Chinese firms using cloud-computing services to outsource advanced-computing needs. The lawmakers frame these requests as updates to the October 7, 2022 rule on advanced computing and semiconductor manufacturing items, supercomputer and semiconductor end uses, and Entity List modifications; the letter itself makes recommendations rather than adopting a new control.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to Commerce Secretary Gina Raimondo and concerns U.S. exporters, semiconductor and advanced-computing businesses, cloud-service providers, PRC technology firms, multinational entities, and the People’s Liberation Army. The letter is signed by Chairman Mike Gallagher and Ranking Member Raja Krishnamoorthi.

**Why it matters** The lawmakers say PRC firms have identified legal workarounds that preserve access to advanced semiconductors capable of efficiently training artificial-intelligence models. They argue that stronger controls are needed to protect U.S. economic and national security and maintain a technological edge over the PLA, while acknowledging that the rules may need updating as new information emerges.

**Key dates and numbers**
- July 28, 2023: Letter date.
- October 7, 2022: Export-control rules the lawmakers ask the Administration to strengthen.
- 600 Gbyte/s: Bidirectional-transfer-rate threshold specifically identified for reconsideration.
