The House Select Committee on the CCP urges the FDA to impose stronger safeguards before relying on clinical-trial data generated in China, citing reported undisclosed patient deaths and opaque research practices.

**What it does** The August 21, 2026 letter asks the FDA to decline China-generated data in U.S. Investigational New Drug (IND), New Drug Application (NDA), and Biologics License Application (BLA) submissions unless the agency has conducted an in-person audit of the trial site within the preceding twelve months. It also requests a comprehensive, publicly released risk assessment of U.S. drug-development and marketing approvals that rely on Chinese investigator-initiated trials (IITs) and Clinical Trial Applications (CTAs), with particular attention to gene-editing therapies.

**Who it affects** The recommendations are directed to Acting FDA Commissioner Kyle A. Diamantas and would affect pharmaceutical and biotechnology companies using, acquiring, or licensing therapies supported by clinical data from China, as well as patients and trial participants. The letter was signed by Chairman John Moolenaar and Representative Ben Cline of the House Select Committee on China.

**Why it matters** The authors argue that China’s restricted information environment and limited visibility into trial conduct, data collection, and adverse-event reporting create risks for U.S. patients and could undermine confidence in FDA decisions. They cite three reported deaths, including two children, in experimental gene-editing trials and note that FDA conducted only 46 inspections of clinical-trial sites in China from fiscal year 2012 through March 1, 2023. The letter frames independent verification as necessary to preserve rigor, ethics, and accountability in U.S. drug regulation.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated August 20, 2026; catalogue date: August 21, 2026.
- Three reported deaths in Chinese experimental gene-editing trials, including two children.
- Proposed audit window: within 12 months before application submission.
- 46 FDA inspections of Chinese clinical-trial sites from fiscal year 2012 through March 1, 2023.
