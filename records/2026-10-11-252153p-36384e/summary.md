The Third Circuit affirmed partial summary judgment for Thomson Reuters, holding that 2,243 Westlaw headnotes were copyrightable and that ROSS Intelligence’s copying of them to train a competing legal-research platform was not fair use.

**What it does** This interlocutory appeal addresses the originality of Westlaw headnotes and ROSS’s fair-use defense. The court held that editors’ selections, wording, factual context, and concise presentation supplied the required “creative spark,” while judicial opinions themselves remain uncopyrightable and freely publishable. Applying the four statutory fair-use factors, the court found that ROSS’s commercial use was minimally transformative, extensive copying was unnecessary because the underlying opinions were available, and the use harmed existing and potential markets, including licensing headnotes as AI-training data. The court therefore affirmed the district court’s order.

**Who it affects** The decision directly affects Thomson Reuters, West Publishing, ROSS, and the contractors LegalEase Solutions and Morae Global whose conduct was attributed to ROSS. It also bears on developers using copyrighted editorial or reference materials to train non-generative AI systems, particularly where the resulting product competes with the source.

**Why it matters** The opinion distinguishes copying for AI training from cases involving more transformative software interoperability or search uses. It emphasizes that AI-related use is not automatically transformative or justified, especially when the copier could use uncopyrighted underlying materials and instead takes copyrighted editorial work because it is easier.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Argued June 11, 2026; filed September 29, 2026.
- Appeal No. 25-2153; district-court case No. 1:20-cv-00613.
- The ruling covers 2,243 headnotes; ROSS’s training memoranda used approximately 25,000 headnotes, compared with Thomson Reuters’s approximately 28 million.
