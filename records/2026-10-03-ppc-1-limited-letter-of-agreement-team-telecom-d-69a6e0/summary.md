The Agreement requires Vocus Group Limited to implement extensive national-security and law-enforcement safeguards for the PPC-1 submarine cable as a condition of the Committee’s support for the FCC’s review of Vocus’s proposed acquisition of PPC 1 Limited and the associated cable landing license.

**What it does** The National Security Agreement appoints DHS, DOJ, and DoD as compliance monitoring agencies for the Committee. It requires Vocus to designate a security officer; provide cable-system, equipment, service-provider, and covered-third-party information; obtain advance review or non-objection for specified changes; control access to facilities, systems, communications, and U.S. records; screen relevant personnel; report incidents and breaches; file annual compliance reports; and submit to possible third-party audits and site visits. The Agreement also gives the Committee a basis to recommend FCC license modification, revocation, or other relief for a breach.

**Who it affects** The obligations bind Vocus and extend through its affiliates, subsidiaries, contractors, service providers, equipment vendors, covered third parties, and successors. They principally concern PPC-1’s U.S. infrastructure, network operations, traffic, and records.

**Why it matters** This is a detailed Team Telecom mitigation framework for a submarine cable transaction, combining operational control, supply-chain scrutiny, personnel screening, foreign-access reporting, and rapid incident disclosure.

**Key dates and numbers**
- FCC referral: February 21, 2025.
- Vocus signature: June 5, 2025; DHS and DoD: June 18, 2025; DOJ: June 23, 2025; the last signature establishes the NSA Effective Date.
- Incident reporting is generally required within 48 hours; service-provider breach notice within 72 hours.
- Covered-third-party and equipment/service-provider notices commonly require 30 days’ advance notice, with CMA responses generally due within 45 days.
