The report finds that weak enforcement of Executive Order 10043 and extensive U.S.-China academic ties have allowed Chinese nationals linked to the PLA and China’s defense-industrial base to access U.S. higher education, federally funded research, and sensitive technology pathways.

**What it does** This Select Committee investigation examines six U.S. universities’ student affiliations, funding, research activities, and collaborations with Chinese institutions. It argues that “fundamental” research can still present national-security risks and describes Chinese military-linked universities, including the Seven Sons of National Defense, Seven Sons of Ordnance/Arms Industry, and SASTIND-affiliated institutions. The report also documents University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign’s decision to terminate its joint engineering institute, joint degree programs, priority-admission agreements, and short-term faculty training program with Chinese universities. It recommends stronger visa screening, eligibility restrictions, interagency review, disclosure and university reporting requirements, limits on sensitive graduate study and federally funded research, and safeguards against transnational repression.

**Who it affects** The recommendations target PRC nationals seeking or holding student and research visas, U.S. universities, federal research agencies, and institutions connected to the PRC defense and security apparatus. The report specifically discusses Maryland, Illinois, Carnegie Mellon, USC, Purdue, and Stanford.

**Why it matters** The report frames academic exchange and taxpayer-funded research as potential channels for military-civil fusion, technology transfer, and PLA capability development. It presents visa enforcement, research security, and institutional due diligence as linked national-security issues.

**Key dates and numbers**
- The report is dated September 19, 2025.
- The investigation covered six U.S. universities.
- EO 10043 was signed May 29, 2020.
- The report identifies 58 SASTIND co-administered Chinese universities.
- UIUC’s Zhejiang University joint institute was set to expire January 31, 2026; its teacher-training program ended August 9, 2025.
