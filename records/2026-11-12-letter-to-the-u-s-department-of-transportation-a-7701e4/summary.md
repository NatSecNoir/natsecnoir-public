The House Committee on Small Business, the House Committee on Science, Space, and Technology, and the House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party ask the Department of Transportation to provide information and procedures addressing alleged foreign exploitation and national-security vulnerabilities in the SBIR and STTR programs.

**What it does** The letter asks DOT for annual data on unsuccessful and flagged applications across Phases I, II, and III; breakdowns of flags involving foreign-adversary ties, export-control or dual-use concerns, inadequate vetting, and other security red flags; and the share of flagged applications that were unsuccessful. It also requests information on common disclosure requirements, flagging criteria, post-flag procedures, interagency communications, and monitoring practices.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to Secretary Sean P. Duffy and concerns DOT’s participation in the taxpayer-funded SBIR and STTR programs, as well as American small businesses applying for or receiving program support. The committees identify China, including Chinese state-sponsored talent programs, PRC-based subsidiaries, and state-controlled venture capital, as a central concern.

**Why it matters** The committees argue that weak due diligence could allow sensitive technology, intellectual property, and taxpayer-funded research to reach foreign adversaries. They cite a 2021 DoD-commissioned report that found insufficient capabilities for assessing national-security risks and seek records that could support legislative and policy reforms.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated February 26, 2025; response requested by March 12, 2025.
- Requested data begins January 20, 2021.
- SBIR/STTR authorization was set to expire September 30, 2025.
- The letter states that small businesses comprise 99.9 percent of U.S. businesses.
