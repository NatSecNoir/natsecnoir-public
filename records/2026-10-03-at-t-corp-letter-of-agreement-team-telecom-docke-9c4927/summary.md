The Letter of Assurances records AT&T Corp.’s commitments to the U.S. Department of Homeland Security (DHS) to address national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety concerns tied to continued operation of the Columbus II submarine cable system, with DHS intending to seek FCC licensing conditions based on those commitments.

**What it does** The agreement requires AT&T to maintain a U.S.-based Security Point of Contact; provide DHS with network, contract, restoration, and principal-equipment information; preserve the ability to interrupt U.S.-bound traffic; give advance notice of equipment, vendor, testing, service, and operational changes; implement logical and physical security, personnel screening, incident reporting, training, and change-in-control procedures; submit annual compliance reports; and support DHS audits, consultations, and facility visits. AT&T also agrees that DHS may ask the FCC to modify, condition, revoke, cancel, terminate, or nullify a relevant authorization if AT&T breaches the LOA.

**Who it affects** The commitments bind AT&T, its successors, assigns, subsidiaries, and affiliates in connection with Columbus II, which links West Palm Beach, Florida, and St. Thomas, U.S. Virgin Islands. They also govern vendors, contractors, subcontractors, personnel, and other consortium participants insofar as their activities affect the cable system or its domestic communications infrastructure.

**Why it matters** The LOA illustrates Team Telecom mitigation for a submarine cable authorization: DHS retains substantial visibility, approval, objection, audit, and inspection rights while AT&T assumes operational and cybersecurity obligations. It converts national-security concerns into continuing conditions that can affect equipment, suppliers, access, ownership, and the FCC authorization itself.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Dated June 22, 2020; associated with FCC cable-landing-license docket SCL-LIC-20190326-00010.
- Columbus II was originally licensed in 1993 and links West Palm Beach with St. Thomas.
- AT&T must propose a Security Point of Contact within 30 days of execution and provide specified system information and equipment lists within 45 days.
- DHS must receive written reports of qualifying incidents or breaches within 48 hours, followed by a detailed report within 15 days.
- AT&T generally must provide 30, 45, or 90 days’ notice depending on the proposed change or objection process; foreign ownership or control changes trigger notice within 10 days, with a 10-percent threshold for AT&T or Columbus II and a greater-than-5-percent threshold for another consortium member.
