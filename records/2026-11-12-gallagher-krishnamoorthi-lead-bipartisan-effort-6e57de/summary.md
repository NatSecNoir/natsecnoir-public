The House Select Committee on the Strategic Competition Between the U.S. and the Chinese Communist Party announced a bipartisan congressional letter supporting a petition that asks the State and Treasury Departments to impose Global Magnitsky sanctions on seven Chinese companies linked to alleged Uyghur forced labor and severe human rights abuses in the seafood industry and supply chain.

**What it does** The letter supports a Global Magnitsky Sanctions Recommendation petition submitted by the Outlaw Ocean Project in collaboration with a human rights NGO. It urges the departments to review and approve sanctions against the named companies, citing evidence that seafood connected to forced labor enters U.S. markets and that implicated entities hold contracts with major grocery retailers, food providers, and the U.S. government. The document describes the petition as a means of pressuring Chinese firms to end the use of Uyghur forced labor; it is an advocacy letter, not itself a sanctions designation.

**Who it affects** The petition concerns Yantai Sanko Fisheries, Yantai Longwin Foods, the Chishan Group and named affiliates, Shandong Meijia Group and named affiliates, Qingdao Tianyuan Aquatic Foodstuffs, Rongsense Group and named affiliates, and Xinjiang Zhongtai Zhihui Modern Service. The letter is signed by Representatives Mike Gallagher, Raja Krishnamoorthi, Jared Huffman, Raul Grijalva, and additional bipartisan House members, and is directed to the State and Treasury Departments.

**Why it matters** The letter connects alleged forced labor in China’s seafood sector to U.S. procurement and imports, while invoking the Global Magnitsky program, the Uyghur Human Rights Policy Act, and the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act as relevant authorities and policy context.

**Key dates and numbers**
- February 15, 2024: letter announcement date.
- Seven Chinese companies or corporate groups are identified in the petition.
- December 6, 2023: a similar petition was submitted in Canada, according to the document.
- The Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act became law in 2021.
