The House Select Committee on the CCP urges President Donald J. Trump to coordinate with U.S. allies on reciprocal measures to pressure China to restore non-discriminatory exports of rare earth materials and magnets.

**What it does**
In a September 18, 2025 letter, Chairman John Moolenaar recommends that the President launch a coordinated effort beginning with the G7 and extending to the Quad and NATO. The proposed measures are to restrict or suspend Chinese airline landing rights at U.S. and allied airports; review export-control licensing policy for commercial aircraft, aircraft parts, and maintenance services sold to China; and restrict or prohibit outbound investment in China’s aviation sector, including joint ventures and wholly owned production facilities.

**Who it affects**
The recommendations would affect China, Chinese airlines and aviation businesses, U.S. and allied airports, commercial aerospace manufacturers and service providers, and investors in China’s aviation sector. The letter also targets coordinated action by the United States and major partners, including the G7, Quad, NATO, and potentially the European Union.

**Why it matters**
The committee characterizes China’s licensing delays and denials for rare earths and magnets as weaponization of critical-mineral supply chains, particularly harming defense and advanced-manufacturing industries. It argues that reciprocal pressure on China’s strategic aviation sector could strengthen U.S. supply-chain resilience, reinforce policy toward Russia, and create leverage in negotiations with Beijing. The letter makes recommendations rather than announcing binding measures.

**Key dates and numbers**
- September 18, 2025: Letter date.
- Three proposed measures: landing-rights restrictions, commercial aerospace export-control review, and outbound-investment restrictions.
- Proposed coordination begins with the G7 and may extend to the Quad and NATO.
- The letter cites three WTO disputes concerning Chinese export restraints or duties on raw materials and critical minerals.
