The House Select Committee on the Strategic Competition Between the United States and the Chinese Communist Party urged Volkswagen Group to comply with the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act (UFLPA), explain its Xinjiang operations and supply-chain practices, and cease operations in Xinjiang.

**What it does** The February 22, 2024 letter from Chairman Mike Gallagher and Ranking Member Raja Krishnamoorthi responds to reports that U.S. Customs blocked thousands of Porsche, Bentley, and Audi vehicles made by Volkswagen Group because they contained parts made with forced labor in Xinjiang. It asks CEO Oliver Blume to explain Volkswagen’s Xinjiang operations and the steps the company is taking to comply with the UFLPA, and urges the company to cease those operations.

**Who it affects** The letter is directed to Volkswagen Group and its CEO, Oliver Blume. It also identifies Volkswagen’s partnership with SAIC Motor Corp., described in the document as a state-owned entity of the People’s Republic of China, and discusses the company’s Porsche, Bentley, and Audi vehicles.

**Why it matters** The lawmakers invoke the UFLPA’s prohibition on importing products made wholly or partly with forced labor and connect reported vehicle detentions to concerns about Uyghur forced labor in Volkswagen’s global supply chain and Xinjiang factory. The letter is a congressional request and does not itself impose an enforcement action or binding regulatory requirement.

**Key dates and numbers**
- February 22, 2024: Letter date.
- March 5, 2024: Deadline given to Volkswagen’s CEO to respond.
- Since 2013: Volkswagen has partnered with SAIC Motor Corp. to construct and operate a factory in Xinjiang, according to the document.
- Thousands: Vehicles were reportedly blocked at the U.S. border.
