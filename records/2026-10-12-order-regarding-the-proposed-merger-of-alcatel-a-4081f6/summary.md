The President prohibited the proposed Alcatel–Lucent merger unless the parties executed specified national-security agreements and related documents on the required schedule.

**What it does** The order, issued under section 721 of the Defense Production Act, finds credible evidence that Alcatel’s control of Lucent might threaten U.S. national security and that other legal authorities were inadequate to address the matter. It conditions any merger on Alcatel, Lucent, and Alcatel USA Inc. executing a National Security Agreement with the Departments of Defense, Justice, Commerce, and Homeland Security by the transaction’s closing date. It also requires a Special Security Agreement with the Department of Defense and execution of both documents by the future Secure Subsidiary. The Attorney General may take steps necessary to enforce the order, while the President reserves authority to issue further orders.

**Who it affects** The order directly affects Alcatel, Lucent, Alcatel USA Inc., the planned Secure Subsidiary, and the participating national-security agencies. It also governs whether the proposed merger may proceed.

**Why it matters** This is a presidential intervention in a cross-border telecommunications merger using national-security agreements and security arrangements as conditions of approval. It illustrates how executive-branch review can require structural and operational protections, backed by a prohibition on completing the transaction.

**Key dates and numbers**
- November 17, 2006: order date.
- November 6, 2006: date of the draft agreements referenced in the order.
- 50 U.S.C. App. 2170: section 721 authority cited.
- 71 FR 67429; FR Doc. 06-9381: Federal Register publication identifiers.
