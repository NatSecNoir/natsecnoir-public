A Santa Fe County District Court judgment holds Meta/Facebook liable for 43,899,720 willful violations of New Mexico’s Unfair Practices Act and awards a reduced civil penalty of $25 billion, with interest.

**What it does** The judgment adopts the jury’s findings that 26 public representations about users’ control of data, third-party data misuse, misinformation, hate speech, and equal enforcement were unfair or deceptive and willful. It also finds five willful unconscionable trade practices. Applying the statutory maximum of $5,000 per violation, the court calculates $219,498,600,000, then reduces the award to $25,000,000,000. Interest accrues at 15% annually, while injunctive relief is reserved for further briefing.

**Who it affects** The order directly affects Meta/Facebook and the State of New Mexico. Its findings concern Facebook users, app developers, business partners, advertisers, public figures, and other entities subject to Facebook’s content-enforcement and data-access systems. The judgment discusses senior executives including Mark Zuckerberg and Sheryl Sandberg, as well as the company’s handling of third-party applications and user data.

**Why it matters** The ruling treats repeated public statements over more than eleven years as a systematic course of deceptive conduct, including undisclosed exceptions to “friend sharing,” an allegedly ineffective App Developer Investigation, and unequal enforcement through CrossCheck/XCheck. The court emphasizes the scale of affected audiences, internal knowledge, repetition, and willfulness in approving the maximum statutory per-violation assessment before reduction.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Filed: October 21, 2026.
- Conduct at issue: May 24, 2010–August 18, 2021.
- Jury findings: 26 willful unfair-or-deceptive representations; 43,899,720 violations; five willful unconscionable-practice findings.
- Penalty: $219,498,600,000 calculated; $25,000,000,000 awarded; 15% annual interest.
- New Mexico Facebook monthly active users cited: 1,386,648 as of June 30, 2020.
