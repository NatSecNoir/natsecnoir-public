The House Select Committee on the CCP and four other congressional committee chairmen request that the Department of Commerce investigate and restrict Chinese-controlled technologies across critical U.S. sectors through the Office of Information and Communications Technology and Services (OICTS).

**What it does** The October 30, 2025 letter asks Secretary Howard W. Lutnick for a briefing “as soon as possible, but no later than November 30, 2025,” on when Commerce will evaluate the listed sectors and publish OICTS guidance intended to eliminate identified technology threats. Appendix I identifies industries for “immediate action,” including networked sensors, medical equipment, AI infrastructure, robotics, cellular IoT, EDA tools, energy systems, industrial controls, PKI, routers, semiconductor manufacturing equipment, subsea cables, trucking, and unmanned systems.

**Who it affects** The proposed scrutiny would reach Chinese or Chinese-controlled entities operating in or supplying the U.S. market, including Hesai Technology, Unitree Robotics, Quectel, Huawei, TP-Link Systems, Sungrow, DJI, BYD, and others named in the appendix. It also directly addresses Commerce and OICTS, while requesting action from Secretary Lutnick.

**Why it matters** The letter frames control of data, software, and connected infrastructure as a national-security issue and urges supply-chain intervention before adversary technology can create vulnerabilities in U.S. economic and critical infrastructure.

**Key dates and numbers**
- October 30, 2025: letter date.
- November 30, 2025: requested deadline for a Commerce briefing.
- Appendix I: 15 listed sectors or industry groupings requiring immediate consideration.
