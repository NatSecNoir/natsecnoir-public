The House Select Committee on the CCP requests written information from AutoZone, O’Reilly Auto Parts, Genuine Parts Company, Advance Auto Parts, and First Brands Group about procurement from Qingdao Sunsong and their controls against tariff evasion and forced labor.

**What it does** The five substantially identical letters allege that Qingdao Sunsong and its U.S. subsidiary may have circumvented Section 301 tariffs by routing Chinese rubber hose assembly products through Thailand without substantial transformation. They describe Qingdao Sunsong disclosures to the Beijing Stock Exchange, including a shift of production to Thailand, relationships with Virayont Group and Imperial Cable Industry, and processing that added only 12 to 23 cents, or approximately 4% to 8% of declared assembly value. The committee asks each recipient to explain purchases, country-of-origin verification, responses to the DHS investigation, supplier due diligence, and controls addressing goods made with forced labor.

**Who it affects** The requests directly affect the five named U.S. automotive-parts companies and their relationships with Qingdao Sunsong, Sunsong North America, and Sunsong Thailand. The letters also identify potential legal exposure for importers or purchasers knowingly involved in unlawful transshipment.

**Why it matters** The document links supply-chain diligence to enforcement of U.S. tariff, customs, and forced-labor laws. It warns that knowingly falsifying origin information or aiding unlawful transshipment can produce civil and criminal consequences, while congressional scrutiny may extend to downstream retailers and distributors.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letters dated September 4, 2024; responses requested by September 20, 2024.
- Section 301 tariffs of 25% on relevant PRC automotive parts took effect May 10, 2019.
- The described Thai processing occurred from 2019 through 2021 and added 12 to 23 cents per assembly.
- The letters cite potential penalties under 19 U.S.C. §§ 1586 and 1592, including imprisonment of up to 15 years under § 1586.
