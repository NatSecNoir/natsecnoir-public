The House Select Committee on the CCP urges Commerce Secretary Gina Raimondo to restrict Huawei’s access to U.S.-produced semiconductor manufacturing equipment (SME) through its suspected network of affiliated Chinese chip companies.

**What it does** This October 16, 2024 letter requests rapid Commerce action to prevent Huawei from using U.S. technology to expand semiconductor fabrication and evade existing restrictions. It identifies Pengxinxu, SwaySure Technology, and Qingdao SiEn as firms reportedly connected to Huawei and the PRC government, and points to PXW Semiconductor’s 2022 placement on the Entity List as a precedent. The members argue that controls on SME should complement restrictions on U.S. chips supplied to Huawei.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to Commerce and would affect Huawei, the named semiconductor firms, U.S. manufacturers of SME, and foreign chipmakers competing in the global market. The letter also copies the Secretaries of Defense, State, and Energy. John Moolenaar and Raja Krishnamoorthi sign as chairman and ranking member.

**Why it matters** The letter frames Huawei’s alleged clandestine fabrication network as a national-security and export-control concern, warning that continued access to U.S. equipment could enable advanced chip production and undermine the purpose of Huawei-related restrictions. It calls for action coordinated with allies and partners while expressing concern that delay allows Huawei to stockpile equipment.

**Key dates and numbers**
- October 16, 2024: letter date.
- 2022: PXW Semiconductor was added to the Entity List.
- Three named suspected network firms: Pengxinxu, SwaySure Technology, and Qingdao SiEn.
- The letter cites eight footnotes and references Commerce’s reported 2024 revocation of licenses for some U.S. chip exports to Huawei.
