The letter asks Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche to direct the Department of Justice and FBI to investigate the vandalism of a California museum commemorating the victims of the 1989 Tiananmen Square Massacre, including possible links to Chinese Communist Party transnational repression.

**What it does** The letter, signed by House Select Committee on China Chairman John Moolenaar and Congressional-Executive Commission on China Co-Chair Chris Smith, requests a federal investigation into the break-in and damage at the June 4th Massacre Memorial Museum in El Monte, California. The lawmakers urge DOJ to determine whether the incident was ordinary vandalism or was planned, supported, or carried out on behalf of the CCP. They frame the requested inquiry as part of protecting Chinese diaspora communities, dissidents, survivors, and critics of the CCP in the United States.

**Who it affects** The request concerns DOJ, the FBI, the El Monte Police Department, the museum, and communities that may face foreign intimidation or transnational repression. The document states that local police opened the case as a hate-crime investigation and that it remained ongoing.

**Why it matters** The letter connects an attack on a site preserving the historical record of Tiananmen to broader congressional efforts against foreign-government harassment, intimidation, surveillance, and coercion inside the United States. It also underscores the lawmakers’ concern that Chinese democracy advocates and Chinese Americans should be able to speak without fear.

**Key dates and numbers**
- July 9, 2026: letter date.
- May 31, 2026: reported date of the museum vandalism.
- 1989: year of the Tiananmen Square Massacre.
- 37th: anniversary approaching when the vandalism occurred.
- H.R. 4829: Transnational Repression Policy Act referenced in the background.
