The House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party and House Homeland Security lawmakers demanded information about Shanghai Zhenhua Heavy Industries (ZPMC) equipment and its potential cybersecurity risks at U.S. maritime ports.

**What it does** The March 8, 2024 letter describes findings from an eight-month joint investigation, including cellular modems discovered on ship-to-shore crane components and in a server room containing cranes’ firewall and networking equipment. Some modems were found to have active connections to crane operational components. The lawmakers state that the components were outside existing port contracts and did not appear necessary to operate the cranes or related infrastructure. They request information about CCP or PRC-government requests involving U.S.-based maritime equipment, ZPMC’s dealings with PRC entities, CCP membership among ZPMC personnel, communications with the PRC government, and contacts with entities posing risks to U.S. foreign-policy or national-security interests.

**Who it affects** The letter concerns U.S. seaports using ZPMC cranes, ZPMC and its parent China Communications Construction Co., relevant PRC entities, and U.S. maritime-security and law-enforcement agencies. It also discusses ABB’s commercial relationship with ZPMC and its responses to committee inquiries.

**Why it matters** The lawmakers warn that ZPMC’s ties to the CCP, proximity to the People’s Liberation Army Navy’s main shipyard, reported intelligence-gathering equipment, and PRC subsidies could create opportunities for cyber espionage, equipment manipulation, or disruption of critical maritime infrastructure. The letter frames dependence on PRC state-owned enterprises as a supply-chain and national-security risk.

**Key dates and numbers**
- March 8, 2024: letter date.
- Eight months: duration of the joint investigation described by the lawmakers.
- 2021: year the FBI reportedly discovered intelligence-gathering equipment on a vessel delivering ZPMC cranes to Baltimore.
- April 2023 and July 2023: earlier committee letters concerning ZPMC and ABB.
