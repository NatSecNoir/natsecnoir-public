The House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party requests information from Nike, Adidas, SHEIN, and Temu about possible Xinjiang forced labor in their supply chains and the adequacy of their compliance and import controls.

**What it does** The four letters, all dated May 2, 2023, ask the companies to respond by May 16, 2023. Nike and Adidas are asked about cotton and other textile inputs, named Chinese textile suppliers, supplier tracing and audits, contractual prohibitions on Xinjiang inputs, verification of labor conditions, and compliance plans under the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act (UFLPA). SHEIN is questioned about cotton content, chain-of-custody documentation, Oritain testing, responsible-sourcing assessments, contract manufacturers, and use of the Section 321 de minimis provision. Temu is asked about third-party sellers, sourcing controls, de minimis shipment volumes and valuations, supplier assessments, and UFLPA compliance. Each letter also directs the recipient to preserve relevant paper and electronic records.

**Who it affects** The requests are addressed to Nike CEO John Donahoe, Adidas North America President Rupert Campbell, SHEIN CEO Yangtian (Chris) Xu, and Temu and PDD Holdings executives Qin Sun and Chen Lei. They also identify textile companies including Jiangsu Lianfa Group, Luthai Textile, Huafu Fashion, Texhong Textile, and Weiqiao Textile.

**Why it matters** The letters frame apparel and marketplace supply chains, especially low-value direct shipments, as potential channels for goods made with forced labor to enter the United States despite the UFLPA. They place congressional investigative pressure on major retailers and platforms to document traceability, supplier due diligence, and import compliance.

**Key dates and numbers**
- March 23, 2023: Committee hearing on the CCP’s ongoing Uyghur genocide.
- December 23, 2021: UFLPA signed into law.
- May 16, 2023: response deadline.
- $800: Section 321 de minimis value threshold described in the letters.
- 3,237 shipments valued at $961 million: CBP figures cited for UFLPA scrutiny.
