The House Select Committee on the Strategic Competition Between the United States and the Chinese Communist Party and the United Kingdom Foreign Affairs Select Committee request information from HSBC about its alleged refusal to permit Hong Kong residents holding British National (Overseas) passports to make early withdrawals from Mandatory Provident Fund accounts.

**What it does** The June 30, 2023 letter to HSBC Group Chief Executive Noel Quinn describes concerns that HSBC, as an MPF trustee institution, declined applications based on BN(O) passports and related U.K. visa documents. It asks five sets of questions about the decision, the role of HSBC’s board and subsidiaries, communications or instructions since January 2021, and contacts with Chinese and Hong Kong authorities. The committees request records, communications, and other documentary evidence.

**Who it affects** The inquiry concerns HSBC, its Hong Kong subsidiaries and personnel, MPF trustee operations, and Hong Kong residents seeking to emigrate using BN(O) passports. It also examines possible interactions with the PRC Ministry of Foreign Affairs, the Hong Kong SAR Government, the Hong Kong Monetary Authority, and the Mandatory Provident Fund Authority.

**Why it matters** The committees frame pension access as a property-rights and rule-of-law issue linked to enforcement of Hong Kong’s National Security Law. The letter states that as much as $2.7 billion in pension assets may be affected and seeks to assess HSBC’s involvement and decision-making.

**Key dates and numbers**
- June 2020: The PRC enacted Hong Kong’s National Security Law.
- January 2021: The United Kingdom launched a BN(O) visa route.
- July 21, 2023: Deadline requested for HSBC’s response.
- Five numbered requests for information and supporting records.
- Up to $2.7 billion in pension assets identified as potentially affected.
