Telxius Cable USA’s Letter of Assurances commits the company to mitigation measures addressing U.S. national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety concerns associated with the BRUSA private submarine cable system and supports FCC approval of its landing and operation application.

**What it does** The letter defines the BRUSA Cable System, domestic communications, domestic communications infrastructure, network operations centers, and principal and wet infrastructure. It requires Telxius to provide network, equipment, vendor, maintenance, and restoration information; maintain the ability to interrupt U.S. traffic; give advance notice of material equipment, vendor, service, and operational changes; report unauthorized access, disruptions, disclosures, and material breaches; notify the U.S. Government Parties of qualifying foreign ownership or control changes; and submit annual compliance reports. It also permits government consultation and reasonable site visits. A breach may support an FCC request to modify, condition, revoke, cancel, terminate, or nullify an authorization.

**Who it affects** The commitments bind Telxius Cable USA and its successors, assigns, subsidiaries, and affiliates. They concern the BRUSA applicants—Telefónica International Wholesale Services USA, TI Wholesale Services Puerto Rico, and Telefónica International Wholesale Services América—and the DHS and DoD parties overseeing mitigation, with the FCC as the licensing authority.

**Why it matters** The LOA makes continued operation of a U.S.-Brazil submarine cable contingent on transparency, controllability, incident reporting, vendor oversight, and change-in-control monitoring. It also links compliance to the FCC authorization, giving the mitigation commitments a potential licensing consequence.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Dated July 30, 2017; associated with FCC docket SCL-LIC-20160330-00011.
- Initial system and equipment information is due within 60 business days of execution; requested information is due within 15 business days.
- Advance notice periods are generally 10 business days for material equipment modifications and 30 business days for vendor or significant operational changes.
- Certain incidents and foreign ownership or control changes must be reported within 10 business days; foreign ownership above 10 percent is a specified trigger.
