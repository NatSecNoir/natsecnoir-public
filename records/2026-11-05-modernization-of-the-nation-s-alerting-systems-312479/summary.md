The FCC adopts targeted cybersecurity requirements for Emergency Alert System (EAS) Participants, declines to impose comparable new requirements on Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA) at this time, and seeks comment on further alerting-system modernization.

**What it does** The Report and Order requires EAS Participants to change default passwords before operation, use strong and unique credentials or comparably secure authentication, promptly test and install security patches and security-related firmware and software updates, and use a firewall or comparable network-segmentation practice to restrict remote management access. It does not adopt the broader cybersecurity risk-management plans, incident-reporting obligations, or fake-base-station safeguards proposed in the earlier proceeding, and terminates PS Docket No. 22-329. The accompanying Further Notice seeks comment on digitally authenticating CAP alerts, improving WEA reliability and geotargeting, using software for EAS functions, and retiring 90-character WEA messages.

**Who it affects** The mandatory rules apply to EAS Participants, including broadcasters, cable systems, satellite providers, and wireline video providers, and cover EAS, studio-transmitter-link, and relevant remotely managed equipment. WEA providers receive no new targeted cybersecurity mandates in this order.

**Why it matters** The FCC treats basic security hygiene as necessary to reduce false alerts and interruptions to legitimate alerts, while preserving the legacy EAS architecture and avoiding the cost of a broader framework. The order emphasizes particular challenges facing small and medium-sized participants.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Adopted June 25, 2026; released June 29, 2026.
- Compliance is required 60 days after Federal Register publication.
- The FCC estimates annual implementation costs of approximately $26 million across about 25,800 entities.
- The Further Notice sets comments at 30 days and replies at 60 days after Federal Register publication.
