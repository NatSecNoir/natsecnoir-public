The Letter of Agreement requires Crestview and WideOpen West Finance, LLC (WOW) to undertake national-security and law-enforcement safeguards in connection with Crestview’s proposed acquisition of control of WOW’s telecommunications subsidiaries and Section 214 authorizations.

**What it does** The agreement requires compliance with CALEA; limits disclosure of U.S. records and communications in response to non-U.S. government requests absent U.S. legal authorization and DOJ consent; designates a U.S. law-enforcement point of contact; and requires timely production of records to lawful U.S. process. It gives DOJ inspection and interview access, requires advance notice and non-objection for certain foreign outsourcing, requires reporting of specified ownership or control changes, and establishes company contacts and annual compliance reports. DOJ may terminate the agreement by written agreement with the applicants, and breach may support a request that the FCC modify, revoke, or otherwise affect relevant authorizations.

**Who it affects** The commitments bind Crestview, WOW, its subsidiaries—including Knology and Sigecom—and applicable owners, personnel, contractors, and outsourced facilities connected to WOW’s FCC-licensed activity. DOJ’s National Security Division receives notices, reviews, and reports, while the FCC is to be notified that DOJ has no objection to a conditional grant.

**Why it matters** The LOA shows Team Telecom mitigation attached to a proposed transfer of control, preserving U.S. government access to records and facilities and constraining foreign access, outsourcing, and ownership changes without imposing a general prohibition on the transaction.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Executed and dated March 8, 2017.
- The application concerns FCC file numbers ITC-T/C-20160111-00005 and ITC-T/C-20160111-00006, WC Docket No. 16-12, and TT Case 16-09/10.
- Non-U.S. government requests for U.S. records must be referred to DOJ no later than five business days after receipt or knowledge.
- DOJ generally receives 14 days’ advance notice for access; foreign outsourcing requires at least 30 days’ notice, and ownership changes involving 10% or more require notice within 30 days.
- A termination-assessment request may be made six years after the agreement’s execution, with DOJ to decide within 180 days.
