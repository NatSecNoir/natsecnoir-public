The House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party asks the Department of Homeland Security and the Forced Labor Enforcement Task Force to add CATL and Gotion to the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act Entity List based on alleged supply-chain links to Xinjiang entities and forced labor programs.

**What it does** The document presents separate evidence summaries and letters concerning Contemporary Amperex Technology (CATL) and Gotion High Tech. It identifies supplier, ownership, investment, and joint-venture relationships involving the Xinjiang Production and Construction Corps (XPCC), Xinjiang Nonferrous, Xinjiang Joinworld, BTR New Material Group, TBEA, and other battery-material companies. It requests that shipments from both battery manufacturers be prohibited from entry into the United States and offers to provide additional sourcing information.

**Who it affects** The requested action would directly affect CATL and Gotion, their suppliers, and U.S. importers or businesses using products connected to their supply chains. DHS and the FLETF are the officials asked to evaluate the evidence and make the listing decision.

**Why it matters** The letters connect battery-material sourcing to alleged Uyghur forced labor, XPCC affiliations, and activity in the Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region. They also illustrate congressional scrutiny of supply-chain risks involving Chinese battery manufacturers seeking access to the U.S. market.

**Key dates and numbers**
- June 5, 2024: letters to Under Secretary Robert Silvers.
- June 6, 2024: investigation summary date.
- 23.6%: CATL’s previously reported stake in Xinjiang Zhicun before divestment in February 2023.
- RMB 118,500 and 126 individuals: figures cited for a Xinjiang Joinworld labor-transfer and training program in 2020.
