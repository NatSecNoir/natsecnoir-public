The FCC institutes a proceeding to withdraw Shenzhen CTB Testing Technology Co. Ltd.’s recognition as an accredited test laboratory, based on a tentative finding that it willfully submitted incorrect or false equipment test results.

**What it does** OET concludes that CTB submitted at least 20 test reports in which testing was completed in implausibly short periods—ranging from 4 to 60 seconds—and that reports for some FCC IDs relied on results for wholly different products. The Order tentatively finds violations of 47 CFR §§ 2.911(e) and 1.17 and determines that CTB’s conduct failed the competence, integrity, and trustworthiness standards applicable to recognized laboratories. It directs CTB to explain why its FCC recognition should not be withdrawn.

**Who it affects** The proceeding directly affects CTB, a Guangdong, China laboratory recognized by the FCC in 2020, and its role in supporting equipment authorization applications. CTB has submitted reports for more than 8,800 applications. The action also concerns applicants and authorizations relying on CTB’s reports, as well as the FCC’s third-party testing and equipment authorization program.

**Why it matters** The Order treats accurate, independently generated laboratory data as a safeguard against harmful radiofrequency interference and risks to communications and national security. It signals that false or unreliable certifications can lead to loss of FCC laboratory recognition and scrutiny of affected equipment authorizations.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Adopted and released: October 2, 2026.
- CTB’s A2LA accreditation was suspended on August 24, 2026; OET requested an explanation on August 5, 2026, and CTB responded on August 11, 2026.
- At least 20 allegedly incorrect or false reports were identified, involving testing durations from 4 to 60 seconds.
- CTB must file an English-language response within 35 calendar days after release; failure to respond may result in withdrawal of recognition.
- FCC number: DA 26-1072; ET Docket No. 26-282.
