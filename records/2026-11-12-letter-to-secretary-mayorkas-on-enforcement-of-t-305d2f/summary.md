The House Select Committee on the Strategic Competition Between the United States and the Chinese Communist Party urged Homeland Security Secretary Alejandro Mayorkas to strengthen enforcement of the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act (UFLPA).

**What it does** The January 22, 2024 letter from Chairman Mike Gallagher and Ranking Member Raja Krishnamoorthi identifies concerns that companies profiting from Uyghur forced labor have not been added to the UFLPA Entity List, that forced-labor goods are being transshipped through third countries, and that the de minimis provision is being used to send such goods into the United States. It asks DHS to intensify enforcement, expand testing at ports of entry, publicize Customs and Border Protection enforcement activity, improve coordination with the Department of Justice, appoint a senior DHS official to lead UFLPA enforcement, and assess restrictions or exceptions for high-risk goods under de minimis treatment.

**Who it affects** The requested actions would affect DHS and CBP enforcement, federal prosecution and penalty practices, importers and shippers of goods from the People’s Republic of China and third countries, and companies associated with forced labor in the Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region, including in the seafood, gold, and critical-minerals industries.

**Why it matters** The letter frames geographic transfers within China, third-country transshipment, and low-value shipments as obstacles to the UFLPA’s presumptive ban on goods tied to the Xinjiang region. It seeks broader entity-list coverage and stronger deterrence, but does not itself add any company to a list or impose an enforcement measure.

**Key dates and numbers**
- January 22, 2024: Letter date.
- March 1, 2024: Requested deadline for DHS’s written response.
- The letter identifies three principal enforcement concerns: incomplete Entity List coverage, third-country transshipment, and use of de minimis treatment.
