The House Select Committee on the CCP recommends that the Department of Commerce strengthen its proposed export controls to require a license for U.S. biopharmaceutical entities conducting clinical trials with medical institutions operated by the People’s Liberation Army (PLA).

**What it does** The January 9, 2025 letter to Secretary Gina Raimondo argues that clinical trials with PLA medical institutions can transfer sensitive clinical-trial data, drug-development information, and related intellectual property to the PLA and the People’s Republic of China. It asks the Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) to revise the definition of “Military End User” so that medical infrastructure owned or operated by a foreign national armed service is treated as a military end-use when a U.S. person seeks to conduct a clinical trial there. The letter also offers to share committee-collected data.

**Who it affects** The requested control would apply to U.S. biopharmaceutical companies and U.S. persons working with PLA medical institutions, while encouraging companies to use non-PLA medical infrastructure in China. The letter identifies the PLA’s Academy of Military Medical Sciences as an entity on the Commerce Department’s Entity List and references the FDA’s review of possible U.S.-PLA clinical-trial activity.

**Why it matters** The recommendation seeks to extend export-control due diligence beyond conventional goods and technology to clinical-trial cooperation and associated data. The committee estimates that the measure would generate only several dozen BIS license applications annually because relatively few hospitals are directly operated by the PLA.

**Key dates and numbers**
- January 9, 2025: letter date.
- July 29, 2024: BIS proposed rule on military and intelligence end uses and end users.
- Several dozen: estimated annual license applications under the proposed approach.
- The letter cites possible PLA involvement in hundreds of clinical trials over the preceding decade.
