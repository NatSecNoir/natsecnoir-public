The House Select Committee on the CCP reports that China became the principal buyer and beneficiary of sanctioned Russian, Iranian, and Venezuelan oil by combining discounted purchases with maritime, commercial, and financial evasion systems, while using the resulting supply to expand its strategic petroleum reserves and support broader gray-zone capabilities.

**What it does** The report presents eight findings on China’s role in the sanctioned-oil trade. It examines a shadow fleet of aging tankers using opaque ownership, false flags, AIS manipulation, ship-to-ship transfers, and cargo relabeling; payment arrangements outside conventional dollar and SWIFT-linked channels; and municipal Chinese guidance supporting alternative settlement methods. It also connects the maritime network to BeiDou-GLONASS navigation cooperation, cyber risks, and the movement of military, intelligence, and private-security personnel. The report recommends sanctions against ports and terminal operators, whistleblower rewards, CFTC and FinCEN investigations, coordinated supply expansion, and expanded Coast Guard cyber capacity.

**Who it affects** The analysis focuses on China and Chinese refiners, Russian, Iranian, and Venezuelan oil exporters, shadow-fleet vessel owners and managers, intermediary firms in Hong Kong, Malaysia, and the UAE, and U.S. agencies responsible for sanctions, financial oversight, commodity markets, and maritime security. It identifies Shandong as the main receiving region and names multiple vessels, buyers, security companies, and Chinese settlement institutions.

**Why it matters** The report argues that sanctions concentrated sanctioned crude in China rather than removing it from global trade, giving Beijing lower-cost oil, greater leverage over isolated exporters, and additional strategic petroleum depth. It warns that opaque shipping and settlement infrastructure can also facilitate military logistics, gray-zone activity, and cyber exposure.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Between 2013 and 2025, nine seized tankers delivered approximately 69.3 million barrels of Venezuelan, Iranian, and Russian crude to China, valued at nearly $4 billion.
- As of March 2026, Kpler tracked 2,291 shadow-fleet tankers; roughly 45% were formally sanctioned.
- About 42.7 million barrels, or 62% of the nine vessels’ deliveries, discharged at Shandong terminals.
- China’s observable crude inventories reached roughly 1.2 billion barrels by early 2026, equivalent to approximately 109 days of seaborne import cover.
- The report’s recommendations include the SHADOW Fleet Sanctions Act of 2026, S. 2904, and additional Coast Guard Cyber Protection Teams.
