The hearing examined the Chinese Communist Party’s cyber threat to U.S. national security, especially its theft of data and intellectual property and its pre-positioning in civilian critical infrastructure for possible disruption or destruction.

**What it does** The transcript records testimony from FBI Director Christopher Wray, CISA Director Jen Easterly, National Cyber Director Harry Coker, and General Paul Nakasone of NSA and U.S. Cyber Command. Witnesses described the Volt Typhoon campaign, including its compromise of hundreds of small office/home office routers to conceal access to communications, energy, transportation, and water systems. They discussed court-authorized efforts to disable that access, public-private cyber defense, incident reporting, resilience, secure-by-design technology, software liability, election security, Section 702, and the risks associated with TikTok’s data collection, recommendation algorithm, and software control. The hearing also referenced the FCC Covered List, naming Dahua, Huawei, ZTE, and Hytera as examples of Chinese equipment.

**Who it affects** The testimony concerns federal, state, local, tribal, and territorial governments; critical-infrastructure owners and operators; technology manufacturers; telecommunications and maritime sectors; election officials; private companies; and users of TikTok. It also addresses Chinese Americans and dissidents as potential victims of transnational repression, distinguishing them from the Chinese government and CCP.

**Why it matters** The witnesses characterized the threat as persistent and capable of producing real-world consequences, including blackouts, disruption of water and fuel, impaired communications, and societal panic during a conflict. They emphasized that defensive measures depend on sustained interagency and industry cooperation, resilient infrastructure, cyber hygiene, and reducing dependence on vulnerable or Chinese-controlled technology.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Hearing held January 31, 2024; questions for the record were due one week later.
- Volt Typhoon activity was described as active since 2021; a related advisory was issued in May 2023.
- The FBI estimated Chinese hackers could outnumber FBI cyber personnel by at least 50 to 1.
- CISA said the Joint Cyber Defense Collaborative grew from 10 participating companies to more than 200.
- Witnesses cited approximately 150 million U.S. TikTok users, 8,800 election jurisdictions, and 80 percent of port cranes as ZPMC.
