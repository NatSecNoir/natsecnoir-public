The agreement sets national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety commitments that Telecom Services Network LLC (TSN) must satisfy for the FCC to grant its international telecommunications authorization.

**What it does** TSN agrees to designate a U.S. law-enforcement point of contact, preserve access to U.S. customer and communications records, respond to lawful U.S. process, and refer non-U.S. government requests involving those records to DOJ. It requires advance notice of records-storage locations and material corporate changes, annual submission of certified tax records and financial statements, and potentially a DOJ-requested neutral third-party audit. If TSN begins providing interconnected VoIP services or becomes facilities-based, it must provide advance notice, comply with CALEA and lawful surveillance requirements, undergo an FBI compliance test, and expand its annual reporting. Breach may support a DOJ request that the FCC modify, condition, revoke, cancel, or nullify TSN authorizations.

**Who it affects** The commitments bind TSN, identified as 50% owned by Syam Sankar and 50% by Shruti Vashishth. They govern TSN’s dealings with DOJ, including its National Security Division, and the FBI, while conditioning FCC authorization.

**Why it matters** The letter illustrates Team Telecom mitigation through enforceable controls over records, foreign-government access, corporate transparency, financial oversight, lawful interception, cybersecurity incidents, foreign telecommunications relationships, and equipment. It also links compliance failures to possible FCC licensing consequences.

**Key dates and numbers**
- October 26, 2016: letter date.
- FCC docket: ITC-214-20131017-00275.
- TSN must identify a law-enforcement point of contact within 14 days after receiving FCC certification.
- Records requests and certain foreign-government requests generally require action or referral within five business days.
- Annual reports, once required, are due January 31; third-party audit reports are generally due within six months of DOJ’s auditor acceptance.
