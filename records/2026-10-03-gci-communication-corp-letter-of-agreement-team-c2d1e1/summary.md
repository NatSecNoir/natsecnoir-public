GCI Communication Corp. commits to national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety safeguards for the AU-Aleutian submarine cable system so DHS can notify the FCC that it has no objection to the pending landing-and-operation application.

**What it does** This Letter of Assurances establishes compliance obligations covering a U.S.-based security point of contact; disclosure of network, operational, vendor, and equipment information; DHS review of principal equipment and changes to equipment, vendors, testing, services, and operations; cybersecurity and physical-security controls; personnel screening; incident and breach reporting; employee instruction; annual compliance reports; and DHS consultation, visits, and on-site reviews. GCI must provide advance notice for many proposed changes and negotiate in good faith over DHS objections or concerns. A breach may lead DHS to request that the FCC modify, condition, revoke, cancel, terminate, or invalidate a relevant authorization.

**Who it affects** The commitments bind GCI Communication Corp. and its successors, assigns, subsidiaries, and affiliates, and reach personnel, contractors, vendors, and subcontractors involved with the AU-Aleutian Cable System. DHS receives review, information, reporting, and visitation rights; the FCC is the licensing authority whose authorization may be conditioned on compliance.

**Why it matters** The letter illustrates Team Telecom mitigation for critical telecommunications infrastructure: it gives DHS continuing visibility into cable architecture, equipment, suppliers, personnel, security practices, and incidents while preserving a path for FCC authorization. It also makes compliance enforceable through potential licensing consequences.
