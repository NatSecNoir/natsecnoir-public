The House Select Committee on the CCP and Congressional-Executive Commission on China request a briefing from DHS on enforcement of the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act (UFLPA) and related forced-labor import laws.

**What it does** The September 3, 2026 letter asks Secretary Markwayne Mullin to provide the briefing within 30 days, together with written materials. It seeks information on DHS, U.S. Customs and Border Protection, and Forced Labor Enforcement Task Force priorities; seafood, textile, metals, polysilicon, critical-mineral, battery, and other supply chains; evidentiary standards for rebutting the UFLPA presumption; staffing and resources; investigations; international coordination; and the use of artificial intelligence to validate shipment and origin documentation. The members also ask about expansion of the UFLPA Entity List and enforcement data on detained, released, excluded, or seized shipments.

**Who it affects** The request concerns DHS and its enforcement partners, importers, supply chains connected to the People’s Republic of China and Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region, and U.S. industries and workers exposed to unfair competition or forced-labor inputs. It is an oversight request, not a new designation, prohibition, or enforcement order.

**Why it matters** The letter argues that forced-labor risks extend beyond a narrow group of products and can be concealed through third-country processing, transshipment, and opaque sourcing. It highlights the need for stronger Entity List targeting, transparent enforcement metrics, and scrutiny of emerging industrial and advanced-technology materials.

**Key dates and numbers**
- September 3, 2026: letter date.
- July 31, 2026: DHS announcement adding 43 companies to the UFLPA Entity List, as cited in the letter.
- 30 days: requested deadline for the DHS briefing.
- 10: numbered areas of requested information.
