The FCC adopts new equipment-authorization and marketing restrictions aimed at preventing Covered List-related national-security risks from entering the communications supply chain, while seeking comment on additional measures.

**What it does** The Third Report and Order prohibits authorization of devices containing logic-bearing hardware components produced by Covered List entities when the finished device would itself be prohibited if produced by that entity. It defines covered logic-bearing hardware using a 9,000-pulses-per-second threshold and digital or RF data-processing functions, but does not extend the rule to software, firmware, or components covered only by production-location-based entries unless the producer is otherwise identified on the Covered List. The order also requires full certification for modifications or permissive changes made by Covered List entities, clarifies the scope of “marketing” for online marketplaces, requires prominent FCC ID display at the online point of sale for covered listings subject to the rule, and revises the definition of “critical infrastructure.”

**Who it affects** Device manufacturers, certification applicants, Covered List entities, Telecommunication Certification Bodies, online marketplaces, retailers, distributors, and third-party sellers are most directly affected. Previously authorized equipment and qualifying pending applications receive specified transitional treatment; small sellers are exempt from the new FCC ID display requirement.

**Why it matters** The order closes a component-level pathway that the FCC says could permit compromised chips or other logic-bearing hardware to create surveillance, disruption, or unauthorized-access risks even when the final device has a different producer or brand. It also places compliance responsibilities on online marketplaces and improves consumers’ ability to verify authorization before purchase.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Adopted July 22, 2026; released July 23, 2026.
- New logic-bearing-component prohibition takes effect 30 days after Federal Register publication.
- Logic-bearing hardware definition uses a threshold above 9,000 pulses (cycles) per second.
- Comments on the accompanying Third Further NPRM are due 30 days after Federal Register publication; replies are due 45 days after publication.
- Proceeding: ET Docket No. 21-232; document FCC-26-50.
