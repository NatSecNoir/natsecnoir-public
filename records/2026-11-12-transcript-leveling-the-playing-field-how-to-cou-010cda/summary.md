The hearing examines how the United States should respond to the Chinese Communist Party’s economic and technological competition, with witnesses advocating stronger trade, investment, capital-market, supply-chain, and innovation policies while differing over the breadth of decoupling.

**What it does** The transcript records opening statements, testimony, and member questioning on strategic economic competition with China. Robert Lighthizer recommends phased strategic decoupling, broader tariffs, restrictions on Chinese and outbound investment, stronger scrutiny of Chinese land and corporate activity, and repeal of China’s permanent normal trade relations. Roger Robinson focuses on U.S. investor exposure to Chinese securities, proposed delisting and sanctions for malign companies, and coordination among Treasury, Commerce, and DoD lists. Eric Schmidt emphasizes investment in AI, quantum, biology, semiconductors, talent, research, and advanced manufacturing, while favoring targeted controls rather than economy-wide separation.

**Who it affects** The discussion concerns Congress and executive agencies, U.S. manufacturers, technology firms, asset managers, pension and retirement investors, small businesses, universities, workers, and allies. It also addresses Chinese companies and entities connected to military, surveillance, human-rights, or technology-transfer concerns, including Huawei.

**Why it matters** The hearing frames economic policy as a national-security issue involving critical technologies, supply-chain resilience, capital flows, data, and innovation capacity. It also records significant disagreement over tariffs, industrial policy, mitigation, trade agreements, and whether restrictions should be broad or narrowly targeted.
