The House Select Committee on the Strategic Competition Between the United States and the Chinese Communist Party urged Secretary of State Antony Blinken not to renew the U.S.–China Agreement on Cooperation in Science and Technology (STA) when it expires.

**What it does**  The letter argues that research partnerships organized under the STA can support the People’s Republic of China’s military objectives through military-civil fusion. It points to a 2018 National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration project with China’s Meteorological Administration involving instrumented balloons, and to U.S. Department of Agriculture research projects with PRC entities involving agricultural technologies such as satellite and drone-image analysis for irrigation management. The signatories recommend allowing the agreement to expire rather than renewing it.

**Who it affects**  The recommendation is directed to the Department of State and would affect the bilateral scientific-research framework, U.S. agencies conducting projects under it, and participating PRC entities. The letter also copies the Secretaries of Defense, Energy, and Agriculture and relevant State Department officials.

**Why it matters**  The committee presents the STA as a potential channel for transferring or developing dual-use capabilities that could contribute to People’s Liberation Army modernization or threaten U.S. sovereignty. It frames nonrenewal as an initial step toward limiting perceived exploitation of civilian research partnerships.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated June 27, 2023.
- STA expiration date: August 27, 2023.
- NOAA balloon research cited from 2018; USDA is described as having more than a dozen active PRC-related projects.
- The committee cites authority under H. Res. 11.
