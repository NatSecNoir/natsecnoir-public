The House Select Committee on the CCP urges the Departments of Defense and Commerce and the FCC to investigate Unitree Robotics and consider designating it under Section 1260H, adding it to the Entity List, and placing it on the FCC Covered List.

**What it does** The May 6, 2025 letter presents Unitree as a PRC robotics manufacturer with alleged ties to PLA-affiliated institutions, CCP entities, military-civil fusion programs, and PRC state funding. It cites the company’s sales to Chinese defense-oriented universities and police, a military exercise involving an armed Unitree robot, the B2 platform’s military-relevant capabilities, and a 2025 partnership with China South Industries Group affiliate Chongqing Jianshe Industry. The letter also highlights reported CloudSail remote access, PRC data storage, use of iFlytek for voice processing, and research concerning robot hacking and surveillance.

**Who it affects** The requested review concerns Unitree and its U.S. users, including law-enforcement agencies, correctional facilities, defense installations, and other institutions whose networks could connect to Unitree equipment. It is addressed to Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, and FCC Chairman Brendan Carr.

**Why it matters** The letter links foreign-manufactured connected robots to national-security, cybersecurity, privacy, and critical-infrastructure risks. It also points to existing FCC equipment authorizations for Unitree’s quadruped robot and remote control, illustrating why the committee seeks coordinated action across the three agencies. The requested measures are recommendations, not designations made by this document.

**Key dates and numbers**
- May 6, 2025: letter date.
- 2A5PE-YUSHU006: quadruped robot FCC identifier, granted October 16, 2023.
- 2A5PE-YUSHU007: remote-control FCC identifier, granted November 18, 2023.
- Researchers reportedly identified 1,900 connected robots using the undocumented remote-access tunnel.
- The letter cites a vendor plan to deploy 100 units in a state prison system, with a longer-term target of 1,000 installations.
