The House Committees on Small Business, Science, Space, and Technology, and the Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party ask NASA to provide information and procedures addressing alleged Chinese exploitation of the SBIR and STTR programs and related national-security risks.

**What it does** The February 26, 2025 letter requests annual application, flagging, and outcome data for Phases I, II, and III; breakdowns of flags involving foreign-adversary ties, export-control or dual-use concerns, inadequate due diligence, and other security red flags; and information about NASA’s disclosure requirements and verification practices. It also seeks copies of criteria and procedures for flagging applicants, handling suspicious applications, coordinating with other agencies, and monitoring each phase.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to Acting NASA Administrator Janet E. Petro and concerns NASA’s administration of SBIR and STTR programs, applicants and participating small businesses, and agencies involved in reviewing national-security risks. The Committees ask for records dating from January 20, 2021, and request a response by March 12, 2025.

**Why it matters** The letter alleges that Chinese talent programs, PRC-based subsidiaries, state-controlled venture capital, and research partnerships can provide access to taxpayer-funded technology and intellectual property. It cites a 2021 DoD-commissioned report stating that DoD SBIR programs lacked adequate due-diligence capabilities and argues that stronger safeguards are needed before the programs’ authorization expires.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter date: February 26, 2025.
- Requested response deadline: March 12, 2025.
- Requested data period begins: January 20, 2021.
- SBIR/STTR authorization expiration identified: September 30, 2025.
- The letter states that small businesses comprise 99.9 percent of U.S. businesses.
