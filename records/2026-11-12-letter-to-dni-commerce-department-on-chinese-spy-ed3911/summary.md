The House Select Committee on the CCP asks the Director of National Intelligence and the Secretary of Commerce to assess whether Chinese telecommunications companies supporting signals intelligence in Cuba have affected U.S. export-licensing decisions.

**What it does** The June 20, 2023 letter raises concerns about the reported expansion of the People’s Republic of China’s signals-intelligence presence in Cuba and its potential access to U.S. military activities and sensitive facilities, including AUTEC. It asks five questions concerning PRC information and communications technology companies that provide technical support or operational cover, whether their identities and activities were shared with Commerce or another End-User Review Committee member, and how export-license applications have been handled. It also asks whether intelligence concerning those companies influenced past or pending licensing decisions.

**Who it affects** The requested review concerns Huawei, ZTE, Great Dragon Information Technology, and other PRC technology companies with operations in Cuba or alleged connections to the PLA’s Strategic Support Force and the Ministry of State Security. The questions are directed to ODNI and Commerce, including Commerce’s role in export licensing and the End-User Review Committee. Committee Chairman Mike Gallagher signed the letter.

**Why it matters** The letter connects PRC commercial telecommunications activity with foreign intelligence collection, military-civil fusion, and possible access to U.S. intellectual property and dual-use technology. Its requests seek information that could inform export-control decisions involving companies that may support PRC SIGINT operations outside China.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter date: June 20, 2023; catalogue date: June 21, 2023.
- The letter identifies 2019 as the year the Intelligence Community reportedly became aware of the SIGINT facilities in Cuba.
- It asks five numbered questions, including counts of approved, rejected, and pending export-license applications.
- H. Res. 11 is cited as the Committee’s authority.
