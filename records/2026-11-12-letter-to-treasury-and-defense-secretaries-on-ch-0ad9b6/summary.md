The House Select Committee on the CCP asks the Secretaries of Defense and the Treasury to brief committee staff on whether Quectel Wireless Solutions Co. Ltd. should be added to the Department of Defense’s Section 1260H list and the Treasury Department’s NS-CMIC List.

**What it does** The January 3, 2024 letter presents information that the authors say may support listing Quectel as a Chinese military company. It describes Quectel’s reported affiliations with China’s Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, including awards, conferences, standards work, and designation of its cellular IoT modules as “Manufacturing Single Champion Products.” It also cites Quectel’s work involving Beidou, China’s major telecommunications operators, Huawei, DJI drones, and ZTE. For the Treasury list, the letter invokes Executive Order 13959 and argues that Quectel may operate in the PRC defense and related materiel or surveillance technology sectors.

**Who it affects** The request concerns Quectel, the Defense and Treasury Departments, and U.S. users and companies connected to Quectel cellular IoT modules. It also names Chinese telecommunications, technology, and defense-related entities with which Quectel is described as having relationships.

**Why it matters** The letter illustrates congressional scrutiny of a widely used Chinese IoT-module supplier under two distinct restrictions: Section 1260H’s Chinese military company framework and Treasury’s capital-markets-focused NS-CMIC regime. It requests a briefing rather than itself adding Quectel to either list.

**Key dates and numbers**
- January 3, 2024: letter date.
- Section 1260H of the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2021: statutory basis discussed for the DoD list.
- Executive Order 13959, section 1(a): authority discussed for the NS-CMIC List.
- 2017–2020: examples cited of Quectel’s MIIT-related activities.
