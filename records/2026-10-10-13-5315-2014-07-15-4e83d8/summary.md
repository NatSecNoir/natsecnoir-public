The D.C. Circuit reversed dismissal of Ralls Corporation’s challenge to the federal blocking of its acquisition of four Oregon windfarm companies, holding that the Presidential Order deprived Ralls of protected property interests without constitutionally adequate due process.

**What it does** The court held that Ralls’s state-law interests in the Project Companies, easements, agreements, permits, and related assets were vested property interests. Before a CFIUS or presidential prohibition could become effective, due process required notice of the action, access to the unclassified evidence relied upon, and an opportunity to rebut that evidence; classified information remained protected. The court rejected arguments that the statutory bar on judicial review or the political-question doctrine foreclosed review of this procedural claim. It also addressed the CFIUS Order claims after the Presidential Order revoked the interim order, applying the “capable of repetition yet evading review” framework.

**Who it affects** The decision directly affects Ralls, its Chinese-national owners Dawei Duan and Jialiang Wu, CFIUS, and future parties to transactions reviewed under section 721 of the Defense Production Act. It concerns executive review of foreign-controlled acquisitions affecting national security, including transactions involving critical infrastructure or military operating areas.

**Why it matters** The ruling distinguishes unreviewable presidential national-security judgments from reviewable constitutional procedures. It establishes that national-security concerns do not eliminate the need to disclose usable unclassified evidence and provide a meaningful opportunity to respond, while allowing classified information to remain undisclosed. The court stated that adequate process at the CFIUS stage could satisfy the President’s obligation.

**Key dates and numbers**
- March 2012: Ralls acquired the four Project Companies.
- June 28, 2012: Ralls submitted a 25-page CFIUS notice.
- July 25 and August 2, 2012: CFIUS issued interim mitigation orders.
- September 28, 2012: The President ordered prohibition and divestiture within 90 days.
- July 15, 2014: The D.C. Circuit decided the appeal.
- Section 721 review includes a 30-day review period and investigation of no more than 45 days.
