The House Select Committee on the CCP asks Bristol Myers Squibb (BMS) to explain its due diligence, data-protection, and clinical-trial practices in China, particularly at sites in Xinjiang and hospitals affiliated with the People’s Liberation Army (PLA).

**What it does** The June 30, 2026 letter from Chairman John Moolenaar identifies publicly reported BMS participation in about 180 clinical studies in China since 2004 and highlights at least eight trials involving Xinjiang hospitals and at least 17 involving PRC military medical centers or hospitals between 2015 and 2024. It requests a detailed response, briefing, and supporting records covering Good Clinical Practice, site inspections, risk assessments, trial locations, CROs and CDMOs, intellectual-property and data safeguards, and agreements with Chinese companies since January 1, 2020.

**Who it affects** The direct recipient is BMS CEO and Chairman Chris Boerner. The inquiry concerns BMS-sponsored or collaborative trials, including trials at Xinjiang medical facilities and PLA-affiliated institutions, as well as licensing, equity, and joint-venture relationships with Chinese companies such as Hengrui Pharmaceuticals.

**Why it matters** The Committee frames clinical-trial data as a sensitive “intellectual supply chain” and raises ethical concerns about informed consent in China, human-rights risks in Xinjiang, and the possibility that research conducted at military hospitals could expose proprietary biotechnology data to the Chinese military. The letter states that it does not allege BMS engaged in illegal activity or wrongdoing.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Response requested by July 17, 2026.
- At least 8 Xinjiang-related and 17 PRC military-site trials identified for 2015–2024.
- The letter requests 9 categories of information.
- BMS’s cited Hengrui partnership could be worth up to $15.2 billion.
