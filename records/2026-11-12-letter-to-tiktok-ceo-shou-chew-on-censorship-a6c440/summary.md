The House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party requests that TikTok explain alleged censorship and user tracking practices and produce related records by May 24, 2023.

**What it does** The May 10, 2023 letter to TikTok CEO Shou Chew describes the suspension and later restoration of the Acton Institute’s account and the censorship of videos concerning Hong Kong media owner Jimmy Lai. It also raises reporting that TikTok tracked users who interacted with gay content and that ByteDance used or maintained systems involving “sensitive words” and content “heating.” The committee requests six categories of information, including records concerning Acton’s moderation decisions, community-guideline enforcement, communications between TikTok and ByteDance, alleged heating on behalf of Chinese entities, TikTok’s views on Lai’s detention, and the methodology for listing or tracking users.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to TikTok and implicates its parent company, ByteDance, as well as users and organizations whose content or activity may be subject to TikTok’s moderation and tracking systems. The letter focuses particularly on content concerning Hong Kong, the Chinese Communist Party, Uyghurs, Tibet, Tiananmen Square, elections, and LGBTQ-related activity.

**Why it matters** The committee frames content moderation and tracking as potential channels through which the Chinese Communist Party or the PRC government could influence Americans’ speech and access to information. It invokes the committee’s authority under H. Res. 11 and seeks a detailed account of how moderation decisions are made, including decisions later reversed after public criticism.

**Key dates and numbers**
- May 2, 2023: Acton’s TikTok account was suspended without prior warning or explanation, according to the letter.
- May 10, 2023: Letter date.
- May 24, 2023: Deadline for TikTok’s response.
- 6: Categories of documents and information requested.
