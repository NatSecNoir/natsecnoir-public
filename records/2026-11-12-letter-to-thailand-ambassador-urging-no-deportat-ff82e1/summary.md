The House Select Committee on the CCP urges Thailand’s ambassador to prevent the deportation of 48 detained Uyghur men to China and facilitate their resettlement in willing third countries.

**What it does** The January 17, 2025 letter describes the men’s prolonged detention in Thailand and asks the Royal Thai Government to release them to third countries on humanitarian grounds and consistent with international human-rights norms, especially non-refoulement. It cites reported poor detention conditions, chronic illness, malnutrition, and fears of persecution, arbitrary detention, torture, or worse if the men are returned to the PRC. The letter also asks the ambassador to convey the concerns to Thailand’s highest officials.

**Who it affects** The request concerns 48 Uyghur men detained in Thailand: 43 at the Immigration Detention Center in Bangkok and five serving sentences at Bangkok Central Prison. It is directed to Ambassador Suriya Chindawongse and the Royal Thai Government, with China identified as the prospective destination of deportation. The letter also references incoming Secretary of State Marco Rubio’s stated commitment to address the matter diplomatically.

**Why it matters** The letter frames the detainees’ possible return as a grave humanitarian and protection concern, emphasizing alleged persecution of Uyghurs in China and the risk that deportation would violate non-refoulement principles. It records congressional pressure for a diplomatic resolution and third-country resettlement.

**Key dates and numbers**
- January 17, 2025: Letter date.
- 48: Uyghur men detained in Thailand.
- 43: Reportedly held at Bangkok’s Immigration Detention Center.
- 5: Serving sentences at Bangkok Central Prison; the letter says five detainees, including two children, have died from inadequate medical care.
- January 8–9, 2025: Reported consent requests for repatriation and detainees’ appeal for assistance.
