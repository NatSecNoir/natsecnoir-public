The House Select Committee on the CCP and the Congressional-Executive Commission on China urge Secretaries Antony Blinken and Janet Yellen to determine whether named Hong Kong officials qualify for sanctions by July 30, 2024, in response to alleged erosion of democracy, autonomy, and human rights.

**What it does** This July 17, 2024 letter asks the Departments of State and Treasury to assess the listed officials under the Hong Kong Human Rights and Democracy Act, the Hong Kong Autonomy Act, and Executive Order 13936. It identifies officials serving as Hong Kong’s secretary of justice, national-security and police officials, prosecutors, and judges or magistrates involved in national-security cases. The signers also request a staff-level briefing on U.S. actions to safeguard Hong Kong democracy and human rights since January 2021.

**Who it affects** The request concerns Hong Kong officials including Paul Lam, Dong Jingwei, Sonny Au, Raymond Siu, Margaret Chiu, Dick Wong, Bruce Hung, prosecutors, and national-security judges and magistrates. It is directed to the Departments of State and Treasury rather than imposing sanctions itself.

**Why it matters** The letter frames the March 2024 Safeguarding National Security Ordinance and the May 29 convictions in the “Hong Kong 47” case as evidence of intensified repression. It argues that visa restrictions announced by the State Department are insufficient and calls for use of statutory and executive sanctions authorities.

**Key dates and numbers**
- July 17, 2024: letter date.
- March 2024: Hong Kong passed the Safeguarding National Security Ordinance.
- May 29, 2024: 14 Hong Kong 47 defendants were convicted after refusing to plead guilty.
- July 30, 2024: requested deadline for the sanctions determination.
- The letter invokes Public Laws 116-76 and 116-149 and Executive Order 13936.
