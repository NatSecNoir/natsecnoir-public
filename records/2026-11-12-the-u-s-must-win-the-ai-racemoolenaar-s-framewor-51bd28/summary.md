The House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party urges Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to make U.S. technological leadership and national-security safeguards the foundation of AI and semiconductor arrangements with the United Arab Emirates and other partners.

**What it does** This letter supports the Trump Administration’s AI diplomacy while proposing an “America First AI Policy.” Its recommendations include using partnerships to strengthen trusted supply chains; requiring city- or state-level location verification for advanced chips; limiting the share of a U.S. hyperscaler’s advanced compute capacity located overseas to 49%; preventing any single non-treaty ally from hosting more than 10% of global frontier-model training compute; framing agreements around aggregate computing power rather than chip counts; barring PRC-origin semiconductors and equipment from certain advanced AI data centers; imposing robust cyber, physical, and personnel security standards; keeping frontier-model training and model weights under U.S. jurisdiction; and conditioning benefits on partners’ strategic alignment and comparable controls.

**Who it affects** The recommendations are directed to Commerce and concern U.S. agencies, American technology companies, hyperscalers, chip manufacturers, overseas data-center operators, partner governments—especially the UAE—and Chinese entities that might access U.S. AI infrastructure or investment.

**Why it matters** The committee frames advanced semiconductors and compute as the critical strategic resources of the AI era. It argues that Chinese access, diversion, or leakage of capable AI systems could increase proliferation and national-security risks, while secure partnerships could expand U.S. influence without creating independent centers of frontier-AI capability beyond U.S. control.

**Key dates and numbers**
- June 27, 2025: letter date.
- 49%: proposed maximum share of a U.S. hyperscaler’s advanced compute capacity located overseas.
- 10%: proposed maximum share of global frontier-AI training compute hosted by one non-treaty ally.
- 500,000: reported annual advanced-chip export cap to the UAE that the letter says should be replaced by a compute-based measure.
- H. Res. 5, section 4(a): authority cited by the committee.
