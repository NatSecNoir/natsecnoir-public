The House Select Committee on the CCP and 13 members of Congress urged Secretary of State Antony Blinken to suspend efforts to renew the U.S.-People’s Republic of China (PRC) Science and Technology Agreement (STA) before January 20, 2025.

**What it does** This December 12, 2024 letter objects to the Biden Administration’s anticipated five-year extension of the STA, which the signatories understood could be announced as early as December 13. It argues that Congress had repeatedly sought either suspension of the agreement or additional safeguards, including human-rights protections and restrictions on dual-use research. The letter asks Blinken to pause renewal efforts and leave the issue for the incoming administration, or negotiate stronger terms.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to Blinken and the State Department, and concerns the Biden Administration’s proposed renewal of the bilateral U.S.-PRC science and technology cooperation framework. It also invokes Representative Andy Barr’s proposed legislation requiring advance congressional notification and specified protections in any renewal.

**Why it matters** The letter reflects congressional opposition to renewing the STA without additional national-security, human-rights, and intellectual-property guardrails. It also argues that completing renewal during the administration’s final days could constrain the incoming administration’s ability to withdraw from or renegotiate the agreement.

**Key dates and numbers**
- December 12, 2024: Letter date.
- December 13, 2024: Earliest anticipated STA-extension announcement cited by the signatories.
- January 20, 2025: Date before which the signatories urged suspension of renewal efforts.
- Five years: Duration of the anticipated extension.
- September 10, 2024: Date of Representative Barr’s cited oversight legislation press release.
