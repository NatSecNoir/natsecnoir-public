The House Select Committee on the CCP urges Secretary of State Antony Blinken to intensify U.S. diplomacy against PRC state-sponsored forced labor and coordinate stronger international import prohibitions.

**What it does**
The April 16, 2024 letter asks the State Department to elevate engagement with U.S. allies and partners, particularly Germany, Italy, the European Union, and the United Kingdom. It requests efforts to prevent countries from becoming alternative markets for goods barred from the United States under the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act (UFLPA), address risks of transshipment, support designation of forced-labor sectors and locations, and improve overseas training on UFLPA scope, enforcement, and compliance. The committee also asks the Department to assess the feasibility of a joint U.S.-EU-UK Forced Labor Enforcement Task Force.

**Who it affects**
The request is directed to the State Department and concerns U.S. diplomatic engagement with governments, companies, and enforcement partners confronting products linked to PRC state-sponsored forced labor, especially in Xinjiang and sectors such as solar, electric vehicles, textiles, and critical minerals.

**Why it matters**
The letter argues that uneven foreign restrictions could produce separate supply chains and create avenues for forced-labor goods to reach European or U.S. markets. It frames coordinated import bans and enforcement as necessary to extend the UFLPA’s effect internationally.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated April 16, 2024; catalogue date: April 17, 2024.
- Requests a State Department staff briefing by April 30, 2024.
- Cites the March 5, 2024 provisional EU agreement on a forced-labor import ban.
- Signed by Mike Gallagher and Representative Raja Krishnamoorthi.
