The House Select Committee on the CCP recommends that Commerce and the Bureau of Industry and Security adopt a rolling technical threshold and aggregate-compute controls to preserve U.S. AI leadership while limiting China’s advanced capabilities.

**What it does** The August 25, 2025 letter to Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick proposes licensing only AI chips that provide a marginal improvement over China’s most advanced commercially produced chips, while capping China’s aggregate compute at 10% of the U.S. total. The proposed framework would measure total processing performance, performance density, interconnect speed, and memory bandwidth, and would apply comparable controls to remote access through U.S. cloud providers. The accompanying committee report presents findings and recommendations concerning DeepSeek’s data practices, alleged censorship, possible model distillation, and reported access to Nvidia chips.

**Who it affects** The recommendations target BIS, U.S. semiconductor and cloud companies, Chinese AI developers, and entities involved in chip manufacturing, sales, or diversion. The report specifically discusses DeepSeek, High-Flyer Quant, China Mobile, Huawei, Nvidia, OpenAI, ByteDance, Baidu, and Tencent.

**Why it matters** The proposal shifts attention from individual chip specifications to China’s combined computing capacity and seeks to close perceived gaps involving cloud access, chip smuggling, and sub-threshold accumulation. The report frames DeepSeek as a national-security, privacy, export-control, and information-manipulation concern, while presenting the framework as a way to sustain U.S. technology advantages and commercial demand.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated August 25, 2025; committee title identifies the proposal as a new framework.
- Proposed ceiling: China’s aggregate compute at no more than 10% of the U.S. aggregate.
- Commerce projection cited: Huawei could manufacture up to 200,000 Ascend AI chips in 2025, compared with approximately 14 million U.S. AI chips deployed that year.
- Reported DeepSeek inventory estimate: at least 60,000 Nvidia chips, including 10,000 A100s, 30,000 H20s, 10,000 H800s, and 10,000 H100s.
