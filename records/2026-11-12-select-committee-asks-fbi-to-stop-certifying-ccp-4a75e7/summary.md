The House Select Committee on the CCP urges FBI Director Kashyap Patel to end certification of biometric products from Chinese military-linked and surveillance companies and to reconcile the FBI’s Certified Products List with existing U.S. national-security restrictions.

**What it does** The July 15, 2025 letter requests that the FBI immediately review all PRC-based companies on its Certified Products List against U.S. sanctions, entity lists, and other security determinations. It asks the FBI to create a comprehensive policy addressing the risks of certifying products from foreign-adversary companies and to provide a written response within 30 days. The letter highlights Hikvision’s HK300 PIV single-finger capture device, certified by the FBI in 2025, as particularly concerning.

**Who it affects** The request concerns 32 PRC-based companies with products on the FBI list, including Hikvision and firms such as Hisign, Eyecool, Luxshare, Miaxis, Wellcom, and ZTEway. The committee identifies at least 14 companies with ties to Chinese intelligence agencies, PRC surveillance programs, or U.S.-sanctioned entities. It is directed to the FBI and affects potential government, law-enforcement, contractor, and other purchasers that may treat FBI certification as a trust or security signal.

**Why it matters** The committee argues that certification can confer legitimacy and encourage procurement despite conflicting U.S. determinations. It specifically contrasts FBI certification with Hikvision’s Commerce Entity List placement, Section 889 contracting restrictions, and designation as linked to China’s military-industrial complex. The letter also describes alleged connections between listed companies and PRC military, intelligence, public-security, and surveillance institutions.

**Key dates and numbers**
- July 15, 2025: Letter date.
- 32: PRC-based companies identified on the FBI Certified Products List.
- At least 14: Firms the letter says have concerning intelligence, surveillance, or sanctioned-entity ties.
- 30 days: Requested period for the FBI’s written response.
