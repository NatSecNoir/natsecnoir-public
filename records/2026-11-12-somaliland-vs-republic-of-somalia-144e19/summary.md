The letter asks the Department of State to issue separate travel-advisory guidance for Somaliland and the Federal Republic of Somalia.

**What it does**
Signed by Chris Smith and John Moolenaar, the letter urges Secretary of State Marco Rubio to distinguish Somaliland from Somalia in the State Department’s travel advisory report. It argues that Somaliland’s security environment, governance, and cooperation with the United States warrant separate treatment, rather than inclusion under Somalia’s existing “Do Not Travel” advisory. The request is presented as consistent with congressional efforts to counter the People’s Republic of China’s influence in the Horn of Africa and support Taiwan.

**Who it affects**
The requested change would affect the State Department’s travel guidance for people considering travel, commerce, investment, and engagement in Somaliland and Somalia. The letter also highlights potential implications for U.S. companies and investors, including interest in Somaliland’s natural resources, and for U.S. diplomatic, security, and development engagement with Somaliland.

**Why it matters**
The authors contend that a bifurcated advisory would better reflect conditions on the ground, encourage economic development, and signal support for Somaliland’s cooperation with the United States, Taiwan, and other partners. They contrast Somaliland’s claimed security and law-enforcement record with Somalia’s continuing civil conflict and point to growing Chinese military and commercial influence in the region.

**Key dates and numbers**
- September 2, 2025: Letter date.
- 2023: The referenced National Defense Authorization Act treated Somaliland as distinct from Somalia’s Federal Member States.
- 2017: The letter states that the People’s Liberation Army established its first overseas military base in Djibouti.
- Two signatories: Chris Smith and John Moolenaar.
