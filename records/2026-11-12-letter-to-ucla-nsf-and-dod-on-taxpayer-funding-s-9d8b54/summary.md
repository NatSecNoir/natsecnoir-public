The House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party and House Energy and Commerce Committee chairs launched an investigation into federal research grants awarded to UCLA AI scientist Song-Chun Zhu, citing his ties to Chinese military research and a CCP talent plan.

**What it does** The letter requests documents from the University of California, Los Angeles, the National Science Foundation, and the U.S. Department of Defense concerning grants and research activities associated with Zhu. The document states that Newsweek reported at least $30 million in federal research grants led by Zhu and that a project once led by him received $1.2 million through two Office of Naval Research grants in 2021, after his departure to China.

**Who it affects** The requests affect UCLA Chancellor Gene Block, NSF Director Sethuraman Panchanathan, and Defense Secretary Lloyd Austin, whose institutions or offices were asked to provide information. The inquiry also concerns Zhu and federal funding streams supporting AI research connected to him.

**Why it matters** The committees frame the inquiry as a national-security review of continued U.S. support for Chinese AI development. The letter highlights allegations that Zhu established a parallel institute near Wuhan, joined a Beijing university supporting Chinese military research, and participated in a CCP talent plan tasked with transferring knowledge and technology to China.

**Key dates and numbers**
- January 17, 2024: Letter and investigation announced.
- November 1, 2023: Newsweek investigation cited by the document.
- At least $30 million: Federal research grants reportedly led by Zhu.
- $1.2 million in two grants: Office of Naval Research funding reported for a project once led by Zhu in 2021.
