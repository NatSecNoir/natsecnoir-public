The report finds that the People’s Republic of China remains heavily dependent on U.S., Dutch, and Japanese semiconductor manufacturing equipment (SME), while entity-based export controls have allowed continued acquisition and diversion of capable tools, and recommends broad country-wide restrictions across the SME supply chain.

**What it does** The House Select Committee on the CCP presents findings from an 11-month investigation of five leading SME manufacturers—Applied Materials, ASML, KLA, Lam Research, and Tokyo Electron—using company information, PRC corporate data, and open-source material. It examines sales, restricted Chinese customers, state-owned-enterprise revenue, lithography equipment, and BIS licensing for SMIC. The report makes nine recommendations, including expanded China-wide controls on SME, components, and consumables; stricter controls on restricted entities and affiliates; anti-diversion measures; restrictions on Chinese SME used outside China; increased BIS resources; a whistleblower program; and support for U.S. and allied toolmakers.

**Who it affects** The recommendations would affect BIS, the State Department, Congress, U.S. and allied SME manufacturers, PRC semiconductor fabs and toolmakers, and companies on the Entity List, Section 1260H Chinese Military Company List, or other referenced restriction regimes. The report specifically discusses SMIC, Huawei-linked fabs, YMTC, CXMT, SwaySure, SiEn, PST, PXW, and SiCarrier.

**Why it matters** The report argues that SME is a strategic chokepoint supporting advanced, foundational, and legacy chip production, military applications, AI, surveillance, and digital authoritarianism. It says the five Toolmakers received $38 billion in PRC revenue in FY2024—39% of their aggregate worldwide revenue—and that 69% of their 2024 PRC revenue came from state-owned enterprises. It emphasizes that the investigation does not allege that any Toolmaker violated applicable law.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Investigation covered roughly 11 months; company data generally covers FY2022–FY2024 and partial FY2025.
- Five Toolmakers accounted for an estimated 80–85% of the global SME market.
- FY2024 PRC revenue: $38 billion, or 39% of aggregate worldwide revenue.
- FY2024 PRC state-owned-enterprise revenue: $26.2 billion, or 69% of PRC revenue.
- From January 2021 through December 2024, one Toolmaker’s 196 SMIC-related license applications included 110 approvals and 22 denials; another’s 66 applications included 39 approvals and 5 denials.
- The report proposes nine recommendations, including country-wide controls covering SME capable of processing 300 mm wafers and additional node-agnostic tools.
