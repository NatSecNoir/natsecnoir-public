Hibernia Group ehf and its affiliated companies undertake national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety commitments to DHS, DOJ, and DoD in connection with the FCC’s consideration of Hibernia Atlantic U.S. LLC’s international Section 214 authorization.

**What it does** This Letter of Assurance (LOA) conditions Hibernia’s commitments on grant of the pending license and provides for notice and disclosure concerning principal equipment, vendors, contractors, network architecture, domestic communications infrastructure, and security practices. Hibernia must protect the physical and logical security of its submarine cable system, preserve and produce U.S. records in response to lawful process, store customer billing records for at least 18 months, and refer non-U.S. government requests for protected communications or related information to the U.S. agencies. It also designates a U.S.-resident citizen point of contact and security officer, requires annual compliance reporting, permits agency site visits, and requires reporting of specified breaches, foreign-government interference, or surveillance-law violations.

**Who it affects** The obligations bind Hibernia Group ehf, Hibernia Atlantic U.S. LLC, and identified subsidiaries and affiliates involved in owning, operating, or supporting the Hibernia Atlantic submarine cable system. They directly affect the company’s U.S. landing stations, network operations centers, data centers, vendors, contractors, and handling of domestic communications and customer records.

**Why it matters** The LOA illustrates Team Telecom mitigation accompanying FCC authorization of international telecommunications facilities and resale services. It gives U.S. agencies continuing visibility into critical telecommunications infrastructure and creates compliance, reporting, access, and lawful-process obligations while preserving existing government enforcement authority.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Dated September 30, 2010.
- Principal-equipment information is due by the later of November 15, 2010, or 60 days after license grant.
- Customer billing records must be retained for at least 18 months.
- Non-U.S. government requests must generally be referred within five days; specified breaches must be reported within 10 calendar days.
