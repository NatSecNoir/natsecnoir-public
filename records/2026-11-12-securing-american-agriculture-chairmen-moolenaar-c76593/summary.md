The House Select Committee on the CCP and the House Committees on Science, Space, and Technology and Education and Workforce seek investigations and records concerning alleged smuggling of dangerous biological materials connected to University of Michigan laboratories and federally funded research.

**What it does** The letters request that NIH and NSF conduct a full review of grants to the University of Michigan’s Molecular Plant-Microbe Interaction Laboratory and report their findings within 30 days. They ask about grant compliance, due diligence, foreign affiliations, disclosures, research security, travel support, intellectual property, and whether funded work could contribute to bioweapons capabilities. A parallel letter to University of Michigan President Domenico Grasso seeks records on laboratory access, disclosures, foreign gifts and contracts, safeguards for visiting researchers, institutional reviews, and the university’s research-security program. All recipients are directed to preserve relevant paper and electronic records.

**Who it affects** The requests target NIH, NSF, and the University of Michigan, particularly Professors Libo Shan and Ping He and researchers Yunqing Jian, Zunyong Liu, and Chengxuan Han. The letters also identify affiliations with Zhejiang University and Huazhong University of Science and Technology.

**Why it matters** The committees frame the alleged conduct as a research-security and agricultural-security risk, citing the potential agroterrorism significance of *Fusarium graminearum*, federal research funding, foreign affiliations, and perceived gaps in implementation of NSPM-33 and related disclosure and research-security requirements.

**Key dates and numbers**
- The letters are dated June 18, 2025; the requested response deadline is July 2, 2025.
- DOJ charges against Jian and Liu are cited as filed June 2, 2025; Han’s arrest is cited as occurring June 8, 2025.
- Shan and He are described as receiving more than $9.6 million in NIH and NSF funding since 2010.
- The University of Michigan letter contains 24 numbered requests; the agency letter contains 18.
