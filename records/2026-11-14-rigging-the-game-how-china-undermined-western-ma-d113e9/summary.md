The report concludes that the Chinese Communist Party has used state-directed industrial policy to dominate strategically important manufacturing sectors, weaken foreign competitors, and increase China’s economic and national-security leverage.

**What it does** The report explains the mechanisms of China’s industrial model—subsidies, regulation, state-owned-enterprise procurement, guidance funds, technology transfer, and protection of domestic demand—and applies them to four case studies: biomanufacturing, aerospace, semiconductors, and machine tools. It argues that these tools reduce China’s dependence on foreign suppliers while fostering global dependence on Chinese production. The report closes by calling for a sustained, bipartisan, whole-of-government U.S. industrial strategy to rebuild critical capacity, supply-chain resilience, technological leadership, and the skilled workforce.

**Who it affects** The analysis addresses U.S. manufacturers, workers, innovators, policymakers, and allied industries exposed to Chinese subsidies, overcapacity, supply-chain concentration, and technology-transfer practices. It highlights COMAC, Airbus, SMIC, Chinese telecommunications carriers, and state-owned machine-tool producers as examples of the affected industrial ecosystems.

**Why it matters** The report treats manufacturing capacity as inseparable from military capacity, emphasizing the dual-use character of shipbuilding, drones, batteries, semiconductors, aerospace, and machine tools. It warns that Chinese dominance can create both defensive self-reliance and offensive leverage over other economies.

**Key dates and numbers**
- China entered the WTO in 2001; Made in China 2025 was announced in 2015.
- China’s civilian shipbuilding capacity is described as 230 times that of the United States.
- China’s industrial-policy spending was estimated at $248 billion, or 1.73% of GDP, in 2019.
- China held 34% of global mature-node foundry capacity in 2024, with approximately 47% projected by 2027.
- The report identifies a proposed U.S. response as a multi-decade national effort.
