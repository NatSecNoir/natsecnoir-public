The House Select Committee on the Strategic Competition Between the United States and the Chinese Communist Party urges congressional leaders to increase Indo-Pacific funding beyond the Biden Administration’s supplemental appropriations request to deter Chinese Communist Party aggression.

**What it does** The November 20, 2023 letter asks Speaker Mike Johnson, Majority Leader Chuck Schumer, Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, and Minority Leader Mitch McConnell to support additional resources for the Indo-Pacific. It endorses the requested $2 billion in Foreign Military Financing for the Indo-Pacific and Taiwan and $3.4 billion for AUKUS-related domestic submarine industrial-base investments, while calling for $2 billion to backfill Presidential Drawdown Authority, including reauthorization of $650 million in Taiwan drawdown authority that expired in fiscal year 2023. It also seeks roughly $10 billion for U.S. defense-industrial capacity, munitions production, infrastructure, logistics, and sustainment.

**Who it affects** The recommendations concern Congress, the Department of Defense, Taiwan and other U.S. allies and partners, including the Philippines, and U.S. military and industrial programs supporting the Indo-Pacific. The letter also advocates reforms to International Traffic in Arms Regulations to advance AUKUS technology sharing.

**Why it matters** The committee frames the Indo-Pacific as the United States’ priority theater and argues that current supplemental funding is inadequate to address China’s military buildup, pressure on Taiwan, and regional coercion. It also supports H.R. 5753, which would create a Funding Indo-Pacific Readiness and Enhancing Stockpiles Fund using unobligated defense balances.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Dated November 20, 2023.
- Requests $2 billion in Indo-Pacific Foreign Military Financing and $2 billion to backfill Presidential Drawdown Authority.
- Identifies $3.4 billion for AUKUS investments and approximately $10 billion in additional defense-related needs.
- Says $11 billion in canceled funds might have been retained under the proposed FIRES Fund, and estimates $125 billion could have been maximized over the prior decade.
