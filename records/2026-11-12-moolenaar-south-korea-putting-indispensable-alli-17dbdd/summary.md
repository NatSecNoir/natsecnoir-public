Rep. John Moolenaar asks Secretary of State Marco Rubio to brief the House Select Committee on China by October 31, 2026, on developments in South Korea–China relations and the Administration’s response, warning that Seoul’s policies may be weakening the U.S.–ROK alliance.

**What it does** The September 28 letter catalogs what Moolenaar describes as nine months of intensified leader-level engagement between Seoul and Beijing, including President Lee Jae Myung’s visit to China, 14 memoranda of understanding, and proposed export-control and supply-chain communication channels. It also raises concerns about South Korean semiconductor exposure in China, alleged regulatory disparities favoring Chinese e-commerce platforms, regional-security disagreements, and reported restrictions on intelligence sharing after an alleged disclosure of U.S. classified information.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to the State Department and concerns the governments of the United States, South Korea, and China. The letter discusses Samsung, SK Hynix, AliExpress, Alibaba Group, Temu, and Coupang, as well as U.S. Forces Korea and South Korean officials and regulators.

**Why it matters** The letter frames economic, technology, data-governance, and defense-policy divergence as connected alliance risks. It argues that Chinese platforms are gaining Korean market share and consumer data while American firms face more aggressive enforcement, and that Seoul’s posture toward Taiwan, export controls, and information security warrants heightened U.S. vigilance.

**Key dates and numbers**
- September 28, 2026: letter date; October 31, 2026: requested briefing deadline.
- January 2026: Lee’s four-day Beijing visit and signing of 14 MOUs.
- July 21, 2026: revised Korean e-commerce enforcement decree took effect; the domestic-representative requirement has a 12-month phase-in.
- First quarter of 2026: AliExpress and Temu reportedly had about 16.5 million Korean monthly active users.
- The letter contrasts more than 400 raids and $409 million in penalties involving Coupang with alleged non-enforcement against AliExpress after an approximately $6 million theft.
