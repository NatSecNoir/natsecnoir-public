The agreement establishes national-security and law-enforcement mitigation obligations for Starfish and Google as a condition supporting Starfish’s application to construct, land, and operate the Nuvem submarine cable system.

**What it does** This National Security Agreement requires Starfish to maintain designated U.S.-citizen security and compliance officers; provide the Compliance Monitoring Agencies with detailed cable-system, covered-third-party, principal-equipment, and service-provider information; obtain advance notice or non-objection for specified equipment, providers, testing, sensing technology, and operational changes; and implement physical, logical, personnel-screening, cybersecurity, incident-reporting, training, audit, and annual-reporting controls. It also requires reporting of qualifying incidents within 48 hours and service-provider breach reporting within 72 hours. The agreement contemplates that the Committee will petition the FCC to condition the Nuvem license on compliance.

**Who it affects** The primary obligations fall on Starfish, with specified obligations for Google, affiliates, service providers, covered third parties, equipment providers, personnel, and auditors. The DHS, DOJ, and DoD representatives on the Committee receive oversight, objection, visitation, audit, and remediation authorities.

**Why it matters** The agreement operationalizes Team Telecom review of a submarine cable connecting South Carolina, Bermuda, the Azores, and Portugal. It links vendor and equipment review to foreign-adversary risks and to providers appearing on the FCC Covered List or Commerce Entity List, while protecting U.S. communications, government-related information, and sensitive records. Breach can support a recommendation to modify, condition, revoke, cancel, terminate, or nullify the relevant FCC authorization.

**Key dates and numbers**
- FCC referral: August 23, 2024.
- Starfish and Google signatures: April 15, 2025; DOJ signature: April 30, 2025. The NSA becomes effective on the last signature date.
- Initial major submissions are generally due within 60 or 90 days of effectiveness; qualifying incidents must be reported within 48 hours.
- The cable route runs between Myrtle Beach, South Carolina; Annie’s Bay, Bermuda; São Miguel, Azores; and Sines, Portugal.
