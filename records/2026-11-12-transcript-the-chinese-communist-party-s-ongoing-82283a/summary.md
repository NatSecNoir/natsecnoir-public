The hearing presents firsthand survivor testimony and expert evidence that the Chinese Communist Party is carrying out genocide and crimes against humanity against Uyghurs and other Muslim minorities in Xinjiang, and urges stronger U.S. and international action.

**What it does** The transcript places testimony from Gulbahar Haitiwaji and Qelbinur Sidik alongside analysis from Adrian Zenz, Nury Turkel, and Naomi Kikoler. Witnesses describe mass internment, torture, forced labor, sexual violence, family separation, reproductive coercion, cultural and religious erasure, and pervasive surveillance. Members also examine alleged forced organ harvesting, supply-chain complicity, investment in Chinese technology companies, and the use of platforms and data systems associated with ByteDance and Douyin. Proposed responses include sanctions, export controls, enforcement of the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act, restrictions on implicated investments, refugee protection, independent investigation, and coordinated diplomacy.

**Who it affects** The evidence concerns Uyghurs and other Muslim minorities in Xinjiang, survivors and their families, Uyghur diaspora communities, U.S. consumers and investors, technology companies, universities, pension funds, and businesses whose supply chains or capital may benefit from forced labor or surveillance. It also implicates Chinese officials, state-linked entities, and international governments and firms interacting with China.

**Why it matters** The hearing connects human-rights abuses to national-security telecommunications and technology concerns: biometric collection, facial recognition, AI-enabled profiling, mobile tracking, surveillance exports, and possible transnational repression. It argues that U.S. policy must address both the underlying abuses and the financial, technological, and commercial networks that enable them.

**Key dates and numbers**
- March 23, 2023: hearing held; questions for the record were due March 30.
- 1 to 2 million: estimated number of people detained at some point, according to Adrian Zenz.
- 2017–2019: Xinjiang birth rates reportedly fell by 50 percent, amid forced sterilization, abortion, and IUD policies.
- 2021: Congress passed the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act; witnesses called for stronger enforcement.
