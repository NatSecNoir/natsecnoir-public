The Letter of Agreement requires SORACOM Global, Inc. to implement national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety safeguards as a condition supporting the Department of Justice’s non-objection to its FCC application for international resale authority.

**What it does** The agreement establishes controls over U.S. communications and records, including customer proprietary network information, call-detail records, subscriber information, and personally identifiable information. SORACOM must maintain a U.S. law-enforcement point of contact approved by DOJ and the FBI; comply with lawful U.S. process; keep responsive information in the United States; restrict foreign access and disclosure; report security incidents and breaches; screen relevant personnel; and obtain advance DOJ review of new outsourced or offshore service providers, material business or service changes, and modem models offered in the United States. It also requires annual compliance reporting and permits DOJ site visits and employee interviews.

**Who it affects** The commitments bind SORACOM, its approved trusted third parties, outsourced and offshore providers, and personnel handling domestic communications infrastructure or U.S. records. DOJ, including the FBI, receives notice, review, and oversight rights, while DHS and DoD are identified as Team Telecom agencies.

**Why it matters** The LOA links SORACOM’s Section 214 authority to enforceable controls on foreign access, lawful surveillance and production requests, data location, vendors, cybersecurity, and corporate change. A material failure could lead DOJ to request that the FCC modify, condition, revoke, cancel, terminate, or nullify a relevant authorization.

**Key dates and numbers**
- March 10, 2020: LOA date and execution by CEO Kenta Yasukawa.
- 15 days: deadline to provide the LEPOC’s information to DOJ.
- 5 days: maximum response period for lawful U.S. process, absent an extension.
- 30 days: advance notice generally required for LEPOC changes, new providers, material business or service changes, and new modem models.
- 48 hours: provider deadline to disclose a breach or loss of U.S. records to SORACOM.
- 15 days: deadline for SORACOM’s written incident notification after discovery, unless law requires sooner.
- 7 business days: CPNI incident reporting deadline after reasonable determination.
