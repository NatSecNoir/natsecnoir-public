The House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party, joined by the House Ways and Means and Energy and Commerce Committees, demanded that Ford provide substantive information about its licensing agreement and partnership with Chinese battery maker CATL.

**What it does** The letter seeks a copy of Ford’s licensing agreement with CATL in English and Chinese, information about Ford’s knowledge of CATL’s connections to alleged forced labor and human-rights abuses, and records concerning CATL’s apparent efforts to shield connections to Xinjiang-based companies barred from exporting products to the United States. It also requests information about the planned use of Chinese employees, discussions with the Biden administration about the partnership and electric-vehicle tax credits, and Ford’s plans for U.S. battery production. The lawmakers issued a document-preservation notice.

**Who it affects** The immediate recipient is Ford CEO Jim Farley. The inquiry concerns Ford’s relationship with CATL and the proposed or planned battery manufacturing partnership, while also implicating the three congressional committees and their investigations.

**Why it matters** The letter escalates congressional scrutiny of a China-linked battery partnership amid concerns about supply-chain ties to Xinjiang, labor and human-rights issues, U.S. jobs, and the eligibility or use of electric-vehicle tax credits. It warns that continued noncompliance could lead to compulsory process or a demand for Ford’s public appearance before Congress.

**Key dates and numbers**
- September 27, 2023: Letter date.
- October 6, 2023: Deadline requested for Ford’s compliance.
- April, July, and September 2023: Earlier congressional inquiries referenced in the letter.
- July 20 and September 1, 2023: Document requests identified as requiring responses.
