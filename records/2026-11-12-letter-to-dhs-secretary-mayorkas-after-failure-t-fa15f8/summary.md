The Committees demand that DHS produce records and provide a staff briefing concerning the national-security risks of Chinese software and operational technology in U.S. port infrastructure, particularly ship-to-shore cranes made by Shanghai Zhenhua Heavy Industries (ZPMC). **What it does** The letter follows an April 3, 2023 request from the Committee on Homeland Security that DHS had not answered with a briefing or requested documentation. It asks Secretary Alejandro Mayorkas for a full production responsive to that earlier request plus nine categories of records covering ZPMC and other Chinese crane manufacturers, risk identification and mitigation, public-private and intergovernmental partnerships, training, allied outreach, cyberattacks and espionage, long-term consequences, interagency coordination, and secure alternatives. The committees request predominantly unclassified responses, with classified material under separate cover. **Who it affects** The request is directed to DHS and concerns federal, state, local, private-sector, and international entities involved in U.S. port security and transportation infrastructure. It specifically highlights ZPMC, described as a Chinese state-owned business operating under China Communications Construction Company’s governing shareholder structure. **Why it matters** The committees frame interconnected Chinese-manufactured cranes and related software as potential vectors for cyberattack, espionage, supply-chain compromise, and disruption of ports during a conflict involving Taiwan. The letter also invokes DHS’s role in securing the Transportation Systems Sector and coordinating mitigation and recovery for critical infrastructure threats. **Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated May 10, 2023; catalogue date provided as May 9, 2023.
- DHS was asked to respond to the document requests by 5:00 p.m. on May 24, 2023.
- A staff-level briefing was requested by May 31, 2023.
- The requests cover records from January 1, 2000, to the present.
- The letter cites estimates of about 70% of the global crane market and nearly 80% of ship-to-shore cranes in use at U.S. ports for ZPMC.
