The House Select Committee on the CCP urges the University of New Haven (UNH) to terminate its joint academic program with Xi’an University of Posts and Telecommunications (XUPT), citing XUPT’s military, defense-industrial, and classified-research connections.

**What it does** The October 1, 2026 letter to UNH President Jens Frederiksen describes the UNH-XUPT School of Information Engineering, established in May 2025 on XUPT’s campus in Xi’an. The program offers undergraduate majors in communications engineering, data science and big data technology, and intelligence science and technology, with roughly one-third of coursework drawn from UNH’s curriculum and taught by UNH faculty. The letter argues that the program operates under PRC law and oversight and is embedded in XUPT’s College of Communications and Information Engineering. It highlights the college’s stated support for military-civil fusion, relationships with PLA and defense-affiliated institutions, collaboration with ZTE, and faculty work involving sensitive communications, cyber, unmanned-systems, and navigation technologies.

**Who it affects** The principal parties are UNH, XUPT, their joint school, and President Frederiksen. The letter also discusses XUPT faculty, Chinese military and defense entities, ZTE, and UNH’s NSA-designated cybersecurity programs.

**Why it matters** The committee contends that the partnership could expose U.S. university expertise, training, and human capital to China’s defense research ecosystem, particularly because UNH supports cybersecurity education and workforce development.

**Key dates and numbers**
- October 1, 2026: letter date.
- May 2025: joint school established.
- 2019: UNH received its NSA Center of Academic Excellence designation.
- Approximately one-third: share of each major’s courses drawn from UNH’s curriculum.
- RMB 300,000: value of satellite-navigation software and receiver code provided to XUPT in a cited collaboration.
