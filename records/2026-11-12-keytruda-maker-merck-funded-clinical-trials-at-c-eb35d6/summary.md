The House Select Committee on the CCP asks Merck to explain and document its due diligence for clinical trials conducted in China, including trials at Xinjiang facilities and PRC military medical centers and hospitals.

**What it does** This June 29, 2026 letter to Merck CEO Robert M. Davis describes publicly identified Merck sponsorship or collaboration in 224 Chinese clinical studies since 2005. The Committee states that at least 31 trials between 2016 and 2025 included hospitals in Xinjiang and at least 40 included PRC military medical centers or hospitals. It requests nine categories of information, including Merck’s Good Clinical Practice policies, site inspections, trial and site data, use of contract research and manufacturing organizations, internal risk assessments, intellectual-property safeguards, data-storage and transfer practices, and agreements with Chinese companies since January 1, 2020.

**Who it affects** The requests are directed to Merck and cover its China-based clinical-trial operations, Xinjiang sites, PRC military hospitals, contractors, and Chinese licensing, equity, and joint-venture relationships. The letter also discusses potential implications for trial participants, American medical data, and biotechnology intellectual property.

**Why it matters** The Committee frames clinical-trial data as a national-security and human-rights concern, citing informed-consent risks, oppression and forced medical testing allegations in Xinjiang, and the possibility that research conducted at military hospitals could support Chinese military biotechnology capabilities. It expressly states that it has no evidence Merck engaged in illegal activity or wrongdoing.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated June 29, 2026; requested response deadline: July 17, 2026.
- 224 publicly identified Merck-associated studies in China since 2005.
- At least 31 Xinjiang-involving trials and 40 military-center or hospital trials from 2016–2025.
- Nine categories of information requested.
