The agreement requires Starfish Infrastructure Inc. and Google LLC to implement extensive national-security and law-enforcement safeguards for the Bulikula submarine cable as a condition of the FCC license sought for the system. Bulikula connects Hawaii, Guam, and the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands (“CNMI”) to French Polynesia and Fiji.

**What it does** It establishes a National Security Agreement between the Companies and the Committee, represented by DHS, DOJ, and DoD. It requires designated security and compliance officers; detailed disclosures about Bulikula’s architecture, equipment, service providers, covered third parties, personnel, and access; prior CMA non-objection for specified equipment, providers, sensing technologies, and foreign individual access; cybersecurity, physical-security, screening, incident-reporting, training, annual-reporting, audit, and inspection obligations; and cooperation with lawful U.S. process. The Committee will petition the FCC to condition the cable landing license on compliance.

**Who it affects** The primary obligations fall on Starfish, as license applicant and operator, and Google, whose obligations include specified compliance and oversight responsibilities. They extend contractually to affiliates, service providers, equipment vendors, covered third parties, personnel, and auditors, while DHS, DOJ, and DoD receive monitoring, objection, audit, and visitation rights.

**Why it matters** The agreement illustrates Team Telecom mitigation for a submarine cable carrying communications and potentially sensitive U.S. records. It links supply-chain controls to the FCC Covered List and Commerce Entity List, requires rapid reporting of incidents, and allows the Committee to recommend license modification, revocation, or other relief for breach.

**Key dates and numbers**
- FCC referral: April 10, 2025.
- Most initial submissions are due within 60 or 90 days of the NSA Effective Date.
- Security incidents must be reported within 48 hours; service-provider breaches within 72 hours.
- CMA objection periods generally run 30, 60, or 90 days, depending on the submission.
- The agreement automatically terminates if Starfish does not receive the FCC license.
