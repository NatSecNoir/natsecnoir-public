The House Select Committee on the CCP asks Pfizer to provide information about its clinical-trial due diligence and activities at Chinese military hospitals and in Xinjiang, citing ethical, human-rights, data-security, and biotechnology risks.

**What it does** The June 29, 2026 letter from Chairman John Moolenaar states that publicly available information indicates Pfizer sponsored or collaborated on more than 300 clinical studies in China since 2000. It identifies at least six trials involving hospitals in Xinjiang and at least 43 involving Chinese military medical centers or hospitals between 2001 and 2023. The letter requests nine categories of information, including Pfizer’s Good Clinical Practice policies, site-inspection data, risk assessments, trial and site information since January 1, 2015, protections for intellectual property and sensitive data, and agreements with Chinese companies since January 1, 2020.

**Who it affects** The requests are directed to Pfizer and concern its clinical-trial sites, contractors, Chinese biotechnology partnerships, and research conducted at facilities affiliated with the People’s Liberation Army or located in Xinjiang. The letter also references Pfizer’s partnerships with Innovent and 3SBio.

**Why it matters** The committee frames clinical-trial data as a strategic biotechnology and medical-data asset. It raises concerns about informed consent, conditions in Xinjiang, possible transfer of sensitive research or intellectual property to the Chinese military, and compliance with Good Clinical Practice. The letter expressly says it presents no evidence that Pfizer engaged in illegal activity or wrongdoing.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated June 29, 2026; metadata date: June 30, 2026.
- Pfizer response requested by July 17, 2026.
- At least 6 Xinjiang-related trials and at least 43 military-hospital trials identified for 2001–2023.
- Requested trial data covers activity since January 1, 2015; requested Chinese-company agreements cover activity since January 1, 2020.
