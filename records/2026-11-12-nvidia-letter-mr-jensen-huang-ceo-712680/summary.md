The House Select Committee on the Strategic Competition Between the United States and the Chinese Communist Party requests information from NVIDIA concerning potential diversion of restricted AI chips and related dealings involving DeepSeek and PRC entities.

**What it does** The May 1, 2025 letter to NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang expresses concern that DeepSeek may have obtained NVIDIA chips subject to U.S. export controls through third countries. It requests a customer list and purchase data for customers buying more than 499 AI-related chips or accelerators since January 1, 2020, when the customer or end-use destination is in an ASEAN member state, China, Hong Kong, or Macau. It also seeks NVIDIA–DeepSeek correspondence and agreements with PRC entities restricted under specified U.S. government authorities and lists.

**Who it affects** The request directly affects NVIDIA and seeks information about its customers, DeepSeek and its affiliates or personnel, and PRC entities covered by the cited restrictions. The Committee states that its authority derives from H. Res. 5, section 4(a).

**Why it matters** The letter connects alleged chip diversion and indirect supply channels to the effectiveness of U.S. export controls and U.S. leadership in artificial intelligence and computing components. It also highlights the national-security implications of advanced AI hardware reaching adversarial regimes.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter date shown: May 1, 2025; the letter is dated April 16, 2025.
- Requested response deadline: April 30, 2025.
- Customer threshold: more than 499 AI-related chips or accelerators.
- Purchase period: January 1, 2020, through the present.
- Covered geography includes 10 ASEAN member states, China, Hong Kong, and Macau.
- Cited authorities include section 1260H, the BIS Entity List, the UFLPA Entity List, the Military End User List, Executive Order 14059, the SDN List, and the Non-SDN Chinese Military-Industrial Complex Companies List.
