The House Select Committee on the CCP urges Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent to designate biotechnology as a prohibited technology under the Comprehensive Outbound Investment National Security (COINS) Act of 2025.

**What it does** The letter argues that Treasury should use the COINS Act’s authority to cover biotechnology, with particular attention to licensing of pharmaceutical intellectual property, drug-discovery platforms, clinical research and development capabilities, and biologics manufacturing and commercialization know-how. It describes U.S. capital, licensing agreements, joint ventures, equity investments, and co-development arrangements as supporting China’s biotechnology ambitions. The letter also raises concerns about clinical trials in Xinjiang and at PRC military hospitals, including risks to sensitive data and the transfer of American biotechnology IP.

**Who it affects** The requested restrictions would affect U.S. investors, pharmaceutical companies, and other parties engaging with Chinese biotechnology companies. The letter highlights recent transactions involving Eli Lilly, Bristol Myers Squibb, Hengrui Pharma, and Innovent, while directing its recommendation to the Department of the Treasury.

**Why it matters** The document frames biotechnology as both a national-security asset and a strategic vulnerability, warning that outbound capital and know-how could deepen U.S. dependence on China’s pharmaceutical innovation supply chain and support Chinese military or surveillance capabilities. It is a policy recommendation, not a Treasury regulation or investment prohibition.

**Key dates and numbers**
- May 21, 2026: Letter date.
- 2025: COINS Act enacted as title LXXXV of the FY 2026 National Defense Authorization Act.
- Approximately $136 billion: Reported value of cross-border out-licensing transactions involving Chinese biotechnology firms in 2025.
- $60 billion: Reported first-quarter 2026 transaction value, described as a 73% year-over-year increase.
- Up to $15.2 billion: Value cited for Bristol Myers Squibb’s partnership with Hengrui Pharma.
