The Letter of Agreement establishes national-security and law-enforcement commitments that support DOJ’s non-objection to IPC’s proposed transfer of control and related domestic and international Section 214 authorizations to Hummingbird Circle.

**What it does** This agreement governs IPC Corp., IPC Systems, IPC Network Services, Hummingbird Circle, and CCP II Finco. It requires a U.S. law-enforcement point of contact, a U.S.-based security officer eligible for a Secret-level clearance, personnel screening, compliance with lawful interception requirements and CALEA, and prompt access to U.S. records in response to lawful U.S. process. It also imposes controls on foreign access, principal equipment, outsourced and offshored providers, network operations centers, ownership changes, service changes, cybersecurity plans, incident reporting, and annual compliance reporting.

**Who it affects** The commitments apply to the IPC Companies and their owners, subsidiaries, personnel, contractors, service providers, equipment suppliers, and foreign persons or governments seeking access to U.S. communications, infrastructure, or records. DOJ, including the FBI, receives review, objection, notification, and site-visit rights.

**Why it matters** The LOA makes national-security safeguards a condition surrounding FCC approval of a telecommunications-control transfer. Breach or unresolved risk may lead to FCC modification, conditioning, revocation, cancellation, or termination of relevant authorizations.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Dated July 5, 2022; FCC approval is the operative trigger for most deadlines.
- Many submissions require 15-, 30-, or 60-day notice or review periods.
- Security incidents and certain breaches must be reported within 72 hours; CPNI incidents require notice within seven business days.
- Annual reports are due one year after FCC approval and every year thereafter.
- Non-U.S. beneficial owners holding or controlling at least 5% must be reported under specified circumstances.
