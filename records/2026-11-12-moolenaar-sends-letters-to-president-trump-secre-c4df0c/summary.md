The House Select Committee on the CCP asks President Donald Trump to press Xi Jinping for the release of Chinese prisoners of conscience and asks Secretary of State Marco Rubio to assess four Hong Kong officials for possible sanctions.

**What it does** The March 19, 2026 letters, signed by Chairman John Moolenaar, describe alleged arbitrary detention, religious and political persecution, forced labor, transnational repression, and erosion of Hong Kong autonomy by the Chinese Communist Party. The letter to President Trump urges him to raise prisoners of conscience at any future meeting with Xi Jinping. The letter to Secretary Rubio recommends determining whether Hong Kong Police Commissioner Joe Chow Yat-ming and National Security Law Judges Esther Toh Lye-ping, Alex Lee Wan-tang, and Susana Maria D'Almada Remedios meet sanction criteria under the Hong Kong Human Rights and Democracy Act, the Hong Kong Autonomy Act, and Executive Order 13936.

**Who it affects** The requests concern Chinese prisoners of conscience, Hong Kong democracy and human-rights advocates, including Jimmy Lai, and the four named Hong Kong police and judicial officials. They are directed to the President and Secretary of State rather than imposing sanctions or other binding measures themselves.

**Why it matters** The letters frame human rights and political freedom as part of U.S. strategic competition with China and call for executive-branch action in response to the sentencing of Jimmy Lai and broader repression in mainland China, Tibet, Xinjiang, and Hong Kong.

**Key dates and numbers**
- March 19, 2026: date on both letters.
- February 9, 2026: Jimmy Lai received a 20-year sentence, according to the letter.
- January 2019–December 2024: cited data identified 1,545 convicted prisoners of conscience, including 123 in Hong Kong.
- Four Hong Kong officials are referred for possible sanctions review.
