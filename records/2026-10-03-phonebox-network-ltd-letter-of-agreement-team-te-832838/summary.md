The Letter of Agreement conditions Team Telecom’s national-security and law-enforcement resolution of PhoneBox Network Ltd.’s Section 214 resale application on extensive compliance, reporting, security, and U.S.-process commitments.

**What it does** This agreement requires PhoneBox to maintain a U.S.-based, FBI-approved law-enforcement point of contact; provide prompt access to U.S. records in response to lawful process; comply with CALEA and lawful-interception requirements; restrict disclosure of U.S. records, domestic communications, and domestic communications infrastructure to foreign governments, entities, or persons; and refer foreign requests to DOJ. It also requires personnel screening, cybersecurity and system-security plans, equipment and service-provider disclosures, advance notice of material changes, annual compliance reports, and DOJ site-visit cooperation. Security incidents and certain breaches must generally be reported within 48 hours.

**Who it affects** The commitments bind PhoneBox, its personnel, vendors, outsourced and offshore providers, network operators, and other parties with access to covered systems or records. DOJ, including the FBI, reviews specified submissions and may object or seek changes.

**Why it matters** The LOA makes national-security mitigation enforceable through potential FCC action, including modification, conditioning, revocation, cancellation, or nullification of relevant authorization if PhoneBox breaches its commitments.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Executed October 26, 2022; FCC File No. ITC-214-20220328-00044 and TT 22-017.
- LEPOC information, equipment lists, and provider lists are generally due within 15–30 days after FCC approval.
- Security incidents and breaches must generally be reported within 48 hours.
- Annual reporting begins one year after FCC approval.
