The House Committee on Small Business, the Committee on Science, Space, and Technology, and the House Select Committee on the CCP ask the Department of Education to provide information and procedures addressing alleged foreign exploitation and national-security risks in the SBIR and STTR programs.

**What it does** The letter raises concerns that China may obtain taxpayer-funded U.S. technology through CCP-linked venture capital, research partnerships, talent-recruitment programs, PRC-based subsidiaries, and state-backed investment. It cites a 2021 Department of Defense-commissioned report stating that DoD SBIR programs lacked adequate due-diligence capabilities to assess national-security risks. The Committees request annual application data, flagging and rejection rates, security-risk categories, disclosure requirements, screening criteria, post-flagging procedures, interagency communications procedures, and monitoring procedures for Phases I, II, and III.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to Acting Education Secretary Denise L. Carter and concerns the Department of Education’s participation in the SBIR and STTR programs, as well as U.S. small businesses seeking or receiving program funds. The letter also addresses oversight by the three congressional committees and coordination with other agencies.

**Why it matters** The Committees frame SBIR and STTR safeguards as both innovation-policy and national-security issues, warning that weak oversight could facilitate technology transfer to China and erode U.S. competitiveness. They indicate that legislative and policy changes may be considered before the programs’ authorization expires.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated February 26, 2025; requested response deadline: March 12, 2025.
- Requested data covering January 20, 2021, onward.
- SBIR/STTR authorization identified as expiring September 30, 2025.
- Requested breakdowns cover Phases I, II, and III and multiple national-security risk categories.
- The letter states that small businesses comprise 99.9 percent of U.S. businesses.
