The President prohibited Beijing Shiji Information Technology Co., Ltd.’s acquisition of StayNTouch, Inc. and ordered the purchaser to divest its interests in the company and related assets under section 721 of the Defense Production Act.

**What it does** The order prohibits the transaction and ownership of any interest in StayNTouch or its assets by Beijing Shiji, its wholly owned subsidiary Shiji (Hong Kong) Ltd., and related entities. It requires divestiture of StayNTouch, its intellectual property, technology, data—including customer data managed and stored by StayNTouch—personnel, customer contracts, and certain operations. Until divestiture is completed and verified by CFIUS, the purchaser must not access hotel guest data, must implement access controls, and must provide weekly compliance certifications. Proposed buyers require advance notice to CFIUS and a 10-business-day period without objection. CFIUS may inspect records and systems, interview personnel, and verify compliance.

**Who it affects** The order directly binds the purchaser and StayNTouch, a Delaware corporation, and gives CFIUS implementation and verification responsibilities. The Attorney General is authorized to enforce it.

**Why it matters** It demonstrates presidential use of CFIUS authorities to unwind a covered foreign acquisition based on a finding that the purchaser might threaten U.S. national security, while imposing interim controls over sensitive hotel guest data and operations.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Adopted March 6, 2020; published March 10, 2020, at 85 FR 13719.
- Divestiture due within 120 days, unless extended by up to 90 days under written CFIUS conditions.
- Access controls due within 7 days; CFIUS verification concludes within 90 days after divestiture certification.
- FR Doc. 2020-05011.
