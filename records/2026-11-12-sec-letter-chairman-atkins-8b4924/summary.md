The letter urges the Securities and Exchange Commission to protect U.S. investors and national security by suspending trading and pursuing delisting of high-risk Chinese companies that the authors say are controlled by, integrated with, or legally compelled to support the Chinese Communist Party.

**What it does** The House Select Committee on the CCP and Senate Committee on Aging identify 20 example companies listed on U.S. exchanges and provide detailed profiles of firms including Hesai, Alibaba, Baidu, JD.com, Pony AI, and XPeng. The letter points to variable-interest-entity structures, Party committees, “golden shares,” military-civil fusion, surveillance, forced-labor exposure, audit deficiencies, and other alleged risks. It asks SEC Chairman Paul Atkins to use the Holding Foreign Companies Accountable Act and Sections 12(j) and (k) of the Securities Exchange Act to suspend trading or revoke registrations where appropriate.

**Who it affects** The request concerns the SEC, U.S. investors, U.S. exchanges, and Chinese companies accessing American capital markets. The profiled companies span technology, finance, e-commerce, logistics, autonomous vehicles, electric vehicles, streaming, solar, and live-streaming sectors.

**Why it matters** The letter frames corporate transparency and investor-protection concerns as national-security risks, arguing that PRC legal and political controls can conceal ownership, military ties, data practices, and forced-labor exposure. It asks for a regulatory response rather than proposing a new statutory regime.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated May 2, 2025; catalogue date: May 5, 2025.
- 20 example companies are listed or profiled.
- 286 Chinese firms were identified as listed on major U.S. exchanges as of March 7, 2025.
- The letter invokes the HFCAA and Sections 12(j) and (k) of the Securities Exchange Act.
