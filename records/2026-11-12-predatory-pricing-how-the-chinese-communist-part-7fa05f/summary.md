The House Select Committee on the CCP finds that the PRC has used state subsidies, control of refining and price-reporting systems, acquisitions, production decisions, and export controls to manipulate critical-minerals markets and entrench dependence on China.

**What it does** This interim report examines coal, rare earth elements, and lithium. It identifies 13 findings concerning alleged PRC support for overseas mineral acquisitions, legal and regulatory pressure on price reporting, state influence over exchanges and refining, and coordinated efforts to suppress rare-earth and lithium prices. It also describes the PRC’s October 2025 export-control regime covering rare-earth items, lithium batteries, and certain related materials. The report proposes 13 policy recommendations, including federal financing, faster permitting, market-transparency tools, a Strategic Resources Reserve, allied coordination, domestic magnet manufacturing, and workforce development.

**Who it affects** The analysis focuses on U.S. and allied miners, refiners, manufacturers, investors, price-reporting agencies, commodity exchanges, and downstream defense and technology industries. It highlights effects on companies including Albemarle, Lithium Americas, and Piedmont Lithium, and on supply chains for batteries, magnets, electronics, and defense systems.

**Why it matters** The Committee argues that artificially depressed or state-directed prices can discourage non-PRC investment while China consolidates upstream assets and midstream capacity. It reports that PRC interests control or own stakes in four of the five lithium mines projected to drive global supply and that China controls major shares of lithium and rare-earth processing.

**Key dates and numbers**
- October 9, 2025: PRC announced the cited global rare-earth and lithium-related licensing regimes.
- 13 findings and 13 policy recommendations.
- Roughly $57 billion in reported PRC aid and subsidized mineral-project credit.
- PRC interests reportedly control four of the top five projected lithium mines; China is estimated to control 60–80% of lithium processing and refining.
