The House Select Committee on the CCP and the Senate Special Committee on Aging request urgent briefings from the SEC and PCAOB on information access, auditing standards, and investor protections concerning China-based companies listed on U.S. exchanges.

**What it does** The July 16, 2025 letter asks SEC Chairman Paul S. Atkins and PCAOB Chairwoman Erica Y. Williams to explain the current agreement between the PCAOB and Chinese securities regulators and its implications for U.S. investors. The signatories argue that the arrangement permits Chinese authorities to control access to auditors and withhold certain categories of information. They also recount prior concerns raised after the agreement became public and request responses from both institutions.

**Who it affects** The letter addresses the SEC, PCAOB, Chinese securities regulators, and Chinese companies accessing U.S. stock exchanges. It focuses on the more than 200 Chinese companies that the letter says were affected by the Holding Foreign Companies Accountable Act (HFCAA), as well as American investors relying on those companies’ disclosures and audits.

**Why it matters** The authors contend that limits on PCAOB inspection and investigation could undermine investor protections and the HFCAA’s delisting mechanism. They connect audit-access concerns to broader allegations that Chinese law and state influence can restrict disclosure about military ties, state ownership, and party influence.

**Key dates and numbers**
- July 16, 2025: Letter date.
- 2020: HFCAA enacted; the letter says it affected more than 200 Chinese companies on U.S. exchanges.
- August 2022: PCAOB and Chinese regulators reached an information-access agreement.
- Two consecutive years: Period identified for delisting noncompliant companies under the letter’s description of the HFCAA.
