The House Select Committee on the Strategic Competition between the United States and the Chinese Communist Party and the House Committee on Small Business request that the Department of Justice brief committee staff on efforts to combat Chinese intellectual-property theft affecting American small businesses.

**What it does** The June 15, 2023 letter to Attorney General Merrick B. Garland asks the Department to provide a briefing by June 30, 2023. It seeks information on measures to help small businesses protect intellectual-property rights, investigations and accountability for theft, implementation of the Protect American IP Act of 2022, plans to strengthen investigative efforts, cooperation with state and local governments, and responses to counterfeit products. The letter also cites the Department’s Intellectual Property Task Force and asks about the number of Chinese entities held accountable.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to the Department of Justice and concerns American small businesses, particularly those facing alleged theft or counterfeiting linked to China. It highlights examples involving a small irrigation company and Ruth Brons, whose patented violin-learning product was reportedly counterfeited in Chinese e-commerce markets.

**Why it matters** The committees frame intellectual-property theft as a threat to U.S. innovation, economic security, and national security. They cite a 2017 estimate that Chinese theft costs the U.S. economy $600 billion annually and seek operational details about federal enforcement and support for affected businesses.

**Key dates and numbers**
- June 15, 2023: Letter date.
- June 30, 2023: Requested deadline for the briefing.
- Six: Categories of information requested from the Department.
- $600 billion annually: Estimate cited for the economic cost of Chinese intellectual-property theft.
