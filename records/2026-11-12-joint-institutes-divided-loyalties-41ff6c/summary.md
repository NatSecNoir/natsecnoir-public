The report concludes that U.S.-China university joint institutes and related academic partnerships provide the Chinese Communist Party and Chinese defense-linked entities with sustained access to U.S. expertise while many universities fail to make required foreign-funding disclosures.

**What it does** The majority staff report presents findings from investigations into joint institutes, dual-degree programs, research partnerships, and foreign gifts and contracts. It identifies eight universities that closed joint institutes, describes other partnerships the Committees characterize as high risk, and examines enforcement of section 117 of the Higher Education Act. It also recommends enactment of the SAFE Research Act and the DETERRENT Act, including restrictions on certain federal research and Department of Defense funding and expanded disclosures.

**Who it affects** The report principally addresses U.S. universities, researchers, joint institutes, and their Chinese institutional partners. It highlights programs involving Duke, Houston, New York University, Arizona, Delaware, and other universities, as well as Chinese institutions described as defense-linked or subject to Chinese government oversight. The report also discusses the Department of Education’s compliance reviews of Harvard, UC Berkeley, the University of Pennsylvania, and the University of Michigan.

**Why it matters** The report frames overseas joint institutes as technology-transfer and talent-development channels operating under PRC law, with implications for military-civil fusion, academic freedom, and research security. It also argues that weak or delayed disclosure enforcement obscures the scale of foreign funding and partnerships.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Sept. 2024: the Committees’ earlier *CCP on the Quad* report.
- Sept. 10, 2025: the House passed the FY2026 NDAA, incorporating the SAFE Research Act as an amendment.
- Section 117 reporting threshold: $250,000 in gifts or contracts in a calendar year.
- The report identifies more than 50 detailed high-risk partnerships and nearly 150 partnerships linked to China’s military or defense industrial base.
- Four section 117 compliance reviews were opened in 2025.
