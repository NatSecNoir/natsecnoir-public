The House Select Committee on the CCP asks Commerce to account for national-security risks and potential technology transfers associated with the U.S.–China Science and Technology Agreement (STA), including patents involving Chinese inventors and U.S.-funded research.

**What it does**
The June 13, 2024 letter from Chairman John Moolenaar, also signed by five other lawmakers, urges Commerce Under Secretary Kathi Vidal to provide information about technology transfer through the STA. It requests annual counts since 2010 of U.S.-government-funded patents filed at the USPTO that included a Chinese inventor; the inventions’ technology classes, funding agencies, and the Chinese employers of those inventors; any military or export-control affiliations; and any Chinese patent applications replicating inventions filed in the United States.

**Who it affects**
The request is directed to the Department of Commerce and its U.S. Patent and Trademark Office. It concerns U.S. government-funded research, Chinese inventors and employers, and entities potentially affiliated with the Chinese military or subject to U.S. export controls. The letter also urges the Administration to allow the STA to expire.

**Why it matters**
The lawmakers characterize the STA as a national-security risk and contend that the Chinese Communist Party has previously used it to advance military objectives. The requested patent and affiliation data would help assess the extent of any dual-use technology transfer and possible duplication of U.S. inventions in China. The document reports that the Administration had extended the agreement twice.

**Key dates and numbers**
- June 13, 2024: Letter date and public announcement.
- 2010 onward: Requested start date for annual USPTO patent data.
- Six lawmakers signed the letter: Moolenaar, Stefanik, Luetkemeyer, Barr, Steel, and Gimenez.
- Four categories of information were requested, covering patent counts, technology and funding details, affiliations, and possible Chinese replication.
