The three House committees ask the Department of Defense to provide information and strengthen safeguards against alleged Chinese exploitation of the SBIR and STTR programs.

**What it does** The February 26, 2025 letter describes concerns that taxpayer-funded research is being diverted through CCP-linked venture capital, university research partnerships, talent-recruitment programs, PRC-based subsidiaries, and state-backed investment. It requests annual data on application outcomes and flags across Phases I, II, and III; information on national-security, foreign-influence, export-control, dual-use, and due-diligence concerns; and copies of disclosure, flagging, applicant-handling, interagency-communication, and monitoring procedures. The committees ask DoD to provide information dating back to January 20, 2021 and to respond by March 12, 2025.

**Who it affects** The request concerns DoD administration and oversight of SBIR and STTR applicants and participating American small businesses, particularly firms developing sensitive or dual-use technologies. It also addresses the committees’ future legislative and policy review as the programs’ authorization approaches expiration.

**Why it matters** The letter frames weak due diligence and inadequate national-security screening as vulnerabilities that can expose proprietary technology and intellectual property to China and undermine U.S. technological leadership. It cites a 2021 DoD-commissioned report stating that DoD SBIR programs lacked adequate capabilities to assess national-security risks associated with applicants, and seeks information that could support reforms.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter date: February 26, 2025.
- Requested response deadline: March 12, 2025.
- Requested data period begins: January 20, 2021.
- SBIR/STTR authorization was scheduled to expire: September 30, 2025.
- The letter states that small businesses comprise 99.9 percent of U.S. businesses.
