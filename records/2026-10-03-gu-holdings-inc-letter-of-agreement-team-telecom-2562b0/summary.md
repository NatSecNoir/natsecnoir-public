The Letter of Assurances sets out GU Holdings Inc.’s mitigation commitments to the U.S. Department of Homeland Security (DHS) in connection with its FCC application to land and operate the Monet Cable System, a private, non-common-carrier submarine fiber-optic cable linking the United States and Brazil.

**What it does** The LOA requires GU Holdings to nominate a resident U.S.-citizen security point of contact and alternate, provide a current Principal Equipment list, and submit annual reports covering equipment changes, contacts, relevant events, and network-topology changes. It also requires advance notice of specified changes in ownership, services, or cable operations. DHS may review proposed points of contact, consult on compliance, and—on reasonable advance notice and subject to law—visit the U.S. portion of the system. The agreement binds successors, assigns, subsidiaries, and affiliates; a breach may lead DHS to request FCC action concerning the relevant authorization.

**Who it affects** The commitments apply primarily to GU Holdings and its successors and affiliates, and concern the Monet Cable System’s U.S.-located domestic communications infrastructure, network operations facilities, and undersea “Wet Infrastructure.” DHS receives the reports and may conduct oversight; the FCC is asked to condition the requested license on compliance.

**Why it matters** The LOA illustrates Team Telecom mitigation for a submarine cable authorization, using continuing disclosure, operational transparency, government consultation, and potential license remedies to address national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety concerns.
