The House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party and the House Veterans’ Affairs Committee asked Secretary Denis McDonough to demonstrate that the Department of Veterans Affairs is ending its reliance on Chinese manufacturing for critical medical supplies.

**What it does** The November 9, 2023 letter describes the VA’s collaboration with the Departments of Defense, Health and Human Services, and Homeland Security on the National Strategy for a Resilient Public Health Supply Chain. Chairman Mike Gallagher and Chairman Mike Bost criticize the strategy report for providing little detail about when and how it will be implemented, warning that it may be “gathering dust on a shelf.” They request documents and answers concerning the VA’s emergency planning.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to Denis McDonough, Secretary of Veterans Affairs, and concerns the VA’s procurement and emergency-planning practices for medical supplies used to care for veterans. It also addresses federal coordination on public-health supply-chain resilience and dependence on Chinese manufacturing.

**Why it matters** The lawmakers connect supply-chain dependence to national security and continuity of veteran care, citing the Chinese Communist Party’s withholding of critical medical-supply exports during the COVID-19 pandemic. They warn that insufficient domestic production and continued reliance on Chinese manufacturing could leave the public-health supply chain vulnerable to manipulation during future emergencies.

**Key dates and numbers**
- November 9, 2023: Letter date.
- November 29, 2023: Requested deadline for the VA to provide documents and answers.
- The letter identifies three partner departments—Defense, Health and Human Services, and Homeland Security—in developing the supply-chain strategy.
