The House Committee on Small Business, the House Committee on Science, Space, and Technology, and the House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party ask the National Science Foundation to provide information and strengthen safeguards against foreign exploitation of the SBIR and STTR programs.

**What it does** The letter describes concerns that China may obtain taxpayer-funded U.S. technology through CCP-linked venture capital, university research partnerships, talent-recruitment programs, PRC-based subsidiaries, and state-backed investment in U.S. companies receiving SBIR/STTR funding. It requests data on application outcomes and flags across Phases I, II, and III; national-security, foreign-influence, export-control, dual-use, and due-diligence concerns; and the agency’s disclosure, screening, monitoring, referral, and response procedures.

**Who it affects** The requests are directed to NSF Director Sethuraman Panchanathan and concern NSF’s administration of the SBIR and STTR programs, participating American small businesses, applicants, and other agencies involved in reviewing suspicious applications. The letter also identifies the Department of Defense’s 2021 report on PRC-sponsored technology transfers as a basis for its concerns.

**Why it matters** The committees argue that weak oversight could allow sensitive research and intellectual property funded by U.S. taxpayers to reach foreign adversaries, undermining national security, economic competitiveness, and the U.S. industrial base. Their requested information is intended to inform legislative and policy changes before program authorization expires.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated February 26, 2025; response requested by March 12, 2025.
- Requested records and data should date back to January 20, 2021.
- SBIR/STTR authorization was set to expire on September 30, 2025.
- The letter states that small businesses comprise 99.9 percent of U.S. businesses.
