Latam Telecommunications, LLC commits to national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety mitigation measures as a condition of the FCC’s consideration of an application involving the AMX1 submarine cable system.

**What it does** This Letter of Assurance requires the company to provide a principal-equipment list within 60 days and updated information on request, including network architecture, management, security, vendors, and contractors. It requires updated information within 15 days when requested; U.S.-based storage of system logs for 18 months; written reporting of unauthorized access, disruption, or corruption within 10 business days of discovery; and reporting of remediation actions within 10 business days of a government recommendation. The company must designate U.S.-resident points of contact, submit annual reports, permit reasonable-notice site visits, provide 30 days’ notice of significant U.S. service changes, support lawful electronic surveillance, and store U.S. customer and billing records in the United States. The commitments bind successors, assigns, subsidiaries, and affiliates, and submissions are intended to receive confidential treatment.

**Who it affects** The obligations apply primarily to Latam Telecommunications and its U.S. AMX1 facilities, equipment, records, personnel, vendors, contractors, and subcontractors. The arrangement also concerns Puerto Rico Telephone Company, Inc. and Claro Chile S.A.’s related FCC application, and gives DOJ and DHS oversight and enforcement-related rights.

**Why it matters** The LOA makes FCC authorization contingent on operational transparency, cybersecurity, domestic records access, and lawful-surveillance support. DOJ and DHS may seek FCC modification, conditioning, revocation, cancellation, or nullification of relevant authorization for breach or inadequate mitigation.
