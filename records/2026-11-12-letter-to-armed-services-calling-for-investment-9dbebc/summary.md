The signatories urge congressional Armed Services Committee leaders to include and strengthen outbound-investment provisions in the fiscal year 2024 National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA) to address national-security risks from U.S. capital, intellectual property, and know-how flowing to countries of concern, particularly the People’s Republic of China.

**What it does** The letter asks Congress to establish at least a notification requirement for U.S. entities’ investments and activities in specified technology sectors in countries of concern. It endorses Amendment No. 931, which the Senate adopted as part of the NDAA process, and points to Executive Order 14105 as an Administration action addressing the same issue. The signatories also urge stronger NDAA language, including prohibitions for some sectors.

**Who it affects** The proposed framework would affect U.S. entities investing in sensitive technology sectors in countries of concern, with the Treasury Department leading an outbound-investment screening program in coordination with Commerce. The letter is addressed to the chairs and ranking members of the Senate and House Armed Services Committees and concerns technology transfers that could support foreign adversaries’ dual-use capabilities.

**Why it matters** The document frames outbound-investment screening as a gap in the existing national-security toolkit, alongside export controls, sanctions, and inbound foreign-investment screening. It argues that collecting information on outbound capital and know-how would help the United States identify vulnerabilities and prevent sensitive technologies from being ceded to adversaries.

**Key dates and numbers**
- November 20, 2023: date printed on the letter.
- July 25, 2023: Senate vote on Amendment No. 931, reported as 91–6.
- August 9, 2023: President issued Executive Order 14105.
- 2024: NDAA to which the letter asks Congress to add robust outbound-investment language.
