The House Select Committee on the CCP urges Commerce to apply antidumping cost rules more broadly and rigorously to address price distortions attributed to Chinese industrial overcapacity and dumping.

**What it does** The October 23, 2024 letter to Secretary Gina Raimondo argues that 19 U.S.C. § 1677b(f)(1)(A) permits Commerce to adjust foreign producers’ reported costs when they do not reasonably reflect production and sale costs. Using the epoxy resin industry as a case study, it identifies six indicators of overcapacity and market harm: falling capacity utilization, overinvestment in new capacity, increasing reliance on exports, falling domestic and export-market prices, and shrinking profit margins. It also points to related concerns involving epichlorohydrin and bisphenol A.

**Who it affects** The requested action would affect Commerce’s antidumping investigations and foreign producers from the PRC and other countries whose costs, prices, or production decisions may be influenced by subsidization and overcapacity. U.S. epoxy resin manufacturers and workers are presented as the immediate domestic stakeholders.

**Why it matters** The letter frames Chinese overcapacity as a broader supply-chain and trade-remedy problem, contending that underreported or distorted costs can weaken antidumping enforcement and disadvantage U.S. industry. It asks Commerce to use its statutory authority and act swiftly, but does not itself impose a regulatory measure.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated October 23, 2024; published November 4, 2024.
- Foreign producers’ capacity utilization fell from 78.4% in 2021 to 71.4% in 2023.
- Capacity increased from 2.65 billion pounds in 2021 to more than 3.23 billion projected in 2025.
- PRC epoxy-resin import unit values fell 22.2%; imports from all subject countries fell 28%, between 2021 and 2023.
