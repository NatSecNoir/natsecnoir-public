The hearing examined the Biden administration’s strategy toward the People’s Republic of China, including its use of diplomacy, alliances, military deterrence, export controls, and domestic investment to manage strategic competition and prevent conflict.

**What it does** The transcript records testimony from State Assistant Secretary Daniel J. Kritenbrink, Defense Assistant Secretary Ely Ratner, and Commerce Assistant Secretary Thea Rozman Kendler. Witnesses described the administration’s “invest, align, compete” approach, support for Indo-Pacific allies and partners, military force-posture initiatives, communication channels with Beijing, and targeted controls on sensitive technologies and entities. Discussion also covered Huawei, the BIS Entity List, advanced-computing controls, fentanyl precursor chemicals, critical minerals, supply-chain resilience, Taiwan, Pacific Island states, and China’s influence in international organizations.

**Who it affects** The hearing is directed to Congress and executive-branch agencies responsible for foreign policy, defense, trade, export administration, sanctions, and industrial policy. Its subjects include U.S. technology exporters, Chinese entities subject to controls, Indo-Pacific allies and partners, Taiwan, and companies or sectors tied to critical technologies and supply chains.

**Why it matters** The exchange highlights a policy tension between engagement to reduce miscalculation and stronger restrictions intended to protect national security. Members repeatedly questioned whether the administration had delayed sanctions or export controls, while witnesses defended calibrated measures, alliance-building, and continued commercial trade that does not harm national security.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Hearing held July 20, 2023; questions for the record were due July 27, 2023.
- Kendler testified that 237 of 772 China parties on the BIS Entity List were added during the administration.
- The hearing discussed a $382 billion U.S. trade deficit with China in 2022 and more than $37 billion requested for nuclear-triad modernization.
