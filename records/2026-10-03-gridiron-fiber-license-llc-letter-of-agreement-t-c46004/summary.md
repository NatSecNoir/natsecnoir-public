The Letter of Assurances requires Lumos Networks Corp. to implement national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety safeguards as a condition of the FCC’s approval of its indirect-control transfer applications.

**What it does** The LOA establishes a compliance framework for Lumos’s domestic communications infrastructure and principal network equipment. It requires Lumos to designate a U.S.-citizen Security Officer, submit equipment and vendor lists for government review, provide advance notice of material equipment or vendor changes, and adopt NIST-compliant cybersecurity, physical-security, personnel-screening, and access-control measures. Lumos must report specified incidents and breaches, train relevant personnel, submit annual compliance reports, and permit reasonable on-site compliance reviews. It also bars Lumos from monitoring, collecting, marketing, or selling data from U.S. Government customers for commercial purposes.

**Who it affects** The commitments bind Lumos and its successors, assigns, subsidiaries, and affiliates, and apply to personnel, contractors, vendors, managed service providers, and others with access to Lumos infrastructure or sensitive communications and customer data. DHS, DOJ, and DoD—the “USG Parties”—receive notice, review, approval, consultation, and visitation rights.

**Why it matters** This is a Team Telecom mitigation instrument linking operational controls to FCC licensing. A material breach may lead the USG Parties to ask the FCC to modify, condition, revoke, cancel, terminate, or nullify relevant authorizations, while the agreement addresses both network security and government access to communications and transactional data.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Executed November 7, 2017; cited applications: ITC-T/C-20170302-00027 and WC Docket No. 17-60.
- Most initial appointments, lists, and notices use 30 business-day periods; security-policy submissions and written instructions are due within 60 business days.
- Reportable incidents must be reported in writing within 10 business days of Lumos’s reasonable determination; annual reports are due on each execution anniversary.
