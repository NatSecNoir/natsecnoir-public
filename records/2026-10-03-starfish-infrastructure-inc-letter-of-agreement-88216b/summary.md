The Committee for the Assessment of Foreign Participation in the United States Telecommunications Services Sector requires Starfish Infrastructure Inc. and Google LLC to comply with a national security agreement as a condition of the proposed FCC license for the Proa submarine cable system.

**What it does** This agreement establishes mitigation, oversight, and reporting obligations for Proa, a non-common-carrier fiber-optic cable connecting Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands, and Japan. Starfish must appoint U.S.-qualified security and compliance officers; provide cable-system, covered-third-party, principal-equipment, and service-provider information; obtain Committee non-objection for specified equipment, providers, personnel, and operational changes; and maintain physical, logical, supply-chain, and personnel-security controls. It also requires incident reporting, annual compliance reports, possible third-party audits, and access for Committee facility reviews. The Committee will petition the FCC to condition the cable landing license on compliance.

**Who it affects** The principal obligations apply to Starfish and, in specified respects, Google, their affiliates, service providers, equipment vendors, covered third parties, and personnel with access to Proa systems, facilities, communications, or U.S. records. DHS, DOJ, and DoD serve as the compliance monitoring agencies.

**Why it matters** The agreement operationalizes Team Telecom mitigation for a submarine cable license and gives the monitoring agencies continuing visibility and objection authority over infrastructure, foreign access, vendors, and changes that could affect national security or law-enforcement interests. A breach may support a recommendation that the FCC modify, condition, revoke, cancel, or terminate the license.

**Key dates and numbers**
- FCC referral: November 24, 2024.
- Starfish and Google signatures: June 26, 2025; DHS and DoD signatures: July 10, 2025; DOJ signature: July 16, 2025.
- Initial security-officer, compliance-POC, system-information, equipment, and service-provider submissions generally are due within 30–90 days of the NSA Effective Date.
- Companies must report covered security incidents to the CMAs within 48 hours; service providers must report breaches within 72 hours.
- The agreement automatically terminates if Starfish does not receive the FCC license.
