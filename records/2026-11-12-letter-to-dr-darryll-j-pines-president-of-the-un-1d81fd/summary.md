The House Select Committee on the CCP requests that University of Maryland President Darryll J. Pines disclose how the university oversees Chinese national students, their funding and research, and possible national-security risks associated with their participation in STEM programs.

**What it does** The March 18, 2025 letter asks for records concerning the prior universities and research affiliations of Chinese national students, tuition and program funding, their research and laboratory participation, and country-by-country applicant, admission, and enrollment data. It also poses questions about the percentage of graduate students and tuition revenue attributable to Chinese nationals; participation in federally funded research; safeguards involving government-funded projects; monitoring of military or dual-use research; China-related academic ties; talent-program or government funding disclosures; export-controlled coursework; post-graduation destinations; and screening for sensitive programs.

**Who it affects** The immediate recipient is the University of Maryland, with President Pines asked to provide written responses. The requests concern university administrators, faculty, laboratories, federally funded research programs, and Chinese nationals enrolled in undergraduate, graduate, and PhD STEM programs.

**Why it matters** The letter frames foreign-student enrollment and academic research access as potential channels for technology transfer, intellectual-property theft, and support for China’s military and technological objectives. It seeks institutional transparency and oversight rather than imposing a regulatory restriction in this document.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated March 18, 2025; known catalogue date: March 19, 2025.
- Responses requested no later than April 1, 2025.
- Six requests for information and 14 additional questions.
- Topics include AI, quantum computing, semiconductors, aerospace, and robotics.
