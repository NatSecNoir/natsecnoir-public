The House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party and House Transportation and Infrastructure Committee leaders urged the Federal Maritime Commission to promptly begin a rulemaking for shipping exchanges to promote competition in global shipping markets.

**What it does** The letter asks the FMC to accelerate work required by the Ocean Shipping Reform Act of 2022, which made shipping exchanges regulated entities and directed the Commission to begin developing rules for them. The authors argue that waiting until the statutory deadline would allow market concentration to worsen. They identify the Shanghai Shipping Exchange as a state-owned exchange jointly owned by China’s Ministry of Transport and the Shanghai Municipal People’s Government, and say it has become influential in regional freight rates and indexes.

**Who it affects** The requested action would affect the FMC, shipping exchanges, American shippers, competing exchanges, and participants in global maritime commerce. The letter was signed by leaders and members of both congressional committees, including Mike Gallagher, Raja Krishnamoorthi, Dusty Johnson, Sam Graves, Rick Larsen, and John Garamendi.

**Why it matters** The letter frames Chinese state-linked influence over shipping exchanges as a competition and supply-chain concern. It warns that the Shanghai exchange’s growing role and rising contract rates could enable a PRC-linked monopoly if the United States does not develop a competing regulatory framework and market alternatives.

**Key dates and numbers**
- February 1, 2024: Letter date.
- June 2022: Ocean Shipping Reform Act of 2022 became law.
- Three years: The period Congress gave the FMC to begin the shipping-exchange rulemaking.
- 35 percent: Increase in the Shanghai Shipping Exchange’s contract rates over the preceding year, according to the letter.
