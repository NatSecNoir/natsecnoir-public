The House Select Committee on the CCP asks Commerce to investigate PRC silicon-photonics entities, consider adding silicon-photonics equipment and products to the Commerce Control List, and strengthen U.S. innovation and national-security protections.

**What it does** The October 27, 2024 letter describes silicon photonics as an emerging semiconductor technology that uses light to transmit information and could improve computing bandwidth, energy efficiency, and speed. It requests that Commerce assess leading PRC photonics companies, particularly firms involved in developing equipment for a second photonic-chip production line, and evaluate whether existing export controls adequately cover photonic equipment, photonic chips, and hybrid electronic-photonic chips. The signatories also ask Commerce to consider resources and talent needed for this work and to support domestic photonics competitiveness.

**Who it affects** The requested measures would affect Commerce, U.S. technology and semiconductor companies, domestic photonics researchers, and PRC entities involved in photonics research, production, or military-relevant applications. The letter specifically discusses Huawei, the Nanjing Electronic Devices Institute (also known as the 55th Research Institute of China Electronics Technology Group Corporation), and SinTone. Huawei and the Nanjing institute are identified as Entity List entries.

**Why it matters** The committee argues that photonics may become a critical chokepoint in future semiconductor supply chains as conventional electronic chips approach physical limits. It warns that existing controls may not expressly cover important photonic products and that PRC investment and state-backed research could enable military diversion or technological leapfrogging.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated October 27, 2024; catalogue date is October 28, 2024.
- Requests a staff briefing by December 1, 2024.
- Cites possible photonic-chip computational improvements of up to 1,000-fold over existing electronic designs.
- Identifies ECCN 6A005 as potentially covering only a narrow subset of photonics equipment.
- Notes the October 7, 2022 export-control rules as an existing control framework.
