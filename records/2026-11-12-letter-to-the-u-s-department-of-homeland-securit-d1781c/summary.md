The three congressional committees request information from the Department of Homeland Security about foreign exploitation of the Small Business Innovation Research (SBIR) and Small Business Technology Transfer (STTR) programs and seek stronger safeguards against related national-security risks.

**What it does** The letter asserts that China may be obtaining taxpayer-funded U.S. technology through CCP-linked venture capital, university research partnerships, talent-recruitment programs, PRC-based subsidiaries, and state-backed investment in SBIR/STTR recipients. It cites a 2021 Department of Defense-commissioned report finding that DoD SBIR programs lacked adequate due-diligence capabilities. The committees ask DHS for application success and flagging rates, security-risk breakdowns, disclosure requirements, flagging criteria, post-flag procedures, interagency communication procedures, and monitoring procedures.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to DHS Secretary Kristi L. Noem and concerns DHS’s participation in SBIR/STTR programs, American small businesses receiving program funds, federal agencies administering or coordinating reviews, and foreign-linked entities alleged to seek access to U.S. research and intellectual property.

**Why it matters** The letter frames SBIR/STTR oversight as both an innovation-policy and national-security issue. It argues that inadequate screening could allow sensitive technology and intellectual property to move to China and asks DHS to support legislative and policy changes before the programs’ authorization expires.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated February 26, 2025; response requested by March 12, 2025.
- Requested data covers January 20, 2021, onward.
- SBIR/STTR authorization was stated to expire September 30, 2025.
- The letter requests breakdowns across Phases I, II, and III and identifies 99.9 percent as the share of U.S. businesses that are small businesses.
