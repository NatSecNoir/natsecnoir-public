AT&T Corp. undertakes a set of national-security and law-enforcement mitigation commitments to DHS and DoD in connection with its FCC application to continue operating the St. Thomas–St. Croix Cable System (STSC) for an additional 25-year term.

**What it does** The Letter of Agreement (LOA) requires AT&T to maintain an approved U.S.-citizen security point of contact; provide cable-system, principal-equipment, vendor, and operational information; preserve the ability to interrupt STSC traffic in the United States or its territories; and provide advance notice of specified equipment, vendor, testing, service, and operational changes. It also requires logical and physical security measures, personnel screening, incident reporting, employee instruction, annual compliance reports, and cooperation with third-party audits and government site visits. Deployment of distributed acoustic sensing technology on the marine portion of STSC requires DoD approval. A breach may support a recommendation that the FCC modify, condition, revoke, cancel, terminate, or invalidate a relevant authorization.

**Who it affects** The obligations bind AT&T and its successors, assigns, subsidiaries, and affiliates, and cover personnel, contractors, vendors, service providers, principal equipment, U.S. records, domestic communications, and the U.S. portions of STSC.

**Why it matters** The LOA illustrates Team Telecom mitigation attached to a submarine-cable licensing matter, using continuing government oversight, supply-chain visibility, access controls, reporting, and approval or objection rights to address national-security and law-enforcement risks.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Executed and signed January 9, 2023; STSC was originally licensed May 8, 1996, and entered service May 1, 1997.
- The FCC matter is SCL-LIC-20220114-00004 (TT #22-010); the prior STSC license is SCL-LIC-19940815-00003.
- Notice periods generally include 30 days for equipment, vendor, and operational changes and 45 days for new-equipment testing or proposed dedicated NOC changes.
- AT&T must report covered incidents within 48 hours; third-party disclosures are subject to reasonable-efforts requirements of no more than 72 hours.
