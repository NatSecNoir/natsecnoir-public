The chairmen of three House committees ask Secretary of State Marco Rubio, in consultation with the Attorney General, to determine whether Chinese Students and Scholars Association (CSSA) branches qualify for designation as foreign missions under the Foreign Missions Act.

**What it does** This March 5, 2026 letter presents evidence that CSSA branches on U.S. college campuses receive direction, funding, and support from Chinese diplomatic and United Front-linked bodies. It requests a formal determination, detailed justification, and supporting analysis on whether the branches are subject to the Act’s disclosure and other statutory requirements, with the response provided to the relevant House committees.

**Who it affects** The request directly concerns CSSA chapters, including the Southwest Chinese Students and Scholars Association, and the Department of State’s Office of Foreign Missions. It also implicates the PRC Ministry of Education, the Chinese Communist Party’s United Front Work Department, Chinese embassies and consulates, U.S. universities, and Chinese students studying in the United States.

**Why it matters** The letter frames campus organizations as potential vehicles for foreign influence, surveillance, intimidation, talent recruitment, and transnational repression. It argues that applying the Foreign Missions Act could improve transparency regarding PRC funding, coordination, and direction while addressing national-security and academic-freedom concerns.

**Key dates and numbers**
- March 5, 2026: letter date.
- 1982: Foreign Missions Act enacted.
- 22 PRC entities reportedly designated as foreign missions by 2024.
- More than 277,000 Chinese students studied in U.S. undergraduate and graduate programs during 2023–2024; approximately 200 CSSA chapters operate on U.S. campuses.
- $6,000: reported annual transfer from a PRC consulate to the University of Pittsburgh CSSA president.
