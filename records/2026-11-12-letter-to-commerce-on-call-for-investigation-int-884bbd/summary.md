The House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party asks the Department of Commerce to investigate TP-Link and its affiliates under the department’s ICTS authorities and, if warranted, impose measures to mitigate national-security risks associated with the company’s Wi-Fi routers and related devices.

**What it does** The letter cites TP-Link’s Chinese ownership and operations, reported U.S. market presence, known router vulnerabilities, and alleged connections between PRC-sponsored cyber activity and compromised SOHO routers. It invokes Executive Order 13873 and requests a Commerce assessment of TP-Link’s national-security risks and whether ICTS authorities are appropriate to address them.

**Who it affects** The request directly concerns TP-Link Technologies Co., Ltd. and its affiliates, Commerce, and U.S. users of TP-Link routers and mesh-network products. It also highlights potential exposure for military personnel and families because TP-Link devices were sold through Army, Air Force, and Navy exchange stores, and for critical infrastructure targeted by groups such as Volt Typhoon.

**Why it matters** The letter frames widely deployed consumer networking equipment as a possible entry point for state-sponsored espionage or disruption. It links product vulnerabilities and PRC legal obligations to broader concerns about supply-chain security, federal procurement, and the security of U.S. communications and critical infrastructure, while making a policy request rather than announcing a Commerce finding or restriction.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated August 13, 2024; the known catalogue date is August 15, 2024.
- Requests a Commerce response by August 30, 2024.
- Cites TP-Link’s reported sales of more than 160 million products annually in more than 170 countries as of 2022.
- Reports 31 TP-Link devices listed by the Army & Air Force Exchange Service and 17 by the Navy Exchange as of August 2024.
- References Executive Order 13873 and a January 2024 DOJ operation involving hundreds of routers.
