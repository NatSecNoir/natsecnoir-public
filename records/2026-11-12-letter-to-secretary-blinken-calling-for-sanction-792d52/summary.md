The House Select Committee on the CCP and the Congressional-Executive Commission on China urge Secretary of State Antony Blinken to determine whether seven Hong Kong and Chinese security officials qualify for sanctions in response to bounties and arrest warrants targeting overseas activists.

**What it does** The December 19, 2023 letter asks the administration to act under the Hong Kong Human Rights and Democracy Act, the Hong Kong Autonomy Act, and Executive Order 13936. It requests a determination on sanctions eligibility for Hong Kong Secretary for Justice Paul Lam Ting-kwok; Commissioner of Police Raymond Siu Chak-yee; Assistant Commissioners Margaret Chiu Wing-lan and Dick Wong Chung-chun; Senior Superintendent Bruce Hung Ngan; Director of Public Prosecutions Maggie Yang Mei-kei; and Dong Jingwei, Director of the Office for Safeguarding National Security. The signatories also ask the administration to work with Congress on transnational repression.

**Who it affects** The request concerns Hong Kong and Chinese security officials, two of whom the letter says are connected to bounties targeting a U.S. citizen and a U.S. resident. It is addressed to the Secretary of State and copied to the President and Secretary of the Treasury.

**Why it matters** The letter frames the December 14 issuance of five arrest warrants and HK$1 million bounties as an attempt to extend Hong Kong’s national-security enforcement and political repression onto U.S. soil. It invokes existing U.S. authorities intended to hold persons responsible for eroding Hong Kong’s autonomy accountable, but does not itself impose sanctions.

**Key dates and numbers**
- December 14, 2023: Hong Kong authorities issued five arrest warrants and HK$1 million (approximately US$128,000) bounties.
- December 19, 2023: Letter date.
- January 19, 2024: Requested deadline for the sanctions-eligibility determination.
- Seven officials are identified for review; two bounties reportedly concern a U.S. citizen and a U.S. resident.
