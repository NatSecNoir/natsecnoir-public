The lawmakers urge the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) to address national-security risks they associate with Alibaba Group’s planned or potential technology role in the 2028 Los Angeles Olympic Games.

**What it does**
The letter asks Secretary Kristi Noem to arrange a classified briefing for the signers on risks arising from Alibaba’s partnership with the International Olympic Committee (IOC), including possible access to Los Angeles infrastructure, security information, and Olympic systems. It also requests information about efforts to advise the IOC and relevant critical-infrastructure owners and operators, mitigation measures, and options to prevent or remove Alibaba’s access. The lawmakers argue that no PRC-controlled provider should receive an operational role unless robust, demonstrable security controls can be verified.

**Who it affects**
The request is directed to DHS and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI), and concerns Alibaba, the IOC, Los Angeles infrastructure and security systems, and the 2028 Olympic Games. The letter also references Alibaba’s cloud, e-commerce, ticketing, and broadcasting services and its prior Olympic involvement.

**Why it matters**
The signers characterize Alibaba as connected to PRC surveillance, censorship, intelligence, and military interests, and warn of espionage, data exploitation, and foreign influence risks. They cite French concerns about Alibaba’s role in hosting systems for the Paris 2024 Olympics and emphasize Los Angeles’s proximity to military installations, defense contractors, critical infrastructure, and technology firms.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated September 10, 2025.
- Requested classified briefing deadline: no later than September 30, 2025.
- Subject event: 2028 Los Angeles Olympic Games.
- The letter cites Alibaba’s Olympic sponsorship and prior Olympic technology services.
