The House Select Committee on the CCP urges Columbia University to end exchange programs and other ties supported by CCP-linked entities, particularly the China–United States Exchange Foundation (CUSEF), and to review foreign funding of student organizations.

**What it does** The November 4, 2025 letter from Chairman John Moolenaar to Acting President Claire Shipman focuses on the Greater China Initiative, a Columbia-recognized student group affiliated with the Weatherhead East Asian Institute. The letter says GCI was promoting a January 2026 “China Trek” exchange to China co-sponsored by CUSEF, with CUSEF covering transportation, board, and lodging for participating SIPA students. It asks Columbia to terminate ties with CUSEF and adopt vetting rules that exclude funding or partnerships with organizations engaged in malign influence operations.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to Columbia University, its recognized student groups, GCI, the Weatherhead Institute, and SIPA students. It also identifies CUSEF, its founder and chairman emeritus Tung Chee-hwa, and the CPPCC as relevant to the committee’s concerns. The letter references CUSEF relationships with other U.S. educational institutions and UT Austin’s decision to reject its funding.

**Why it matters** The committee frames foreign-funded academic exchanges as potential channels for United Front influence, coercion, and concealment. It argues that CUSEF’s activities can shape views of the PRC, restrict discussion of issues Beijing calls the “Five Poisons,” and compromise academic freedom and institutional integrity.

**Key dates and numbers**
- November 4, 2025: Letter date shown in the document.
- January 2026: Proposed “China Trek” exchange.
- 2005–2023: Tung Chee-hwa’s cited tenure as a CPPCC vice chairman.
- 2023: House Select Committee release of its United Front 101 memorandum.
