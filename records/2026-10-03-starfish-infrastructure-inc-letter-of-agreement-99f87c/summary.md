The Agreement requires Starfish Infrastructure Inc. and Google LLC to implement national-security and law-enforcement safeguards as a condition of Starfish’s proposed Halaihai submarine-cable license.

**What it does** This National Security Agreement (NSA) establishes compliance, security, reporting, and oversight obligations for the Halaihai Cable System, a proposed non-common-carrier fiber-optic system connecting Guam and the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands to French Polynesia and Chile. Starfish must appoint a U.S.-citizen Security Officer and compliance points of contact; provide cable, equipment, service-provider, and covered-third-party information; obtain Committee non-objection for specified equipment, providers, personnel, and changes; protect communications and U.S. records; and report qualifying incidents within 48 hours. The Agreement also provides for annual reporting, on-site reviews, and discretionary third-party audits.

**Who it affects** The primary obligations apply to Starfish, with specified oversight and compliance responsibilities for Google and binding effects on successors, affiliates, service providers, equipment providers, and covered third parties. The Committee is represented by DHS, DOJ, and DoD, which monitor compliance and may object to proposed arrangements.

**Why it matters** The NSA makes national-security mitigation enforceable through the FCC licensing process. It expressly screens principal equipment for foreign-adversary connections and providers appearing on the FCC Covered List or Commerce Entity List, and permits the Committee to recommend license modification, revocation, or other relief for breach.

**Key dates and numbers**
- FCC referral to the Committee: January 24, 2025.
- Starfish and Google signed: July 8, 2025; DHS signed July 10, 2025; the final signature shown is dated July 15, 2025.
- Initial disclosures generally are due within 60 or 90 days of the NSA Effective Date; qualifying incidents must be reported within 48 hours.
- The Agreement automatically terminates if Starfish does not receive the FCC license.
