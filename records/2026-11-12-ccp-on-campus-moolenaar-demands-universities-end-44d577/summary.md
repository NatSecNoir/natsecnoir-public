The House Select Committee on the CCP urged seven U.S. universities to reconsider China Scholarship Council partnerships and provide information about foreign-funded research, student vetting, and possible technology transfer, while presenting a broader staff report on risks from U.S.-PRC academic collaborations.

**What it does** The July 8, 2025 letters to Dartmouth, Temple, UC Davis, UC Irvine, UC Riverside, Notre Dame, and the University of Tennessee request agreements, student-origin and post-graduation data, STEM enrollment information, federal grant details, communications with the Departments of State and Homeland Security, and explanations of how CSC programs serve U.S. interests. Each recipient is asked to respond by July 22, 2025 and preserve related records. The accompanying staff report describes alleged transfers of federally supported expertise through joint institutes, talent programs, and research with PRC defense-linked or blacklisted entities.

**Who it affects** The letters directly affect the seven universities and their CSC-sponsored PRC students, faculty, and research programs. The report also discusses UC Berkeley, Georgia Tech, Tianjin University, Tsinghua University, Sichuan University, Huawei, and other companies and institutions.

**Why it matters** The Committee frames CSC scholarships and joint institutes as potential channels for military-civil fusion, surveillance, and acquisition of dual-use technologies. It calls for stronger research-collaboration guardrails, post-award restrictions, expanded foreign-funding disclosures, and adoption of the DETERRENT Act.

**Key dates and numbers**
- July 8, 2025: letters issued.
- July 22, 2025: requested response deadline.
- Up to 60 CSC-sponsored graduate students annually at Temple; up to 50 at UC Riverside.
- The report identifies 8,829 DOD-funded publications with PRC coauthors and 185 with intelligence-community funding.
