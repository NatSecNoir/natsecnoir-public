The House Select Committee on the CCP urges JPMorgan Chase & Co. to withdraw from underwriting Contemporary Amperex Technology Co., Limited’s planned Hong Kong IPO and demands records and explanations concerning the transaction’s national-security, sanctions, human-rights, and investor risks.

**What it does** The April 17, 2025 letter from Chairman John Moolenaar to JPMorgan Chairman and CEO Jamie Dimon characterizes CATL as a Chinese military company designated by the Department of Defense under Section 1260H of the National Defense Authorization Act. It cites CATL’s battery business, alleged connections to China’s military modernization and the Xinjiang Production and Construction Corps, and reported pursuit of the IPO by JPMorgan and other banks. If JPMorgan would not withdraw, the committee requested detailed responses and documents covering diligence, compliance reviews, communications, risk assessments, investor disclosures, pricing, marketing, government contacts, advisors, and prospectus changes.

**Who it affects** The letter directly addresses JPMorgan and Dimon, while focusing on CATL, its alleged military and sanctioned-entity relationships, U.S. investors, and relevant U.S. government authorities. It also invokes the committee’s investigative authority under H. Res. 5, section 4(a).

**Why it matters** The document links a proposed financial transaction to the DoD’s 1260H designation, possible forced-labor and sanctions concerns, U.S. investment policy, and competition with American battery manufacturing. It illustrates congressional scrutiny of Wall Street financing for Chinese companies viewed as connected to military and human-rights risks.

**Key dates and numbers**
- April 17, 2025: letter date.
- Section 1260H: statutory basis cited for CATL’s DoD designation.
- 21: numbered questions and document requests directed to JPMorgan.

- H. Res. 5, section 4(a): authority cited by the committee.
