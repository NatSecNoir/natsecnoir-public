The House Select Committee on China asks Monumental Sports & Entertainment (MSE) to end its business relationship with Alibaba and provide information about that relationship in light of Alibaba’s designation as a Chinese military company under Section 1260H.

**What it does** The June 30, 2026 letter from Chairman John Moolenaar to MSE Chairman and CEO Ted Leonsis describes Alibaba’s alleged support for China’s military-civil fusion, including cloud and AI services linked to the BeiDou satellite system. It asks MSE to confirm whether it has discontinued its relationship with Alibaba and affiliates, and requests agreements and data showing MSE’s current relationships with Alibaba Group Holding Limited, Alipay.com Co Ltd, and their affiliates.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to MSE and concerns its partnerships with Alibaba and related entities, including Alipay, which the letter says has been implemented throughout Capital One Arena. It also implicates the Washington Wizards and Capitals through MSE’s sports and venue operations, while identifying Alibaba as the national-security concern.

**Why it matters** The letter connects a private sports-and-technology sponsorship relationship to the Department of Defense’s Section 1260H designation of Alibaba as a Chinese military company. It frames Alibaba’s alleged role in military-civil fusion and BeiDou-enabled positioning, navigation, and timing as a threat to U.S. security and requests corporate records to assess MSE’s ongoing ties.

**Key dates and numbers**
- June 8, 2026: Department of Defense date cited for Alibaba’s Section 1260H designation.
- June 30, 2026: Letter date.
- July 15, 2026: Deadline for MSE to provide the requested information.
- Two categories of information requested: agreements and data sufficient to show current relationships.
