The four committee chairmen ask nine American universities for information about foreign financial gifts and research collaborations involving countries of concern, citing risks to sensitive military research, technology, and intellectual property.

**What it does**
Letters from Chairman John Moolenaar, Senator Bill Cassidy, Chairman Tim Walberg, and Senator Jim Banks request information from Bryant University, the University of Texas at Arlington, the University of Arizona, Georgia Institute of Technology, the University of California–Berkeley, the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, Portland State University, the University of Minnesota–Twin Cities, and the University of California–Irvine. The requests follow a new U.S. Department of Education foreign funding report and portal concerning foreign entities aligned with the Chinese Communist Party and other adversaries.

**Who it affects**
The immediate recipients are the nine named universities. The document also concerns foreign entities tied to U.S. adversaries, university research partnerships, financial gifts, donations, and research with potential military applications.

**Why it matters**
The lawmakers warn that foreign funding and collaborations could provide adversaries access to sensitive research and technology. They cite prior Select Committee reporting on theft of military research and intellectual property, including a Chinese Academy of Engineering account crediting a U.S. professor’s collaboration with a Chinese institution for helping develop materials and technologies for advanced defense weapons and equipment.

**Key dates and numbers**
- May 16, 2026: date of the letters and publication.
- Nine: universities receiving letters.
- 34: institutions identified by the Department of Education portal as receiving covered foreign funding.
- Over $183 million: reported funding from foreign entities tied to American adversaries, including the CCP.
