The House Select Committee on Strategic Competition between the United States and the Chinese Communist Party and the House Committee on Oversight and Accountability requested USPS records concerning inbound mail and de minimis shipments from China.

**What it does** The June 28, 2023 letter asks Postmaster General Louis DeJoy to provide available USPS data on mail from the People’s Republic of China (PRC), including duties, revenues, pieces, and weights for fiscal years 2021 and 2022. It also requests data specifically concerning PRC de minimis shipments, the total dollar value of terminal dues and inward land rates assessed to the PRC, and the complete terminal dues schedule for every country.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to USPS and concerns Chinese companies, PRC-origin shipments, U.S. consumers, and agencies involved in import oversight, including U.S. Customs and Border Protection. The committees ask USPS to respond by July 19, 2023.

**Why it matters** The committees said shipments valued below $800 generally enter the United States without duties, taxes, and some reporting requirements, and expressed concern that companies may divide larger shipments to use the threshold. They argued that publicly available CBP information does not capture USPS shipments by country and that USPS data are needed to assess shipment volume and value, potential tariff avoidance, and related economic and national-security implications.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated June 28, 2023; requested response by July 19, 2023.
- Requests cover FY 2021 and FY 2022 records and data.
- De minimis threshold identified as less than $800.
- Authorities cited include H. Res. 11 and House Rule X.
