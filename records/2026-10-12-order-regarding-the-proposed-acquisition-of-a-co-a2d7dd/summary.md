The President prohibited the proposed acquisition of Aixtron’s U.S. business by Grand Chip Investment GmbH and affiliated purchasers because it might threaten U.S. national security.

**What it does** This order, issued under section 721 of the Defense Production Act, bars the proposed acquisition of Aixtron US and any substantially equivalent transaction carried out directly or indirectly through the purchasers’ shareholders, partners, subsidiaries, or affiliates. It requires the purchasers and Aixtron to take the steps necessary to fully and permanently abandon the transaction, certify completion to CFIUS, and submit weekly compliance certifications until termination is certified. Transactions or devices intended to avoid or circumvent the order are also prohibited, and the Attorney General is authorized to enforce it.

**Who it affects** The order applies to Grand Chip, its Luxembourg parent Grand Chip Investment S.a.r.l., Fujian Grand Chip Investment Fund LP, Fujian Grand’s partner Zhendong Liu, Xiamen Bohao Investment Co. Ltd., Aixtron SE, and AIXTRON, Inc. It concerns the U.S. business of Aixtron, including AIXTRON, Inc., related equity interests, and specified U.S. intellectual-property interests.

**Why it matters** This is a direct presidential intervention in a foreign acquisition reviewed under CFIUS authority, finding that other legal authorities were inadequate to protect national security in the matter. It illustrates the use of section 721 to block a transaction and require unwind-related compliance measures.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Adopted December 2, 2016; published December 7, 2016, at 81 FR 88607.
- Abandonment deadline: 30 days after the order, unless CFIUS extends it by up to 90 days.
- Statutory authority: section 721 of the Defense Production Act, 50 U.S.C. 4565.
