The House Select Committee on the CCP asks Secretary of Commerce Gina Raimondo to address national-security and economic risks it associates with the PRC’s growing role in RISC-V chip-design architecture and to consider export-control and supply-chain authorities.

**What it does** This letter describes RISC-V as an open-source instruction set architecture that can be used to develop custom processors without royalties or licensing fees. It argues that PRC participation in RISC-V could help Chinese firms circumvent U.S. export controls, acquire U.S. semiconductor expertise, and advance technological self-reliance. The members ask the Administration to explain its strategy, assess the security implications of RISC-V, work with U.S. companies, and consider authorities under the Export Control Reform Act of 2018 and Executive Order 14017. They request written answers to five questions.

**Who it affects** The inquiry is directed to the Department of Commerce and potentially affects U.S. persons, semiconductor and chip-design companies, RISC-V participants, and agencies responsible for export controls and supply-chain security. It specifically mentions Biren, HiSilicon Technologies, and the National University of Defense Technology as PRC-linked or Entity List-related examples.

**Why it matters** The letter frames open-source chip architecture as a potential channel for transferring U.S. technical know-how to the PRC and links RISC-V dominance to cybersecurity risks involving the Internet of Things and critical infrastructure. It seeks administrative action rather than announcing a new restriction.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated November 1, 2023; the known catalogue date is November 2, 2023.
- Responses requested by December 1, 2023.
- The letter cites a report that the PRC represented 50% of globally shipped RISC-V chips in 2022.
- Authorities discussed include the Export Control Reform Act of 2018 and Executive Order 14017.
