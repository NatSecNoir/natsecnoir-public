Telxius Cable USA commits to national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety mitigation measures for the MAREA submarine cable system in support of FCC authorization.

**What it does** The Letter of Assurances requires Telxius, formerly Telefónica International Wholesale Services USA, to provide U.S. government parties with designated security contacts, detailed cable-system and equipment information, vendor and contractor disclosures, security policies, personnel screening, incident reports, annual compliance reports, and access for consultations, site visits, and possible third-party audits. It also requires operational capability to interrupt U.S. traffic, advance notice of material equipment or vendor changes, notice of significant service changes, and reporting of certain foreign ownership or control changes. The commitments apply to Telxius and its successors, assigns, subsidiaries, and affiliates.

**Who it affects** The commitments govern Telxius’s operation of the U.S. portion of the private, non-common-carrier MAREA Cable System linking the United States and Spain. They support the FCC application submitted by Edge Cable Holdings USA, Microsoft Infrastructure Group, and Telefónica entities. DHS and DoD are the USG Parties; the FCC is the licensing authority.

**Why it matters** The LOA shows Team Telecom mitigation being used as a condition supporting approval of an international submarine-cable landing authorization. Breach could lead the USG Parties to request that the FCC modify, condition, revoke, cancel, terminate, or nullify a relevant authorization.
