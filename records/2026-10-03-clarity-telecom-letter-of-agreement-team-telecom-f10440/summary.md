The U.S. Department of Justice advises the FCC that it has no objection to approving the specified transfers of control of international Section 214 authorizations, provided the approvals are conditioned on Clarity Telecom and NTS, Inc.’s compliance with an August 16, 2019 Letter of Agreement (LOA).

**What it does**
DOJ submits a petition under FCC Rule 1.41 in the Section 214 transfer proceedings. It identifies the LOA as the basis for its no-objection recommendation and asks the Commission to make compliance with that agreement a condition of its consent. DOJ explains that the Executive Branch review, led by DOJ with DoD and DHS as Team Telecom, addresses national-security and law-enforcement concerns connected to foreign ownership. The petition does not resolve the FCC’s separate character-qualification analysis.

**Who it affects**
The filing concerns Clarity Telecom, LLC DBA Vast Broadband; T3 North Intermediate Holdings, Python Intermediate II, and PC III CTH Holdings; and NTS, Inc. and its identified subsidiaries, including NTS Communications. The authorizations and licenses at issue are international Section 214 authorities being transferred in the named proceedings.

**Why it matters**
The petition illustrates Team Telecom mitigation in an FCC transaction review. DOJ specifically discusses NTS-Comm’s 2002 mail-fraud guilty plea, five-year probation, and $22 million restitution, while deferring to the FCC on character qualifications. Its recommendation shows how national-security and law-enforcement concerns can be addressed through enforceable conditions rather than opposition to the transaction.

**Key dates and numbers**
- August 16, 2019: date of the LOA on which DOJ’s recommendation is conditioned.
- August 19, 2019: petition date.
- March 2002: NTS-Comm pleaded guilty to mail fraud; it received five years’ probation and paid $22 million in restitution.
- Proceedings include five FCC IBFS transfer identifiers and WC Docket Nos. 19-7 and 19-16.
- Relevant authorities include Communications Act Section 214(a) and FCC Rules 1.41 and 63.18.
