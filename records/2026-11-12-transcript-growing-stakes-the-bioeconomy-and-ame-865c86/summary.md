The House Select Committee on the CCP’s March 7, 2024 hearing examined biotechnology as an emerging national-security, economic-security, supply-chain, biosecurity, and ethical-competition issue in the United States–China relationship.

**What it does** The transcript records opening statements, expert testimony, and member questions about China’s biotechnology strategy; genetic-data collection; AI models trained on DNA; biomanufacturing; pharmaceutical and agricultural supply chains; research security; export controls; privacy; and potential military, police, and surveillance applications. Witnesses proposed stronger domestic and allied manufacturing capacity, secure and accessible biological databases, standardized data and manufacturing interfaces, improved cyber-biosecurity, research-security screening, and updated agricultural biotechnology regulation. Members also discussed the BIOSECURE Act, NSPM-33, CFIUS, the CHIPS and Science Act, BARDA, and the Commerce Department’s Entity List.

**Who it affects** The discussion is directed to Congress, federal agencies, universities, laboratories, biotechnology and pharmaceutical companies, agricultural innovators, investors, allies, and researchers handling genetic, clinical, or other biological data. Companies and institutions named include Ginkgo Bioworks, BGI Group, WuXi AppTec, MITRE, In-Q-Tel, Illumina, and Neuralink.

**Why it matters** Witnesses characterized biotechnology and biomanufacturing as foundational to future health, food, energy, materials, and national-security capabilities. The hearing argues that dependence on China for manufacturing and data, combined with AI-enabled biological design and weak research and data safeguards, could create strategic vulnerabilities, while emphasizing that U.S. leadership requires investment, standards, trust, and careful biosecurity rather than speculation alone.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Hearing held March 7, 2024; questions for the record were due one week later.
- Witnesses cited China’s 17 AI-bio hubs and BGI’s prenatal testing data from 8 million women.
- The transcript compares U.S. or North American biologics manufacturing shares of 35% in 2018 and 30% in 2022 with China’s increase from 7% to 15%.
