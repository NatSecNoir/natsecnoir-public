The United States asks the District Court for the District of Columbia to enforce the President’s July 8, 2025 order prohibiting Suirui’s acquisition of Jupiter Systems and requiring divestment of its equity and assets.

**What it does** The complaint alleges that Suirui International acquired all equity interests in Jupiter Systems on February 28, 2020, without submitting a CFIUS notice. After CFIUS identified national-security risks involving Jupiter products integrated into military and critical-infrastructure systems, the President prohibited the transaction under Section 721 of the Defense Production Act, 50 U.S.C. § 4565, and ordered divestment. The United States seeks declarations, injunctions, compelled divestiture, transfer of the assets to a third-party fiduciary pending a compliant transaction, costs, and other relief.

**Who it affects** The defendants are Suirui Group, its Hong Kong subsidiary Suirui International, and Jupiter Systems. The requested relief would end Suirui’s ownership and control of Jupiter and restrict Jupiter’s interests in certain Jupiter Asia Companies assets and operations.

**Why it matters** The filing illustrates judicial enforcement of a presidential CFIUS action against a completed foreign acquisition after mitigation and voluntary abandonment efforts failed. It alleges that the defendants missed an extended February 3, 2026 divestment deadline and continued to hold Jupiter equity and assets when the complaint was filed.

**Key dates and numbers**
- February 28, 2020: Suirui International acquired Jupiter Systems.
- April 7, 2025: Treasury initiated a formal CFIUS review.
- July 8, 2025: President signed the prohibition and divestment order.
- 120 days: Original divestment period, later extended to February 3, 2026.
- February 9, 2026: Complaint filed, D.D.C. No. 1:26-cv-00369.
