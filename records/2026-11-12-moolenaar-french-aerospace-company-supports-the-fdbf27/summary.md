The House Select Committee on the CCP asks Secretary of War Pete Hegseth to brief the Committee on French aerospace companies’ activities in China and their potential contribution to Chinese military capabilities, focusing on Safran and, previously, Airbus.

**What it does** The March 19, 2026 letter supplements a December 19, 2025 letter about Airbus by outlining Safran’s joint ventures, manufacturing, engine development, component sales, training, and research relationships in China. It identifies Safran ties to AVIC subsidiaries and other Chinese aerospace entities, including work involving C919 electrical wiring, jet-engine nacelle components, the WZ-16 helicopter engine, and AC313 systems. The Committee requests information from the Department of War and offers the material to inform policy concerning U.S. defense contractors and China-related relationships.

**Who it affects** The request concerns Safran, Airbus, their Chinese partners, and U.S. national-security officials responsible for the defense industrial base. It also discusses AVIC, XAC, SAM, COMAC, AECC, Avicopter, Beihang University, and Russian defense-linked entities.

**Why it matters** The Committee argues that ostensibly civilian aerospace technology, expertise, and institutional partnerships can be repurposed for military use and support China’s military-civil fusion strategy. It highlights U.S. designations or references involving AVIC, XAC, SAM, and Beihang University, and raises possible indirect benefits to Russian defense companies.

**Key dates and numbers**
- December 19, 2025: Airbus letter requesting a Department briefing.
- March 19, 2026: Safran letter, seven pages.
- 2011–2012: Safran joint ventures with XAC and SAM described in the letter.
- 2005: Safran’s Beijing engineering-school partnership began.
