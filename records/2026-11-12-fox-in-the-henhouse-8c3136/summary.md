The report holds that fragmented Department of Defense (DOD) research-security policies and weak due diligence have allowed taxpayer-funded research to proceed with Chinese military-, defense-, talent-program-, surveillance-, and human-rights-linked entities, creating risks of technology diversion and adversary military advancement.

**What it does** The House Select Committee on the CCP reviews approximately 1,400 publications from June 2023 through June 2025 that acknowledged DOD funding or support and involved PRC entities; it says more than 700 involved organizations affiliated with China’s defense research and industrial base. Case studies cover hypersonics, quantum technologies, semiconductors, advanced materials, cyber capabilities, genomics, autonomous systems, and nuclear-related research. The report criticizes the DOD R&E risk matrix, inconsistent component practices, limited post-award monitoring, inadequate data sharing, and failure to treat entities on the 1260H List, BIS Entity List, or other restricted lists as categorically prohibited.

**Who it affects** The findings concern DOD-funded universities, researchers, laboratories, grant offices, and research-security personnel, as well as collaborations with Chinese universities, laboratories, companies, talent programs, and state-directed research institutions.

**Why it matters** The report argues that “fundamental” and openly published research can still provide military value through access to laboratories, data, methods, unpublished findings, and technical expertise. It recommends broader prohibitions, standardized training and databases, cross-component sharing, universal monitoring of mitigated awards, and passage of the proposed SAFE Research Act, including enhanced disclosures and restrictions on covered partnerships.

**Key dates and numbers**
- June 2023–June 2025: publication period analyzed.
- Approximately 1,400 publications reviewed; more than 700 reportedly involved China’s defense research and industrial base.
- January 2025: FY2025 NDAA provisions cited as prohibiting DOD-funded collaboration with entities on the Section 1286 List.
- September 5, 2025: report date.
