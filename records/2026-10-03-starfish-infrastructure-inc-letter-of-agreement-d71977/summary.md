The Committee and Starfish Infrastructure, Inc. and Google LLC establish a national-security mitigation agreement for the proposed Taihei non-common-carrier submarine cable connecting the United States and Japan, with compliance to be made a condition of the FCC cable-landing license.

**What it does** The agreement requires Starfish to maintain a U.S.-citizen Security Officer, compliance points of contact, detailed inventories of cable facilities, covered third parties, principal equipment, and service providers, and policies for logical security, physical security, personnel screening, incident response, and employee training. It gives the Compliance Monitoring Agencies (DHS, DOJ, and DoD) advance-notice, objection, inspection, reporting, and audit rights. Starfish must be able to interrupt U.S.-bound or U.S.-originating traffic, obtain prior non-objection for specified equipment, providers, testing, and foreign unescorted access, and report qualifying incidents within 48 hours.

**Who it affects** The principal obligations apply to Starfish and, where specified, Google, their affiliates, contractors, service providers, equipment vendors, covered third parties, and personnel with access to Taihei, its network operations centers, principal equipment, domestic communications, or U.S. records. The CMAs receive the required submissions and may object to people, equipment, providers, policies, and auditors.

**Why it matters** This is a Team Telecom mitigation framework linking national-security controls to authorization of a U.S.-connected submarine cable. Breach may lead the Committee to recommend that the FCC modify, condition, revoke, cancel, terminate, or invalidate the relevant license or authorization.
