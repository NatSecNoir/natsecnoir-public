The hearing examined how the United States can preserve leadership in critical and emerging technologies amid strategic competition with the People’s Republic of China, emphasizing investment, talent, security, and allied cooperation rather than relying only on defensive restrictions.

**What it does** The transcript records opening statements, witness testimony, and member questioning on artificial intelligence, quantum technologies, semiconductors, biotechnology, autonomous systems, nuclear energy, critical minerals, 5G, and privacy-enhancing technologies. Witnesses and members discussed intellectual-property theft, cyber and insider threats, military-civil fusion, supply-chain dependencies, outbound investment, export controls, data protection, and the role of the CHIPS and Science Act. Recommendations included sustained NIH and NSF funding, stronger research security, an analytic center to identify technology chokepoints, immigration reforms to retain skilled researchers, allied supply chains, and international guardrails for AI and autonomous weapons.

**Who it affects** The discussion addresses Congress, federal research and national-security agencies, universities, investors and venture-capital firms, technology and biotechnology companies, advanced manufacturers, defense suppliers, and allies. It specifically references risks involving Huawei, Intellifusion, BGI Group, Complete Genomics, Ford’s proposed relationship with CATL, and U.S. investment in Chinese technology companies.

**Why it matters** The hearing frames technological leadership as both an economic and national-security issue. Members and witnesses argued that U.S. openness, public research, private capital, immigrant talent, privacy, and democratic alliances are competitive advantages, but warned that theft, dependency, surveillance technology, and poorly protected data could erode them.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Hearing held July 26, 2023, from 7:01 p.m. to 9:09 p.m.
- Witnesses cited estimated annual U.S. losses from Chinese intellectual-property and trade-secret theft of $300 billion to $600 billion.
- Members discussed 37 Chinese technology leads among 44 areas tracked by the Australian Strategic Policy Institute and 25 U.S.-located leaders among 35 Chinese-identified chokepoint technologies.
- Testimony stated that 37 percent of global funding raised by Chinese AI companies from 2015 through 2022 involved at least one American investor.
