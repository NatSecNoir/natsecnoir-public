The House Select Committee on the CCP requests that Futurewei Technologies provide extensive information and records concerning its relationship with Huawei, its U.S. operations, and its alleged role in advancing Chinese Communist Party technology objectives, with a response and preservation obligation by September 28, 2025.

**What it does** The letter, addressed to Futurewei Vice President Jason Chao and signed by Chairman John Moolenaar and Ranking Member Raja Krishnamoorthi, seeks organizational charts, governance and financial records, board materials, contracts, litigation documents, personnel affiliations, and information about transfers or directives from PRC-based entities. It also asks for details and source-code records concerning OpenHarmony and Oniro, related security audits, data practices, compliance with PRC law, and research involving artificial intelligence, GPUs, encryption, wireless communications, and 5G/6G. The committee invokes House Resolution 5 as authority for its investigation.

**Who it affects** The principal recipient is Futurewei, including its U.S. personnel, affiliates, contractors, and technology relationships. The inquiry also addresses Huawei, OpenHarmony, OpenAtom Foundation, Oniro, the International Telecommunication Union, Internet Society, Linux Foundation, NVIDIA, and other standards, open-source, and technology organizations.

**Why it matters** The letter presents Futurewei as potentially maintaining substantive ties to Huawei despite public claims of separation, and examines whether open-source projects, standards bodies, policy institutions, and U.S. operations could support Huawei’s or Beijing’s technology strategy. It also connects the inquiry to federal criminal allegations, Futurewei’s 2020 Air Force contracting suspension, Huawei’s Commerce Department Entity List designation, and concerns about U.S. data, intellectual property, and critical technologies.

**Key dates and numbers**
- September 14, 2025: letter date.
- September 28, 2025: requested production and document-preservation deadline.
- 17: numbered requests for information and records.
- January 1, 2020: recurring start date for requested records and activities.
- 2020: cited federal contracting suspension and rejection of Huawei’s New IP proposal by ITU study groups.
