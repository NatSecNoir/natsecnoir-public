The House Select Committee on the CCP asks the American Chamber of Commerce in Hong Kong to explain its participation in a Hong Kong government promotional video and its views on the territory’s rule of law, judicial independence, and National Security Law.

**What it does** The February 19, 2023 letter from Chairman Mike Gallagher expresses concern that AmCham Hong Kong’s appearance in a promotional video lent credibility to claims that Hong Kong has a “sound legal system.” It requests answers to six sets of questions concerning the compatibility of the National Security Law with the 1984 Sino-British Joint Declaration; the meaning of AmCham’s stated “rule of law” value; the trial of 47 pro-democracy activists; judicial independence; the meaning of “sound legal system”; and the organization’s support for Hong Kong’s democratic institutions or implementation of the National Security Law.

**Who it affects** The inquiry is directed to Geoffrey Siebengartner, chairman of the American Chamber of Commerce in Hong Kong. It also addresses the Hong Kong government, the Government of China, and the broader U.S. business community’s role in presenting Hong Kong’s legal and political conditions.

**Why it matters** The letter links AmCham’s promotional participation to U.S. concerns that Beijing has eroded Hong Kong’s autonomy, democratic institutions, and fundamental rights since enactment of the National Security Law. It seeks clarification rather than imposing a legal or regulatory measure, while placing the chamber’s public messaging and stated values under congressional scrutiny.

**Key dates and numbers**
- February 19, 2023: Letter date.
- February 27, 2023: Requested response deadline.
- June 2020: China’s National People’s Congress Standing Committee passed the Hong Kong National Security Law.
- July 2020: Congress enacted the Hong Kong Autonomy Act, P.L. 116-149.
- 47: Pro-democracy activists whose trial is referenced in the inquiry.
