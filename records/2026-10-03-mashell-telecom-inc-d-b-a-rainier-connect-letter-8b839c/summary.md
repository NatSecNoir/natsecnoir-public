The Letter of Agreement conditions the proposed Section 214 transfer of control of Mashell Telecom to Alphaboost on extensive national-security and law-enforcement safeguards administered by the U.S. Department of Justice, including the FBI.

**What it does** The Alphaboost Companies commit to maintain a U.S.-based law-enforcement point of contact and security officer; support lawful surveillance and requests for U.S. records; keep responsive records and domestic communications infrastructure within U.S. control when required; restrict foreign access and disclosure; and implement cybersecurity, system-security, personnel-screening, supply-chain, and incident-response measures. The agreement also requires advance notice or DOJ review of changes involving storage locations, access countries, principal equipment, service providers, network operations centers, ownership, and service offerings. Annual reports, site visits, compliance audits, and updated network information are required.

**Who it affects** The commitments bind Alphaboost Holdings and its subsidiaries, including Mashell Telecom, as well as relevant personnel, foreign persons with access, equipment vendors, managed network service providers, and other service providers. DOJ, including the FBI, reviews designated personnel, plans, equipment, providers, and certain changes.

**Why it matters** The LOA links approval of a communications-control transaction to continuing controls over sensitive U.S. records, lawful U.S. process, domestic communications, and network infrastructure. Breach or unresolved national-security concerns may support FCC action to modify, condition, revoke, cancel, or terminate relevant authorizations.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Signed December 14, 2023; tied to FCC File No. ITC-T/C-20230120-00004 and related WC and TT files.
- LEPOC information is due within 15 days after FCC approval; responsive lawful-process records must generally be provided within five days.
- Cybersecurity plans, security-screening descriptions, and certain equipment, provider, and access-country information are generally due within 30–60 days after FCC approval.
- Known or suspected security incidents and material breaches must be reported to DOJ within 48 hours; qualifying CPNI breaches must be reported under 47 C.F.R. § 64.2011 within seven business days.
