The National Security Agreement requires GU Holdings Inc. and Google LLC to implement extensive security, access-control, reporting, and oversight measures as the mitigation framework for GU Holdings’ proposed TPU submarine cable license.

**What it does**
The agreement establishes obligations covering the cable’s U.S. connections, domestic communications infrastructure, network operations centers, principal equipment, service providers, contractors, covered customers, and other third parties. GU Holdings must appoint a U.S.-citizen Security Officer, submit equipment, provider, personnel, and customer information, obtain Committee non-objection for specified changes, screen personnel, protect U.S. records and communications, and report specified incidents within 48 hours. The Committee may conduct site visits, require annual reporting and meetings, and request third-party audits. The agreement also permits the Committee to recommend that the FCC modify, condition, revoke, or terminate the relevant license for a breach.

**Who it affects**
The direct obligations apply chiefly to GU Holdings and, in specified respects, Google and their affiliates. They also flow through contracts to service providers, equipment vendors, covered customers, lenders, strategic partners, and other third parties with access to TPU or its principal equipment. DHS, DOJ, and DoD, acting as the Compliance Monitoring Agencies, receive submissions and exercise objection, review, audit, and enforcement-related functions.

**Why it matters**
This agreement illustrates Team Telecom mitigation for a submarine cable connecting Arcata, California; Guam; the Philippines; and Taiwan. It links operational control, supply-chain scrutiny, foreign-person access, cybersecurity, data protection, and third-party governance to continued FCC licensing.

**Key dates and numbers**
- The FCC referred the application to the Committee on June 2, 2023; the filing is FCC File No. SCL-LIC-20230511-00013 (TT 23-032).
- The agreement bears a January 24, 2025 date; its effective date is the date of the last signature.
- GU Holdings generally must report qualifying security incidents within 48 hours; service providers must report breaches within 72 hours.
- TPU includes five unused branching units that may be built out later, potentially requiring agreement modifications.
