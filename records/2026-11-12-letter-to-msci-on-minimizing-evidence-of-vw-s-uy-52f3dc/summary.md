The letter asks MSCI to address concerns that it improperly removed Volkswagen’s forced-labor red flag after relying on a flawed audit of the company’s factory in Xinjiang.

**What it does** The letter, sent by House Select Committee Chairman John Moolenaar, Ranking Member Raja Krishnamoorthi, and Senators Marco Rubio and Chris Coons to MSCI President Henry Fernandez, describes concerns about MSCI’s December 2023 decision to lift Volkswagen’s forced-labor rating. It connects that decision to allegations of Uyghur forced labor in the Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region (XUAR), criticism of the audit, and Volkswagen’s continuing partnerships with companies in China that appear to use forced labor.

**Who it affects** The document directly addresses MSCI and its treatment of Volkswagen. It also concerns investors and supply-chain stakeholders relying on MSCI’s environmental, social, and governance ratings, as well as companies operating in or sourcing from the XUAR.

**Why it matters** The letter frames MSCI’s rating decision as potentially minimizing evidence of state-imposed forced labor and weakening scrutiny of supply-chain risks linked to the Chinese Communist Party’s treatment of Uyghurs and other predominantly Muslim ethnic groups. It highlights the consequences of relying on an audit despite widespread concerns, including concerns attributed to the auditors themselves.

**Key dates and numbers**
- June 3, 2024: Letter date.
- December 2023: MSCI reportedly lifted Volkswagen’s “red flag” rating.
- 2022: MSCI reportedly granted Volkswagen the red flag rating.
- More than two million people: Number the letter says are at risk in the XUAR’s forced-labor system.
