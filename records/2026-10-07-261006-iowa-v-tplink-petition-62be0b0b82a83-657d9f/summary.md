===
The Iowa complaint alleges that TP-Link Systems Inc. violated the Iowa Consumer Fraud Act by making deceptive security, China-separation, supply-chain, manufacturing, and data-practice representations about its routers and related applications.

**What it does** This state-court petition seeks declarations, preliminary and permanent injunctions, consumer notices, reimbursement, disgorgement, civil penalties, costs, and attorneys’ fees. It alleges that TP-Link marketed HomeShield and specific router models as secure while devices—including the TL-WR940N, Archer C7, and Archer AX21—had known vulnerabilities, lacked automatic updates, or had reached end of life. It also challenges representations that TP-Link had fully separated from its Chinese operations and that Vietnamese manufacturing provided additional supply-chain security, and alleges omissions concerning Chinese intelligence and vulnerability-reporting laws.

**Who it affects** The action targets TP-Link and concerns Iowa consumers, including purchasers of routers, smart-home devices, and users of Tether, Tapo, Deco, and Kasa Smart applications. The petition discusses alleged exploitation by Chinese state-linked actors, the Russian GRU, and botnets including Quad7 and Flax Typhoon.

**Why it matters** The filing connects consumer-protection claims to national-security telecommunications concerns, including the FCC’s addition of foreign-produced routers to the Covered List and DoD’s June 2026 Section 1260H designation of TP-Link Technologies as a Chinese military company. It does not itself decide whether TP-Link routers may be sold nationally.

**Key dates and numbers**
- March 23, 2026: FCC router Covered List public notice, WC Docket No. 18-89, DA 26-278.
- June 8–10, 2026: DoD Section 1260H listing and Federal Register notice identifying TP-Link Technologies.
- CVE-2023-50224 and CVE-2023-1389 are among the vulnerabilities discussed.
- The petition seeks up to $40,000 per violation, plus up to $5,000 for violations against older individuals.
