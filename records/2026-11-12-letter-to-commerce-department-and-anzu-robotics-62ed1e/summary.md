The House Select Committee on the CCP asks Anzu Robotics to explain its relationship with DJI and asks the Commerce Department to investigate whether Anzu and Cogito are used to circumvent U.S. restrictions on DJI products.

**What it does** The letter to Anzu CEO Randall Warnas describes the Anzu Raptor T as substantially replicating DJI’s Mavic 3, including hardware, firmware, remote-control technology, and software functionality. It seeks contracts, manufacturing and software supply-chain information, details about DJI payments and technical support, firmware keys and controls, use of DJI SDK and OcuSync technology, and a chronology of Anzu’s dealings with DJI. A separate letter to Commerce Secretary Gina Raimondo asks what authorities and actions could identify and address PRC companies white-labeling products through passthrough firms.

**Who it affects** The requests directly concern Anzu, DJI, Cogito Tech, and the Specta Air drone, while implicating Commerce, the FCC, DoD, Treasury, and other executive-branch restrictions. The committee also asks Anzu for a staff briefing.

**Why it matters** The letters frame white-labeling as a potential strategy to evade the Entity List, prospective FCC Covered List restrictions, and DoD-related limits, while raising supply-chain, cybersecurity, privacy, and national-security concerns. They do not themselves impose a restriction or make a regulatory determination.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Document date shown: August 20, 2024; catalogue date: August 27, 2024.
- Anzu response requested by September 6, 2024; briefing requested by September 13, 2024.
- The letter invokes Section 1260H and H. Res. 11.
