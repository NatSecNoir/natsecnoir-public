The House Select Committee on the CCP and House Committee on Homeland Security report that PRC-linked ownership, manufacturing, and technology create significant cybersecurity, supply-chain, and national-security risks for U.S. ports, especially through ZPMC ship-to-shore cranes.

**What it does** The majority staff report presents findings from a joint investigation launched in June 2023 into port equipment, software, foreign intelligence threats, and supply-chain vulnerabilities. It examines ZPMC’s relationships with U.S. ports and component suppliers ABB, TMEIC, and Siemens; reports the discovery of cellular modems on some U.S.-bound cranes; and describes concerns about remote access, PRC legal access to source code, and assembly of third-party components in China. It also reviews PRC-linked interests in five U.S. ports and conditions at Guam’s port infrastructure. The committees recommend that DHS and the Coast Guard disconnect unauthorized modem connections, deploy operational-technology monitoring, improve Guam’s cybersecurity, strengthen protections for strategic seaports, and support non-adversarial and U.S. crane manufacturing.

**Who it affects** U.S. ports and terminal operators using ZPMC equipment, federal agencies responsible for maritime and military readiness, crane manufacturers and component suppliers, and policymakers overseeing grants, procurement, and critical infrastructure security are the principal affected parties.

**Why it matters** The report identifies dependence on PRC-produced or PRC-assembled cranes as a potential avenue for unauthorized access, espionage, disruption, and coercive supply restrictions. It emphasizes that ZPMC supplies nearly 80% of ship-to-shore cranes used at U.S. ports and that Guam’s strategic role heightens the consequences of infrastructure weaknesses.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Investigation launched: June 2023; report dated September 2024.
- ZPMC share: nearly 80% of U.S. ship-to-shore cranes and 70% of the global market.
- PRC-linked entities control portions of five U.S. ports.
- FBI discovered intelligence-gathering equipment near or on ZPMC cranes at Baltimore in February 2021.
- Volt Typhoon targeted Guam and other U.S. critical infrastructure in early 2023.
