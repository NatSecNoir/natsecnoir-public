The House Select Committee on the CCP concludes that U.S. index providers, asset managers, and investment funds channeled billions of dollars into PRC companies identified by U.S. national-security and human-rights red-flag lists, and urges Congress to restrict and disclose those investments.

**What it does** The report examines MSCI and BlackRock in depth and canvasses the broader financial industry. Using 2023 data, it identifies approximately $6.457 billion invested through 526 funds, 152 asset managers, and more than 37 index providers in 63 PRC companies or affiliated entities appearing on seven of 11 reviewed government lists. The report describes exposure involving the 1260H, NS-CMIC, Entity, FCC Covered, Military End User, 889, and UFLPA lists, and recommends legislation covering listed companies and their subsidiaries, affiliates, parents, and holding companies.

**Who it affects** The findings concern U.S. investors, retirement and savings accounts, financial institutions, index providers, asset managers, and PRC companies tied to PLA modernization, surveillance, forced labor, or other abuses. MSCI-linked products accounted for $3.666 billion, while BlackRock’s reviewed funds held $1.899 billion.

**Why it matters** The Committee says the activity was not illegal under the law as described, but argues that existing restrictions inadequately address indirect investment through affiliates and subsidiaries and that fund disclosures understate risks associated with CCP policies and abuses.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Report released April 18, 2024; analysis primarily covers 2023.
- $6.457 billion total; $5.331 billion associated with 1260H-listed companies.
- 67 securities, 63 companies, and 58 BlackRock funds reviewed.
- MSCI removed six identified PRC securities effective March 1, 2024.
