The House Select Committee on the CCP asks Commerce to address national-security risks from PRC-manufactured or controlled unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) through its Information and Communications Technology and Services (ICTS) authorities.

**What it does** The June 13, 2024 letter to Secretary Gina Raimondo requests that the Bureau of Industry and Security expand its connected-vehicle rulemaking to include UAVs and other non-automotive vehicles. If Commerce does not broaden that proceeding, the Committee asks BIS to launch a separate ICTS investigation into PRC UAV risks. It also requests a meeting with Commerce representatives by July 15, 2024.

**Who it affects** The request concerns PRC UAV manufacturers and operators, including DJI, as well as U.S. law-enforcement agencies, government agencies, utility companies, and other users whose data or infrastructure may be exposed. The letter is signed by Chairman John Moolenaar and Ranking Member Raja Krishnamoorthi.

**Why it matters** The Committee argues that UAVs present risks similar to connected automobiles because they collect and transmit sensitive information, including location and activity data, and may permit remote access or undisclosed software updates. It cites federal warnings that PRC laws can facilitate government access to company-held data and contends that UAV data could help the PRC or the People’s Liberation Army map and exploit critical U.S. infrastructure. The letter supports executive-branch action alongside the American Security Drone Act and pending congressional measures.

**Key dates and numbers**
- June 13, 2024: Letter date; page headers show June 14, 2024.
- July 15, 2024: Requested deadline for a Commerce discussion.
- March 1, 2024: Connected-vehicles ICTS advance notice appeared at 89 Fed. Reg. 15,066.
- The letter states that PRC-headquartered UAV companies control 90% of the U.S. consumer drone market and 70% of the global market.
