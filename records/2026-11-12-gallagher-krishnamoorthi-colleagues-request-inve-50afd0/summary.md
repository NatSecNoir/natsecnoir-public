The House Select Committee on the CCP asks the Secretaries of Commerce, Defense, and the Treasury to investigate PRC LiDAR companies for possible inclusion on the Defense Department’s Chinese Military Companies List, the BIS Entity List, and Treasury’s Non-SDN Chinese Military-Industrial Complex Companies List, and to assess related export-control and supply-chain risks.

**What it does** The letter describes LiDAR as a dual-use remote-sensing technology used in autonomous vehicles, robotics, transportation, agriculture, manufacturing, smart-city infrastructure, and military systems. It urges agency investigations into PRC LiDAR companies, consideration of export controls on relevant U.S. technologies, and greater transparency in defense-industrial-base supply chains to identify PRC-made LiDAR embedded in U.S. defense platforms. It highlights concerns about data access, cybersecurity, software updates, mapping of U.S. infrastructure, and use in surveillance and military applications.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to Commerce, Defense, and Treasury. It identifies Hesai Technology, Robosense, DJI’s Livox Technology, and Huawei as examples of PRC-linked LiDAR activity, and discusses CETC, Xilinx, and Altera in connection with military ties or semiconductor supply.

**Why it matters** The letter argues that commercial and dual-use LiDAR is not comprehensively addressed by existing export controls or procurement restrictions, creating potential exposure for U.S. infrastructure, capital markets, military systems, and sensitive information. It also invokes the Committee’s authority under H. Res. 11.

**Key dates and numbers**
- November 28, 2023: letter date.
- The letter states that Hesai had 47% of the global LiDAR market by sales revenue.
- H. Res. 11 is cited as the Committee’s authorizing resolution.
- LiDAR systems specially designed for military end users are noted as subject to ITAR controls.
