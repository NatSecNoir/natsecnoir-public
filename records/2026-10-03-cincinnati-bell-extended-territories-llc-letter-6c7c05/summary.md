The Letter of Agreement establishes national-security and law-enforcement mitigation commitments by Cincinnati Bell and Red Fiber in connection with their proposed transfer of control of international Section 214 authorization holders and related foreign investment in microwave licenses.

**What it does** This Team Telecom agreement governs access to U.S. records, domestic communications, and domestic communications infrastructure. Cincinnati Bell must maintain U.S.-based law-enforcement points of contact and a U.S. security officer; comply with lawful U.S. process and CALEA; keep responsive information in the United States; screen personnel; restrict foreign access and disclosures; submit cybersecurity and system-security plans; report security incidents within 72 hours; and provide advance notice or obtain review of specified equipment, vendors, outsourced or offshored services, ownership changes, and other business changes. It also requires recurring compliance reports, site visits, and replacement reporting for ZTE-manufactured network gateways and TV/DVR equipment.

**Who it affects** The commitments apply primarily to Cincinnati Bell, with Red Fiber Parent LLC as the transaction counterparty, and give the DOJ, DoD, FBI, and other U.S. government parties review, objection, inspection, and enforcement roles. The agreement also reaches foreign personnel, vendors, contractors, managed network service providers, and other service providers with relevant access.

**Why it matters** The LOA conditions the national-security review of a telecommunications ownership transfer on continuing controls over data, lawful interception, network equipment, foreign access, outsourcing, and cybersecurity. Breach or unresolved risk may support FCC action against relevant authorizations.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Executed March 12, 2021; the LOA defines its operative date as the date of execution.
- Related FCC matters include WC Docket No. 20-146 and four ITC/ISP file numbers, identified as TT 20-057 to 063.
- Cincinnati Bell generally must report security incidents within 72 hours; third-party breaches or losses of U.S. records within 48 hours.
- Initial LEPOC and security-officer steps are generally due within 30 days; equipment and outsourced-service-provider lists within 45 days; annual reports begin one year after execution.
