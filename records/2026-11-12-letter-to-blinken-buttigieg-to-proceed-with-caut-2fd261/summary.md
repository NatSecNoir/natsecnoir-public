The House Select Committee on the CCP urges the Secretaries of State and Transportation to proceed cautiously with approving additional passenger flights between the United States and the People’s Republic of China until China complies with the existing bilateral air-services agreement, reciprocal route access improves, and passenger demand recovers.

**What it does**
The April 10, 2024 letter asks Antony Blinken and Pete Buttigieg to condition any expansion of U.S.-China passenger air service on reciprocal parity in carrier-operated routes, a gradual increase tied to passenger demand, and protection against unnecessary security risks. It specifically calls for prohibiting PRC airlines from using Russian airspace on routes serving American passengers. The signatories argue that China’s market-access restrictions, operational and crew-treatment rules, and suspension of the bilateral agreement disadvantaged U.S. carriers.

**Who it affects**
The requested approach would affect U.S. and PRC passenger airlines, aviation workers, travelers, and the agencies responsible for U.S.-China air-service decisions. The letter is addressed to Secretary of State Blinken and Secretary of Transportation Buttigieg and is signed by Chairman Mike Gallagher and Ranking Member Raja Krishnamoorthi.

**Why it matters**
The committee frames new route approvals as both a competition and aviation-security issue. It argues that Chinese carriers’ continued use of Russian airspace creates an artificial cost and operational advantage over U.S. carriers, which stopped using that airspace after Russia’s invasion of Ukraine. The letter seeks to preserve negotiating leverage while the United States evaluates safety, operational integrity, market reciprocity, and demand.

**Key dates and numbers**
- April 10, 2024: Letter date.
- March 2022: U.S. carriers stopped flying through Russian airspace after Russia’s invasion of Ukraine.
- Four major Chinese airports identified as having imposed slot constraints: Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, and Shenzhen.
- The letter asks that additional routes be phased in slowly rather than expanded without addressing the cited concerns.
