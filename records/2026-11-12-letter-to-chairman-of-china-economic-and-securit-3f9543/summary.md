The House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence and House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party ask the U.S.–China Economic and Security Review Commission to provide Congress with bipartisan policy approaches and analysis on technology, investment, procurement, artificial intelligence, and biotechnology risks involving China.

**What it does**
The letter requests Commission expertise on reducing or mitigating risks from Chinese software and hardware in the United States; limiting inadvertent U.S. support for Chinese technological capabilities through investment and federal procurement; analyzing China’s military applications of advanced artificial intelligence and biotechnology; and addressing advances in biotechnology and possible future U.S. dependence on Chinese biotechnology products. It frames the request within the Commission’s statutory responsibility to monitor and report on the national-security implications of the U.S.–China trade and economic relationship.

**Who it affects**
The request is directed to the United States–China Economic and Security Review Commission and its chair, Carolyn Bartholomew. Its intended audience is Congress, while the requested subjects implicate federal policymakers, private-sector technology and national-security leaders, investors, federal procuring entities, and companies developing or using strategic technologies.

**Why it matters**
The letter records congressional interest in coordinating economic, technology, investment, procurement, and national-security policy toward China. It specifically links software and hardware supply-chain risks with outbound investment, defense innovation, artificial intelligence, and biotechnology, and seeks bipartisan recommendations to inform future legislative and administrative action.

**Key dates and numbers**
- December 20, 2023: date of the letter.
- Four requested areas: Chinese technology risks; inadvertent U.S. support for Chinese capabilities; military applications of artificial intelligence and biotechnology; and biotechnology dependence.
- The letter cites the Commission’s charge to submit an annual report to Congress and provide recommendations where appropriate.
