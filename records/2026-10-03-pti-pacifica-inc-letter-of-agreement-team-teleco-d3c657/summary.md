The Letter of Agreement conditions the Committee’s recommendation that the FCC grant PTI Pacifica a new 25-year cable landing license for the MTC Interisland Cable System on the company’s compliance with extensive national-security and law-enforcement safeguards.

**What it does** The LOA requires PTI Pacifica to maintain a U.S.-citizen security point of contact; provide system, equipment, vendor, and restoration information; obtain or provide advance notice for specified changes involving operations, principal equipment, vendors, and testing; and maintain measures for logical security, physical security, personnel screening, incident response, and supply-chain risk. It also requires reporting of specified incidents or breaches within 72 hours, annual compliance reports, possible third-party audits, and access for CMA consultations and site visits. The CMAs may object to equipment or providers and may seek FCC action if commitments are breached.

**Who it affects** The direct obligations apply to PTI Pacifica, doing business as IT&E, and bind its successors, assigns, subsidiaries, and affiliates. The agreement gives DHS and DoD, as the Compliance Monitoring Agencies, oversight and approval roles, while relating to FCC licensing and incorporating representations made during review involving DOJ and the FBI.

**Why it matters** The LOA illustrates Team Telecom mitigation for a submarine cable serving U.S. communications. It links continued FCC authorization to controls over U.S. records, network access, equipment, foreign investment, cybersecurity, and the ability to interrupt U.S. traffic in response to lawful process.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Executed June 10, 2022; the prior MICS license expired February 13, 2022.
- The FCC granted an STA on February 16, 2022, expiring August 16, 2022.
- PTI Pacifica sought a new license for an additional 25-year term.
- Many notices and reports use 30-, 45-, 60-, 72-, and 90-day periods; breach reporting is generally due within 72 hours.
