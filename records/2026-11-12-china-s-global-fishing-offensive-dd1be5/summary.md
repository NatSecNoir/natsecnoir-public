The report finds that the People’s Republic of China has built a state-directed distant-water fishing system that combines commercial extraction, maritime coercion, labor exploitation, environmental destruction, and control over global seafood supply chains, creating a direct threat to U.S. national security and economic resilience.

**What it does** The majority staff report describes the fleet’s scale, command structure, offshore support network, overseas ports, satellite communications, reflagging practices, and Chinese processing hubs. It examines five principal findings: China removes distance as a limit on extraction; forces seafood through Chinese processing infrastructure; maintains state-supported cost advantages; converts processing dominance into pricing power; and uses subsidies, forced labor, fraud, and below-cost competition to weaken U.S. processing capacity. It concludes with recommendations for congressional action, including expanded Coast Guard partnerships, greater vessel and supply-chain transparency, a broader definition of IUU fishing, and stronger U.S. seafood competitiveness.

**Who it affects** The report focuses on U.S. consumers, fishermen, seafood processors, federal agencies, coastal states, partner nations, and workers aboard or in Chinese-linked fishing and processing operations. It also addresses affected fisheries and communities in West Africa, Latin America, the Pacific, and the South China Sea.

**Why it matters** The report links fishing activity to maritime militia operations, intelligence collection, forced labor, trafficking, environmental damage, seafood fraud, and strategic dependence. It argues that existing fisheries, port, labor, and trade enforcement systems do not adequately address an integrated state-backed network operating across jurisdictions.

**Key dates and numbers**
- The report is dated January 2026.
- China’s distant-water fleet is estimated at 2,000 to 16,000 vessels and accounted for about 44% of global fishing effort from 2022 to 2024.
- The fleet reportedly logs more than 110 million hours annually in waters of more than 90 countries.
- AIS data recorded 8,947 Chinese-flagged carrier-fishing encounters between January 2024 and January 2025, or 83% of global encounters.
- The report states that the United States imports more than 80% of its seafood.
