House Foreign Affairs Committee Chairman Brian Mast and House Select Committee on China Chairman John Moolenaar ask the Secretaries of State and the Treasury to investigate named foreign persons and entities connected to Southeast Asian online scam-center operations and determine whether they qualify for sanctions.

**What it does** This letter requests a determination within 180 days under four legal authorities, including the Global Magnitsky Human Rights Accountability Act. It identifies 28 individuals and two organizations—Fully Light Group of Companies, LTD and White Sands Palace Casino—as potentially connected to online scams, human trafficking, torture, other gross human-rights violations, or cybercrime. The authors also cite Executive Order 14390 and commend recent U.S. sanctions and anti-trafficking actions.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Secretary of the Treasury Scott Bessent. The proposed review concerns named persons and organizations associated with scam-center networks in Cambodia, Burma, Laos, and the wider Southeast Asian region, while addressing harms to Americans and trafficking victims from many countries.

**Why it matters** The letter frames industrial-scale cryptocurrency, romance, and other online scams as a combined national-security, human-trafficking, and human-rights threat. It argues that corruption and political protection have allowed the networks to persist despite regional crackdowns, and urges targeted sanctions or other consequences where statutory criteria are met.

**Key dates and numbers**
- September 16, 2026: document date.
- 180 days: requested period for the departments to investigate and determine sanctions eligibility.
- 28 individuals and 2 organizations: persons and entities identified for review.
- Approximately 400,000: estimated trafficking victims in Southeast Asian scam centers from more than 70 countries.
