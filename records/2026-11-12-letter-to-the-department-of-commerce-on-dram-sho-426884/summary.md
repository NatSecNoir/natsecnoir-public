The letter urges the Department of Commerce to account for severe DRAM and HBM3E shortages when applying its new “America First” certification requirement to export licenses for advanced AI chips, particularly Nvidia H200 chips destined for China.

**What it does** The House Select Committee on the CCP commends Commerce’s January 13, 2026, revision to the license review policy for advanced computing commodities and describes its certification that license applicants establish sufficient U.S. supply. The letter asks Commerce to brief the committee on domestic HBM3E availability, how supply affects license approval, and efforts to prioritize American customers.

**Who it affects** The request concerns Commerce, advanced-chip license applicants, U.S. customers, and China-based customers seeking Nvidia H200 GPUs. It also identifies Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron Technology as the three global leaders in memory production, and discusses Nvidia, TSMC, and HBM3E used in H200 and Blackwell chips.

**Why it matters** The letter argues that persistent DRAM shortages could make the certification difficult to satisfy and could force a tradeoff between exports to China and existing or potential U.S. orders. It cites reported Chinese orders exceeding two million H200 chips against approximately 700,000 in available Nvidia inventory, while noting that HBM3E supply is already constrained.

**Key dates and numbers**
- January 13, 2026: Commerce’s revised advanced-computing license review policy was released.
- January 14, 2026: The letter was dated.
- January 25, 2026: Requested deadline for Commerce’s briefing.
- More than 2 million: Reported H200 orders from Chinese technology firms.
- Approximately 700,000: Reported current Nvidia inventory estimate.
