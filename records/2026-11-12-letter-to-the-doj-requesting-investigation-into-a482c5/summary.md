The House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party and Representative Elise Stefanik urged the Department of Justice to investigate whether the DJI-funded Drone Advocacy Alliance violated the Foreign Agents Registration Act (FARA).

**What it does** This May 22, 2024 letter asks DOJ to examine the Drone Advocacy Alliance’s activities and its relationship with DJI. The letter states that the group is funded and maintained by DJI and works to influence Americans to oppose legislation that could restrict DJI’s use in the United States, including the American Security Drone Act and the Countering CCP Drones Act. The lawmakers argue that advocacy and lobbying on behalf of a foreign entity may trigger FARA’s registration and disclosure requirements.

**Who it affects** The request directly concerns DOJ, DJI, and the Drone Advocacy Alliance. It also bears on U.S. policymakers considering restrictions on DJI drones and on advocacy organizations operating in the United States with foreign funding or direction.

**Why it matters** The letter frames FARA as a national-security transparency mechanism requiring covered individuals and organizations to disclose their relationships, activities, receipts, and disbursements. It asks federal prosecutors to assess whether a foreign-funded campaign opposing drone restrictions should have registered, while also linking DJI to broader alleged national-security concerns involving the Chinese Communist Party.

**Key dates and numbers**
- May 22, 2024: Letter date.
- Two lawmakers signed or joined the request: Chairman John Moolenaar and Congresswoman Elise Stefanik.
- The request concerns potential violations of FARA and advocacy related to two named drone bills.
