The House Select Committee on the CCP urges NOAA to respond more forcefully to illegal, unreported, and unregulated (IUU) fishing and forced labor associated with the People’s Republic of China (PRC), including by considering trade restrictions on tainted seafood.

**What it does** The letter points to NOAA’s 2023 Report to Congress on Improving International Fisheries Management, which gave the PRC a negative certification determination after finding failures to address U.S. concerns and violations of international fishery conservation measures. It asks NOAA to enforce restrictions against specific seafood products if the PRC does not take corrective action after bilateral consultations. The members also cite forced labor in the PRC’s distant-water fishing fleet and a Customs and Border Protection Withhold Release Order involving Dalian Ocean Fishing Co. Ltd.

**Who it affects** The requested actions would affect the PRC, its fishing vessels and seafood exporters, U.S. importers and consumers, and U.S. fishermen and seafood producers. NOAA, the Department of Commerce, and other agencies named in the letter would have roles in considering the requested response.

**Why it matters** The letter links IUU fishing and forced labor to marine ecosystem damage, economic security, and unfair competition. It emphasizes that a negative certification can lead to denial of U.S. port privileges and potentially to import restrictions under the Moratorium Protection Act.

**Key dates and numbers**
- October 10, 2023: letter date shown in the document.
- August 31, 2023: NOAA report issued.
- 90 days: period referenced for corrective action and consultations before potential import restrictions.
- 2022: CBP issued a Withhold Release Order involving Dalian Ocean Fishing.
