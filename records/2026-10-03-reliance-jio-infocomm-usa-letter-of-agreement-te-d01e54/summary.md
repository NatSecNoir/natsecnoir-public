The Letter of Assurances conditions DOJ’s non-objection to Reliance Jio Infocomm USA’s Section 214 authorization on extensive national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety commitments.

**What it does** The agreement requires RJI USA to notify DOJ of current and changed service offerings; comply with lawful U.S. process and CALEA; store specified domestic communications, transactional and call-associated data, CPNI, subscriber information, and related billing records in the United States for at least one year; and restrict disclosure of U.S. records or communications for non-U.S. government requests. It also requires access logging, a compliance plan, employee training, a U.S.-based law-enforcement point of contact, disclosures about suppliers and principal equipment, DOJ inspection and interview access, possible independent audits, annual compliance reports, and notice of qualifying ownership changes.

**Who it affects** The obligations bind RJI USA, a U.S. subsidiary of India’s Reliance Jio Infocomm Limited and ultimately Reliance Industries Limited. They extend to relevant owners, successors, vendors, contractors, foreign service providers, equipment manufacturers, and personnel with access to the company’s infrastructure or U.S. records. DOJ, including the FBI, administers the commitments, while the FCC’s Section 214 authorization is the principal regulatory leverage.

**Why it matters** The agreement illustrates Team Telecom mitigation for a foreign-owned carrier seeking international Section 214 authority. It centralizes sensitive records and lawful-interception responsibilities in the United States, gives DOJ visibility into infrastructure and foreign access, and permits DOJ to seek FCC action—including modification, conditioning, revocation, cancellation, or termination of authorization—after an uncured breach.

**Key dates and numbers**
- February 12, 2016: LOA date and execution date shown in the document.
- FCC File No. ITC-214-20150724-00185: pending Section 214 application.
- 60 days: deadlines for several notices, service-change reports, CALEA status reporting, and supplier disclosures.
- At least one year: required U.S. storage period for specified records.
- 5 business days: deadline to refer non-U.S. government legal requests to DOJ and provide the records-storage location.
- 30 days: recurring CALEA reports, annual-report due date of January 30, and cure period for a breach (unless shorter is appropriate).
