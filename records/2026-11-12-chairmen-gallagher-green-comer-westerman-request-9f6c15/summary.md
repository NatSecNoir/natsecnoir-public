The House chairmen requested a briefing from the Departments of Homeland Security and the Interior about Chinese influence, military activity, mining operations, and strategic land purchases in and around Alaska and the broader North American Arctic.

**What it does**
The March 15, 2024 letter expresses concern that companies backed by or affiliated with the Chinese Communist Party could increase Chinese ownership of rare-earth minerals and other critical resources on U.S. soil. It asks Secretaries Alejandro Mayorkas and Deb Haaland to explain how their agencies are mitigating foreign threats to Arctic communities and to brief the committees “as soon as possible” and no later than March 29, 2024. The letter also emphasizes the need to inform and empower tribal communities.

**Who it affects**
The request is directed to DHS and DOI, with implications for Alaska communities, tribal communities, U.S. critical-mineral supply chains, and companies connected to the PRC that seek mining or strategically significant land interests in the region. It was signed by Mike Gallagher, Mark E. Green, James Comer, and Bruce Westerman in their capacities as committee chairmen.

**Why it matters**
The document frames Chinese activity in the Arctic as a national-security and economic-security concern, linking access to Alaskan minerals with supply-chain vulnerability and geopolitical competition. It cites U.S. import dependence on critical minerals and prior testimony describing China’s rapidly expanding Arctic capabilities.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated March 15, 2024.
- Requested briefing deadline: no later than March 29, 2024.
- The letter states that the United States is 100 percent net import reliant on 15 critical minerals and at least 50 percent import reliant on China for 24 mineral commodities.
- Background references a November 2023 House Homeland Security Committee hearing.
