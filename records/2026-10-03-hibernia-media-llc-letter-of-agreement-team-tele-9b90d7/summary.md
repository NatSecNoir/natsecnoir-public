The Letter of Assurance conditions Hibernia’s proposed international Section 214 authorization on commitments addressing U.S. national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety concerns regarding the Hibernia Atlantic submarine cable system.

**What it does** The agreement requires Hibernia to provide equipment, vendor, network-management, architecture, and security information to DHS, DOJ, and DoD; protect the cable system and domestic communications infrastructure; preserve and produce U.S. records in response to lawful process; and route non-U.S. government demands through the U.S. government process. It also establishes U.S.-based points of contact and security personnel, annual compliance reporting, agency inspection rights, breach reporting, and good-faith procedures for modifying burdensome or inadequate terms. The commitments bind Hibernia and specified subsidiaries and affiliates only if the FCC grants the license.

**Who it affects** The obligations apply to Hibernia Group ehf and its Hibernia Atlantic operating companies, including Hibernia Media LLC and Hibernia Atlantic U.S. LLC. They support oversight by DHS, DOJ, DoD, and the FCC of the company’s U.S. facilities, records, network operations, contractors, and submarine cable infrastructure.

**Why it matters** The agreement illustrates Team Telecom mitigation for a foreign-owned international telecommunications operator. It combines technical transparency, U.S. legal-process access, restrictions on foreign-government access to domestic communications, security oversight, and continuing compliance review as conditions of FCC authorization.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Dated September 30, 2010.
- The proposed authorization is identified as FCC File ITC-214-20100303-00093.
- Hibernia must provide the initial principal-equipment list by the later of November 15, 2010, or 60 days after license grant.
- Customer billing records must be retained for at least 18 months; certain non-U.S. government requests must be referred within five days, and reported breaches within 10 calendar days.
