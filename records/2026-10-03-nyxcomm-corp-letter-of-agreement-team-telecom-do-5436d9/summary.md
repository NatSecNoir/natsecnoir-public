The Letter of Agreement records Nyxcomm Corp.’s binding commitments to the U.S. Department of Justice to address national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety concerns associated with its Section 214 application, enabling DOJ to notify the FCC that it has no objection to consent.

**What it does** This Team Telecom agreement requires Nyxcomm to comply with lawful-interception obligations, including CALEA; use only CALEA-capable underlying carriers for U.S. services; submit a DOJ-approved Network and Systems Security Plan; protect U.S. Records and Domestic Communications from unauthorized foreign access or disclosure; store U.S. Records in the United States; consult DOJ before specified foreign telecommunications or data arrangements; and provide notice and information concerning foreign personnel, outsourcing, equipment, and business changes. It also requires a U.S.-based, DOJ-approved law-enforcement point of contact, annual compliance reports, and cooperation with DOJ site visits and interviews.

**Who it affects** The commitments bind Nyxcomm, including its successors, assigns, subsidiaries, and affiliates, and govern its handling of U.S. customer records, call-detail records, domestic communications, network infrastructure, lawful process, foreign personnel, and outsourced services. DOJ receives oversight, approval, notice, reporting, and inspection rights; the FCC may act on a relevant authorization if the commitments are breached.

**Why it matters** The agreement conditions Team Telecom’s non-objection on operational controls over data storage, lawful access, foreign involvement, cybersecurity, and network management. It demonstrates how a Section 214 authorization could be supported by enforceable commitments protecting U.S. communications and records, with breach potentially leading DOJ to request FCC modification, cancellation, or termination of authorization.

**Key dates and numbers**
- October 30, 2017: date shown on the LOA.
- FCC docket: ITC-214-20170413-00069; application sought global and/or limited resale authority under Section 214.
- 60 days: deadline to forward the proposed Network and Systems Security Plan to DOJ.
- 30 days: advance notice generally required for specified service, business, records-storage, and LEPOC changes; Principal Equipment list due within 30 days.
- October 31, 2018: first annual report due; reports are due each October 31 thereafter.
- Five business days: deadline to refer certain non-U.S. government requests to DOJ and, generally, for the LEPOC to produce records after lawful process, subject to the agreement’s qualifications.
