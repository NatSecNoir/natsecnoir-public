The FCC proposes broad certification and information-collection requirements to identify foreign-adversary ownership, control, jurisdiction, or direction across entities holding FCC licenses, authorizations, permits, and other approvals.

**What it does** The Notice of Proposed Rulemaking would require each covered regulatee to certify whether it is directly or indirectly owned by, controlled by, or subject to the jurisdiction or direction of a foreign adversary. An entity reporting such control would disclose all direct or indirect equity and/or voting interest holders at the proposed 5% threshold, identify the relevant foreign adversary, describe the nature of the relationship, and certify the information’s accuracy. The FCC proposes applying the framework across wireless, satellite, broadcast and other media, submarine cable, domestic and international section 214, VoIP numbering, equipment certification, and additional communications authorizations. It also seeks comment on public disclosure, exemptions, duplicative filings, annual certification, and changes reported within 30 days.

**Who it affects** The proposal reaches current and future licensees, authorization and permit holders, equipment-certification applicants, antenna-structure registrants, frequency coordinators, and other regulated entities. It would use the Department of Commerce’s definitions and list of foreign adversaries, currently including China, Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Russia, and the Maduro Regime.

**Why it matters** The proceeding would give the FCC and the public a more comprehensive view of foreign-adversary involvement in U.S. communications networks, extending beyond ownership to control, jurisdiction, and direction. The FCC says the information could improve threat awareness and support mitigation or further scrutiny while limiting detailed reporting to entities with reportable control.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Adopted May 22, 2025; released May 27, 2025.
- Comments are due 30 days after Federal Register publication; replies are due 60 days after publication.
- Proposed reporting threshold: 5% or greater direct or indirect equity or voting interest.
- Proposed change-related certification deadline: within 30 days.
