The Letter of Agreement records Lexitel’s commitments to the U.S. Department of Justice to address national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety concerns associated with its pending Section 214 application, and provides for DOJ notification to the FCC that it has no objection to consent.

**What it does** Lexitel agrees to comply with lawful-interception requirements, including CALEA, and with U.S. legal process. It must submit a Network and Systems Security Plan for DOJ and FBI review, protect U.S. Records and Domestic Communications from unauthorized foreign-government access, refer foreign-government requests to DOJ or obtain appropriate U.S. authorization, and store U.S. Records in Saipan for at least three years. The agreement also requires a U.S.-based, FBI-approved law-enforcement point of contact; advance notice of specified outsourcing, business, service, and location changes; annual compliance reporting; and reasonable accommodation of DOJ site visits and interviews. A breach may support a DOJ request that the FCC modify, revoke, or terminate a relevant authorization.

**Who it affects** The commitments bind Lexitel and, where applicable, its successors, assigns, subsidiaries, and affiliates. They cover Lexitel’s domestic communications infrastructure, customer and subscriber information, call records, geolocation data, network operations, contractors, foreign-owned telecommunications relationships, and equipment suppliers. DOJ, the FBI, the FCC, and U.S. federal, state, and local law-enforcement agencies are the principal government actors involved.

**Why it matters** The LOA is a Team Telecom mitigation instrument tying international telecommunications authorization to operational controls over lawful access, data location and retention, foreign outsourcing, cybersecurity, and corporate transparency. It also gives the FCC a stated enforcement consequence if Lexitel breaches the commitments.

**Key dates and numbers**
- November 20, 2017: date of the Letter of Agreement.
- FCC application number: ITC-214-20161109-00292.
- 60 days: deadline to forward the Network and Systems Security Plan to DOJ.
- 30 days: advance notice generally required for service-portfolio, U.S.-Records storage-location, and certain LEPOC or business changes.
- 5 business days: deadline to refer certain non-U.S.-government requests to DOJ and to make U.S. Records available after lawful U.S. process.
- At least 3 years: required retention period for U.S. Records.
- January 31 annually, beginning March 1, 2018: stated annual-report schedule.
