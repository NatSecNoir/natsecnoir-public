The agreement extends GU Holdings Inc.’s provisional national security agreement as a condition of continued FCC special temporary authority to operate the U.S.-Taiwan portion of the Pacific Light Cable Network System.

**What it does** The extension incorporates the April 7, 2020 Provisional National Security Agreement, updates the Department of Defense contact, and extends the agreement until the expiration or voluntary surrender of GU Holdings’ August 17, 2021 STA, but no later than March 14, 2022. It authorizes the Government Parties—the Departments of Justice, Homeland Security, and Defense—to seek FCC conditions, objections, oversight, audits, and enforcement if GU Holdings does not comply. The underlying agreement requires U.S.-located and screened network operations, protected access to domestic communications infrastructure, reporting of incidents, advance notice of equipment and vendor changes, controls on foreign influence, and restrictions on Pacific Light Data Communications Co. Ltd. (PLDC).

**Who it affects** The principal regulated entity is GU Holdings, identified in the underlying agreement as an indirect, wholly owned subsidiary of Google LLC. The commitments apply to the U.S.-Taiwan segment, its cable landing and network operations facilities, personnel, vendors, contractors, equipment, and service providers; the FCC is the licensing authority whose STA is conditioned on compliance.

**Why it matters** This document shows Team Telecom mitigation operating through a time-limited submarine-cable authorization. It links continued commercial operation to detailed national-security, cybersecurity, physical-access, foreign-influence, and compliance-monitoring obligations, while preserving Government authority to seek modification, revocation, sanctions, or other remedies through the FCC and courts.

**Key dates and numbers**
- August 17, 2021: GU Holdings filed the STA request in FCC File No. SCL-STA-20210817-00033.
- September 3, 2021: date shown for DHS signature and the latest legible signature date.
- March 14, 2022: outside date for the extension.
- 180 days: maximum temporary-authorization period cited under 47 U.S.C. § 309(f).
- 10 percent: foreign ownership threshold triggering notice obligations.
- Two days: deadline for reporting specified incidents or breaches to the CMAs.
