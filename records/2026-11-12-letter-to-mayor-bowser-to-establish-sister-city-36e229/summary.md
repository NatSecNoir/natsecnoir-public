The House Select Committee on the CCP urges Washington, D.C., Mayor Muriel Bowser to establish a sister-city relationship with Taipei.

**What it does**
The May 13, 2024, letter requests that the mayor create formal sister-city ties between the U.S. capital and Taipei. It presents the relationship as a symbolic cultural and people-to-people partnership, emphasizing Congress’s longstanding role in U.S.-Taiwan relations, the Washington metropolitan area’s Taiwanese American community, and the recent 45th anniversary of the Taiwan Relations Act. The signatories argue that a municipal pact would demonstrate solidarity with Taiwan and reject efforts by the Chinese Communist Party to dictate U.S. relationships.

**Who it affects**
The immediate recipient is Mayor Muriel Bowser and her administration. The proposed action would concern the governments and residents of Washington, D.C., and Taipei, while also signaling support to Taiwanese Americans and Taiwan more broadly. The letter acknowledges that international actors could object but urges the city not to let threatened retaliation determine its policy.

**Why it matters**
The document illustrates congressional advocacy for subnational U.S.-Taiwan engagement outside formal diplomatic recognition. It frames a sister-city agreement as a largely symbolic but meaningful response to political pressure and as an expression of community ties and support for Taiwan.

**Key dates and numbers**
- May 13, 2024: Letter date.
- 45th anniversary: Taiwan Relations Act anniversary referenced by the signatories.
- Signatories: John Moolenaar, Marco Rubio, Andy Barr, and Pete Ricketts.
