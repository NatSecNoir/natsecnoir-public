The hearing examined the Chinese Communist Party’s efforts to shape global information through propaganda, censorship, disinformation, economic coercion, and social-media platforms, with particular attention to TikTok and ByteDance.

**What it does** The transcript records opening statements, testimony, and questions concerning the CCP’s concept of “discourse power,” which witnesses described as an effort to dominate international narratives. Members and witnesses discussed TikTok’s ownership by ByteDance, algorithmic reach, alleged content suppression, access to U.S. user data, and the potential use of the platform during elections or a Taiwan conflict. They also addressed WeChat, Tencent, X, Chinese-language media, foreign influence operations, deepfakes, data brokers, and self-censorship by U.S. businesses. Proposed responses included transparency and data-protection laws, restrictions on data transfers, algorithmic disclosure, support for independent journalism, and consideration of the RESTRICT Act.

**Who it affects** The discussion focused on U.S. social-media users, especially young people and the Chinese diaspora; technology and media companies; universities, civil society, and news organizations; and policymakers responsible for national security, privacy, and foreign influence. It also considered effects on democratic institutions, elections, public discourse, and international audiences.

**Why it matters** The hearing framed information technology as a national-security arena in which capability and intent may combine to influence public opinion at decisive moments. Witnesses emphasized that the record establishes serious risks and policy questions, while also noting that evidence of specific manipulation should be evaluated empirically and that democratic resilience remains significant.

**Key dates and numbers**
- November 30, 2023: hearing date; questions for the record were due December 7, 2023.
- 150 million: U.S. TikTok users cited in the hearing.
- 85 pro-CCP accounts across 15 platforms: example of a disinformation campaign discussed.
- TikTok/Instagram post disparities cited for “Stand with Israel,” “Stand with Ukraine,” Uyghurs, Tibet, and Tiananmen: approximately 5×, 12×, 9×, 30×, and 153×, respectively.
