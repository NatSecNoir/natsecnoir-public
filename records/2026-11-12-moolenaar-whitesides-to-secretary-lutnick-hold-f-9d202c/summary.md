The House Select Committee on the CCP urges Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to oppose facilitating U.S. or allied purchases of Chinese memory chips and to strengthen restrictions on YMTC and CXMT.

**What it does** The July 14, 2026 letter frames the global DRAM shortage as a national-security, economic-security, and supply-chain risk rather than a purely commercial issue. It asks the Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) to expand Entity List restrictions on YMTC and expedite a formal review of CXMT for Entity List designation. It also recommends guidance barring U.S. persons and U.S.-incorporated entities from procuring DRAM, high-bandwidth memory, or other memory components from those companies, or from entities on the BIS Entity List or DoD Section 1260H list, for AI systems, data centers, federal information technology, and critical infrastructure.

**Who it affects** The recommendations target YMTC, which the letter identifies as an Entity List company, and CXMT, which it identifies as a DoD Section 1260H Chinese Military Company. They would affect U.S. technology companies and their suppliers, including companies reportedly evaluating Chinese memory, as well as allied supply chains.

**Why it matters** The lawmakers argue that buying subsidized Chinese memory could support PLA-linked dual-use technology, weaken Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron, and create long-term dependence on China for AI infrastructure. They call for coordinated action with South Korea, Japan, and the European Union.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated July 14, 2026; catalogue date is July 16, 2026.
- Three recommended actions: stronger YMTC restrictions, procurement prohibitions, and allied coordination.
- The letter cites CXMT’s DoD Section 1260H designation and YMTC’s 2022 BIS Entity List placement.
