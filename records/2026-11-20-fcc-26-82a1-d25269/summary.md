The FCC adopts new national-security and transparency requirements for interconnected VoIP providers seeking or holding direct access to U.S. telephone numbering resources, while seeking comment on additional safeguards and disclosure obligations.

**What it does** The Fourth Report and Order requires new applicants and existing authorization holders to certify that they are not identified on the FCC Covered List, that their networks do not use covered equipment or services subject to producer- or provider-based determinations, and that they have not been subject to a national-security- or law-enforcement-based denial or revocation of a section 214 authorization. New applicants must also identify the states where they initially intend to request numbers. The FCC reorganizes the direct-access rules, clarifies Bureau oversight, and directs the North American Numbering Plan Administrator to deny pending and future number requests when changed information requires investigation or executive-branch review. The accompanying Further Notice seeks comment on presumptive disqualification, foreign-adversary financing and partnerships, senior officials, cybersecurity and physical-security measures, corporate-history and physical-address disclosures, business-model and affiliate information, facilities readiness, renewal, and other process reforms.

**Who it affects** The rules apply to interconnected VoIP direct-access applicants and existing authorization holders, with particular implications for providers using covered equipment or services or facing section 214-related national-security concerns. State commissions, NANPA, and numbering administrators may gain more information about applicants and intended service areas.

**Why it matters** The order links access to finite numbering resources with the FCC’s broader Covered List and communications-supply-chain security regime, enabling denial, suspension, termination, or revocation when authorization no longer serves the public interest.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Adopted November 19, 2026; released November 20, 2026.
- Comments are due 30 days after Federal Register publication; replies are due 60 days after publication.
- Existing holders and pending applicants must comply within 30 days after required OMB approval is announced.
- Estimated first-year industry burden is less than $600,000, based on approximately 3,000 providers.
