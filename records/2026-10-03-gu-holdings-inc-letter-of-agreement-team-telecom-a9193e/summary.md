The National Security Agreement conditions the proposed FCC submarine cable landing authorization for GU Holdings’ U.S.–Taiwan segment of the Pacific Light Cable Network on extensive national-security, law-enforcement, cybersecurity, access-control, and compliance-monitoring commitments.

**What it does** The agreement requires GU Holdings and, where expressly stated, Google to maintain designated security, information-security, and compliance officers; provide detailed cable-system, equipment, vendor, and network-operations information; secure and screen personnel with access; report incidents and breaches within 48 hours; conduct annual risk assessments; permit audits and site visits; and support lawful U.S. process. It gives the Compliance Monitoring Agencies review or approval authority over equipment, vendors, managed network service providers, facilities, personnel, policies, and certain operational changes. It also restricts Pacific Light Data Communications Co., Ltd. (PLDC) from access, operations, equipment control, and specified connections involving Hong Kong.

**Who it affects** The primary obligations fall on GU Holdings, with specified obligations for Google. DOJ, DHS, and DoD act as the monitoring agencies; the FCC remains the licensing authority. Edge Cable Holdings USA, LLC is addressed in access and operational provisions concerning the cable system.

**Why it matters** The NSA is a Team Telecom mitigation instrument linking protection of U.S. communications infrastructure and data to the FCC license. Breach can support requests to modify, condition, revoke, cancel, or terminate FCC authorization, disable the U.S.–Taiwan segment, impose monetary or other sanctions, and pursue civil or criminal remedies.

**Key dates and numbers**
- FCC referral: September 11, 2020; Committee deferral request: September 16, 2020.
- Agreement effective upon the last signature; signatures are dated December 10 and December 13, 2021.
- GU Holdings generally must report covered incidents within 48 hours and provide follow-up reports within 15 days.
- The agreement covers PLCN’s U.S.–Taiwan segment and limits PLDC access to the segment and related equipment.
