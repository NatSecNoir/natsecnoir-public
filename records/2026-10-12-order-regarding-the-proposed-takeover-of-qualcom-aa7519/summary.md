The order prohibits Broadcom Limited’s proposed takeover of Qualcomm Incorporated and related substantially equivalent transactions on national-security grounds under section 721 of the Defense Production Act.

**What it does** The President finds credible evidence that Broadcom, its partners, subsidiaries, or affiliates could take action threatening U.S. national security. The order permanently prohibits the proposed takeover and substantially equivalent mergers, acquisitions, or takeovers; disqualifies the 15 candidates on Broadcom’s February 20, 2018 Form of Blue Proxy Card from standing for election as Qualcomm directors; and bars Qualcomm from accepting their nominations or votes. It also requires the purchaser and Qualcomm to abandon the transaction, certify completion to CFIUS, and provide weekly compliance certifications until termination is certified. The order supersedes CFIUS’s March 4, 2018 Interim Order.

**Who it affects** The principal parties are Broadcom Limited and its identified affiliates, Qualcomm, the 15 proxy candidates, CFIUS, and Qualcomm stockholders. Qualcomm must provide meeting notice as soon as possible and hold its annual stockholder meeting no later than 10 days after that notice.

**Why it matters** This is a presidential blocking order addressing a proposed cross-border acquisition through CFIUS’s national-security authority. It reaches direct and indirect efforts to circumvent the prohibition and reserves authority for further protective orders.

**Key dates and numbers**
- March 12, 2018: order issued at the White House.
- March 4, 2018: CFIUS Interim Order superseded.
- February 20, 2018: Form of Blue Proxy Card filed with the SEC.
- 15: proxy candidates disqualified.
- 10 days: maximum interval between Qualcomm’s meeting notice and annual stockholder meeting.
- Weekly: compliance certifications required until termination is certified to CFIUS.
