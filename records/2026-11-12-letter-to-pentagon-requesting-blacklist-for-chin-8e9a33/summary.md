The House Select Committee on the CCP asks the Department of Defense to add BOE Technology Group and Tianma Microelectronics to the Chinese Military Companies List, also known as the 1260H List, based on alleged ties to China’s military-civil fusion system and the strategic importance of display technology.

**What it does** This September 24, 2024 letter from Chairman John Moolenaar to Secretary of Defense Lloyd Austin requests that the Pentagon designate the two Chinese display manufacturers under the 1260H framework. It argues that LCD, OLED, and emerging micro-display technologies are important inputs for weapons and augmented- and virtual-reality systems, and asks the Department to develop a plan to reduce U.S. reliance on Chinese display production. The letter itself does not make a designation.

**Who it affects** The request directly concerns BOE and Tianma, including Tianma’s relationship with Aviation Industry Corporation of China (AVIC), and the companies’ operations in the Chengdu Hi-Tech Industrial Development Zone. It also discusses Huawei’s role in that zone and its separate inclusion on the Commerce Department’s Entity List. The intended regulatory audience is the Department of Defense and its 1260H listing process.

**Why it matters** The letter presents display manufacturing as a national-security and defense-supply-chain issue rather than only a consumer-electronics matter. It cites alleged Chinese state subsidies, military-civil fusion connections, and PLA-related relationships, while asserting that disruptions in trade with China could affect production of systems such as Javelin missiles and drones. The document is an advocacy request, and its claims and cited market figures are attributed to the sources and arguments described in the letter.

**Key dates and numbers**
- September 24, 2024: letter date.
- 1260H: Department of Defense list to which the letter asks BOE and Tianma to be added.
- LCD production capacity attributed to China: 72%, compared with 0% in 2004; OLED capacity: 51%, compared with 1% in 2014.
- Chinese companies and government attributed 90% of global micro-display capital expenditures.
- BOE and Tianma together attributed 48% of global LCD smartphone-screen shipments and 28% of global OLED smartphone shipments.
