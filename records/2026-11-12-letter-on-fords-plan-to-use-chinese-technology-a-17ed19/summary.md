The committees urge Commerce and Treasury to investigate Chinese technology companies tied to Ford’s planned CATL battery plant, consider Entity List or sanctions designations, and require Ford to explain its due diligence.

**What it does** The January 22, 2024 correspondence transmits requests to Commerce’s Bureau of Industry and Security to investigate four PRC-based companies for possible addition to the Commerce Entity List. A separate letter asks Treasury’s Office of Foreign Assets Control to investigate one company’s alleged provision of IT, cloud-integration, online-payment, and software services to North Korean sanctions-evasion networks and Xinjiang surveillance activity. The committees also ask Ford to make an official available for an interview about its agreements with CATL and its due diligence. The supplied text redacts the names of the companies under investigation.

**Who it affects** The requests concern Ford Motor Company, Contemporary Amperex Co. Limited (CATL), Commerce, Treasury, BIS, OFAC, and the four unnamed PRC companies. They also implicate entities described as connected to the PLA, North Korean government-related activity, China’s Ministry of Public Security, Xinjiang surveillance, and companies already on U.S. sanctions or export-control lists.

**Why it matters** The letters frame the proposed Michigan battery facility as a supply-chain, cybersecurity, human-rights, and sanctions-compliance concern. They allege that Chinese providers could supply plant design, integration, authentication, business-process, payment, and other IT systems, creating risks to Ford’s sensitive information and potentially supporting sanctioned activity.

**Key dates and numbers**
- January 22, 2024: letters to Commerce, Treasury, and Ford.
- February 5, 2024: requested deadline for agency responses and Ford interview dates.
- Four: PRC companies Commerce was asked to investigate for Entity List placement.
- One: company Treasury was asked to investigate for possible sanctions designation.
