The House Select Committee on the CCP and the House Committee on Education and the Workforce request that University of California officials produce extensive records and answer questions about Berkeley’s joint institute with Tsinghua University and the Shenzhen government.

**What it does** The July 13, 2023 letter expresses concern that the Tsinghua-Berkeley Shenzhen Institute (TBSI) provides the People’s Republic of China access to Berkeley research, expertise, students, and networks. It asks for documents concerning TBSI’s contracts, governance, funding, research, intellectual-property arrangements, export-control compliance, affiliations, publications, and relationships with Chinese entities. It also directs the recipients to preserve hard-copy and electronic records.

**Who it affects** The requests are addressed to University of California President Michael V. Drake and UC Berkeley Chancellor Carol T. Christ. They cover UC and Berkeley, TBSI, Tsinghua University, the Shenzhen government, and Tsinghua Education Foundation North America, as well as research involving or connected to Entity List companies and organizations including Huawei, ZTE, and Tencent.

**Why it matters** The letter links university research and funding relationships to concerns about civil-military fusion, advanced semiconductors, dual-use technology, foreign funding disclosure, and possible access by restricted Chinese entities. It specifically raises Berkeley’s reported failure to disclose foreign gifts or contracts of $250,000 or more under section 117 of the Higher Education Act.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated July 13, 2023; response requested by July 27, 2023.
- TBSI team reportedly won a 7-nanometer chip-design competition in April 2023.
- The letter cites a reported $220 million Shenzhen government agreement and 23 numbered information requests.
- Section 117 disclosure threshold: $250,000 or more from a foreign source in a calendar year.
