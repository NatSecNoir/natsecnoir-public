The House Committee on Small Business, the House Committee on Science, Space, and Technology, and the House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party ask HHS to provide information and strengthen safeguards against foreign exploitation of the SBIR and STTR programs.

**What it does** The letter asserts that China is exploiting taxpayer-funded innovation through CCP-linked venture capital, research partnerships, talent-recruitment programs, PRC-based subsidiaries, and state-backed investment. It cites a 2021 DoD-commissioned report finding that DoD SBIR programs lacked adequate due-diligence capabilities to assess national-security risks. The committees request annual data on unsuccessful and flagged applications across Phases I, II, and III, including applications flagged for foreign influence, national-security risks, export-control or dual-use concerns, inadequate vetting, and other security red flags. They request the information by March 12, 2025, with data dating back to January 20, 2021.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy, Jr. and concerns HHS participation in the SBIR and STTR programs, applicant companies, American small businesses, and federal agencies responsible for program oversight and national security.

**Why it matters** The committees contend that weaknesses in oversight may allow foreign adversaries to obtain sensitive technology and intellectual property, undermining U.S. technological leadership, economic competitiveness, and national security. They indicate that SBIR/STTR authorization was scheduled to expire on September 30, 2025, and that Congress would consider legislative and policy changes.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated February 26, 2025.
- Requested response deadline: March 12, 2025.
- Requested data period begins January 20, 2021.
- SBIR/STTR authorization was set to expire September 30, 2025.
- The letter says small businesses comprise 99.9 percent of U.S. businesses.
