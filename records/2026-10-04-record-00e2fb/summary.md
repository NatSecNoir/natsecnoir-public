The FCC seeks comment on limiting existing authorizations to prohibit the continued importation and marketing of specified Anzu Robotics communications and video-surveillance equipment covered by section 1709 of the FY2025 NDAA, while preserving existing user operation and defined government and testing uses.

**What it does** This Public Notice tentatively classifies Anzu equipment, including FCC IDs 2BBYS-RAPTOR and 2BBYS-RRC01, as covered equipment and proposes to prohibit its importation and marketing under 47 CFR § 2.939(e). The proposed restriction would cover advertising, distribution, and sale, and would apply to Anzu and its affiliates, subsidiaries, and partners for the specified equipment. It would not revoke underlying authorizations, prohibit federal-government use or commercial testing and product development, or affect continued use of devices already purchased. Anzu would be required to cease importation and marketing 30 days after Federal Register publication if the proposal is adopted.

**Who it affects** The proposal directly affects Anzu, its importers, distributors, retailers, and customers, as well as commenters and manufacturers of covered UAS-related equipment. Existing users could continue operating equipment already in their possession.

**Why it matters** The notice extends the FCC’s Covered List framework to previously authorized equipment added to the list after authorization. The FCC relies on a 2025 national-security determination concerning foreign-produced UAS and section 1709 equipment, citing risks including unauthorized surveillance, sensitive-data exfiltration, and supply-chain vulnerabilities, while seeking evidence on economic and supply-chain effects.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Released August 10, 2026; comments are due 30 days after Federal Register publication.
- PS Docket No. 26-184; DA 26-832.
- Section 1709 of the FY2025 NDAA; proposed 30-day implementation period after publication.
- Proposed scope includes FCC IDs 2BBYS-RAPTOR and 2BBYS-RRC01.
