The letter from House Select Committee on the CCP Chairman John Moolenaar to imprisoned publisher Jimmy Lai expresses solidarity with Lai and condemns the Chinese Communist Party’s persecution of religious faith, free expression, and democratic institutions in Hong Kong and China.

**What it does** The letter praises Lai’s testimony and resistance, presents his Christian faith as a source of freedom that the CCP cannot control, and argues that the Party’s campaign of “sinicization of religion” is subjugation rather than religious policy. It describes alleged repression of churches, missionaries, Falun Gong, the Church of Almighty God, Uyghur Muslims, journalists, and pastors, and pledges that Congress will continue holding those responsible for Lai’s imprisonment accountable.

**Who it affects** The immediate recipient is Jimmy Lai, identified as being held at Stanley Prison in Hong Kong. The letter also addresses people suffering religious and political persecution in China and Hong Kong, while criticizing the CCP and the government and institutions operating under its authority, including under Xi Jinping.

**Why it matters** The document frames Lai’s imprisonment and the CCP’s treatment of religion as part of a broader conflict between state control and individual conscience. It places Hong Kong’s erosion of the rule of law, suppression of free expression, and religious persecution within the Select Committee’s stated concerns about the CCP’s global ambitions.

**Key dates and numbers**
- October 17, 2025: date of the letter.
- The letter references more than half a million Uyghur Muslims allegedly remaining in camps in Xinjiang.
- Lai is addressed at Stanley Prison, Hong Kong.
