The report alleges that NASA and universities have inconsistently enforced the Wolf Amendment, allowing NASA-supported bilateral research with Chinese entities—including defense-affiliated institutions—to proceed without required disclosures or certifications.

**What it does** This investigative report examines NASA’s implementation of the Wolf Amendment, which restricts NASA and the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy from using appropriated funds for bilateral cooperation, collaboration, or coordination with the PRC or Chinese-owned companies unless Congress authorizes the activity or the FBI certifies that it poses no national-security risk. It analyzes more than 2,000 publications identified through bibliometric research, highlights hundreds of potential violations, and presents case studies involving NASA awards, co-authorships, dual affiliations, Chinese defense-linked institutions, and ambiguous arrangements involving third-country data or joint-venture universities. The report also discusses alleged false certifications by Arizona State University and Stanford University concerning NASA Award No. 80NSSC23K0265.

**Who it affects** The report focuses on NASA, NASA-funded universities and researchers, NASA’s Office of Inspector General, the FBI, the Department of Justice, and institutions collaborating with PRC universities, laboratories, or companies. Named examples include the University of Delaware, Ohio State University, Duke University, Beihang University, Harbin Institute of Technology, and Northwestern Polytechnical University.

**Why it matters** The committee argues that weak disclosure, screening, and post-award monitoring expose taxpayer-funded research and dual-use knowledge to diversion supporting China’s technological and military modernization. It commends NASA’s recent creation of a research-security office and related reforms but calls for stronger investigations, standardized award-number disclosures, clearer guidance, possible suspensions or debarments, and a centralized National Research Security, Integrity, and Compliance Center.

**Key dates and numbers**
- 2011: The Wolf Amendment was enacted.
- February 12, 2026: Committee leaders requested NASA records concerning potential violations.
- Fewer than 50: Wolf Amendment certifications NASA reportedly issued to date.
- More than 2,000: Publications identified in the committee’s bibliometric search; hundreds were characterized as potential violations.
- January 2023–December 2026: Period of performance for NASA Award No. 80NSSC23K0265.
