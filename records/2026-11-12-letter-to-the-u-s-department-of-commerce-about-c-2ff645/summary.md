The three congressional committees request that the Department of Commerce provide information and procedures concerning alleged foreign exploitation of the SBIR and STTR programs and associated national-security risks.

**What it does** The letter describes concerns that China and Chinese Communist Party-linked actors obtain taxpayer-funded U.S. technology through venture capital, university partnerships, talent-recruitment programs, PRC-based subsidiaries, and state-backed investment. It asks Commerce for annual application, flagging, and outcome data; criteria and procedures for identifying and handling suspicious applicants; interagency communication procedures; monitoring practices; and information about adoption and verification of common disclosure requirements.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to Secretary of Commerce Howard W. Lutnick and concerns applicants and recipients in all three SBIR/STTR phases, including U.S. small businesses whose technologies may present foreign-influence, export-control, dual-use, or other security concerns. The letter is signed by the chairmen of the House Small Business Committee, House Science, Space, and Technology Committee, and House Select Committee on the CCP.

**Why it matters** The committees characterize weak due diligence and oversight as vulnerabilities that can enable transfer of sensitive research and intellectual property to a strategic adversary. They seek data to support legislative and policy changes before the programs’ authorization expires, while emphasizing protection of U.S. innovation and the industrial base.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated February 26, 2025; requested response deadline: March 12, 2025.
- Requested data period begins January 20, 2021.
- SBIR/STTR authorization was scheduled to expire September 30, 2025.
- The letter states that small businesses comprise 99.9 percent of U.S. businesses.
