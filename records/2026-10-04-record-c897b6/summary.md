The FCC proposes a $734,872 forfeiture against Eken Group Limited for submitting incorrect U.S.-agent information in three equipment-authorization applications and failing to answer an Enforcement Bureau Letter of Inquiry.

**What it does** The Notice of Apparent Liability finds that Eken apparently violated sections 1.17(a)(2) and 2.911(d)(7) of the FCC’s rules by identifying GSS Service Inc. at a physical Colorado address where the agent was not located and could not accept service of process. The FCC also finds that Eken willfully failed to respond to an April 19, 2024 LOI concerning alleged privacy and security issues in Eken’s wireless consumer IoT devices. The proposed penalty consists of $551,154 for three continuing inaccurate-address violations and $183,718 for the continuing failure to respond.

**Who it affects** The action directly affects Eken, a Hong Kong-based manufacturer of video doorbells, security cameras, and other smart-home devices sold through U.S. online marketplaces. It also underscores obligations for foreign equipment-authorization applicants and their designated U.S. agents.

**Why it matters** The FCC treats a valid U.S. agent and accurate certifications as essential to serving foreign manufacturers and enforcing equipment rules. The action links truthful filings and cooperation with investigations to consumer privacy, device security, and national-security oversight.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Adopted and released November 21, 2024; FCC 24-122.
- Applications at issue: December 18, 2023; February 29, 2024; March 4, 2024.
- LOI response deadline: May 19, 2024.
- Proposed forfeiture: $734,872; payment or a challenge due within 30 days of release.
