The agreement extends the provisional national security agreement governing GU Holdings’ temporary operation of the U.S.–Taiwan portion of the Pacific Light Cable Network System as a condition of FCC authorization.

**What it does** The parties incorporate the April 7, 2020 Provisional National Security Agreement and extend its terms through the expiration or voluntary surrender of GU Holdings’ March 8, 2021 STA application, but no later than September 17, 2021. The underlying agreement requires U.S.-based and screened network operations, government review of personnel, facilities, vendors, equipment, and network changes, incident reporting, security policies, audits, and compliance oversight. It also restricts connections involving Hong Kong and bars Pacific Light Data Communications Co. Ltd. from operating equipment or performing network-management functions for the U.S.–Taiwan segment.

**Who it affects** The principal regulated party is GU Holdings, an indirect wholly owned subsidiary of Google LLC. Oversight is assigned to DOJ, DHS, and DoD as Compliance Monitoring Agencies, with FCC authorization conditioned on compliance.

**Why it matters** The extension preserves national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety controls over continued commercial submarine-cable operations while the FCC considers the temporary authorization. Breach can support requests to modify, revoke, cancel, or terminate FCC authorization and other civil or criminal remedies.

**Key dates and numbers**
- April 7, 2020: provisional agreement; April 8, 2020: initial STA granted.
- September 22, 2020: prior STA granted through March 21, 2021.
- March 8, 2021: application for the extended STA, FCC File No. SCL-STA-20210308-00015.
- March 16–18, 2021: signature dates shown; September 17, 2021: outside expiration date.
- The agreement invokes reporting periods of 2, 5, 10, 15, 30, 45, 60, and 72 days for specified compliance matters.
