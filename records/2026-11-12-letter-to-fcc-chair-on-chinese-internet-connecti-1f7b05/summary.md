The House Select Committee on the Strategic Competition Between the United States and the Chinese Communist Party asks FCC Chair Jessica Rosenworcel to provide information and consider actions addressing security risks from PRC-linked cellular connectivity modules.

**What it does** The August 7, 2023 letter asks whether the FCC and partner agencies can track Quectel, Fibocom, and other PRC-based cellular IoT modules in U.S. networks; whether the FCC shares the committee’s concerns; whether it is considering the Covered List or certification requirements for individual modules; and whether additional statutory authority is needed. The committee requests a response by August 21, 2023.

**Who it affects** The inquiry is directed to the FCC and concerns cellular modules embedded in IoT devices, including cars, medical equipment, tractors, smart devices, electric vehicles, drones, first-responder body cameras, and telecommunications equipment. It specifically identifies Quectel and Fibocom and notes their reported connections to international technology companies and PRC legal and state-security obligations.

**Why it matters** The committee presents connectivity modules as remotely controllable links that can disable devices or access data moving between devices and web servers. It argues that PRC control could create risks for critical infrastructure, sensitive data, and U.S. communications networks, while noting that alternative U.S. and allied suppliers may reduce the effect of restrictions.

**Key dates and numbers**
- August 7, 2023: letter date.
- August 21, 2023: requested response deadline.
- Four questions address tracking, risk assessment, Covered List or certification options, and statutory authority.
- FCC 22-84, paragraph 282, is cited regarding component-part measures.
- H. Res. 11 is cited as the committee’s authority.
