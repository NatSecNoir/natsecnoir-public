The House Select Committee on the CCP, joined by Senate Armed Services Committee Ranking Member Roger Wicker and House Education and the Workforce Committee Chair Virginia Foxx, requests records and explanations from Georgia Tech concerning its collaborations, funding, and research links involving Tianjin University and affiliated Chinese entities.

**What it does** This May 9, 2024 letter asks Georgia Tech President Ángel Cabrera to respond by May 23, 2024, to 19 categories of requests. The requests cover entities and researchers involved with Tianjin University, contracts and memoranda, joint laboratories, conferences, recruiting, student and alumni affiliations, research-security policies, and communications concerning reporting under section 117 of the Higher Education Act. The letter also asks for a rationale concerning the reported absence of at least $250 million in Shenzhen government funding for the Georgia Tech Shenzhen Institute.

**Who it affects** The principal recipient is Georgia Tech, including the Georgia Tech Research Institute, Georgia Tech Shenzhen Institute, and related programs such as Tianjin Nanosystems. The letter names Tianjin University, Bright Stone Innovation Technology Group, Beijing 718 Yousheng Electronics, and China Aerospace Science and Technology Corporation, and focuses on collaborations with entities described as linked to the People’s Liberation Army or listed by the Commerce Department.

**Why it matters** The committee frames the relationships as potential national-security, export-control, research-security, and foreign-funding-reporting concerns. It specifically raises questions about Georgia Tech’s compliance with Entity List restrictions and the use of Department of Defense-supported research infrastructure in work involving sensitive semiconductor technology.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated May 9, 2024; requested response deadline May 23, 2024.
- Georgia Tech–Tianjin University collaboration is described as continuing since at least 2013.
- The requests cover 19 categories of records and information.
- The letter identifies four China-origin funding entries totaling $5,320,340 from 2020 and 2021.
- It cites at least $250 million allocated for construction of the Georgia Tech Shenzhen Institute.
