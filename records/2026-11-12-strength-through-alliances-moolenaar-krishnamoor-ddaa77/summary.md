The House Select Committee on the CCP’s chairman and ranking member urge Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth to sustain and resource AUKUS as a central response to the People’s Republic of China’s military expansion and coercive activity.

**What it does**
The July 22, 2025 letter expresses bipartisan congressional support for AUKUS, the strategic partnership among Australia, the United Kingdom, and the United States. It endorses both Pillar I, focused on undersea capabilities, and Pillar II, focused on advanced defense capabilities, and calls for continued investment in submarine industrial bases, workforces, and force structure. The lawmakers highlight Australia’s $3 billion commitment to these efforts and urge an “all hands on deck” approach to implementation.

**Who it affects**
The letter is addressed to the Secretary of Defense and concerns the Department of Defense, Congress, and the governments and defense establishments of the three AUKUS partners. It frames the partnership as relevant to U.S. and allied deterrence in the Indo-Pacific, particularly in light of the PLA Navy’s growing reach and recent activity near Australia and New Zealand.

**Why it matters**
The document records congressional support for maintaining and strengthening AUKUS amid concern over the CCP’s nuclear, conventional, cyber, and space modernization. It links alliance investment and expanded undersea capacity to collective defense, deterrence, and protection of U.S. interests and partners in the Indo-Pacific.

**Key dates and numbers**
- July 22, 2025: Date of the letter.
- 3: AUKUS partner countries—Australia, the United Kingdom, and the United States.
- $3 billion: Australia’s stated commitment to support AUKUS-related efforts.
- 2: AUKUS pillars endorsed in the letter.
- 2: PLA Navy aircraft carriers reportedly deployed simultaneously to the western Pacific in June 2025.
