The House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party raises concerns about Alfred University’s Confucius Institute, research relationship with a Chinese military-linked university, and receipt of Department of Defense funding for hypersonic weapons research.

**What it does** The document announces that Chairman Mike Gallagher sent letters to Defense Secretary Lloyd Austin and Alfred University President Mark Zupan seeking information about the university’s continued Confucius Institute, its research agreement with China University of Geosciences in Wuhan, and safeguards for sensitive U.S. military research. It cites Section 1062 of the 2021 National Defense Authorization Act, which bars the Department of Defense from funding an institution of higher education that hosts a Confucius Institute after the specified statutory date unless the institution closes the institute or receives a waiver.

**Who it affects** The inquiry concerns the Department of Defense, Alfred University, its research partners, and officials responsible for administering or receiving the cited funding. The document states that Alfred received a five-year, $13.5 million contract for research related to hypersonic weapons and maintains ties to a Chinese university described as participating in classified Chinese military defense projects.

**Why it matters** The letters frame the arrangement as a research-security and taxpayer-funding concern, particularly because the Chinese university’s advanced materials work is described as related to Alfred’s DOD-funded research. They also seek clarification before the statutory funding restriction takes effect.

**Key dates and numbers**
- May 31, 2023: Letters announced.
- October 1, 2023: Section 1062 funding prohibition is stated to apply after this date.
- 2027: Alfred’s five-year contract is described as ending.
- $13.5 million: Value of the contract identified in the document.
