The President modifies the January 3, 2025 prohibition of Nippon Steel’s proposed acquisition of United States Steel, allowing the transaction only if the parties execute and maintain a qualifying national security agreement (NSA).

**What it does** The order affirms the prior finding that the transaction could threaten U.S. national security but finds that the risk can be adequately mitigated by specified conditions. It amends the earlier order to prohibit the proposed transaction, and substantially similar transactions, unless Nippon Steel, its U.S. affiliates, and U.S. Steel execute with the Department of the Treasury and appropriate CFIUS agencies an NSA materially consistent with the draft provided by the government on June 13, 2025, by closing. CFIUS is authorized to monitor and enforce the arrangement and to use remedies available under section 721. The order strikes two provisions of the January 3 order and reserves authority for further action.

**Who it affects** The direct parties are Nippon Steel Corporation, Nippon Steel North America, Inc., 2023 Merger Subsidiary, Inc., and United States Steel Corporation. The order also directs the Secretary of the Treasury to transmit it to those parties and empowers CFIUS agencies to oversee compliance.

**Why it matters** It illustrates presidential review under section 721 of the Defense Production Act and replaces an unconditional prohibition with a conditional path dependent on a binding national security agreement and continuing oversight.

**Key dates and numbers**
- March 14, 2024: CFIUS received the voluntary notice.
- December 23, 2024: CFIUS referred the matter to the President.
- January 3, 2025: President Biden’s order prohibited the transaction.
- April 7, 2025: A de novo review was directed.
- May 21, 2025: CFIUS submitted its recommendation.
- June 13, 2025: This order was issued and the draft NSA was submitted.
