The licensees commit to extensive security, operational, reporting, and oversight measures to address national-security and law-enforcement risks associated with the Amitié submarine cable system linking the United States, United Kingdom, and France.

**What it does** This Letter of Assurances conditions the proposed FCC cable landing license on compliance with commitments made to DHS. The licensees must designate U.S.-citizen security points of contact; provide cable-system, equipment, vendor, and contractor information; enable interruption of U.S. traffic; obtain DHS approval for network operations center locations and operators; and give advance notice of equipment, vendor, testing, service, and operational changes. The agreement also requires logical and physical security, personnel screening, incident reporting, employee training, annual compliance reports, third-party audits, and DHS consultation and site visits.

**Who it affects** The obligations bind Edge Cable Holdings USA, Aqua Comms, Cable & Wireless Americas Systems, and Microsoft Infrastructure Group, including their successors, assigns, subsidiaries, and affiliates. DHS receives approval, inspection, reporting, and objection rights, while the FCC may act on the license if the commitments are breached. The U.S. Navy must approve deployment of distributed acoustic sensing technology on the marine portion of the cable.

**Why it matters** The LOA illustrates Team Telecom mitigation for a privately operated international submarine cable: it gives the government continuing visibility into infrastructure, personnel, vendors, changes, access, and incidents rather than relying only on licensing review.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Dated April 1, 2021; FCC File No. SCL-LIC-20200807-00036.
- Primary and secondary security points of contact must be nominated within 30 days of execution.
- System and principal-equipment information is due within 60 days; many security policies and training are due within 90 days.
- DHS generally has 90 days to approve or disapprove notified actions; breaches must be reported within 48 hours.
- Foreign ownership or control above 10 percent triggers notice within 15 days.
