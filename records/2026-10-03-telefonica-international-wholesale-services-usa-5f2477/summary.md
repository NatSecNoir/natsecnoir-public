Telxius Cable USA undertakes binding national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety commitments to the U.S. Department of Homeland Security and Department of Defense in connection with the FCC application to land and operate the private BRUSA submarine cable system linking the United States and Brazil.

**What it does** This Letter of Assurances requires Telxius to provide system, network-management, equipment, vendor, and restoration information; maintain the ability to interrupt U.S. traffic; give advance notice of material equipment, vendor, service, and operational changes; report unauthorized access, disruptions, disclosures, and material breaches; notify the U.S. Government of specified foreign ownership or control changes; and file annual compliance reports. It also permits government consultation and reasonable-notice site visits, including to the network operations center in Lurín, Peru. A breach may prompt the U.S. Government parties to ask the FCC to modify, condition, revoke, cancel, terminate, or nullify relevant authorization.

**Who it affects** The commitments bind Telxius Cable USA and its successors, assigns, subsidiaries, and affiliates, and concern the BRUSA Applicants and the cable’s U.S. domestic communications infrastructure, wet infrastructure, principal equipment, and network operations. DHS, DoD, and the Defense Information Systems Agency receive information, consultation, and visitation rights; the FCC is the licensing authority whose pending authorization may be conditioned on compliance.

**Why it matters** The LOA illustrates Team Telecom mitigation for a foreign-linked submarine cable authorization. It gives the U.S. Government continuing visibility into the cable’s architecture, equipment, suppliers, operations, incidents, and ownership, while preserving a mechanism to require interruption of U.S. traffic and seek FCC action if commitments are breached.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Dated July 30, 2017; associated FCC application docket: SCL-LIC-20160330-00011.
- System information and the initial principal-equipment list: within 60 business days of execution, and system information within 15 business days upon request.
- Advance notice: 10 business days for material equipment modifications and 30 business days for vendor, service, or significant operational changes.
- Incident and control-change reports: generally within 10 business days of discovery or learning of the relevant information; foreign ownership threshold: above 10 percent.
- Annual compliance report: on or before each anniversary of execution.
