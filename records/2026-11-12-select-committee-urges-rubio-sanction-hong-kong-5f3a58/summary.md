The House Select Committee on the CCP urges Secretary of State Marco Rubio to determine whether Hong Kong Police Commissioner Joe Chow Yat-ming qualifies for U.S. sanctions over alleged involvement in the erosion of Hong Kong’s autonomy and freedoms.

**What it does** This July 9, 2025 letter asks the State Department to review Commissioner Chow under the Hong Kong Human Rights and Democracy Act, the Hong Kong Autonomy Act, and Executive Order 13936. The committee cites recent charges against pro-democracy activist Joshua Wong and police actions surrounding the anniversary of the Tiananmen Square Massacre as evidence of continuing repression. It does not itself impose sanctions or make a final eligibility determination.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to Secretary Rubio and concerns Chow, the successor to former Hong Kong Police Commissioner Raymond Siu. It also addresses the broader treatment of Hong Kong activists and residents, including Wong and people detained during the June 4 anniversary period.

**Why it matters** The letter seeks continuity after the United States sanctioned six Beijing and Hong Kong officials in March 2025, including then-Commissioner Siu. It frames possible action against Chow as a response to Chinese Communist Party-directed efforts to undermine Hong Kong’s autonomy, democracy, and human rights.

**Key dates and numbers**
- July 9, 2025: Letter date.
- March 2025: The State Department sanctioned six officials, and sanctions against then-Commissioner Siu were cited by the committee.
- June 6, 2025: New charges against Joshua Wong were reported in the letter.
- June 4 anniversary period: Police detained or took away individuals during commemorations in Hong Kong.
