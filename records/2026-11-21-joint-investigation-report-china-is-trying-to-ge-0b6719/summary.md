The Committees find that PRC-developed open-weight AI models are becoming widely embedded in American products and operations, creating national-security, cybersecurity, provenance, and dependency risks that warrant federal restrictions alongside investment in trusted American alternatives.

**What it does** The report presents five findings based on company productions, briefings, classified briefings, public research, and three case studies. It describes adoption driven by low cost, customization, and near-frontier performance; alleged industrial-scale distillation of American models; PRC state financing and policy support; changing commercial terms; and security behavior that ordinary benchmarks may not detect. It recommends an American open-weight AI initiative, model-provenance standards and an AI bill of materials, restrictions on PRC-developed models and derivatives in federal systems, federal contract work, and high-risk critical-infrastructure functions, recurring model evaluations, stronger information sharing, sanctions and Entity List use against illicit distillers, scrutiny of American training-data sales, and allied standards work.

**Who it affects** The recommendations would affect federal agencies and contractors, critical-infrastructure owners and operators, state and local grant recipients, AI developers, cloud and inference providers, model repositories, and companies already using PRC models. The report’s case studies concern Airbnb, Anysphere, and DoorDash, and it identifies Alibaba, DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, Zhipu, MiniMax, ByteDance, StepFun, and Xiaomi in its broader analysis.

**Why it matters** The report argues that downloaded and modified weights can conceal model lineage and make dependence difficult to inventory or unwind. It distinguishes reduced direct data exposure from U.S.-based hosting from unresolved developer, behavior, continuity, and supply-chain risks, and warns that open-weight cyber capabilities can spread rapidly.

**Key dates and numbers**
- The joint investigation opened in April 2026; the report is dated November 20, 2026.
- PRC models represented 41% of downloads on a leading open-model repository over the preceding year.
- DoorDash reported that replacing one embedded PRC model could take up to six months.
- NIST assessed Zhipu’s GLM-5.3 as capable of autonomously building end-to-end cyber exploits; safeguards were reportedly reduced to 3–6% refusal rates for about $4,400.
