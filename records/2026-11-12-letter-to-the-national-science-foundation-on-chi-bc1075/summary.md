The House Select Committee on the CCP urges the National Science Foundation (NSF) to revoke ACCESS credentials and computing access for Chinese entities appearing on U.S. restricted or national-security-related lists, and to review the program’s eligibility, credentialing, allocation, and oversight controls.

**What it does** The January 15, 2026 letter to NSF Interim Director Brian Stone describes the Advanced Cyberinfrastructure Coordination Ecosystem: Services & Support (ACCESS) program and its mechanism for allowing international collaborators to obtain access through a U.S.-based principal investigator. It asks NSF to review all PRC-based entities with approved credentials, immediately revoke access for listed entities, and provide records concerning credentials, projects, allocations, resource providers, and possible priority given to Chinese users over U.S. applicants.

**Who it affects** The request concerns NSF, ACCESS, U.S.-funded high-performance computing providers, U.S.-based principal investigators, and Chinese institutions identified by the Committee, including the People’s Liberation Army’s National University of Defense Technology, the “Seven Sons of National Defense” universities, and institutions associated with SASTIND. It specifically discusses systems at the Pittsburgh Supercomputing Center, Texas Advanced Computing Center, and Texas A&M University.

**Why it matters** The Committee argues that remote access could enable restricted Chinese entities to use advanced U.S. computing for AI, modeling, simulation, and other dual-use work without directly obtaining controlled hardware or export licenses. The letter highlights NVIDIA H100 GPUs on Texas A&M’s ACES system and raises deemed-export and national-security concerns.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated January 15, 2026; requested information was due February 6, 2026.
- ACCESS was funded through a five-year, $52 million NSF award.
- The letter identifies NVIDIA H100 GPUs as subject to U.S. export controls for certain destinations, including the PRC.
