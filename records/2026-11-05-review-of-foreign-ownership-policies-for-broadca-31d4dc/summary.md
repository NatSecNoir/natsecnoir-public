The FCC proposes to codify and streamline its review of foreign ownership in broadcast, common carrier, and aeronautical radio licensees under Section 310(b) of the Communications Act.

**What it does** The Notice of Proposed Rulemaking seeks comment on defining the “controlling U.S. parent,” clarifying deemed voting interests and advance approvals, specifying disclosures for trusts and trustees, and requiring complete restatements for amended petitions. It also proposes clarifying that foreign investors need not reside in the United States. The FCC asks whether to extend its ownership-calculation methodology and remedial safe harbor to privately held entities, define the contents of remedial petitions, and establish procedures for broadcast applications and noncommercial educational and low-power FM stations during foreign-ownership reviews.

**Who it affects** The proposals affect U.S. and foreign investors, licensees, applicants, spectrum lessees, privately held and publicly traded controlling parents, and petitioners seeking approval for ownership above statutory benchmarks. Executive Branch agencies participating in national-security and law-enforcement review may also be affected.

**Why it matters** The FCC frames the proposals as ways to improve predictability, reduce supplemental filings and processing burdens, and preserve review of national-security, law-enforcement, foreign-policy, and trade-policy concerns.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Adopted April 28, 2025; released April 29, 2025.
- Comments are due 30 days after Federal Register publication; replies are due 60 days after publication.
- Section 310(b)(3) generally establishes a 20% direct foreign-ownership benchmark; Section 310(b)(4) establishes a 25% benchmark for foreign ownership of a controlling U.S. parent.
- Proposed advance approval could permit up to 49.99% non-controlling ownership or, where control is approved, up to 100%.
