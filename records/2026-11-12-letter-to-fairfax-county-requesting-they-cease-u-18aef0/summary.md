The House Select Committee on the CCP urges Fairfax County to exclude PRC-made drones from future procurement and partnership programs because of asserted national-security, data-security, and supply-chain risks.

**What it does** The September 26, 2024 letter describes concerns about unmanned aerial systems manufactured in or owned by the People’s Republic of China, identifying Shenzhen DJI Sciences and Technologies Ltd. and Autel Robotics as examples. It cites prior federal actions and reports, including DJI’s addition to the Commerce Department’s Entity List, its identification by Treasury as part of the PRC Military-Industrial Complex, and the federal prohibition on the Department of Defense buying and using PRC-made drones and related components. The letter also warns that DJI technology may be marketed through “white-labeling,” including an arrangement involving Anzu Robotics.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to the Fairfax County Board of Supervisors and concerns the County’s public-safety, emergency-services, and fire-department UAS operations. It also calls on Fairfax County to assist other counties and Virginia in adopting a drone-procurement policy consistent with state and federal approaches.

**Why it matters** The committee contends that PRC-made UAS and sensors may expose high-resolution imagery and other sensitive information to the PRC government. It also argues that PRC government subsidization creates competitive pressure on local governments and suggests federal grant programs should help them select more secure alternatives.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter date: September 26, 2024; catalogue date: September 27, 2024.
- DJI was placed on the Commerce Entity List in December 2020 and identified by Treasury as part of the PRC Military-Industrial Complex in December 2021.
- The letter cites a January 2024 CISA and FBI report on Chinese-manufactured UAS and states that seven states had grounded PRC-related UAS fleets as of May 2023.
