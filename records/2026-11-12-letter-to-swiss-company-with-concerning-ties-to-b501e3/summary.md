The House Homeland Security Committee and House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party requested that ABB make its U.S. country holding officer, Michael Gray, available to testify publicly about the company’s relationship with ZPMC and security risks involving ship-to-shore cranes.

**What it does** The letter says the committees’ prior efforts to work with ABB in good faith, including meetings with company personnel and counsel and an offer to brief ABB’s U.S. head of security, did not resolve their concerns. It asks ABB’s counsel to provide Gray’s availability for an upcoming joint-committee hearing and to contact committee staff by 5:00 p.m. on January 23, 2024, to schedule his appearance.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to ABB and concerns its provision of hardware and equipment for the U.S. maritime industry in cooperation with Shanghai Zhenhua Heavy Industries Company Limited (ZPMC). The committees identify ZPMC as accounting for nearly 80 percent of ship-to-shore cranes at U.S. maritime ports and note ABB’s contracts with the Department of Homeland Security and Department of Defense.

**Why it matters** The letter advances a congressional investigation into maritime-sector cybersecurity and supply-chain risks linked to the People’s Republic of China. It asks whether ABB should continue working for U.S. government agencies while maintaining commercial ties with Chinese state-owned enterprises and calls the installation of ABB equipment and technology in China onto U.S.-bound cranes unacceptable.

**Key dates and numbers**
- January 18, 2024: letter announcement date.
- January 23, 2024, at 5:00 p.m.: deadline to contact committee staff.
- Nearly 80 percent: ZPMC’s stated share of ship-to-shore cranes at U.S. maritime ports.
- July 2023: committees’ earlier private letter to ABB.
