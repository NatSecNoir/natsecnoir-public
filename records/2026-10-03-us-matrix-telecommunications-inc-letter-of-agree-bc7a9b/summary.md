U.S. Matrix Telecommunications, Inc. offers the U.S. Department of Justice binding assurances intended to address national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety concerns associated with its pending FCC application, with DOJ stating that it will notify the FCC it has no objection to grant after execution.

**What it does** This Letter of Agreement requires U.S. Matrix to make U.S. customer billing, subscriber, and related records available in the United States in response to lawful U.S. process; protect those records and domestic communications from unauthorized access, foreign-law destruction requirements, and unauthorized foreign-government disclosure; and provide DOJ with security architecture, network diagrams, and NOC concepts of operations. It also establishes reporting, notification, annual certification, access, and inspection obligations. DOJ may seek FCC action on relevant authorizations if the commitments are breached.

**Who it affects** The commitments apply to U.S. Matrix and any subsidiary or affiliate providing domestic communications services. They affect U.S. records, domestic communications, network and gateway operations, personnel with route or sensitive-data access, overseas operational personnel, and the company’s U.S. point of presence and point of contact.

**Why it matters** The agreement places operational and data-governance controls on a telecommunications applicant as a condition supporting FCC authorization. It channels foreign-government requests through DOJ, preserves U.S. legal-process access, requires rapid reporting of cyber incidents and material changes, and gives DOJ a mechanism to seek FCC licensing consequences for noncompliance.

**Key dates and numbers**
- July 15, 2015: Letter dated and signed by CEO Eber Lacerda.
- FCC file number: ITC-214-20130111-00010.
- 30 days: deadlines for post-consent architecture and CONOPS submissions, specified notices, and route-access identification.
- 5 business days: deadlines for reporting unauthorized disclosures, cyber threats or incidents, and certain foreign-government or legal developments.
- 48 hours: advance written notice for DOJ domestic-facility visits.
