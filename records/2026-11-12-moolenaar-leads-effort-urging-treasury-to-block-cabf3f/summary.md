A bipartisan group of congressional national-security leaders urges Treasury to strengthen the Outbound Investment Security Program (OISP) under the COINS Act of 2025 to prevent U.S. capital, expertise, and financial services from advancing the Chinese military and strategic-technology base.

**What it does** The February 26, 2026 letter asks Treasury to expand outbound-investment restrictions to biotechnology investments that could support military, coercive, or strategic capabilities; cover additional transaction types such as IPO underwriting and management consulting; and prohibit U.S. investment in publicly traded firms that warrant designation on the Non-SDN Chinese Military-Industrial Complex Companies (NS-CMIC) List. It identifies multiple companies already subject to or associated with U.S. national-security designations as candidates for evaluation, including ChangXin Memory Technologies, Yangtze Memory Technologies, Tencent, Zhipu AI, and DJI.

**Who it affects** The recommendations target U.S. investors, banks, consulting firms, biotechnology companies, and other service providers, as well as Chinese entities in biotechnology, semiconductors, artificial intelligence, telecommunications, and defense-related sectors. Treasury, Commerce, State, and other executive-branch agencies are asked to implement or coordinate the policy response.

**Why it matters** The letter argues that restricting private investment alone leaves channels for U.S. capital and expertise to reach foreign-adversary firms through public markets and professional services. It frames biotechnology dependence, technology transfer, and blacklisted companies’ continued access to American capital as linked national-security risks.

**Key dates and numbers**
- February 26, 2026: Letter date.
- 2025: COINS Act enacted in the National Defense Authorization Act.
- Section 809: Authority cited for restricting additional transaction types and technologies.
- Three recommendations: biotechnology, additional transactions, and public-security investments.
