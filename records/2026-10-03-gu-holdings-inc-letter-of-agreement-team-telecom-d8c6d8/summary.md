The National Security Agreement establishes mitigation conditions for Edge Cable Holdings USA, LLC and Meta Platforms, Inc. in connection with the proposed FCC license for the Pacific Light Cable Network (PLCN), a submarine cable linking the United States with Taiwan and the Philippines.

**What it does** The agreement requires Edge USA and Meta to implement physical, logical, personnel, supply-chain, and cybersecurity controls for the U.S.-Philippines segment. It gives the Compliance Monitoring Agencies—the Departments of Justice, Homeland Security, and Defense—review and approval rights over key personnel, network operations centers, principal equipment, vendors, managed network service providers, and certain changes to the cable system. It also requires incident reporting, annual compliance reports and risk assessments, possible third-party audits, site visits, lawful-process capabilities, and the ability to interrupt U.S.-bound traffic within 24 hours. Pacific Light Data Communications Co., Ltd. is barred from specified access, operations, and equipment involvement absent agency approval.

**Who it affects** The obligations bind Edge USA and Meta, and extend to relevant successors, assigns, subsidiaries, and affiliates. They directly regulate operation of the PLCN U.S.-Philippines segment and constrain PLDC’s role. The FCC is the licensing authority notified when the agreement is executed or modified.

**Why it matters** The agreement is a Team Telecom mitigation instrument tying national-security and law-enforcement safeguards to a submarine cable landing license. Breach may support requests to modify, revoke, terminate, or condition FCC authorization, disable the segment, impose monetary or civil remedies, or pursue other sanctions.

**Key dates and numbers**
- FCC File No. SCL-LIC-20200827-00038 was referred to the Committee on September 11, 2020; review was requested on September 16, 2020.
- Edge USA signed on December 14, 2021; the agreement takes effect on the last signature date, shown as December 14, 2021.
- Key deadlines include 24-hour traffic interruption capability, 48-hour incident reporting, 60-day information submissions, and annual reporting and risk assessments.
