Prepa Networks, LLC agrees to security, reporting, access-control, and operational commitments intended to address DHS national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety concerns related to the Taino-Carib cable landing license.

**What it does** This Letter of Assurances requires Prepa Net to maintain a U.S.-citizen security point of contact; provide DHS with cable-system, facility, equipment, vendor, and organizational information; give advance notice of specified equipment modifications and vendor changes; and permit DHS to approve or object to covered actions. It also requires physical-security and personnel-screening policies, incident reporting, employee instruction and training, recurring compliance reports, and DHS consultations and site visits. Prepa Net must notify DHS of specified foreign ownership or control changes and significant changes in consortium ownership. A breach may lead DHS to ask the FCC to modify, condition, revoke, cancel, terminate, or nullify a relevant authorization.

**Who it affects** The commitments bind Prepa Net, its successors, assigns, subsidiaries, and affiliates, principally concerning the Prepa Net cable landing facility in Isla Verde, Carolina, Puerto Rico, and the U.S. portion of Taino-Carib. DHS receives oversight, approval, objection, inspection, and information-access rights; the FCC is the licensing authority whose authorization may be affected.

**Why it matters** The LOA shows Team Telecom mitigation applied to a submarine cable facility even though Prepa Net held less than five percent of Taino-Carib. It concentrates government visibility and intervention rights over critical equipment, vendors, personnel, facility security, traffic interruption, ownership changes, and incidents.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Taino-Carib was originally licensed in 1992; the cited earlier application is SCL-LIC-19920107-00005.
- Prepa Net generally must provide initial specified information within 45 days of execution and requested information within 30 days.
- Equipment or vendor changes generally require 30 days’ advance notice; emergency modifications require notice within 72 hours when advance notice is impracticable and a follow-up report within 15 days.
- DHS generally has 60 days to approve or disapprove notices, or 30 days for specified emergency equipment actions.
- Security, screening, and training policies or instructions are generally due within 90 days; incidents must be reported within 48 hours, and specified ownership changes within 10 days.
