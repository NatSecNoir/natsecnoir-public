Quintillion Subsea Operations, LLC commits to mitigation measures addressing U.S. national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety concerns in connection with its proposed Alaska submarine-cable authorization.

**What it does** The Letter of Assurances defines Quintillion’s domestic communications infrastructure and related equipment, facilities, providers, and operations, and makes compliance a condition the Agencies will ask the FCC to attach to the requested license. It requires U.S.-citizen security points of contact resident in the United States; equipment and managed-service-provider disclosures; advance notice of material equipment changes, new or changed vendors, outsourcing, and offshoring; policies for foreign-person visits; annual compliance reporting; and notice of specified ownership, control, joint-venture, service, and operational changes. The Agencies receive consultation, inspection, visitation, and information-access rights.

**Who it affects** The commitments bind Quintillion, its successors, assigns, subsidiaries, and affiliates, and cover the Quintillion Cable System, its U.S. facilities and network operations centers, principal equipment, vendors, contractors, subcontractors, and managed network service providers. DHS, DOJ/NSD, FBI, and DoD are the reviewing Agencies.

**Why it matters** The LOA illustrates Team Telecom mitigation for a U.S.-only submarine cable system while preserving oversight if the system expands internationally or its ownership, suppliers, operations, or services change. A breach could support a DHS request that the FCC modify, condition, revoke, cancel, terminate, or nullify relevant authorization.

**Key dates and numbers**
- March 24, 2016: FCC application date identified in the LOA.
- March 24, 2017: LOA date.
- 60 days: deadline for security POCs and the initial equipment/provider list.
- 14, 30, and 45 days: recurring notice, objection, and advance-notice periods.
- More than 10%: foreign ownership interest triggering notice under specified circumstances.
