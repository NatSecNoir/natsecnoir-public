The three congressional committees ask the Department of Energy to provide information and procedures addressing alleged foreign exploitation of the Small Business Innovation Research (SBIR) and Small Business Technology Transfer (STTR) programs, particularly risks linked to China, by March 12, 2025.

**What it does** The letter identifies alleged channels for transferring taxpayer-funded innovation abroad, including CCP-linked venture capital, partnerships with U.S. universities, talent-recruitment programs, PRC-based subsidiaries, and state-backed investment. It requests annual application outcomes and flagging rates for Phases I, II, and III; breakdowns of national-security, foreign-influence, export-control, dual-use, due-diligence, and other security concerns; information on common disclosure requirements and verification; and copies of criteria and procedures for flagging, handling, interagency communication, and monitoring.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to Secretary of Energy Christopher A. Wright and concerns DOE’s SBIR/STTR program administration. It also implicates U.S. small businesses applying for or receiving program funding, federal agencies coordinating security reviews, and foreign-linked entities alleged to seek access to funded technology.

**Why it matters** The committees characterize weaknesses in due diligence and oversight as a national-security and economic-competitiveness risk. They cite a 2021 DoD-commissioned report stating that DoD SBIR programs lacked adequate capabilities to assess such risks, and seek data dating to January 20, 2021 to inform legislative and policy changes before the programs’ authorization expires.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated February 26, 2025; response requested by March 12, 2025.
- Requested data covers January 20, 2021 onward.
- SBIR/STTR authorization was set to expire September 30, 2025.
- The letter refers to America’s small businesses as 99.9 percent of U.S. businesses.
