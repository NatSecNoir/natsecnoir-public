SHUF, INC. commits to national-security and law-enforcement safeguards in exchange for the Department of Justice notifying the FCC that it has no objection to grant of SHUF’s Section 214 application.

**What it does** SHUF agrees to implement lawful-interception capabilities before offering services subject to the Communications Assistance for Law Enforcement Act (CALEA), maintain those capabilities while providing such services, and undergo a CALEA compliance test. It must report implementation status to the FBI every 90 days until completion, provide at least 30 days’ notice of its U.S. law-enforcement point of contact, and maintain that contact. SHUF must store U.S. customer and subscriber records in the United States, make them available promptly in response to lawful U.S. process, and refer non-U.S. government requests concerning U.S. records or surveillance matters to DOJ.

**Who it affects** The commitments apply to SHUF, its U.S. telecommunications resale operations, and any future facilities-based or interconnected VoIP services subject to CALEA. They support the FBI, DOJ, FCC, and other U.S. law-enforcement agencies’ access to records and authorized surveillance assistance.

**Why it matters** The agreement conditions Team Telecom clearance on operational access, U.S. records custody, and controls over foreign-government requests. A breach could lead the FBI to seek FCC modification, conditioning, revocation, cancellation, termination, or nullification of SHUF’s authorization.

**Key dates and numbers**
- March 5, 2015: Letter of Agreement date.
- FCC file number: ITC-214-20140421-00130.
- Status reports: every 90 days until lawful-interception capabilities are fully implemented.
- U.S. point of contact: provided at least 30 days before CALEA-covered services begin.
- Record production: promptly, and no later than five business days, in response to lawful U.S. process.
