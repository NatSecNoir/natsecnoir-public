The report concludes that DeepSeek poses significant U.S. national-security risks through alleged data transfers to China, censorship aligned with Chinese law, possible unauthorized distillation of U.S. AI models, and use of export-controlled Nvidia chips.

**What it does** The House Select Committee on the CCP examines DeepSeek’s ownership and affiliations, including founder Liang Wenfeng’s control and the company’s relationship with High-Flyer Quant. It describes alleged connections between DeepSeek’s backend infrastructure and China Mobile, a company identified by the Department of Defense as a Chinese military company under Section 1260H. The report also discusses data collection, possible unencrypted transmissions, politically directed output manipulation, alleged use of OpenAI models for distillation, and estimates of DeepSeek’s Nvidia chip inventory. It recommends expanded export controls, stronger enforcement, remote-access restrictions, end-user tracking, anti-diversion measures, and federal procurement limits on PRC-origin AI models.

**Who it affects** The report focuses on Americans using DeepSeek, U.S. AI developers and semiconductor companies, Commerce BIS and other enforcement agencies, and policymakers responsible for export controls and national-security planning. It also identifies Chinese companies and Singapore-linked intermediaries as relevant to possible data and chip-diversion risks.

**Why it matters** The report frames DeepSeek as both a privacy and information-integrity concern and a test of whether U.S. export controls can prevent PRC access to advanced AI capabilities. Its findings are presented as policy recommendations rather than adjudicated violations.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Report date: April 16, 2025.
- The report cites estimates of at least 60,000 Nvidia chips: 10,000 A100, 30,000 H20, 10,000 H800, and 10,000 H100.
- It reports that DeepSeek responses were altered or suppressed on politically sensitive topics in 85% of tested cases.
- Proposed measures include expanded chip controls, remote-access controls, end-user reporting, location verification, and restrictions on PRC-origin AI models.
