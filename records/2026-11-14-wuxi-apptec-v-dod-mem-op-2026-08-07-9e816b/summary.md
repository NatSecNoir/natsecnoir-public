The U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia preliminarily enjoined the Department of Defense from enforcing, implementing, or otherwise giving effect to WuXi AppTec’s designation as a Chinese military company under Section 1260H.

**What it does** The court held that WuXi AppTec was likely to succeed on its Administrative Procedure Act claim that the designation was arbitrary and capricious. DoD’s three stated rationales were likely defective: it treated 5.32% of an investment fund’s portfolio as 5.32% ownership of WuXi, characterized WuXi’s third-party laboratory role in studies as receiving approval and conducting research with SASTIND-supervised universities, and relied on the same mischaracterization for a study involving the PLA General Hospital. The injunction applies while the case proceeds; the court did not decide that WuXi could never be designated and said DoD could designate it again on an accurate, adequately explained record.

**Who it affects** The order directly protects WuXi AppTec from the challenged designation and its associated consequences, including market and customer reactions. It does not disturb other Section 1260H designations, Section 805 or 10 U.S.C. § 4663, or the BIOSECURE Act as applied to other entities.

**Why it matters** The opinion recognizes that the 1260H label was causing ongoing, difficult-to-repair commercial and reputational harm, including canceled, paused, or redirected programs. It also emphasizes that national-security deference does not excuse inaccurate evidence or missing reasoning in an agency’s stated justification.

**Key dates and numbers**
- DoD’s designation was published in the Federal Register on June 10, 2026.
- WuXi filed suit on June 11, 2026; the court heard argument on July 22, 2026.
- The opinion was dated August 7, 2026.
- The court required a $1.00 injunction bond.
