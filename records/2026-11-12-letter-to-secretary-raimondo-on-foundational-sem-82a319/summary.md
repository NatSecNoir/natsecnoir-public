The House Select Committee on the CCP urges the Departments of Commerce and the U.S. Trade Representative to act against the strategic and economic risks posed by a potential surge of PRC-made foundational semiconductors and requests a briefing within 60 days.

**What it does** The letter argues that foundational, or “mature” and “legacy,” chips underpin critical civilian and military systems and that PRC subsidies, industrial policy, and expanding fabrication capacity could displace competitors and create U.S. supply-chain dependence. It asks whether existing trade authorities can establish “component tariffs” on foundational chips themselves, what additional authorities may be needed, and how the United States can coordinate with trading partners and support alternative markets.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to Commerce Secretary Gina Raimondo and U.S. Trade Representative Ambassador Katherine Tai. It concerns U.S. semiconductor producers and downstream sectors that rely on foundational chips, as well as PRC manufacturers and exporters, including Semiconductor Manufacturing International Corporation. The letter is signed by Committee Chairman Mike Gallagher and Ranking Member Raja Krishnamoorthi.

**Why it matters** The committee frames PRC semiconductor overcapacity and state support as a national-security, economic-security, and industrial-base risk. It warns that concentrated PRC production could give Beijing leverage over the global economy and asks the administration to move from assessing the problem to using trade tools or seeking new authority.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated January 5, 2024; catalogue document date: January 8, 2024.
- Requested agency briefing: within 60 days.
- PRC state-backed semiconductor fund described as seeking an additional $41 billion.
- The letter cites 22 planned or major 300mm fabrication projects in the PRC from 2022 to 2026, compared with 10 in North America.
- It cites estimated PRC exports of $125 billion in semiconductors in 2022.
