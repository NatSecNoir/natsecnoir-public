The House Committees on Small Business, Science, Space, and Technology, and the Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party ask the U.S. Department of Agriculture to provide information and procedures addressing alleged foreign exploitation and national-security risks in its SBIR and STTR programs.

**What it does** The letter describes concerns that China may access taxpayer-funded U.S. innovation through CCP-linked venture capital, university research partnerships, talent-recruitment programs, PRC-based subsidiaries, and state-backed investment. It requests annual data from January 20, 2021, including application success and flagging rates for Phases I, II, and III; the share of flagged applications involving foreign ties, export-control or dual-use concerns, deficient vetting, or other security red flags; and the outcomes of flagged applications. It also seeks USDA’s disclosure requirements, validation practices, flagging criteria, procedures for handling suspicious applicants, interagency communication, and phase-by-phase monitoring.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to Secretary Brooke L. Rollins and concerns USDA’s administration of SBIR and STTR programs, participating American small businesses, applicants, and agencies coordinating national-security reviews. The committees identify China and Chinese state-linked entities as the principal foreign threat.

**Why it matters** The committees argue that weak due diligence can permit the transfer of sensitive research and intellectual property to China, undermining U.S. technological leadership and national security. They cite a 2021 DoD-commissioned report stating that DoD SBIR programs lacked adequate capabilities to assess these risks and seek comparable information from USDA.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated February 26, 2025.
- Requested response deadline: March 12, 2025.
- Requested data period begins January 20, 2021.
- SBIR/STTR authorization was scheduled to expire September 30, 2025.
- The letter states that small businesses comprise 99.9 percent of U.S. businesses.
