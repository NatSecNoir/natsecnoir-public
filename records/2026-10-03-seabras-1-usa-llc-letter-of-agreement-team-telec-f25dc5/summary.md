The Letter of Assurances sets Seabras 1 USA’s commitments to DHS to address national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety concerns as a condition of FCC authorization to land and operate the Seabras-1 submarine cable between New Jersey and Brazil.

**What it does** Seabras 1 USA agrees to nominate a U.S.-citizen Security Point of Contact and alternate, provide DHS with an updated Principal Equipment list, submit annual compliance reports, consult with DHS, and permit reasonable-notice visits to the U.S. portion of the cable system. The equipment disclosures cover domestic communications infrastructure, wet infrastructure, manufacturers, models, vendors, contractors, software, network topology, and changes to network operations centers or landing stations. The commitments bind successors, assigns, subsidiaries, and affiliates. If Seabras breaches them, DHS may ask the FCC to modify, condition, revoke, cancel, terminate, or nullify a relevant authorization.

**Who it affects** The obligations apply primarily to Seabras 1 USA and covered successors and affiliates. DHS receives ongoing reporting, consultation, and inspection rights; the FCC is asked to condition the pending license on compliance.

**Why it matters** This agreement illustrates Team Telecom mitigation for a privately operated, carrier-neutral submarine cable connecting the United States and Brazil. It makes security governance, equipment transparency, and government access enforceable through the FCC licensing process.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Dated November 7, 2016.
- Security Point of Contact and updated Principal Equipment list due within 60 business days of the LOA.
- Proposed POC changes require at least 10 business days’ advance notice.
- Compliance reports are due on each anniversary of the LOA’s effective date.
- FCC File No. SCL-LIC-20160115-00002 covers the license application.
