The Letter of Agreement records Smarteletec S.L.’s binding mitigation commitments to the U.S. Department of Justice, including the FBI, addressing national-security and law-enforcement risks associated with its application for global resale-based international telecommunications authority under Section 214.

**What it does** The agreement requires Smarteletec to establish a U.S. law-enforcement point of contact; screen personnel with access to domestic communications, principal equipment, lawful U.S. process, or U.S. records; report foreign persons with access and the countries from which access occurs; and obtain or provide DOJ review of storage locations, equipment, and service providers. It also requires security and cybersecurity plans, annual compliance reports, site visits, possible third-party audits, and written reporting of known or suspected security incidents within 72 hours.

**Who it affects** The obligations apply principally to Smarteletec, its owners, personnel, affiliates, contractors, service providers, equipment vendors, and successors. DOJ, including the FBI, receives notice, objection, non-objection, approval, audit, and inspection rights; the FCC is the licensing agency whose Section 214 authorization may be conditioned or subject to enforcement action.

**Why it matters** The LOA ties continued telecommunications authority to controls over U.S. customer and communications data, lawful-intercept requests, foreign access, supply-chain risks, and corporate changes. It expressly requires disclosure of covered-list or Commerce Entity List-related equipment and services and permits FCC action if Smarteletec breaches the commitments or fails to resolve identified risks.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Executed and dated May 22, 2026.
- FCC file: ITC-214-20230720-00076; Team Telecom case: 2025-015.
- Initial LEPOC, foreign-access, equipment, service-provider, and storage submissions are generally due within 30 days after FCC approval; plans are due within 60 days.
- Security incidents and service-provider breaches must be reported within 72 hours of discovery or learning of the relevant information.
- Annual reports are due one year after FCC approval and each year thereafter.
