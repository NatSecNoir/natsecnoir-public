The National Security Agreement conditions the proposed FCC authorization for the JUNO submarine cable system on Seren Juno’s implementation of extensive national-security, law-enforcement, cybersecurity, and operational safeguards.

**What it does** The agreement requires Seren Juno and Seren Juno America to appoint a U.S.-citizen Security Officer, provide the Compliance Monitoring Agencies (CMAs) detailed cable, network, equipment, vendor, and personnel information, and obtain or accommodate CMA review of specified facilities, equipment, vendors, testing, and changes. It requires policies for logical and physical security, personnel screening, incident response, protection of U.S. Records, and training. The companies must be able to interrupt U.S. traffic, report qualifying incidents within 48 hours, support audits and site visits, and submit annual compliance reports. Breach may support a recommendation that the FCC modify, revoke, or terminate the license.

**Who it affects** The obligations bind Seren Juno Network Co., Ltd. and Seren Juno Network America, Inc., their successors, affiliates, contractors, service providers, personnel, and entities with access to or capacity on JUNO. DHS, DOJ, and DoD serve as the CMAs through the Committee.

**Why it matters** The NSA is the Team Telecom mitigation framework for a private fiber-optic submarine cable connecting California and Washington with Japan. It links continued authorization to government oversight of foreign participation, supply-chain risk, access to communications infrastructure, and protection of U.S. data.

**Key dates and numbers**
- FCC referral to the Committee: February 10, 2023.
- Signatures: October 13, 16, and 19, 2023; obligations take effect on the last signature.
- Security Officer nomination: within 30 days; incident reporting: within 48 hours.
- Foreign ownership or control notice threshold: above 5 percent.
