This executive order directs the Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States (CFIUS) to apply and elaborate existing statutory national-security factors in reviewing covered transactions, while leaving its jurisdiction unchanged.

**What it does** It instructs CFIUS to consider supply-chain resilience and security, U.S. technological leadership, aggregate investment and acquisition patterns, cybersecurity risks, and risks involving sensitive data. The order identifies sectors and capabilities including microelectronics, artificial intelligence, biotechnology and biomanufacturing, quantum computing, advanced clean energy, critical materials, and climate adaptation technologies. It also directs the Office of Science and Technology Policy to periodically publish technology-sector information relevant to technological leadership, permits CFIUS to request industry analyses from the Commerce Department’s International Trade Administration, and requires periodic review and reporting on CFIUS processes and regulations.

**Who it affects** The direction applies to CFIUS’s review of covered transactions and principally affects foreign persons, U.S. businesses, and transactions involving critical supply chains, technologies, infrastructure, cybersecurity capabilities, or sensitive data concerning U.S. persons.

**Why it matters** The order broadens the context in which CFIUS may assess national-security risk, including cumulative foreign investment, third-party ties, cyber access, and the potential exploitation or transfer of sensitive data. It emphasizes that risks may arise beyond the defense industrial base and from transactions whose implications become apparent only when viewed alongside earlier investments.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Signed September 15, 2022; published September 20, 2022, at 87 FR 57369.
- Implements section 721 of the Defense Production Act of 1950, 50 U.S.C. 4565, as amended by FIRRMA.
- Federal Register document number: 2022-20450.
- Sections 2 and 3 elaborate factors under section 721(f)(1)–(10) and add considerations for aggregate investment, cybersecurity, and sensitive data.
