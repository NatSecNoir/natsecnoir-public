The FCC announces that Department of War Conditional Approvals exempt specified advanced robotic devices and power inverters from the FCC Covered List, subject to continuing conditions.

**What it does** The Public Safety and Homeland Security Bureau updates the Covered List and its accompanying Conditional Approvals appendix to exclude Volley Automation’s automated guided vehicles and autonomous mobile robots; Dynapower’s CPS-2500, CPS-1250, and MPS-125 series power inverters; and Power Electronics España’s GEN3 utility-scale solar inverters, including specified battery-storage inverters. The exemptions depend on compliance with the companies’ onshoring plans and updated product vetting. The notice also republishes the broader Covered List and prior approvals.

**Who it affects** The action directly affects the three named manufacturers and users, sellers, equipment authorization applicants, test labs, and telecommunications certification bodies dealing with the identified devices. It also clarifies the status of foreign-produced advanced robotic devices and power inverters generally, which remain covered unless conditionally approved.

**Why it matters** The notice illustrates how Executive Branch national-security determinations and company-specific approvals modify the scope of the FCC’s supply-chain restrictions without removing the underlying categories from the Covered List. A failure to meet conditions, or a false statement in an application, may cause an approval to terminate and equipment to return to the list.

**Key dates and numbers**
- July 28, 2026: advanced robotic devices and power inverters were added to the Covered List.
- August 20, 2026: the power-inverter entry was updated after a Department of War determination.
- October 15, 2026: the FCC received the three new Conditional Approvals.
- October 16, 2026: notice released and Covered List updated; cited rules are 47 CFR §§ 1.50002(a) and 1.50003.
