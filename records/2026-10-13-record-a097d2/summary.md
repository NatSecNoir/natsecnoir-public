The FCC revokes Odyssey Robot LLC’s equipment authorizations for two uncrewed-aircraft-system devices because Odyssey made false statements that the equipment was not covered communications equipment prohibited from authorization.

**What it does** The Order determines that FCC IDs 2BSYT-FMAWZOD (UAS) and 2BSYT-YMAWZOD (Remote Controller) are covered UAS and UAS critical components produced in a foreign country, and that Odyssey’s applications and supporting materials falsely represented otherwise. It finds that the devices were improperly authorized, and revokes both authorizations under section 2.939(d) of the FCC’s rules, effective immediately as of the Order’s date. The FCC also relied on Odyssey’s failure to respond to investigative inquiries and an Order to Show Cause.

**Who it affects** The action directly affects Odyssey and the marketing or operation of the two identified devices. It also concerns eTak Worldwide Corporation, which Odyssey identified as a Texas assembler but which denied any business or assembly relationship, and TÜV Rheinland (Shenzhen) Co. Ltd., where testing activities were conducted.

**Why it matters** The decision illustrates the FCC’s Covered List rule that prohibited equipment cannot receive authorization and that applicants must provide signed attestations. It also shows that the Commission may use a streamlined revocation process when an applicant falsely attests that equipment is not covered, including where the applicant does not respond to FCC inquiries or show-cause proceedings.

**Key dates and numbers**
- January 12, 2026: Odyssey made the relevant attestations; April 20, 2026: the two certifications were granted.
- June 10, 2026: the FCC sent Odyssey a Letter of Inquiry; June 25: it sent a deficiency letter.
- July 21, 2026: the FCC issued an Order to Show Cause, allowing 10 calendar days to respond; Odyssey did not respond.
- August 11, 2026: the Order was adopted and released, revoking FCC IDs 2BSYT-FMAWZOD and 2BSYT-YMAWZOD.
