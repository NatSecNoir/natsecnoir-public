The House Select Committee on the CCP urges the National Science Foundation (NSF) to review and revoke ACCESS credentials for Chinese entities—especially entities on U.S. restricted or national-security-related lists—that can remotely use U.S.-funded high-performance computing resources.

**What it does** The January 16, 2026 letter asks Interim NSF Director Brian Stone to immediately review PRC-based ACCESS users and revoke credentials and associated computing access for designated entities. It requests records of Chinese entities with approved credentials, information about whether they used U.S. principal-investigator allocations for independent non-U.S. research, project and resource-provider details, and information about access granted while U.S. applicants awaited approval or compute time.

**Who it affects** The request concerns NSF’s ACCESS ecosystem, U.S.-based principal investigators and collaborators, major U.S. computing providers, and Chinese institutions identified by the Committee, including the People’s Liberation Army’s National University of Defense Technology, the “Seven Sons of National Defense” universities, and institutions associated with SASTIND. The letter specifically discusses systems containing NVIDIA H100 GPUs.

**Why it matters** The Committee argues that remote access could allow Chinese or blacklisted entities to conduct advanced simulations, data processing, and AI model training without directly obtaining export-controlled GPUs or licenses. It frames ACCESS eligibility, credentialing, allocation, and oversight as potential deemed-export and national-security risks.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter date: January 16, 2026.
- Requested response deadline: February 6, 2026.
- ACCESS was funded through a five-year, $52 million NSF award.
- The letter contains four information requests.
