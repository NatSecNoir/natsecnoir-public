The report finds that the People’s Republic of China is systematically using financial contributions, personnel placements, peacekeeping deployments, and affiliated organizations to reshape the United Nations around Chinese Communist Party objectives.

**What it does** The Select Committee examines four channels of influence: China’s growing contributions to the UN and its agencies; placement of Chinese nationals in senior UN positions; deployment of Chinese peacekeeping personnel to strategically important regions; and use of government-organized non-governmental organizations (GONGOs) with consultative status at the UN Economic and Social Council. It also recommends greater transparency, stronger oversight, reassessment of China’s “developing country” status, and a coordinated U.S. strategy to counter Chinese influence.

**Who it affects** The analysis concerns the United Nations, its specialized agencies and peacekeeping missions, U.S. policymakers, UN member states, genuine civil-society organizations, and groups from the PRC, Hong Kong, and Macau holding or seeking consultative status. It identifies the Chinese Communist Party’s United Front system as a key mechanism linking state, party, diaspora, academic, and ostensibly nongovernmental actors.

**Why it matters** The report argues that China converts financial and institutional participation into diplomatic leverage, access, and military influence. It highlights risks to UN impartiality, human-rights scrutiny, peacekeeping integrity, and U.S. influence over international standards and governance.

**Key dates and numbers**
- China’s UN regular-budget share rose from 2.053% in 2006 to 20.004% in 2026.
- China contributed $2.48 billion to the UN in 2024, compared with $14.26 billion from the United States.
- Chinese nationals employed by the UN increased from 579 in 2005 to 1,664 in 2023.
- China had 1,659 peacekeeping personnel deployed as of January 2026.
- At least 134 PRC organizations held ECOSOC consultative status as of December 2022.
