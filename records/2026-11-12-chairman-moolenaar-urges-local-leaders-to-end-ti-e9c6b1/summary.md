Chairman John Moolenaar urges the leaders of Montgomery County, Maryland, and Des Moines, Iowa, to end or reconsider exchanges and sister-city ties involving Chinese Communist Party-linked organizations that he characterizes as vehicles for influence operations.

**What it does** The two letters describe student exchanges sponsored by the People’s Republic of China and either the China Education Association for International Exchange (CEAIE) or the Chinese People’s Association for Friendship with Foreign Countries (CPAFFC). The Montgomery County letter recommends ending future engagements with CEAIE and the CCP and reviewing county exchange programs with the PRC. The Des Moines letter recommends reconsidering the city’s sister-city arrangement with Shijiazhuang City and ceasing future engagements with CPAFFC pending review. The letters also provide an appendix identifying CEAIE leaders and their reported affiliations.

**Who it affects** The recommendations are directed to Montgomery County Executive Marc Elrich and Des Moines Mayor Connie Boesen, with copies to the respective public school boards. The cited exchanges involved Montgomery County students and Roosevelt High School’s Bridges 2 Harmony gospel choir. The letters also discuss the sister-city relationships of Montgomery County with Xi’an and Des Moines with Shijiazhuang City.

**Why it matters** The committee frames ostensibly educational, cultural, and youth exchanges as potential United Front influence activity. It relies on cited assessments from the National Counterintelligence and Security Center concerning hidden ties of quasi-official entities and subnational partnerships, and raises additional privacy and censorship concerns about requiring students to use WeChat. The recommendations are congressional oversight and policy requests, not binding orders.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letters dated August 18, 2025; interior page headers also state August 14, 2025.
- The Montgomery County exchange involved 31 students from 12 high schools and occurred in April 2025.
- The Des Moines exchange ran July 12–21, 2025.
- The letters reference H.R. 4519, a proposed restriction on certain sister-city relationships.
