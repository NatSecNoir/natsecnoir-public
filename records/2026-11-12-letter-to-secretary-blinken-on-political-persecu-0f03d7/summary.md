The House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party urged Secretary of State Antony J. Blinken to press Hong Kong authorities to end prosecutions under the Hong Kong National Security Law, release political prisoners, and take measures against Hong Kong’s representatives in the United States.

**What it does**
In a February 28, 2023 letter, Chairman Mike Gallagher described the trial of 47 Hong Kong democracy advocates and broader detentions under the Hong Kong National Security Law. He asked the Secretary of State to call on the Hong Kong Government to end what the letter characterizes as political prosecution and immediately release all political prisoners. He also requested actions to hold the Hong Kong Government accountable, including revoking the privileges, exemptions, and immunities of Hong Kong Economic and Trade Offices (HKETOs) in the United States and shutting them down or consolidating them with the People’s Republic of China’s diplomatic missions.

**Who it affects**
The requests concern the Hong Kong Government, HKETOs operating in the United States, Hong Kong political prisoners and opposition activists, and the State Department. The letter invokes the Hong Kong Autonomy Act and Executive Order 13936 while discussing Hong Kong’s reduced autonomy and the application of the Hong Kong National Security Law.

**Why it matters**
The letter presents congressional pressure for a U.S. response to Hong Kong’s political repression and argues that the national security law has undermined judicial independence and the “One Country, Two Systems” principle. It asks the executive branch to use diplomatic and accountability measures rather than treating the issue as solely an internal Hong Kong matter.

**Key dates and numbers**
- February 28, 2023: Letter date and the second anniversary of charges against the 47 democracy advocates.
- February 6, 2023: Start of the cited 90-day trial.
- June 2020: Enactment of the Hong Kong National Security Law in Beijing, as described in the letter.
- March 28, 2023: Requested deadline for the Secretary of State’s reply.
- The letter cites at least 150 people taken into custody since enactment and more than 1,200 political prisoners detained in Hong Kong.
