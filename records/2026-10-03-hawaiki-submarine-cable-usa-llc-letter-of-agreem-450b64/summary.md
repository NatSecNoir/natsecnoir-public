The Letter of Assurances sets out Hawaiki’s national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety commitments as a condition of FCC authorization to land and operate a submarine cable connecting the United States, Australia, and New Zealand.

**What it does** The agreement requires Hawaiki to designate U.S.-citizen security points of contact; provide network, equipment, vendor, and restoration information; maintain the ability to interrupt U.S. traffic; and obtain U.S. government review or non-objection for specified equipment, vendor, and operational changes. It also requires cybersecurity, physical-security, personnel-screening, incident-reporting, employee-instruction, change-in-services, foreign-ownership, and annual-compliance-reporting measures. The USG Parties may conduct compliance visits and may ask the FCC to modify, condition, revoke, cancel, terminate, or nullify relevant authorizations if the commitments are breached.

**Who it affects** The commitments bind Hawaiki and its successors, assigns, subsidiaries, and affiliates, and cover the Hawaiki Cable System, its domestic communications infrastructure, network operations centers, principal equipment, vendors, contractors, and personnel with relevant access. DHS and DoD are the USG Parties; the FCC is the licensing authority.

**Why it matters** This is a Team Telecom mitigation agreement linking FCC submarine-cable licensing to continuing government oversight of system architecture, access, supply-chain participants, foreign control, and incident response.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Dated December 15, 2017.
- FCC docket: SCL-LIC-20160906-00019.
- Security contacts and key policies are generally due within 60 business days of execution.
- Equipment and vendor changes generally require 30 business days’ advance notice; material equipment modifications generally require 10 business days’ notice.
- Foreign ownership or control above 10 percent must be reported within 10 business days.
