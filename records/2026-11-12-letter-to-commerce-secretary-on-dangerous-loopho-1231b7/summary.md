The House Select Committee on the CCP urges Commerce Secretary Gina Raimondo to close alleged loopholes in BIS’s December 2, 2024, export-control rule on advanced semiconductor technology destined for China.

**What it does** The letter acknowledges the addition of some Huawei semiconductor facilities to the Entity List and BIS controls on high-bandwidth memory, but criticizes the rule’s new License Exception Restricted Fabrication Facility and facility-specific licensing policies. Chairman John Moolenaar argues that exemptions for certain SMIC facilities, Huawei-related companies, and connected or nearby fabrication facilities could permit continued access to U.S. technology and enable diversion. He asks Raimondo to strengthen the controls and preserve documents and communications concerning the rule for the incoming transition team.

**Who it affects** The concerns principally involve BIS, Commerce, Huawei and its semiconductor-facility network, Semiconductor Manufacturing International Corporation (SMIC), Si’En Qingdao, SwaySure Technology, Shenzhen Pengxinxu Technology, and ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT). The letter also addresses U.S. companies that may ship advanced technology to covered or connected Chinese semiconductor fabs.

**Why it matters** The letter presents the committee’s view that entity-by-entity and fab-by-fab controls may be vulnerable to circumvention, including transfers through “wafer bridges” or tunnels between facilities with different licensing policies. It calls for stronger counter-diversion measures and questions why BIS would adopt policies the committee believes could benefit Chinese semiconductor firms.

**Key dates and numbers**
- December 2, 2024: BIS export-control update discussed in the letter.
- December 4, 2024: Letter date shown on the document.
- October 16, 2024: Earlier committee letter on Huawei’s semiconductor network referenced.
- SMIC policies cited for Beijing, Shanghai, and Shenzhen facilities differ, including a 200mm-wafer carveout for SMIC Shanghai.
