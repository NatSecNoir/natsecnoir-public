The FCC proposes a $188,491 forfeiture against Zhejiang Dahua Technology for apparently willfully and repeatedly violating the requirement that Covered List entities publicly provide contact information for covered-equipment-producing affiliates and subsidiaries.

**What it does** The Notice of Apparent Liability finds that Zhejiang Dahua missed the March 8, 2023 deadline under 47 CFR § 2.903(d), and that its later filings did not cure the violation because they were incomplete, confidential, or not made under the rule. The FCC treats the failure as continuing until the company submitted a compliant list on September 22, 2025, after an enforcement investigation began. It proposes the statutory maximum forfeiture rather than issuing a final penalty.

**Who it affects** The action directly affects Zhejiang Dahua, a video-surveillance-equipment manufacturer headquartered in Hangzhou, China, and establishes consequences for a Covered List entity’s failure to disclose relevant affiliates and subsidiaries. The required information is intended for the FCC, equipment-authorization applicants, Telecommunications Certification Bodies, government stakeholders, and the public.

**Why it matters** The FCC says accurate, public affiliation information supports enforcement of the Secure Networks Act and Secure Equipment Act and helps prevent covered or insecure equipment from receiving authorization. The proposed amount reflects a $3,000 daily base forfeiture applied to a continuing violation, capped by statute.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Adopted February 12, 2026; released February 19, 2026.
- Filing deadline: March 8, 2023; compliant filing: September 22, 2025.
- Proposed forfeiture: $188,491; base forfeiture: $3,000 per day.
- Zhejiang Dahua has 30 calendar days after release to pay or seek reduction or cancellation.
