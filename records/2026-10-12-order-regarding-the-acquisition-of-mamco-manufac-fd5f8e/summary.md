The President prohibited CATIC’s acquisition of control of MAMCO Manufacturing, Inc. and required CATIC and its affiliates to divest their interests in MAMCO and its assets.

**What it does** The order, issued under section 721 of the Defense Production Act of 1950, finds credible evidence that CATIC’s control of MAMCO “might take action that threatens to impair” U.S. national security and concludes that other legal authorities were inadequate. It prohibits the acquisition, requires divestiture, and authorizes CFIUS to impose written conditions for a limited extension of the divestiture deadline. Until divestiture is complete, CFIUS may verify MAMCO’s operations through government access to relevant facilities, records, equipment, technical data, and personnel. The Attorney General may take steps necessary to enforce the order.

**Who it affects** The order directly binds CATIC, its subsidiaries and affiliates, and MAMCO, a Washington-incorporated corporation. It also assigns implementation and enforcement roles to CFIUS and the Attorney General.

**Why it matters** This is a presidential national-security intervention in a foreign acquisition under section 721, using prohibition, mandatory divestiture, compliance verification, and enforcement authorities rather than approval subject to a mitigation agreement.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Adopted February 1, 1990; published February 6, 1990, at 55 FR 3935–3936.
- Divestiture deadline: May 1, 1990, three months after the order, with a possible extension of no more than three months.
- Authority cited: section 721 of the Defense Production Act of 1950, 50 U.S.C. App. 2170.
