The report concludes that weak safeguards around federally funded research and U.S.-China university partnerships have enabled the transfer of dual-use expertise and technology to the PRC’s defense and security establishment.

**What it does** The majority staff report examines DOD- and intelligence-community-funded publications coauthored with PRC-affiliated researchers; six case studies involving nuclear weapons materials, artificial intelligence, lasers, nanotechnology, graphene semiconductors, and robotics; and three joint institutes—TBSI, GTSI, and SCUPI. It also alleges failures by UC Berkeley and Georgia Tech to report foreign gifts and contracts under section 117 of the Higher Education Act. The report recommends stronger restrictions on research collaborations, post-award limits involving blacklisted entities, adoption of the DETERRENT Act, and tougher enforcement.

**Who it affects** The analysis concerns federally funded researchers, DOD and other grant-making agencies, U.S. universities, PRC universities and companies, and institutions connected to the Commerce Department Entity List or DOD research-security lists. Georgia Tech had decided to terminate GTSI, while Berkeley reported that it was beginning to unwind its TBSI ownership.

**Why it matters** The report argues that fundamental-research publication rules do not protect applied know-how developed through taxpayer-funded work. It presents university partnerships and talent programs as channels for military-civil fusion, technology transfer, surveillance capabilities, and PRC defense modernization.

**Key dates and numbers**
- 8,829 DOD-funded publications and 185 intelligence-community-funded publications with PRC-affiliated coauthors were identified.
- More than 2,500 of 5,000 surveyed DOD-funded collaborations involved the PRC defense research and industrial base.
- Section 117 requires reporting of foreign gifts or contracts worth $250,000 or more in a calendar year.
- The DETERRENT Act, H.R. 5933, passed the House 246–170 on December 6, 2023.
