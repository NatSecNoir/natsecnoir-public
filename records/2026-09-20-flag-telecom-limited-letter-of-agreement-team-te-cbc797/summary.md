The Letter of Agreement (LOA) sets binding national-security and law-enforcement mitigation commitments for FLAG Telecom Limited, GCX Holdings Limited, and 3i Infrastructure plc in connection with their proposed transfer of control of FLAG and the FLAG Atlantic-1 (FA-1) submarine cable system to Reef Bidco Limited.

**What it does** The LOA requires the parties to maintain a U.S.-based security officer; provide detailed cable-system, network, equipment, vendor, and access information; preserve the ability to interrupt U.S. traffic; and obtain agency review of network operations centers, principal-equipment changes, vendors, testing, and certain technology deployments. It also establishes logical and physical security, personnel-screening, incident-reporting, training, change-in-control, annual-reporting, audit, consultation, and site-visit obligations. The parties must report specified incidents within 48 hours and require relevant third-party disclosures within 72 or 48 hours, depending on the event.

**Who it affects** The commitments bind FLAG, GCX, 3iN, and their successors, assigns, subsidiaries, affiliates, vendors, contractors, and relevant personnel concerning FA-1, which connects the United States, France, and the United Kingdom. DHS and DoD are the compliance-monitoring agencies; the Committee for the Assessment of Foreign Participation in the United States Telecommunications Services Sector is expected to recommend FCC approval conditioned on the LOA.

**Why it matters** The agreement makes national-security mitigation a condition of FCC action on the transfer application and gives the monitoring agencies continuing approval, objection, audit, and enforcement leverage. A breach may support a recommendation to modify, condition, revoke, cancel, or terminate relevant FCC authorization. The LOA supersedes the November 25, 2020 Letter of Assurances, while preserving outstanding DHS obligations under that agreement.
