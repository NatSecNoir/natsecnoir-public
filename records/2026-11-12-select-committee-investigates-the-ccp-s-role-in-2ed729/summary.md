The report concludes that the Chinese Communist Party and the People’s Republic of China materially enable the global illicit fentanyl trade through export subsidies, government support for trafficking companies, state-linked ownership, weak enforcement, and obstruction of U.S. investigations.

**What it does** The bipartisan Select Committee investigation examines the PRC fentanyl supply chain, combining review of government websites and records, web scraping, undercover communications, corporate materials, and consultations with officials and experts. It identifies VAT rebates for at least 17 illegal Schedule I narcotics, including 14 fentanyl analogues, and describes grants, awards, ownership interests, online sales, censorship gaps, and alleged protection of traffickers. The report recommends a Joint Task Force—Counter Opioids, stronger sanctions, trade and customs measures, and action against payment and e-commerce platforms.

**Who it affects** The findings concern PRC government entities and chemical companies, online marketplaces, financial institutions, shipping and transshipment firms, Mexican cartels, U.S. law-enforcement and intelligence agencies, Congress, and Americans affected by fentanyl and other synthetic narcotics.

**Why it matters** The report frames fentanyl trafficking as a national-security as well as public-health and law-enforcement threat. It argues that legitimate commercial ties make PRC companies and enablers vulnerable to coordinated U.S. economic, legal, trade, and diplomatic pressure, while warning that nitazenes and xylazines could intensify the crisis.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Dated April 16, 2024; the report says the subsidy program existed by at least 2018 and expanded during 2019–2020.
- The Select Committee reviewed seven e-commerce sites, identifying over 2,048 PRC companies and more than 31,000 illicit sales in a dataset of 37,000 unique data points.
- Reported VAT rebates reached 13%; one undercover review found 26 of 27 responding vendors offered immediate sales.
- The report states fentanyl kills over 200 Americans daily and recommends five broad policy actions.
