The House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party asks the Treasury Department to independently assess whether Airwallex’s corporate structure, China and Hong Kong ties, and handling of U.S. financial data create national-security or regulatory risks.

**What it does** The September 17, 2026 letter presents evidence concerning Airwallex’s mainland-China workforce, Chinese investors and banking relationships, Hong Kong contractual entities, payment-card compliance assessor, global engineering and compliance functions, and processing of sensitive financial and identity data. It requests review under Executive Order 14117 and its implementing rules, CFIUS or other authorities, and the Bank Secrecy Act, followed by a classified briefing as appropriate. The Committee expressly says the letter is informational and does not conclude that Airwallex engaged in unlawful conduct.

**Who it affects** The request concerns Airwallex and its U.S., Hong Kong, Singapore, and mainland-China entities, as well as U.S. customers—including companies in artificial intelligence, semiconductors, defense technology, cybersecurity, and financial services—that use Airwallex directly or through platforms such as Brex, Rippling, Deel, Navan, and Zip. Treasury, DOJ, FinCEN, and CFIUS are asked to consider the matter.

**Why it matters** The Committee argues that shared systems, Hong Kong data-processing arrangements, PRC-based personnel, and legal-compulsion authorities could expose U.S. customer financial and personal data, while noting that its technical review found no covert exfiltration channel. It highlights an estimated $130 billion in annualized transaction volume and a conservative count of 712 mainland-China employees.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated September 17, 2026; response requested by October 17, 2026.
- Airwallex founded in 2015; PBOC payment license obtained in 2023.
- EO 14117 signed February 28, 2024.
- Committee identified 181 China-based global-facing roles, including at least 77 senior positions.
