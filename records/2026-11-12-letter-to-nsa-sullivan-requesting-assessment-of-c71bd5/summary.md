The House Select Committee on the Strategic Competition between the United States and the Chinese Communist Party requests that National Security Advisor Jake Sullivan provide a classified-level assessment of Russia’s adaptation to U.S. weapons in Ukraine and the extent to which Russia has shared those lessons with the PRC.

**What it does**
The July 15, 2024 letter asks for information on Russia’s ability to mitigate or counter U.S. weapons systems; Russian sharing of battlefield lessons with the PRC; possible People’s Liberation Army adoption of those lessons or Russian innovations; administration plans to preserve the future effectiveness of American weapons against Russia and the PRC; and steps to hold the PRC accountable for supporting Russia’s war in Ukraine. The request is made by Chairman John Moolenaar and Ranking Member Raja Krishnamoorthi and seeks disclosure at the “appropriate classification level.”

**Who it affects**
The request is directed to Sullivan and copied to Secretary of Defense Lloyd Austin. It concerns U.S. and allied weapons systems supplied to Ukraine, Russian military adaptation and electronic-warfare capabilities, the PRC and PLA, and the broader U.S. defense and industrial base.

**Why it matters**
The committee frames Russian battlefield innovation and potential transfer of countermeasures to China as a national-security risk. It argues that Chinese support has helped sustain Russia’s war effort and that lessons from the conflict could affect the effectiveness of American systems in future theaters. The letter seeks both intelligence on technology transfer and policy responses.

**Key dates and numbers**
- July 15, 2024: letter date.
- July 9, 2024: date referenced for Sullivan’s warning that adversaries are not supporting Russia “for free.”
- Five: requested assessments or actions listed by the committee.
- Two signatories: Moolenaar and Krishnamoorthi.
