The House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party urges Treasury Secretary Janet Yellen to expand CFIUS jurisdiction over land transactions near Coast Guard facilities and other sensitive national-security sites, and to permit retroactive review of certain earlier purchases.

**What it does** This letter responds to Treasury’s proposed rule concerning CFIUS coverage of certain real-estate transactions near military installations. It argues that Coast Guard facilities, Department of Energy national laboratories, maritime ports, and critical telecommunications and energy infrastructure should receive stronger protection, and asks Treasury to close gaps in the proposed regulatory framework. In particular, the members seek authority for CFIUS to review land purchases made before a facility is designated as a sensitive site.

**Who it affects** The recommendations are directed to Treasury and would affect foreign persons and companies seeking to purchase or invest in real estate near Coast Guard facilities, national laboratories, and other critical sites. The letter also concerns CFIUS, the Coast Guard, and other agencies responsible for protecting national-security infrastructure.

**Why it matters** The committee contends that omitted facilities remain vulnerable to foreign-adversary exploitation and that nonretroactivity could allow adversaries to acquire land before a site is listed, leaving those transactions outside future review. It cites recent Russian and Chinese military activity near U.S. maritime areas as context for the Coast Guard’s security mission.

**Key dates and numbers**
- September 17, 2024: date recorded for the letter; the document page is dated September 16, 2024.
- June 2024: Russian ships were reported approximately 30 miles off Florida.
- The letter references Treasury’s proposed rule on CFIUS coverage near military bases and requests further changes before finalization.
