The FCC adopts comprehensive new safeguards and reporting requirements for international section 214 authorization holders while proposing additional measures to address foreign-adversary risks to U.S. telecommunications services and infrastructure.

**What it does** The Report and Order requires targeted disclosures about cross-border facilities used across the U.S.-Mexico and U.S.-Canada borders, including locations, facility owners, IP prefixes, autonomous system numbers, principal equipment, services, and foreign-adversary access. It establishes a 5% ownership-reporting threshold, requires information about services and geographic markets, mandates disclosure of certain third-party service providers, and adds cybersecurity, Covered List, and character-qualification certifications. It also creates a one-time information collection, requires reporting of material changes within 30 days, strengthens commencement and discontinuance rules, and provides for revocation of authorizations held by entities that failed to respond to the earlier collection. The Commission declines to adopt a renewal or formal periodic-review requirement. The accompanying Further Notice seeks comment on presumptive disqualifying conditions, prohibited arrangements and equipment, access restrictions, cybersecurity and physical-security plans, incident reporting, and related requirements.

**Who it affects** The rules apply to applicants and holders of international section 214 authority, including applications for modification, assignment, and transfer of control; facilities-based applicants face the most detailed cross-border disclosures.

**Why it matters** The order expands the FCC’s continuing visibility into network infrastructure, equipment, routing, ownership, and access that could enable traffic rerouting, monitoring, disruption, or exposure of sensitive U.S. communications and records. It also standardizes information already sought through Executive Branch mitigation agreements.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Adopted October 29, 2026; released November 5, 2026.
- Approximately 1,235 holders responded to the earlier collection out of roughly 7,000 listed in ICFS.
- Four respondents reported foreign-adversary ownership, with at least two additional nonresponding holders identified from Commission records.
- New material-change reports are due within 30 days.
