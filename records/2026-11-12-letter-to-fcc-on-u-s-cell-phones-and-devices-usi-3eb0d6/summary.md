The House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party asked the FCC to address U.S. devices’ reported reception and processing of signals from the PRC BeiDou and Russian GLONASS satellite navigation systems, which the letter says are unauthorized under current FCC rules.

**What it does**
Chairman Mike Gallagher’s letter to FCC Chairwoman Jessica Rosenworcel describes the committee’s understanding that U.S. mobile phones and other connected devices are using signals from Chinese and Russian GNSS constellations. It asks the FCC to explain what enforcement measures it applies to such use, whether it plans to authorize additional satellite systems from U.S. allies, and how it assesses the national-security risks associated with adversarial satellite signals.

**Who it affects**
The concerns reach mobile-phone users, carriers, device and component manufacturers, and operators of systems that rely on satellite positioning, including the energy grid, stock exchanges, aviation, and wireless 9-1-1 services. The letter also puts the FCC’s rules and approval process in focus.

**Why it matters**
The letter emphasizes that FCC rules permit GNSS receivers to use the U.S. GPS system and foreign systems approved through an FCC process adopted in 2011. It notes that only Europe’s Galileo GNSS had been approved as of the letter, and argues that reliance on unauthorized Chinese or Russian signals raises compliance and national-security questions. It also attributes some use to the United States’ delayed deployment and activation of next-generation GPS satellites.

**Key dates and numbers**
- March 12, 2024: letter date.
- 2011: year of the FCC approval process cited in the letter.
- 1 foreign GNSS identified as approved: European Galileo.
- 2 unauthorized constellations identified: PRC BeiDou and Russian GLONASS.
