IP Network America LLC’s letter of assurances commits the company and covered affiliates to specified national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety safeguards as a condition supporting FCC action on its pending application.

**What it does** IP Network agrees to make U.S. customer billing records, subscriber information, and related records available in the United States in response to lawful U.S. process. It must provide DOJ with security-architecture materials and a concept of operations for domestic and remote network operations centers, keep those materials updated annually, and refer non-U.S. government requests for U.S. records or domestic communications to DOJ unless U.S. law or a U.S. court order prohibits disclosure. The commitments also require reporting material ownership, network, service, and security changes; maintaining a U.S. point of presence and DOJ-approved point of contact; reporting cyber threats and incidents; allowing DOJ site reviews; and identifying personnel with root access. Breach may support a DOJ request that the FCC modify, revoke, or terminate relevant authorizations.

**Who it affects** The obligations apply to IP Network and its controlled subsidiaries or affiliates providing domestic communications services, and govern interactions with DOJ, the FCC, and U.S. law-enforcement agencies.

**Why it matters** The LOA illustrates Team Telecom mitigation attached to FCC authorization, emphasizing U.S. access to communications records, control over foreign-government requests, operational transparency, cybersecurity reporting, and continuing DOJ oversight.

**Key dates and numbers**
- FCC file number: ITC-214-20130204-00048.
- Security architecture and CONOPS are due within 45 business days after FCC consent; both are updated annually from the 214 grant date.
- Non-U.S. government requests must be referred to DOJ as soon as possible and no later than 15 business days after receipt or discovery.
- Cyber threats and incidents must be reported within 10 business days; material changes generally within 30 business days.
- DOJ may conduct domestic-facility reviews with 48 hours’ advance notice.
