The House Select Committee on Strategic Competition Between the United States and the Chinese Communist Party urges President Biden to demand concrete human-rights, security, and economic concessions from Xi Jinping before or during the November 2023 APEC summit.

**What it does** The letter argues that the administration’s recent emphasis on bilateral engagement with the PRC has delayed or weakened defensive actions, despite earlier sanctions and strengthened semiconductor export controls. It presents ten requested deliverables, including releasing wrongfully detained or exit-banned U.S. citizens and other named detainees, dropping charges against Jimmy Lai and the “Hong Kong 47,” establishing know-your-customer requirements for fentanyl-precursor shipments, ending unsafe military encounters, stopping specified activity around Taiwan and the Second Thomas Shoal, and suspending Xinjiang “mutual pairing assistance” programs.

**Who it affects** The direct addressee is President Biden, with Xi Jinping and the PRC as the intended counterpart. The requests concern U.S. citizens, Hong Kong and Uyghur detainees and their families, U.S. military and maritime or aviation assets, the Philippines, and entities involved in fentanyl-precursor shipments and Xinjiang labor programs.

**Why it matters** The letter frames the APEC meeting as a final opportunity to test Beijing’s good faith and calls for a more assertive U.S. posture if the requested actions are not delivered. It also records the committee’s criticism of the administration’s pace of sanctions and Entity List additions.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Dated November 8, 2023; the known document date is November 9, 2023.
- The requested actions comprise 10 numbered demands.
- The demands were to be announced before the end of the November APEC summit in San Francisco.
