The House Select Committee on the CCP requests that Purdue University disclose policies, enrollment and funding data, and oversight mechanisms concerning Chinese national students’ access to federally funded, sensitive, and dual-use research.

**What it does**
The March 18, 2025 letter from Chairman John Moolenaar to Purdue President Mung Chiang frames Chinese-national enrollment in advanced STEM programs as a national-security and technology-transfer concern. It asks Purdue to provide written responses by April 1, 2025, including the prior universities and research affiliations of Chinese national students, their tuition and program funding sources, research and laboratory participation, and country-by-country applicant, admission, and enrollment data. It also asks 14 questions about graduate-student composition, tuition revenue, federally funded research participation, foreign-national restrictions, monitoring, China-based collaborations, talent-program ties, export-controlled coursework, post-graduation destinations, screening, and faculty research relationships.

**Who it affects**
The immediate recipient is Purdue University, particularly officials responsible for graduate admissions, research compliance, export controls, and national-security oversight. The requested information concerns Chinese national undergraduate, graduate, and PhD students; university programs and laboratories; faculty collaborations; and research funded by DARPA, DoD, DOE, or NSF. The letter also identifies Chinese institutions, talent-recruitment programs, and companies as subjects of inquiry.

**Why it matters**
The letter illustrates congressional scrutiny of universities as potential conduits for foreign access to critical and emerging technologies, including AI, quantum computing, semiconductors, robotics, and aerospace. It links university enrollment and research practices to concerns about intellectual-property theft, dual-use technology transfer, talent recruitment, and the retention of U.S. technical expertise. It invokes the Select Committee’s investigative authority under H. Res. 5, section 4(a).

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated March 18, 2025; catalogue date is March 19, 2025.
- Requested response deadline: April 1, 2025.
- The letter contains 6 requests for information and 14 numbered questions.
- It cites a September 2024 joint committee report and H. Res. 5, section 4(a).
