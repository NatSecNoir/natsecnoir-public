The House Select Committee on the CCP asks National Security Advisor Michael Waltz to support tighter controls on advanced AI chips, restrictions on PRC AI systems in U.S. government and critical infrastructure, and stronger safeguards against transshipment.

**What it does** This January 29, 2025 letter urges the National Security Council to consider placing export controls on Nvidia’s H20 and similarly sophisticated chips, while addressing chips designed for AI inference as well as training. It also requests updates to the Federal Acquisition Regulations to restrict federal acquisition of AI systems based on PRC models such as DeepSeek, except for appropriate intelligence and research purposes; consideration of Executive Order 13873 to limit PRC AI systems in U.S. critical infrastructure; and stricter licensing for shipments through high-risk third countries.

**Who it affects** The requests primarily concern U.S. agencies reviewing export controls and procurement, Nvidia and other advanced-chip suppliers, DeepSeek and other PRC AI providers, U.S. critical-infrastructure operators, and jurisdictions used for potential diversion or transshipment, including Singapore.

**Why it matters** The letter argues that regulatory gaps could enable the PRC to advance its AI capabilities and obtain U.S. user data. It points to DeepSeek’s use of Nvidia H20 and H800 chips and to the company’s stated storage of collected information on servers in the PRC as reasons for coordinated export, procurement, infrastructure, and data-security measures.

**Key dates and numbers**
- January 20, 2025: Presidential memorandum directed State and Commerce to review the export-control system.
- January 29, 2025: Letter date shown in the document; the catalogue record dates it January 30, 2025.
- Nvidia’s H20 is described as outside the cited U.S. export controls; DeepSeek also used the H800.
- Nvidia reported Singapore as representing 22% of recent quarterly revenue, despite disclosures about onward destinations.
