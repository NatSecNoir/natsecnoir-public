The Letter of Agreement makes Bharti Airtel (USA) Ltd.’s telecommunications authorization contingent on extensive commitments addressing U.S. national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety concerns.

**What it does** Bharti agrees to maintain its headquarters in the United States; screen personnel with unescorted access to domestic communications infrastructure; comply with CALEA and lawful U.S. process; designate DOJ-approved compliance and law-enforcement points of contact; and submit a DOJ-approved Network and Systems Security Plan. It must protect U.S. records and domestic communications from unauthorized foreign-government access, store U.S. records at its Hoboken, New Jersey address, provide equipment and vendor inventories, give advance notice of specified corporate, service, outsourcing, vendor, and network changes, report cybersecurity incidents, permit site visits, and provide books, records, audits, and annual compliance reports. DOJ may object to equipment, providers, or proposed changes, and a breach may support a request that the FCC modify, revoke, or terminate relevant authorization.

**Who it affects** The obligations bind Bharti and its successors, subsidiaries, and affiliates, as well as personnel, contractors, vendors, managed service providers, and foreign-owned telecommunications partners whose activities could expose U.S. communications or customer information. DOJ receives review, approval, notice, audit, and objection rights; the FCC is notified that DOJ has no objection after execution.

**Why it matters** The agreement converts Team Telecom national-security and lawful-access concerns into continuing operational controls for a Section 214 applicant, especially concerning foreign access, network equipment, records custody, and corporate or service changes.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated November 6, 2017; Bharti’s security plan is due within 60 days.
- Bharti must nominate a law-enforcement point of contact within 15 days.
- Equipment inventory is due within 180 days; DOJ has 45 days to object to listed equipment or providers.
- Required notices commonly provide 5, 15, 30, or 60 days, depending on the event.
- Annual reports and recurring records submissions begin November 1, 2018; records are required annually for four years.
