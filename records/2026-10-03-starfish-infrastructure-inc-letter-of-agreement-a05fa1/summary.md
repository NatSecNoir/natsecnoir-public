The Agreement requires Starfish Infrastructure Inc. and Google LLC to implement national-security and law-enforcement mitigation measures as a condition of Starfish’s proposed FCC license for the Tabua private submarine cable system connecting the United States, Fiji, and Australia.

**What it does** This National Security Agreement establishes compliance obligations monitored by the Committee for the Assessment of Foreign Participation in the United States Telecommunications Services Sector, represented by DHS, DOJ, and DoD. Starfish must appoint vetted security and compliance officials; provide cable-system, covered-third-party, principal-equipment, and service-provider information; obtain advance review or non-objection for specified equipment, providers, personnel, facilities, and sensing technologies; and maintain physical, logical, cybersecurity, personnel-screening, incident-reporting, training, audit, and annual-reporting programs. The Companies must report qualifying incidents to the monitoring agencies within 48 hours, while service providers must report breaches to Starfish within 72 hours.

**Who it affects** The principal regulated parties are Starfish and Google, including their affiliates, contractors, service providers, equipment suppliers, covered third parties, and personnel with access to Tabua, its network operations center, principal equipment, communications, or sensitive U.S. records. The FCC is to condition the cable landing license on compliance.

**Why it matters** The Agreement links Team Telecom mitigation to submarine-cable licensing and creates continuing controls over foreign access, supply-chain risk, sensitive records, and operational resilience. Breach may lead the Committee to recommend that the FCC modify, condition, revoke, cancel, terminate, or nullify the relevant authorization.

**Key dates and numbers**
- May 14, 2025: DoD and DOJ signatures; the Agreement states obligations begin on the last signature date.
- September 20, 2024: FCC referral of the application to the Committee.
- 30–90 days: recurring deadlines for nominations, notices, policies, equipment/provider submissions, and CMA objections.
- 48 hours: Company deadline to report specified incidents; 72 hours: service-provider breach-reporting deadline.
- Five percent: foreign ownership or control threshold triggering notice obligations; one year after service, then every two years: required risk assessments.
