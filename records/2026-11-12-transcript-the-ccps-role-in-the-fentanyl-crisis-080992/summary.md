The hearing examines evidence and testimony concerning the Chinese Communist Party’s alleged role in producing, exporting, subsidizing, and facilitating the distribution of fentanyl and precursor chemicals reaching the United States.

**What it does** The transcript records opening statements, witness testimony, and questions from members of the House Select Committee on the CCP. Witnesses describe China as the principal source of fentanyl precursor chemicals and discuss alleged government subsidies, tax incentives, awards, ownership interests, online sales, money laundering, and limited cooperation with U.S. law enforcement. Testimony also addresses Mexican cartels, ports of entry, social media, cryptocurrency, mail and freight shipments, and the effects of the 2019 Chinese scheduling of fentanyl-related substances.

**Who it affects** The discussion concerns Americans affected by overdose and addiction, victim families, federal agencies including DEA and CBP, Congress, chemical producers and online vendors in China, Mexican trafficking organizations, social-media platforms, and international counternarcotics partners.

**Why it matters** Members and witnesses frame fentanyl as both a public-health and national-security threat, while differing on the effectiveness of renewed U.S.-China engagement. Proposed responses include sanctions and economic pressure, stronger interdiction and data sharing, regulation of online sales and cryptocurrency, action against money laundering, treatment and harm-reduction measures, and disruption of production at its source.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Hearing held April 16, 2024; the committee report was described as issued the same day.
- Testimony cites 97 percent of illegal fentanyl precursors as coming from China and more than 76,000 U.S. fentanyl deaths in 2022.
- RAND-related testimony identifies 166 unique illegal Chinese sellers and 21 key action items supported by 78 enabling recommendations.
- Witnesses discuss the 2019 scheduling of fentanyl-related substances, the 2020 Wuhan lockdown, and the November 2023 Biden-Xi agreement.
