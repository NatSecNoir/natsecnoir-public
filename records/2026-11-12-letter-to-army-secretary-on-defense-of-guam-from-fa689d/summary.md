The House Select Committee on the Strategic Competition Between the United States and the Chinese Communist Party asks Army Secretary Christine Wormuth to explain delays in the IFPC Increment 2 program and the Army’s plans to protect Guam from Chinese cruise-missile attacks before that system is operational.

**What it does** The letter describes Guam’s strategic importance and vulnerability, then requests answers on whether IFPC Inc 2 remains on schedule, the causes and location of supply-chain problems affecting Dynetics launcher prototypes, and the expected dates for prototype delivery, operational assessment, and fielding. It also asks what interim defenses the Army will provide, including possible use of NASAMS, Iron Dome Defensive Systems-Army, Navy Aegis systems, or allied Japanese and Australian air-defense capabilities. The committee requests a response by December 1, 2023.

**Who it affects** The inquiry is directed to the Secretary of the Army and concerns Army acquisition and deployment decisions involving IFPC Inc 2, Dynetics, Guam, the Navy, and potential allied air-defense integration. It also addresses congressional oversight under the Select Committee’s authority established by H. Res. 11.

**Why it matters** The letter argues that delays could push IFPC Inc 2 fielding beyond FY26 and leave Guam exposed to cruise missiles threatening U.S. bases, troops, and regional operations. It highlights the cost and limited availability of Patriot PAC-3 as an alternative and presses the Army to identify an affordable interim defense posture.

**Key dates and numbers**
- November 13, 2023: Letter date.
- December 1, 2023: Requested response deadline.
- 2021: Army selected Dynetics to develop the IFPC Inc 2 launcher prototype.
- March 2024: Previously planned receipt of 16 launcher prototypes and 60 fieldable interceptor prototypes.
- FY26: Previously anticipated fielding timeframe for IFPC Inc 2; operational assessment had shifted to early FY25.
- $4.1 million: Cited cost of one PAC-3 missile round.
- 170,000 Americans and more than 20,000 U.S. troops: Population and troop presence cited for Guam.
