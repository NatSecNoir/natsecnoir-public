The House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party and Senator Marco Rubio urge Commerce Secretary Gina Raimondo not to use ICTS authorities to approve TikTok’s proposed “Project Texas” mitigation arrangement, which they argue cannot adequately address the national-security threat posed by a PRC-controlled application.

**What it does** The letter requests Commerce records concerning changes in the June 16, 2023 Final Rule amending the ICTS rules, including the shift from risks being “addressed” through independently verifiable measures to risks being “mitigated” through measures verified by independent third parties. It seeks communications about that change, TikTok and ByteDance, possible ICTS restrictions on TikTok, federal agencies, and third-party companies. The lawmakers also urge Commerce to abandon any approach short of fully addressing the threat.

**Who it affects** The inquiry focuses on Commerce, TikTok, ByteDance, Oracle’s proposed role in Project Texas, and other PRC-controlled connected software applications that could seek similar arrangements. It also concerns executive-branch officials responsible for implementing ICTS authorities and congressional oversight of those authorities.

**Why it matters** The letter treats the Final Rule’s terminology as a possible signal that Commerce could authorize mitigation rather than prohibit a transaction. It warns that approving Project Texas could create a precedent for foreign-adversary-controlled applications to operate in the United States under third-party monitoring or other limited safeguards.

**Key dates and numbers**
- June 16, 2023: Commerce’s ICTS Final Rule was published.
- June 23, 2023: The letter was issued.
- June 30, 2023: Deadline requested for Commerce’s production of documents.
- H.R. 1081 and S. 347: ANTI-SOCIAL CCP Act numbers cited in the letter.
- H. Res. 11: Resolution cited as the Select Committee’s jurisdictional authority.
