The House Select Committee on the CCP asked U.S. Trade Representative Katherine Tai to consider opening a new Section 301 investigation into PRC subsidies, discriminatory policies, and other practices affecting automobile and electric-vehicle markets.

**What it does** The November 7, 2023 letter argues that the PRC is using subsidies, localization requirements, technology-transfer pressure, control of battery inputs, and other state-directed measures to build globally dominant automobile and EV industries. It requests evaluation of whether a new Section 301 investigation is warranted and what countermeasures should be adopted. The members support maintaining and increasing existing Section 301 tariffs on PRC automobiles, coordinating with trading partners, strengthening rules of origin, and addressing transshipment or production in third countries.

**Who it affects** The request is directed to USTR and concerns PRC automakers, foreign automakers producing vehicles in the PRC, U.S. automakers and workers, consumers, and trading partners. The letter specifically names BYD, Chery, SAIC Motors, NIO, and Tesla in discussing PRC-linked production, expansion, or market access.

**Why it matters** The letter frames the growth of PRC EV exports as a national economic-security and supply-chain concern, warning that subsidies and overseas production could undermine U.S. automotive capacity and circumvent country-specific tariffs. It seeks a coordinated response before PRC manufacturers gain greater access to the U.S. market.

**Key dates and numbers**
- November 7, 2023: date of the letter.
- 851%: reported increase in PRC EV exports over the preceding three years.
- 60%: stated PRC share of global electric-car sales.
- 76%: stated PRC share of global battery-cell production capacity.
- 25%: additional Section 301 tariff on PRC-imported vehicles referenced in the letter.
- 2025: year by which NIO was reported to plan entry into the U.S. market.
