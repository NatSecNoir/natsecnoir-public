The House Select Committee on Strategic Competition Between the United States and the Chinese Communist Party urges the Department of State to sponsor a federal prize competition for satellite technology that provides internet access to people in repressed nations and countries at war.

**What it does** The December 18, 2024 letter to Secretary of State Antony Blinken and U.S. Agency for Global Media CEO Amanda Bennett argues that a prize competition could create a measurable federal demand signal for satellite connectivity designed to help people circumvent government censorship and communication disruptions. It highlights potential benefits identified by the Office of Management and Budget and the Office of Science and Technology Policy, including paying for successful results, encouraging private investment, broadening participation, and attracting smaller companies and new entrants.

**Who it affects** The proposal is directed to the Department of State and would affect satellite and rocket companies, including Boeing, Lockheed Martin, and SpaceX, as well as people in authoritarian nations or countries at war who lack unfettered internet access. The letter specifically cites Russia, Iran, and China among countries with declining internet freedom.

**Why it matters** The members frame reliable internet access as essential to freedom of thought, movement, and knowledge. They argue that connectivity-focused satellite technology would advance democratic values and human rights, rather than being pursued solely for economic-development or military purposes.

**Key dates and numbers**
- December 18, 2024: date on the letter.
- October 2023: cited publication date of Freedom House’s *Freedom on the Net* report.
- Thirteenth consecutive year: period during which global internet freedom had declined, according to the cited report.
- 25 percent: share of Asia–Europe data traffic reportedly affected by four severed Red Sea telecommunications cables.
- Roughly 25: companies worldwide identified as having relevant satellite and rocket capabilities.
