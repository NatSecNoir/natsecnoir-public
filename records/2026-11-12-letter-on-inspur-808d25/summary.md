The House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party urges the Department of Commerce to add Inspur’s subsidiaries to the Entity List and close what it describes as a loophole in export-control restrictions.

**What it does** This letter, signed by Chairman Mike Gallagher and addressed to Secretary Gina Raimondo, expresses frustration that the End-User Review Committee had not extended Entity List treatment or licensing restrictions to Inspur subsidiaries after listing Inspur Group Co., Ltd. It asks Commerce to explain its failure to act and to take “immediate and regular actions” against subsidiaries allegedly facilitating transactions that would otherwise be impermissible with the listed parent. The letter also points to Commerce’s enforcement regulations and the parent company’s prior designation by the Department of Defense as a Chinese military company.

**Who it affects** The requested action would affect Inspur Group and its subsidiaries, U.S. technology firms supplying them, and Commerce’s Entity List and licensing review processes. The letter is also copied to senior officials at the Departments of State, Defense, and Energy and to Commerce’s Bureau of Industry and Security.

**Why it matters** The document frames Inspur as a major PRC supercomputing and artificial-intelligence supplier and argues that subsidiary shipments could undermine the March 2023 listing. It invokes Huawei’s stockpiling of U.S.-designed chips and recent rapid additions of entities linked to a PRC surveillance-balloon program as precedents for faster action.

**Key dates and numbers**
- March 2, 2023: Commerce added Inspur Group Co., Ltd. to the Entity List.
- March 24, 2023: Gallagher’s letter was issued.
- October 7, 2022: Commerce established the cited enhanced licensing restrictions.
- The letter references Section 1260H of the FY2021 National Defense Authorization Act.
