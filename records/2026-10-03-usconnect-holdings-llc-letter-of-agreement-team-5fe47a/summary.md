The Department of Homeland Security, with the concurrence of the Department of Justice and the FBI, states that it has no objection to removing a national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety condition from USConnect Holdings, Inc.’s international Section 214 authorization.

**What it does** This March 19, 2017 response addresses the FCC’s February 28 inquiry concerning USConnect’s request to modify authorization ITC-214-20000310-00140. The agencies confirm that, based on their review of the information presented, they do not object to removing the condition requiring compliance with commitments in a May 18, 2005 Letter of Assurance from David Cosson, which described commitments made by Eastern Kansas Holdings, Inc. to the agencies.

**Who it affects** The response directly concerns USConnect Holdings, Inc. and the requested modification of its international 214 authorization. It also relates to Eastern Kansas Holdings, Inc., whose earlier assignment transaction involved the authorization, and to the FCC, DHS, DOJ, and FBI as the coordinating and reviewing agencies.

**Why it matters** The letter records the executive-branch agencies’ non-objection to ending specified mitigation commitments associated with the carrier’s international telecommunications authorization. It also links the 2017 modification request to the earlier assignment of the authorization from Mid-South Telecommunications, Inc. to Eastern Kansas.

**Key dates and numbers**
- March 19, 2017: DHS response date.
- February 28, 2017: FCC inquiry referenced in the letter.
- ITC-MOD-20170224-00023: modification docket.
- ITC-214-20000310-00140: international 214 authorization.
- May 18, 2005: date of the referenced Letter of Assurance.
- ITC-ASG-20050405-00140: earlier assignment application.
