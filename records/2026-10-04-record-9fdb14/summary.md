The FCC seeks comment on limiting existing equipment authorizations to prohibit the continued importation and marketing of certain foreign-produced, Covered List military-grade uncrewed aircraft systems (UAS) and UAS critical components.

**What it does** This Public Notice proposes to apply the FCC’s equipment-authorization security framework to previously authorized foreign-produced UAS and critical components that meet one or more military-grade criteria. The proposed scope includes UAS weighing at least 55 pounds at takeoff; UAS capable of dispensing an “economic poison”; systems with thermal-imaging or LiDAR sensors; UAS docking stations; systems specially designed to incorporate a defense article; and specified swarming systems. The proposal would limit existing authorizations rather than revoke them.

**Who it affects** The proposal would affect manufacturers, importers, distributors, retailers, and other parties marketing or importing covered foreign-produced military-grade UAS or critical components for non-federal-government purposes. It would not cover domestic products, non-military-grade equipment, federal-government use, commercial testing or product development, already-purchased devices’ continued use, or equipment exempt from the Covered List, including qualifying Blue UAS, domestic end products, and Conditional Approvals.

**Why it matters** The FCC tentatively finds that restrictions would protect communications networks and address risks identified in the underlying national-security determination, including unauthorized surveillance, sensitive-data exfiltration, and supply-chain vulnerabilities. It also tentatively finds economic and supply-chain impacts would be limited, while seeking evidence on those effects and possible benefits to domestic production.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Released July 21, 2026; comments are due 30 days after Federal Register publication.
- Proposed cessation of importation and marketing: 180 days after Federal Register publication.
- Blue UAS and qualifying domestic end-product exemptions currently run until January 1, 2027.
- Proceeding: DA 26-758, PS Docket No. 26-189.
