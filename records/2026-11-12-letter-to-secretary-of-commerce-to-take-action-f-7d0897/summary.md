The House Foreign Affairs Committee and House Select Committee on the Strategic Competition between the United States and the Chinese Communist Party ask Commerce Secretary Gina Raimondo to respond to China’s ban on Micron memory chips and take action against efforts that could reinforce the ban.

**What it does** The letter characterizes China’s May 21, 2023 prohibition on sales of Micron products to critical information infrastructure operators as economic coercion and urges Commerce to coordinate with Japan and South Korea so allied companies do not replace Micron’s lost sales. It asks whether the Anti-Boycott Act of 2018 can prohibit U.S. and other companies from supporting or furthering the boycott, and whether ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) has sufficient connections to military and surveillance uses to warrant placement on the Entity List with a footnote 4 restriction. The committees also request written responses and a joint-committee staff briefing.

**Who it affects** The requests principally concern Commerce, the Bureau of Industry and Security, Micron, CXMT, Samsung, SK Hynix, and companies involved in memory-chip fabrication using U.S. technology. The letter is signed by Michael McCaul and Mike Gallagher and copied to State Secretary Antony Blinken, National Security Advisor Jake Sullivan, and Commerce Under Secretary Alan F. Estevez.

**Why it matters** The letter links the Micron dispute to export controls, allied coordination, and congressional oversight of coercive trade practices. It advocates using existing anti-boycott and export-control authorities to impose costs on PRC conduct, while acknowledging that the Anti-Boycott Act had apparently not previously been used for PRC economic coercion.

**Key dates and numbers**
- China’s Micron ban: May 21, 2023.
- Requested response and briefing deadline: June 15, 2023.
- Letter date: June 1, 2023.
- Relevant authorities: Anti-Boycott Act of 2018, Export Control Reform Act of 2018, and H. Res. 11.
