The Letter of Agreement establishes national-security and law-enforcement commitments connected to the proposed transfer of control of Peerless Network’s domestic and international Section 214 authority to Infobip.

**What it does** The agreement requires the PNI Companies—Peerless Network, Inc., Airus, Inc., and WaveNation, LLC—and Infobip Ltd. and Infobip Inc. to maintain U.S.-based law-enforcement and security contacts, support lawful U.S. process and CALEA compliance, protect U.S. Records and domestic communications infrastructure, and restrict foreign access or disclosure absent USDOJ consent or a U.S. court order. It also imposes security-plan, incident-reporting, equipment, vendor, network-operations-center, outsourcing, ownership-change, annual-reporting, and site-visit requirements.

**Who it affects** The commitments apply principally to the PNI Companies and Infobip, including relevant employees, foreign personnel, vendors, contractors, outsourced or offshored service providers, and network-equipment suppliers. USDOJ, including the FBI, reviews or objects to numerous proposed personnel, systems, locations, providers, and plans.

**Why it matters** The LOA conditions the proposed telecommunications ownership transfer on continuing controls over sensitive customer and network information, lawful interception, foreign-government requests, cybersecurity, and supply-chain risk. Breach or unresolved national-security concerns could support FCC action affecting the relevant authorization.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Dated May 23, 2022; concerns the transfer application identified as ITC-T/C-20211108-00165 and WC 21-434.
- The PNI Companies must generally report security incidents or breaches within 72 hours of knowledge; CPNI incidents also trigger reporting within seven business days under the cited rule.
- Many post-approval submissions are due within 15, 30, or 60 days; annual reports begin one year after FCC approval.
- The existing international authorization is FCC File No. ITC-214-20080304-00146.
