The FCC adopts rules clarifying and streamlining its review of foreign ownership in broadcast, common carrier, aeronautical radio licensees, and common carrier spectrum lessees under Section 310(b), while preserving review of national-security and other public-interest concerns.

**What it does** The Report and Order codifies the definition of the “controlling U.S. parent” and the Commission’s treatment of deemed voting interests held by certain limited partners and LLC members. It requires petitions to identify trustees, extends the remedial process for inadvertent foreign-ownership noncompliance to privately held entities, and requires remedial petitions to include the information required for an initial petition. It also codifies procedures for substantial and ministerial amendments, clarifies that foreign investors need not maintain U.S. residency, and directs the Media Bureau to develop processing guidelines for broadcast applications during a pending remedial process. For NCE and LPFM stations, voting interests generally will be assessed through governing boards or other governing entities.

**Who it affects** The changes apply to applicants, licensees, spectrum lessees, and foreign investors subject to Section 310(b). They particularly affect entities with complex ownership chains, trusts, limited partnerships, LLCs, privately held controlling parents, and noncommercial or low-power broadcast stations.

**Why it matters** The FCC states that codifying existing practices will improve certainty, reduce follow-up filings, and shorten processing while retaining comprehensive foreign-ownership review. The order does not adopt a categorical ban on foreign ownership or a fixed processing deadline.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Adopted January 29, 2026; released January 30, 2026.
- Rules become effective 30 days after Federal Register publication, after OMB review of any nonsubstantive information-collection changes.
- Section 310(b)(4) benchmark: 25%; Section 310(b)(3) limit for applicable interests: 20%.
- Certain deemed non-controlling interests may receive advance approval up to 49.99%.
