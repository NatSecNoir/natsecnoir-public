The National Security Agreement conditions the proposed FCC license for the California-Hawaii $1 Cable System (CHI) on Verizon Business Global LLC and Hawaiian Telcom Services Company, Inc. implementing specified national-security and law-enforcement safeguards.

**What it does** The agreement establishes the Committee for the Assessment of Foreign Participation in the United States Telecommunications Services Sector as the monitoring authority, represented by DHS, DOJ, and the Department of War. It requires security officers, system and facility disclosures, traffic-interruption capability, inventories of covered third parties, principal equipment, and service providers, advance notice and non-objection for specified changes, personnel screening, physical and logical access controls, incident reporting, annual reports, audits, and government visits. The Committee will petition the FCC to condition the CHI cable landing license on compliance.

**Who it affects** The primary obligations apply to Verizon and Hawaiian Telcom as the cable’s licensees, and extend contractually to covered third parties, equipment vendors, service providers, and personnel with access to CHI, its network operations centers, principal equipment, communications, or U.S. records.

**Why it matters** This is a Team Telecom mitigation agreement for a U.S. subsea cable connecting Makaha, Hawaii, and Morro Bay, California. Breach may lead to a recommendation that the FCC modify, condition, revoke, cancel, terminate, or invalidate the relevant authorization.

**Key dates and numbers**

- Security incidents: report to the CMAs within 48 hours; third-party breaches within 72 hours.
