The Department of Homeland Security, with the concurrence of the Department of Justice, asks the FCC to terminate Redes Modernas de la Frontera SA de CV’s international Section 214 authorization and associated Letter of Assurance because the company is no longer in business.

**What it does** The letter requests that the FCC terminate, declare null and void, and no longer give effect to file number ITC-214-20070515-00189 (DA No. 07-3505), granted on July 27, 2007, together with Redes’s July 10, 2007 Letter of Assurance. The agencies base the request on information from the company’s designated point of contact, DHS open-source research, and the absence of FCC filings reporting international traffic and revenue since 2007.

**Who it affects** The requested action directly concerns Redes Modernas and its Section 214 authorization. It also concerns the DHS, DOJ, and FBI officials responsible for oversight of the mitigation commitments, and the FCC as the authority asked to terminate the authorization. The document identifies Sharon Thomas of Technologies Management, Inc. as the former point of contact.

**Why it matters** The letter illustrates Team Telecom follow-up when a carrier subject to national-security commitments appears to have ceased operations. It connects the requested termination to the carrier’s July 10, 2007 LOA and to the agencies’ prior non-objection to the authorization, rather than alleging a substantive violation of the LOA.

**Key dates and numbers**
- April 13, 2016: letter date.
- July 27, 2007: international Section 214 authorization granted.
- July 10, 2007: LOA date.
- July 31, 2012: point of contact informed DHS that Redes was no longer in business.
- ITC-214-20070515-00189; DA No. 07-3505: authorization identifiers.
