The Agreement establishes national-security, law-enforcement, and public-safety mitigation obligations for the U.S. portions of the Gemini Bermuda Cable System and supports conditioning FCC approval of the pending transfer-of-control application on compliance.

**What it does** The parties require a U.S.-based network operations center, capabilities to interrupt U.S. traffic, inventories and security, restoration, network, and facility plans, advance notice of material changes, personnel screening, incident reporting, DHS access and review, and annual compliance reporting. The Gemini Parties must also request that future relevant FCC authorizations be conditioned on the Agreement. DHS may seek enforcement, including FCC modification or revocation, civil or criminal sanctions, and injunctive relief. The Agreement terminates the prior 2008 National Security Agreement upon its Effective Date.

**Who it affects** The obligations apply separately to Verizon Business Global LLC, CWC New Cayman Limited, Columbus Networks USA, Inc., and Liberty Global plc, collectively described as the Gemini Parties, with DHS responsible for oversight and the FCC positioned to impose the authorization condition. The measures focus on domestic communications infrastructure, U.S. facilities, network-management information, vendors, contractors, and personnel with access.

**Why it matters** This is a Team Telecom mitigation instrument linking submarine cable transfer approval to operational control, access restrictions, cybersecurity, ownership-change reporting, and government verification. It illustrates how national-security commitments become enforceable conditions on FCC submarine cable authorization.

**Key dates and numbers**
- The FCC transfer-of-control application was filed November 30, 2015, under FCC File No. SCL-T/C-20151130-00033.
- The document identifies March 3, 2008 as the date of the prior NSA and the supplied record’s document date; the new Agreement becomes effective upon the last signature.
- Principal equipment and specified plans are due within 90 business days; U.S. points of contact within 60 days.
- Material incidents generally require notice within 10 business days; material equipment changes require 10 business days’ advance notice, and new NOCs require 30 business days’ advance notice.
