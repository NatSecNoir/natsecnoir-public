The House Select Committee on the CCP asks the Departments of State and Defense to strengthen action against potentially military-relevant Chinese dual-use research conducted in the Arctic territories of U.S. NATO allies.

**What it does**
The letter describes research associated with CETC’s China Research Institute of Radiowave Propagation (CRIRP) at the Yellow River Station in Svalbard, including ionospheric observation, over-the-horizon radar, satellite tracking, missile guidance, and space-weather work. It also examines the China-Iceland Arctic Observatory (CIAO), established by the Polar Research Institute of China and the University of Iceland. The committee requests departmental assessments and information about U.S. engagements with Norway, Iceland, and Sweden, including possible measures to restrict access to sensitive technologies and data.

**Who it affects**
The requests are directed to Secretary of State Antony Blinken and Secretary of Defense Lloyd Austin. The concerns principally involve PRC research institutions, the PLA, NATO allies hosting facilities or collaborations, and U.S. military interests in missile defense, submarine detection, satellite tracking, communications, and early warning.

**Why it matters**
The committee argues that Arctic data and infrastructure may improve PRC surveillance, radar, space, electronic-warfare, and strategic communications capabilities. It highlights CETC’s identification as a Chinese Military Company under the DoD 1260H framework and urges coordinated action with allies while recognizing that legitimate Arctic research may continue.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Letter dated October 16, 2024; the known catalogue date is October 17, 2024.
- Requested responses by November 15, 2024.
- Svalbard research projects cited as RiS IDs 2318, 3790, and 10972.
- CIAO reportedly received approximately $5 million in PRIC investment by 2023.
- The letter references the 1920 Svalbard Treaty and the 1951 U.S.-Iceland defense treaty.
