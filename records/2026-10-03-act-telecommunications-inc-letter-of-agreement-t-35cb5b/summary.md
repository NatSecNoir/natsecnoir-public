The Department of Justice, with the concurrence of the Department of Homeland Security, notified the FCC that ACT Telecommunications, Inc. materially breached the commitments supporting its international telecommunications authorization and requested that the FCC terminate, cancel, declare void, or revoke it.

**What it does** The letter identifies ACT’s failure to submit its required 2012 and 2013 annual certifications under the Letter of Assurances, maintain a current U.S. point of contact for lawful-access matters, and timely notify the Agencies of the change in that contact. It states that compliance with the LOA was an express condition of both the Agencies’ non-objection and the FCC’s grant of facilities-based and resale authority.

**Who it affects** The request directly concerns ACT and its FCC international telecommunications certificate, file number ITC-214-20081201-00519. It also concerns the FCC, DOJ, DHS, and the U.S. law-enforcement personnel responsible for enforcing the LOA’s compliance and lawful-demand provisions.

**Why it matters** The filing illustrates Team Telecom mitigation enforcement through a request to withdraw an FCC authorization when a carrier fails ongoing national-security and law-enforcement cooperation obligations. It does not itself state that the FCC granted the requested revocation.

**Key dates and numbers**
- May 9, 2014: DOJ letter filed with the FCC.
- October 27, 2009: FCC authorization and DA No. 09-2331.
- October 20 each year: annual LOA certification deadline; ACT missed 2012 and 2013.
- September 23, 2013: former point of contact Eric Fishman reported that he could no longer serve.
- More than 228 days had elapsed without a replacement designation when the letter was written.
- The LOA referenced Communications Assistance for Law Enforcement Act, 47 U.S.C. § 1001 et seq., compliance.
