Eighty-two percent of FCC equipment testing now happens in Chinese labs. The commission's new reciprocity rule bars recognition of labs in non-reciprocal economies for applications filed after December 1, 2028.

The FCC adopts rules limiting recognition of equipment-authorization testing and conformity-assessment bodies to the United States and economies with reciprocal arrangements, while opening a further rulemaking on additional integrity safeguards.

**What it does** The Third Report and Order prohibits recognition of Test Labs located in non-Reciprocal Economies and applies comparable restrictions to Telecommunications Certification Bodies (TCBs) and laboratory accreditation bodies, including covered activities conducted in those economies. The Test Lab prohibition applies prospectively to applications received on or after December 1, 2028; existing grants and applications received before then are not disturbed. The FCC will maintain and update a list of Reciprocal Economies. The accompanying Third Further Notice seeks comment on measures involving legal-entity identifiers, ownership and control, post-market surveillance, intellectual property, and modernization of the Equipment Authorization System.

**Who it affects** FCC-recognized Test Labs, TCBs, accreditation bodies, device manufacturers, and applicants for RF equipment authorization are affected. The rule particularly changes the position of the hundreds of recognized Test Labs in non-Reciprocal Economies, while preserving eligibility for U.S. and qualifying foreign facilities.

**Why it matters** The FCC cites national-security access to pre-market equipment and sensitive technical data, weak reciprocal oversight, and excessive concentration of testing in the PRC. It reports that PRC laboratories handled more than 82% of distinct FCC application testing in 2025, while U.S. testing fell below 4%. Commenters warned of higher costs, delays, and capacity constraints; supporters emphasized accountability, intellectual-property protection, reciprocity, and domestic testing resilience.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Adopted October 29, 2026; released October 30, 2026.
- Test Lab prohibition effective December 1, 2028.
- FCC currently recognizes 609 Test Labs; 27% are in non-Reciprocal Economies.
- The FCC participates in eight MRAs; the docket record received 59 comments and two reply comments on the earlier notice.
