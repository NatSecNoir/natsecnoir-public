The hearing’s central holding is that the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) poses a broad, urgent threat to U.S. national security, economic competitiveness, democratic values, and the rules-based international order, requiring bipartisan action rather than continued complacency.

**What it does** The transcript records the Select Committee’s first hearing, including opening statements, sworn testimony, member questioning, and closing remarks. Witnesses describe CCP political repression, surveillance, influence operations, espionage, intellectual-property theft, military-civil fusion, coercive economic practices, forced labor, fentanyl precursor flows, and pressure on Taiwan. Members discuss policy responses including stronger deterrence, allied coordination, targeted decoupling, supply-chain resilience, export and investment controls, domestic manufacturing, semiconductor capacity, scrutiny of Chinese land and infrastructure investments, and enforcement of the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act. The hearing also addresses TikTok, WeChat, Huawei, and the risks of Chinese telecommunications and surveillance technology.

**Who it affects** The discussion is directed to Congress, executive agencies, U.S. companies and investors, manufacturers, workers, farmers, technology and telecommunications users, allies and partners, and Chinese dissidents and diaspora communities. Speakers repeatedly distinguish the CCP from the Chinese people and warn against anti-Chinese or anti-Asian stereotyping.

**Why it matters** The testimony frames economic dependence, capital flows, technology transfer, and vulnerable supply chains as sources of coercive power. It links national security to domestic industrial capacity and democratic resilience, while emphasizing that competition should protect U.S. values and avoid discrimination.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Hearing held February 28, 2023, from 7:01 p.m. to 9:58 p.m.; questions for the record were due March 7, 2023.
- Speakers cite China’s intended 2049 global leadership milestone and a possible 2027 military capability target concerning Taiwan.
- The transcript cites $1.3 trillion in U.S. investment in China, $600 billion in annual intellectual-property losses, 3.7 million manufacturing jobs lost, and 90,000 factory closures.
- Witnesses discuss 1,771 Taiwanese air-defense-zone violations in the prior year, 2,300 forced-labor seizures, and a $19 billion backlog of Taiwanese defense purchases.
