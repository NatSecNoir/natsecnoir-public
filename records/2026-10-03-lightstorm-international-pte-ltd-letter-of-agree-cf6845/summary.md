The National Security Agreement conditions RTI’s proposed U.S. submarine-cable operations on extensive security, access, reporting, and government-oversight commitments for the Japan-Guam-Australia North System (JGA-N).

**What it does** The agreement requires RTI to provide cable-system, ownership, customer, equipment, vendor, and managed-service-provider information; maintain the ability to interrupt U.S. traffic; obtain Compliance Monitoring Agency review of key facilities, equipment, vendors, testing, and security policies; screen personnel; protect physical and logical access; report specified incidents within 48 hours; and submit annual compliance reports. It also provides for government consultations, facility visits, third-party audits, and notice of foreign ownership or control changes. A breach may support a request that the FCC modify, condition, revoke, cancel, terminate, or nullify an authorization.

**Who it affects** The obligations bind RTI Solutions, RTI JGA, RTI Connectivity, their successors and affiliates, and relevant contractors, vendors, managed-service providers, personnel, and auditors. DHS, DOJ, and DoD act as the monitoring agencies, while the FCC is the licensing authority whose submarine cable authorization is conditioned on compliance.

**Why it matters** The agreement illustrates Team Telecom mitigation for a strategically sensitive submarine cable linking Guam and Japan. It places operational control, supply-chain visibility, cybersecurity, personnel screening, and continuing government review at the center of the license condition.

**Key dates and numbers**
- RTI filed its submarine cable application on November 16, 2018 (FCC File No. SCL-LIC-20181106-00035); DHS sought deferral on November 19, 2018 in TT #18-064.
- The agreement became effective upon the last signature; the document includes signature dates in June and July 2020, including July 2, 2020 and July 6, 2020.
- RTI generally has 45 days for initial disclosures and advance notices, 90 days for security policies and initial training, 48 hours for specified incident reports, and 15 days for follow-up incident reports.
- Foreign ownership or control above 5 percent requires notice within 15 days; bankruptcy-related proceedings require notice within 30 days.
