The Letter of Assurance commits Hibernia and its affiliates to security, access, disclosure, reporting, and compliance measures intended to resolve U.S. Government national-security and law-enforcement concerns about Hibernia’s international telecommunications authorization.

**What it does** The agreement covers Hibernia’s U.S. and foreign affiliates and the Hibernia Atlantic Cable System. It requires controls over principal equipment and vendors, prompt updates to equipment and supplier information, access to network-management and security information, advance notice of material maintenance, protection against unauthorized access, and cooperation with lawful U.S. process. It also restricts disclosure of covered U.S. communications information to non-U.S. governments absent required U.S. legal authorization, requires at least 18 months of relevant records, and establishes annual compliance reports, site visits, breach reporting, and a designated U.S.-based security officer.

**Who it affects** The commitments bind Hibernia Group plc, Hibernia Atlantic U.S. LLC, and identified subsidiaries and successors. They support review by the Departments of Homeland Security, Defense, and Justice, including the Defense Information Systems Agency, in connection with Hibernia’s application under Section 214 of the Communications Act.

**Why it matters** The LOA illustrates Team Telecom mitigation for an international submarine cable operator. It makes national-security assurances enforceable through continuing reporting, inspection, notification, and renegotiation obligations while preserving U.S. legal and enforcement authority.

**Key dates and numbers**
- Dated September 30, 2010.
- Concerns a pending Section 214 application identified in the document as FCC File ITC-214-20090612-0028 and related FCC proceedings.
- Initial compliance deadline: November 15, 2010, or 60 days after authorization, as applicable.
- Annual compliance reports are due on each anniversary of the LOA’s effective date.
- Certain non-U.S. legal requests must be referred to the U.S. Government within five days; relevant records must be retained for at least 18 months.
